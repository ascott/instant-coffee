<a href="/instant-coffee/listings/2024-03-13-02-vag-2-new-exhibitions-mar-9">02. EXHIBITION | VAG | 2 new exhibitions | MAR 9</a>
------------------------------------------------------------------------
The Vancouver Art Gallery will unveil two new exhibitions drawn from
the Gallerys permanent collection, celebrating a pair of significant figures in
conceptual art: American artist Lawrence Weiner (1942-2021) and Canadian artist
Garry Neill Kennedy (1935-2021).

One of the leading figures in the development of conceptual art, Weiner was a
pioneer of language as art in the mid-1960s. Throughout his fifty-year career,
Weiners text-based works have appeared in museums, galleries and public
buildings around the globe. The Vancouver Art Gallerys relationship with Lawrence
Weiner was established when the Gallery commissioned its first public artwork for
the collection"PLACED UPON THE HORIZON (CASTING SHADOWS)"in 1990,
which remains on view today on the Gallery facade along the Robson Plaza and
x"nq Xwtl'e7n Square.

Lawrence Weiner originally began making posters in the 1960s, as a way to reach a
broad audience outside of museums and galleries. The Lawrence Weiner Poster
Archive was established at the Nova Scotia College of Art and Design in 1978 and
then transferred to the Vancouver Art Gallery in 1991. The Archive has grown
substantially over time through annual gifts from the artist and is now the most
significant representation of Weiners work in this form held in a public collection.
Opening March 9, OF & ABOUT POSTERS: THE LAWRENCE WEINER POSTER
ARCHIVE (1965-2021) AT THE VANCOUVER ART GALLERY showcases the most
comprehensive collection of the artists posters to date and marks the Gallerys 30-
year relationship with Lawrence Weiner. Guest Curator Grant Arnold has
collaborated with Lawrence Weiner since the 1990s to develop
exhibitions of the
artists poster archive at venues worldwide; he began work on this landmark
exhibition in 2018 in close consultation with Weiner, prior to the artists passing in
2021.

A total of 250 posters made between 1965 and 2021 will be complemented by a
selection of Weiners editions, books, audio works and ephemera that attest to the
various ways the artists work has permeated visual culture and changed the way we
think about text and art.

Presented as a companion to OF & ABOUT POSTERS, HORIZONS is a conceptual
art project by Garry Neill Kennedy in which a selection of landscape paintings are
rehung so that the horizon lines are at the eye level of the artist, and"when viewed
together"these otherwise disparate landscapes form one continuous horizon line
around the gallery walls. Conceived in 1980, Kennedys proposal extended from the
artists parallel engagements with the medium of painting and institutional critique in
the late 1970s and early 1980s. The concept is
characteristic of Kennedy in its
playfulness, but the idea also quietly undermines curatorial control and intervenes
within the conventions of museum display. The exhibition features a selection of
historical and modern paintings from the Gallerys collection, and has been
developed collaboratively with Cathy Busby, a celebrated visual artist and Kennedys
spouse.

HORIZONS is being revisited today to offer visitors the opportunity to witness the
realization of Kennedys concept for the first time, and to help build an understanding
of the longstanding friendship and professional ties between Kennedy and Weiner,
which spanned more than forty years. The exhibition features a selection of
correspondence, drawings and notes from Weiner to Kennedy that offers a glimpse
into the nature of their relationship and conveys a sense of support, respect and
mutual admiration between the two artists.

At this extraordinary moment in the Gallerys history, it is timely to be highlighting
two luminaries
who boldly encourage visitors to consider new ways of thinking,
making and experiencing art, says Anthony Kiendl, CEO & Executive Director of the
Vancouver Art Gallery.

<a href="/instant-coffee/listings/2024-03-13-03-west-vancouver-art-museum-home-and-away-ian-wallace-6pm-mar-">03. OPENING | West Vancouver Art Museum | HOME AND AWAY: IAN WALLACE | 6PM | MAR 12</a>
------------------------------------------------------------------------
At the age of 10, Ian Wallace moved with his family from the interior of BC to West
Vancouver, where he developed his interest in becoming an artist by sketching local
views in the community. It was at this time and in this place where his life-long
admiration for the modern painting of West Vancouver artist Gordon Smith began,
when Wallace saw new work displayed at the New Design Gallery on Marine Drive.
While Wallace was a student at Inglewood Middle School, he attended a
presentation by B.C. Binning with his class. Wallace, who went on to become one of
the internationally-renowned founders of the Vancouver School of
photoconceptualism, credits these early experiences in West Vancouver as being
seminal to his development as an artist.

This exhibition begins with a selection of drawings and watercolours from the artists
youth, which are presented in tandem with new photographs taken from the same
vantage points within the community. This hyper-local work is presented together
with Wallaces 2014 work from his ongoing Hotel Series, created and exhibited in
Baden-Baden, Germany. Despite being devised far away from West Vancouver, this
series has strong ties to the community of Wallaces youth. Place plays a crucial role
in this exhibition, with an unstated acknowledgement that home is never far away,
regardless of ones physical location in the world.

With support from the Audain Foundation.

Festival 2024.

<a href="/instant-coffee/listings/2024-03-13-04-vivo-media-arts-learning-in-a-fantastically-public-medium-6p">04. OPENING | VIVO Media Arts | “Learning in a fantastically public medium” | 6PM | MAR 13</a>
------------------------------------------------------------------------
Conal McStravick: Stuart Marshall and dialogues in HIV/AIDS and 2SLGBTQIA+
video activism in Vancouver, 1983-1993.

March 13, 2024

Archival Exhibit: 6pm

Talk: 7pm

2625 Kaslo Street, Vancouver BC

Opening and Researcher Talk

Conal McStravick is a UK-based, queer, non-binary artist currently undertaking a
collaborative PhD between Northumbria University, Newcastle-upon-Tyne, UK and
the UK artists moving image organization LUX, London, UK. McStravicks research
aims towards the first complete historical and critical account of British artist and
AIDS activist, Stuart Marshall.

Marshall (1949-1993) was an influential figure in UK artists video praxis and
2SLGBTQIA+ and AIDS activist networks in Canada and North America in the 1980s
and early 1990s, before his untimely death of an AIDS-related illness. As well as
helping to cultivate UK artists' video theory and practice in the 1970s, Marshall was a
co-founder of the first UK-wide video distributor London Video Arts (LVA). LVA and its
predecessor, LUX, had a long-standing relationship with VIVO through the periodic
UK-Canada Video Exchange, which Marshall helped to establish.

In 1983, Marshall gave a workshop at Video Inn in 1983
as part of the SAW
International Festival of Video. In 1989, he was invited as artist-in-residence at Video
In and participated in the Deluding Documentary seminar. His time on the west coast
led to the production of the AIDS activist video, Robert Marshall, (1991).

This event explores and invites dialogue around the numerous ways that Stuart
Marshall's participation in practices of collaboration and cultural exchange, through
the UK and Canada's video networks, established new precedents for exploring
HIV/AIDS and 2SLGBTQIA+ histories on screen

By exploring some of the collaborative and community-based knowledge, methods
of dialogue and technologies that Marshall engaged with, we will consider how these
histories and practices speak to present and future crises and common concerns.

Archival Exhibit

The exhibit, Stuart Marshall: AIDS Activist Video vs Media Queer Bashing, features
archival materials related to Marshalls relationship with the west coast. In 1983,
Marshall traveled
to Vancouver as a presenter at the SAW International Festival of
Video. In 1989, he was invited as artist-in-residence at Video In and participated in
the Deluding Documentary seminar curated by Sara Diamond. His time here led to
the production of the AIDS activist video, Robert Marshall, (1991). In addition to
documents and ephemera, the exhibit includes videos produced by and about
Marshalls activism and personal experience of AIDS. Works will be presented using
projection and at a recreation of Video Inns 1980s-era videotheque-style viewing
station. The exhibit runs from March 14 - 21 during VIVOs regular opening hours.

VIVOs regular opening hours are Tuesday through Friday, noon - 6pm. See our
website for more information about the event and venue accessibility.

From the river to the sea invites you to their next screening event, a presentation of
a classic, rarely seen feature gem, "Leila and the Wolves" by Heiny Srour (1984,
90:00), that speaks to the intersectionality of womens struggles in occupied
Palestine and the surrounding region and their often hidden roles in history.

At the Reliance Theater, Emily Carr University of Art + Design, 520 E. 1st Ave,
Vancouver, Fri., March 15th
Doors open at 5:00, screening starts at 5:30pm

Admission by cash donation to Palestine relief in

The film delves into the collective memory of Arab women weaving together re-
enactments, archive footage and fanciful sequences to create a testament to the
history of Palestinian and Lebanese womens political struggles over the past
eight decades. It was filmed in often dangerous conditions over a period of seven
years. In the film, the protagonist Leila, a Lebanese woman living in London, time
travels through the 1900s to the 1980s, with each trip focusing on the centrality of
women in Palestinian and Lebanese resistance movements.

Following the screening, stick around for an engaging discussion.
Food graciously donated by Mazahr Lebanese Kitchen (mazahr.ca)
Childcare will be provided.
This event is being hosted by the BIPOC Creative Collective as part of Apartheid
Awareness Month @ECU.

Let's come together in these difficult times and share our presence in solidarity!

----
Bio: Born in Beirut, 23rd March 1945, Heiny Srour studied Sociology at the French
University of Beirut and read Social Anthropology at the Sorbonne in Paris. Without
film training she became a script writer, director and producer in documentary and
feature films. Innovative in form and content, in Arab and Third World Cinema, her
films often shot under danger, deal with social justice and womens issues. First
woman of the Third World selected at Cannes Film Festival in 1974 with her
documentary THE HOUR OF LIBERATION, she continued pioneering with LEILA
AND THE WOLVES (1984), THE SINGING SHEIKH (1991), RISING ABOVE :
WOMEN OF VIETNAM (1995), whilst writing and painting.

<a href="/instant-coffee/listings/2024-03-13-09-deluge-contemporary-art-aesthetic-paralysis-until-mar-16-mar">09. EXHIBITION | Deluge Contemporary Art | Aesthetic Paralysis | UNTIL MAR 16 | MAR 16</a>
------------------------------------------------------------------------
Aesthetic Paralysis
Enzo Cillo, Sarah Fuller, Michael Heindl, Ed Janzen, Jillian McDonald, Brad Muir,
Jens Pecho, Vera Sebert

February 17 to March 16, 2024
Online at deluge.ca

Deluge Contemporary Art
636 Yates Street, Victoria BC | deluge.ca

How does one make art about being unable to make art? Weve used the phrase
aesthetic paralysis to describe this place of inbetweenness, realizing it could be a
temporary or permanent phenomenon. Creative blocks are usually depicted as
clichs"the hamfisted tropes presented
in popular entertainment. In reality, this
ambivalence is a common occurrence in the lives of working artists but it is largely
hidden from view, a hopefully passing condition. How is this limbo overcome? When
does it end? (It always involves time but, of course, time ends everything.)

The eight moving image artists in this online exhibition have engaged with the
frustration of the blank page, canvas and especially the screen in the extraordinary
way that art can address thorny or liminal problems. Who better to see the contours
of voids and the poetry of silence even as they fear being surplus or unoriginal?
They shift the frame to create structure and image from nothingness and sit
comfortably with stasis. They consider how they are in thrall to their artistic output,
the privilege of their creative exploration, when capitulation or cessation is a useful
strategy and whether recombination can offer a new approach to old problems.

Is any artistic action or imagined narrative an
appropriate response to perceived
futility in the face of world crises? We think so, in the unflinching acceptance of the
failures and triumphs of the creative process: Try again. Fail again. Fail better.

Deluge Contemporary Art
636 Yates Street
Victoria, BC
