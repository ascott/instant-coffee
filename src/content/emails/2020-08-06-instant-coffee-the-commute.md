<a href="/instant-coffee/listings/2020-08-06-01-arts-assembly-the-commute-jaye-simpson-migrational-memories">01. EXHIBITION | Arts Assembly | the commute: jaye simpson | Migrational Memories</a>
------------------------------------------------------------------------

the commute: jaye simpson | Migrational Memories
to listen and read full curatorial statement >>> artsassembly.ca/the-commute and on
soundcloud at soundcloud.com/artsassembly

Were pleased to present the third audio artwork in the commute by jaye simpson.
Migrational Memories, follows the #20 bus as simpson narrates a series of what they
initially thought were disconnected experiences, but later realize all occurred around
Commercial Drive. We, the listener, are guided through their experiences; love, family
history, the systemic racism and discrimination of the countrys social welfare and
educational systems.

jaye simpson is an Oji-Cree Saulteaux Indigiqueer writer, artist, performer and activist
from the
Sapotaweyak Cree Nation, while their settler ancestry is Scottish and French.
Their poems and essays are published in Poetry Is Dead, This Magazine, PRISM
international, SAD Magazine, GUTS Magazine, Room, Todays Parent, Grain, and
SubTerrain. simpson is also published in Hustling Verse: An Anthology of Sex Worker
Poetry, as well as Love After The End: An Anthology of Two-Spirit and Indigiqueer
Speculative Fiction. simpson is currently resisting, ruminating and residing on
Musqueam, Tsleil-waututh, and Squamish First Nations territories, colonially known as
Vancouver. it was never going to be okay is their first book.

<a href="/instant-coffee/listings/2020-08-06-04-the-alternator-centre-for-contemporary-art-kelowna-audie-mur">04. EXHIBITION | The Alternator Centre for Contemporary Art | Kelowna | Audie Murray | Amanda Wood | JUL 31</a>
------------------------------------------------------------------------

Audie Murray - As Old As The Hills
July 31 - September 12

The Alternator Centre for Contemporary Art is pleased to have artist Audie Murray
exhibiting in the main space of the gallery. Audie Murray is an artist who also practices
handpoke and skin stitching tattooing traditions. In As Old As The Hills, Audie explores
how the adornment of our bodies through clothing, jewelry and tattoos carry with them
power and protection. She is curious about tattoo culture as a whole and how the term
traditional tattoos carry multiple definitions. In this exhibition, Audie asks many
questions, such as how do old school traditional American tattoos reflect in current
culture? How does historical slang continue to perpetuate colonial perspectives? And, at
what point does tattoo culture at large converge with Indigenous cultures?

Find the answers to these inquiries and more in the main space from July 31 to
September 12.

https://www.alternatorcentre.com/events/audie-murray-as-old-as-the-hills

Amanda Wood - Robustness To Uncertainty
July 31 - September 12

The Alternator Centre for Contemporary Art is pleased to host Vancouver based artist in
the window space of the gallery. Amanda Wood is an interdisciplinary artist living and
working on the traditional, ancestral and unceded territory of the Coast Salish peoples.
Her practice is process and materials driven. Current work considers time, labour,
physical gesture, connectivity, digital space and self-organizing systems.

In Robustness To Uncertainty, Amanda explores how digital processes and physical
experiences
can be connected through weaving. She creates complex forms through
manipulation of materials in a systematic way.

Robustness To Uncertainty opens Friday, July 31 and runs until September 12.

https://www.alternatorcentre.com/events/amandawood

<a href="/instant-coffee/listings/2020-08-06-05-fonderie-darling-conversation-vikky-alexander-vincent-bonin-">05. TALK | Fonderie Darling | Conversation | Vikky Alexander, Vincent Bonin & Caroline Andrieux | AUG 6</a>
------------------------------------------------------------------------

Find out more about Vikky Alexander's work and her Nordic Rock exhibition during a
conversation between the artist, the author Vincent Bonin and the curator Caroline
Andrieux. The event will be streamed on YouTube, between 5:30 p.m. and 6:30 p.m.
The first version of the series of sculptures that will be the subject of this discussion was
conceived in 1988. The series consisted of permutated glass panels placed together to
resemble minimalist furniture, summoning a zero degree of the bourgeois
interior. The
sculptures were shown during the first portion of the 1990s through different
configurations in individual and group exhibitions settings, in which they blurred the
boundaries separating context and artwork. The discrepancy between the inaugural
series, which was presented in the 1980s / 1990s, and the recent version, will be one of
the topics of this conversation. Alexander and her interlocutors will address the way in
which the artist has produced new readings of her own works of the past in recent years
by reprinting them or building them anew with added features. The conversation will also
revolve around the artist's murals, which, since the 1980s, have used the register of the
decorative to examine social variables of taste in opposition to modernist architectures
criteria of optical authenticity.

VINCENT BONIN is a curator and author. In 2012, with curator Catherine J. Morris, he
co-organized Materializing Six Years: Lucy Lippard and the Emergence of Conceptual
Art at the Elizabeth A. Sackler Center for Feminist Art, Brooklyn Museum. In 2014, he
organized the exhibition Dun discours qui ne serait pas du semblant / Actors, Networks,
Theories at the Leonard and Bina Ellen Art Gallery and Dazibao, Montreal, focusing on
the reception of French Theory. In 2014, he curated Passages to abstraction:
Genevive Cadieux at de Muse dart de Joliette. In 2016, he organized Response, a
dialogue with philosopher Catherine Malabou, at the Muse dart contemporain des
Laurentides, Saint-Jrme. Bonin has edited and written several books. His essays
have been published by the magazines Canadian Art and Fillip, the Centre Andr
Chastel, the Muse dart contemporain de Montral, Les presses du rel, Sternberg
Press and the Vancouver Art Gallery, among others.

https://fonderiedarling.org/en/Conversation-Vikky-Alexander-Vincent-Bonin-Caroline-
Andrieux.html

<a href="/instant-coffee/listings/2020-08-06-10-archive-run-center-victoria-cabin-fever-aug-8">10. EXHIBITION | Arc.hive Run Center (Victoria) | Cabin Fever | AUG 8</a>
------------------------------------------------------------------------

Title: Cabin Fever
Artists: Bronwyn McMillin and Seb Evans
Venue: Arc.hive Run Center in Victoria
Dates: August 8-24 (weekends or by appointment)

In this exhibition both Bronwyn McMillin and Seb Evans depart from their traditional
ways of working to create small scale paintings, drawings and sculptures of everyday
objects and arrangements. Rather than representation, it is the form, colour,
and
composition of these objects that will take precedent in this exhibition. These works are
a reflection on time, specifically addressing the distortion in the experience of time
between rural and urban life. The artists experience of rural life allows for a deeper
exploration of the everyday objects and routines which in other cases pass by without
careful observation or documentation.

The impetus for this project comes from the lived experience of both artists, who have
recently moved to a rural community. This shift in lifestyle has led to a deep reflection on
time and its elasticity and changeability in their lives. The choice to create small works
requires a careful selection of the subject that is supported by the newfound space in
their daily routines. As such, these works are a reflection of the artists expanding
imaginative world that has been incubated by isolation.

<a href="/instant-coffee/listings/2020-08-06-14-ccbc-gallery-looking-glass-apertura-series-aug-13">14. EXHIBITION | CCBC gallery | Looking Glass: Apertura Series | AUG 13</a>
------------------------------------------------------------------------

Looking Glass: Apertura Series is an exhibition of fifteen new cut and polished sculptural
works by Vancouver glass artist Laura Murdoch. While these works stand together as a
series, each vessel is painted with layers of intricate and exuberant patterns, which
distinguishes it from the next.In this exhibition, Murdoch explores the idea of
containment. Using a complex and labor-intensive process, the artist has encased
numerous layered patterns within the walls of each glass vessel. When light enters the
container, the intricacies of its form become evident and demonstrate the mastery that
the artist
has over her craft.

The exhibition runs from August 13th to September 24th, 2020 at the CCBC Gallery
space on Granville Island.

Benjamin Lumb Art House is pleased to present Written on Water, a group exhibition
featuring the work of Canadian artists Pierre Coupey, Kristin Man, and Benjamin Lumb.
While the work of each artist is well known in their own right, this exhibition threads the
idea of water as subject matter and inspiration for a series of works which all derive their
inspiration from the various waters of our planet.

These artists work across various mediums, giving meaning to works, which transcend
the materials themselves. The title of the exhibition and the emotions it provokes from
the viewer can be interpreted visually through the shapes and forms found on Pierre
Coupeys iconic canvas Rock Pool VIII, which is a focal point of the exhibition. Coupeys
works can be found in notable private and public collections in Canada, the United
States, Europe, and Japan. Gallery Jones in
Vancouver and Odon Wagner in Toronto
currently represent his work. Kristin Mans delicately hand woven photographic works,
Tribute to Zong! andWhen Vesuvius Rises in Vancouver, exhibited for the first time in
Written on Water, derive from her series A-MARE. With roots in the Italian culture,
amare being the verb to love and Al mare meaning to the sea, as well as the
exploration of the celebration of diversity and the use of water as a channel which rises
above territorial and cultural borders, Mans series is born from the concept that as the
sea has no boundaries, there is no specific location where we are to love. The work of
Benjamin Lumb derives influence and inspiration from the sea in a mixed media neon
and LED installation work entitled Be Wild While You Can. Plastic objects washed up on
a Costa Rican shoreline and words found graffitied inside a derelict building by the
beach, form the basis of this piece. The synthetic materials juxtaposed with the plant
life found in nature,
illustrate the environment's resilience and power in light of its
treatment by humans. Lumbs work, like that of Be Wild While You Can, gives rise to the
creation of accompanying photographic works which illustrate his process of moving and
working in a 3 dimensional plane to that of a 2 dimensional one. Lumbs work is known
for attaching ethereal meaning to seemingly concrete objects, the meaning of which he
hopes can transcend the object itself and allow the viewer the opportunity to connect
their own metaphor to the work.

Written on Water gives the public access to ground breaking work by some of the most
progressive contemporary artists in Canada and provides a platform for thought
provoking discussion of the critical importance of water, as both a life force and vehicle
for better understanding our planet and the people in it.
