WITCHCRAFT
Laura Brothers, Sara Ludy, Brenna Murphy & Krist Wood
Curated by Nicolas Sassoon
February 19 - March 21, 2015

Opening Reception
Thursday, February 19 6-9 pm

Performance by MSHR @ 7 pm

Computer systems and networks in the 21st century are creating a seismic
shift in the human experience, affecting how we create, organize and extend
identities both individually and as a society. Within this shift, artists
engage with pervasive technologies to convey digital impressions on
primeval questions of identity, spirituality and virtuosity.

Witchcraft is a group exhibition with four American artists - Laura
Brothers, Sara Ludy, Brenna Murphy and Krist Wood - examining notions of
craftsmanship, personal mythologies and spiritual queries within the online
realm.

Within the exhibition, Sara Ludy creates beaded vessels for otherworldly
entities, Laura Brothers portrays digital expressions of arcane figures,
Krist Wood elaborates composite images of transfixing dreams, and Brenna
Murphy unfolds the vernacular structures of a meditative practice.

Curated by Nicolas Sassoon, this exhibition brings a unique contemporary
outlook on one of the most compelling areas of Internet Art. Witchcraft
offers a conversation between four major artists on the applications of the
digital in the construct of allegorical and spiritual identities.

<a href="/instant-coffee/listings/2015-02-19-06-presentation-house-gallery-we-are-continually-allen-ginsberg">06. OPENING | Presentation House Gallery | “We Are Continually..." - Allen Ginsberg | FEB 20</a>

“We Are Continually Exposed to the Flashbulb of Death:” The Photographs of
Allen Ginsberg (1953-1996).
February 21 - April 5, 2015
Opening reception February 20, 7PM.

Presentation House Gallery’s forthcoming exhibition celebrates the artistic
pursuits—both visual and verbal—of Allen Ginsberg (1926-1997), one of the
most prolific poets of the twentieth century.

During his career as a revolutionary writer, composer, and activist,
Ginsberg extensively documented his life in photographs, amassing an
archive that now encompasses thousands of images. Last year, the University
of Toronto received a donation of 7,600 of Ginsberg’s prints, the largest
single collection in the world. Presentation House Gallery is thrilled to
bring a selection of these images to British Columbia for the first time.

Taken over the course of four decades, the photographs juxtapose Ginsberg’s
ever-changing surroundings with a steadfast group of friends, lovers and
muses, alongside fellow Beat poets including Jack Kerouac, Lucien Carr,
Neal Cassady and William S. Burroughs. The prints extend beyond mere
depiction, however, tracing the poet’s literary influences through music,
political freedoms and even Buddhist philosophy. Ginsberg captioned each of
the gelatin silver prints in handwritten script, further integrating his
photographic and textual mediums. Indeed, the same shrewd honesty and
liberated spontaneity that guide Ginsberg’s poetry are also channelled
through his lens.

Organized by the University of Toronto Art Centre and the Justina M.
Barnicke Gallery with the Thomas Fisher Rare Book Library at the University
of Toronto, with special thanks to the Larry & Cookie Rossy Family
Foundation. This exhibition features a brochure with essays by Louis
Kaplan, Professor of History and Theory of Photography and New Media,
University of Toronto, and curator John Shoesmith of the Thomas Fisher Rare
Book Library, University of Toronto.

Presentation House Gallery
333 Chesterfield Avenue
North Vancouver, British Columbia
604.986.1351

<a href="/instant-coffee/listings/2015-02-19-08-skylight-gallery-erik-h-zepka-comprehensive-malfunction-feb-">08. OPENING | Skylight Gallery | Erik H Zepka - Comprehensive Malfunction | FEB 20</a>
------------------------------------------------------------------------
Erik H Zepka / x-o-x-o-x.com
Comprehensive Malfunction

Opening: February 20, 7-11pm
Feb 20-27
Skylight Gallery
163 East Pender St
Vancouver, BC

Saturated colours, science fiction shapes, a lack of coordination
- this is a room of speculative objects. Kinetic visuals and foreign
mechanical processes, each aspect disconnected. What does art
have to say about science? What role does hypothesis have in
aesthetic contexts?

Comprehensive Malfunction asks about knowledge in practice,
about materialized and manifest things, and what they have to
do with proper use. Comprehensive as in complete containers,
grasping, a notion of understanding - and malfunction exploring
how things work, when they work, and when they don't.
Understanding is tested when put into practice, failure becoming
apparent upon breakdown.

Glitches, noise, errors and the fallible make up a visual approach
to the limits of our action. Comprehensive Malfunction looks to
explore that, to explore the new technological products of knowing,
and to find the disjunctive seams of both their understanding and
undoing.

--

Erik H Zepka is an interdisciplinary researcher and conceptual
new media artist with a background in various sciences. His work
can be found at x-o-x-o-x.com and erikzepka.com.

<a href="/instant-coffee/listings/2015-02-19-10-221a-vancouver-especially-feb-21">10. OPENING | 221A | Vancouver Especially | FEB 21</a>
------------------------------------------------------------------------
February 21, 2–4pm
Outdoors: Likely Cold, Hot or Rainy at 271 Union Street

Vancouver Especially (A Vancouver Special scaled to its property value in
1973, then increased by 8 fold) is an installation by Ken Lum presented at
221A’s new outdoor site at 271 Union Street.

The installation is a 3:1 scale replica of a mass-produced, Vancouver
architectural style of homes known as the “Vancouver Special”. The scale of
the artwork is determined by the $45,000 artwork production budget,
comparable to the value of a Vancouver Special in the 1970s. Buying a
Vancouver Special with that budget today would be tiny in size (a small
relief in the front of the platform shows the actual scale), thus the
artwork was multiplied eightfold. Therefore, the artwork would be most
appropriately considered an enlargement of accepted value. Lum’s Vancouver
Especially gives us the ability to imagine an insubordinate architecture of
the future by looking to the past.

Curated as part of the Semi-Public program by Brian McBay.

<a href="/instant-coffee/listings/2015-02-19-11-burrard-arts-foundation-antoni-wojtyra-artist-talk-feb-21">11. TALK | Burrard Arts Foundation | Antoni Wojtyra: Artist Talk | FEB 21</a>
------------------------------------------------------------------------
Antoni Wojtyra: How Little I Know
Feburary 21, 2015 / 12:00 PM
108 E Broadway

Burrard Arts Foundation was pleased to host Antoni Wojtyra as artist in
residence from October 2014 to January 2015. His time at BAF resulted in an
exhibition of paintings currently on view at the gallery until February 28.
On February 21, Wojtyra will deliver an artist talk in which he will
discuss "framing" as a basic principle of pictures.

A frame is a structure that underlies or supports a system or concept. An
artist talk - generally a lecture - is an educational framing device that
artists use to promote and disseminate their work and ideas to an audience.
In the realm of paintings and photos frames protect pictures. Frames also
delimit or contain pictorial space and so, frames create pictures. How?
Why? The difference between a picture and an object necessarily becomes a
focus. Modern painting since Malevich's Black Square of 1915 should be
understood as both picture and object. Paintings exist as a tension between
being immaterial pictorial space and material physical objects. This
tension between object and picture will rambles Wojtyra towards what he
thinks of as "The Spectrum of Painting". This is a framework to attempt to
know, map and appreciate the possibilities of paint and painting. In
speaking about pictorial framing, what is outside of a picture's frame
becomes taboo. Or becomes "not a picture" or "not an object" or "not art"
and "nothing" at all. Wojtyra's talk will discuss his past work and the
work up at BAF in order to frame it for an audience by addressing framing
itself.

<a href="/instant-coffee/listings/2015-02-19-12-publication-eth-press-donato-mancini-snowline-feb-21">12. PUBLICATION | eth press | Donato Mancini - snowline | FEB 21</a>

NEW PUBLICATION
Donato Mancini
snowline
eth press
Feb 21

Print: 6×6 in., 102 pp.
$14/€12/£10 print | free download

“Mais où sont les neiges d’antan?” François Villon’s most famous line is a
kind of translation, a variation of the old “ubi sunt” trope: Where are the
things that used to be? But Villon specifically asks: Where are the snows?
Even in the thick of a snowy winter, this snow is not the same as the
remembered snows. The difference is affective, but it is also ecological:
the world’s climate is dramatically changing. Winter itself is changing.

Donato Mancini has collected over eighty translations of Villon’s line,
from Thomas Urquhart’s 1653 translation of Rabelais’s quotation of the
line, all the way up to translations by Florence Dujarric (2013) and
Michael Barnholden (2014). From these he has arranged forty – a number that
once stood for a countless number, like the forty thieves or the forty
years of the biblical flood – into a booklength poem.

Taking a cue from Caroline Bergvall’s “Via”, but deviating from it in
significant ways, snowline traces how Villon’s line has changed and yet
stubbornly stayed the same over six hundred years. It is a meditative and
pointedly nostalgiac book: You will grow older as you read it, and the
world around you will continue to melt into air.

Donato Mancini is the author of Ligatures (2005), Æthel (2007), Buffet
World (2011), Fact ‘N’ Value (2011), You Must Work Harder to Write Poetry
of Excellence (2012), and Loitersack (2014). He lives in Vancouver.

To order a print copy or download a free pdf, visit:
http://ethpress.com/snowline/

Visual Art Forums | Peter von Tiesenhausen
Monday, February 23, 2015 | 6pm
Lecture Theatre | Room 301, South Building

Join us for a talk by artist Peter von Tiesenhausen, as part of the Visual
Art Forums.

Peter von Tiesenhausen is a gifted public speaker who has presented to
audiences across Canada, the United States and Europe. He speaks from the
heart and from his personal experience. A recognized Canadian artist and
self described "reluctant activist", who most recently garnered
international attention for his 1996 "land as art" copyright claim, forcing
a gas pipeline to abandon crossing his property, von Tiesenhausen tells an
engaging story.

Against the odds, Peter has made a life as a visual artist in rural Canada,
spearheaded the construction of a straw bale, timber frame community centre
valued at $2.5 million "in the middle of nowhere" and fended off
corporations that were trying to compromise his property with pipelines.

His multidisciplinary work includes painting, drawing, sculpture,
installation, social sculpture/activisim, performance and video, and has
been exhibited widely.

This talk is part of the Visual Art Forums, presented by the Faculty of

Carr University of Art + Design, in collaboration with the Contemporary Art
Society of Vancouver and Trading Routes.

deliberately having no starting or stopping point. Many works are identical
