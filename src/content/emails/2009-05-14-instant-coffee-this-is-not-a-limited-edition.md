<a href="/instant-coffee/listings/2009-05-14-01-catriona-jeffries-gallery-loaded-may-14">01. Catriona Jeffries Gallery | Loaded | MAY 14</a>
------------------------------------------------------------------------
15 May – 20 June 2009 
Opening Reception: Thursday 14 May, 7 – 9pm

In this moment of major socio-political and economic shifts globally, Loaded presents tendencies 
within a group of artists' work that speak to the social and political. The exhibition marks a 
particular moment in history while presenting practices that transcend the specificity of time 
through a consistent probing of the complexities and contradictions of 21st century culture. 
Attending to trajectories in art history through various mediums, the artists explore the conditions 
of the world through questions of power, accountability, democracy, history, and activism.

Pointedly, the business suit appears as a key symbol throughout the exhibition, as a stand in for
power and wealth and the current failure of the cultural logic of late capitalism. Brian Jungen’s
parachute, which is constructed from men’s dress shirts and perpetually filled with air, alludes to a 
life saving device, supporting one’s fall, but also suggests artificial inflation or a hot air balloon. 
Rebecca Belmore weighs down the notion of the power suit, creating a trench-like barricade of 
bags filled with sand, while Myfanwy MacLeod’s grid of drawings entitled Do You Believe?, 
derived from depictions of the Second World War, are accompanied by texts from life insurance 
manuals that bestow the qualities of a good salesperson and the benefits of selling peace of 
mind. In these works, existential references to protectionism, self-defense, afterlife and the 
indelible connection to capital are ever-present. 

Kevin Schmidt offers an intervention into the exhibition that alarms and warns through a imitative 
bird call, as Alex Morrison reconsiders local protest movements from the 1960s in the form of 
permanent bronze monuments to freedom and a commemorative plaque reclaimed as an 
ephemeral graphite rubbing. In a similar move towards historical revisionism, Sam Durant also tips 
the history of protest on its head by inverting and providing an eternal mirror image of the iconic 
Black Panther wicker chair that served as a back drop for Huey Newton’s message of black 
power. In Durant’s early photo series Empty he also literally empties loaded contents through the
repetitive process of purging and draining bottles of alcohol, as if enacting a cleanse from over-
consumption.

Activating a common apathetic phrase and transposing it to the past tense, Ron Terada re-
configures his neon work It Is What It Is, It Was What It Was, which reflects a global attitude of 
irresponsibility and complacency. In contradistinction, following the democratic gestures of Felix-
Gonzales Torres, Geoffrey Farmer presents a sculpture initiated during time spent with a Quaker 
community in Boston Common which is linked to his ongoing interest in the rights and freedoms 
associated with the liberty bell and the reoccurring figure of the Hunchback of Notre-Dame. With a 
similar sensibility, Jin-me Yoon examines our larger metaphysical relationship to the world through 
the embodiment of a distorted phantom figure who subjugates human-centric verticality by 
crawling on the horizontal plane amidst thermal hot-spring sites in Japan that are communally 
shared.

<a href="/instant-coffee/listings/2009-05-14-02-artspeak-phinigins-wyake-aaron-carpenter-may-15">02. Artspeak | Phinigins Wyake | Aaron Carpenter | MAY 15</a>
------------------------------------------------------------------------

May 15 - May 24, 2009 

Presented on the Bonnis Media CoreVision dual outdoor screens at the intersection of Robson 
and Granville Streets every 3 minutes for 10 days. An Artspeak OFFSITE project co-presented 
with Other Sights for Artists' Projects.

Viewing and drinks, Friday, May 15, 6-8pm at the Lennox Pub (800 Granville Street)

As an OFFSITE extension of the Literally exhibition currently installed at Artspeak, Aaron 
Carpenter's new video, Ffinnigans Wwake, will be shown on the outdoor screens at Robson and 
Granville Streets. In consort with his drawings for the exhibition, Carpenter has taken James 
Joyce's text and rendered it as a dramatic text crawl akin to the one at the opening of the Star 
Wars films. Intrigued (and perplexed) by Finnegans Wake's multi-lingual puns, fractured dream 
narratives, and language experiments, Carpenter's work conflates Joyce's impenetrable opus with 
the disparately populist genre that is science fiction. Taking this notoriously difficult and 
experimental work of literature to filmic proportions, specifically referencing science fiction films, 
Carpenter sets up an interesting parallel; Joyce's work is meant to recreate the experience of 
sleep and dreams, and science fiction (as both a literary and cinematic genre) is a fantasy based
on speculative scientific discoveries and parallel universes.

<a href="/instant-coffee/listings/2009-05-14-03-henry-art-gallery-collection-access-and-outreach-manager-may">03. Henry Art Gallery | Collection Access and Outreach Manager | MAY 15</a>
------------------------------------------------------------------------

Henry Art Gallery, University of Washington, Seattle, seeks an individual with a strong academic 
and museum background to join the staff as our Collection Access and Outreach Manager. This 
unique new position will serve as a critical information bridge between departments as well as 
play a key role in outreach and education. The individual will manage the museum’s online 
experience, oversee our collections database, manage physical access to our collections, explore 
new programming and education opportunities related to the collection, and brainstorm future 
Web 2.0 strategies and projects. 
Responsibilities include: coordinating access to the Henry’s Eleanor Henry Reed Collection Study 
Center (RCSC) by serving as the RCSC liaison for the region’s educators, overseeing class visits 
and other programs, devising and implementing in-house and on-line educational programs 
related to the Henry’s collections, and managing our Visiting Fellow Program. The Henry’s 
permanent collection includes over 23,000 objects from American, European, and Asian cultures, 
primarily 20th century, including over 18,000 costume and textiles. Candidates must have an 
M.A. in Art History, Museology, Education, or a related field; a minimum of three years of museum
experience; experience handling art objects; a high degree of comfort designing and managing 
Website initiatives; excellent writing and interpersonal skills, and an ability to speak effectively to 
diverse audiences. In addition, candidates must have solid technical skills, including familiarity 
with a collections or content management system (CMS), the background and scholarship to 
learn about the collections resources of the museum, and the desire and ability to work 
collaboratively in a dynamic, fast-paced environment. This individual, an employee of the Henry 
Gallery Association, will report to the Chief Curator and Director of Exhibitions & Collections and 
work closely with the Curator of Collections, Associate Director for Communications and Outreach,
and the Curatorial team. 
To initiate and manage outreach to our diverse audience, duties include: 

Manage access to the Reed Collection Study Center and take primary responsibility for 
coordinating use of the Center by university faculty, regional educators, students, and others.
Administer the collections management system (CMS), including data exports, generating and 
editing reports using Crystal Reports, overseeing relationship with our web host, and reporting 
and correcting any technical issues.
Train incoming employees, interns, and work study students on CMS system, Website 
administration, and other appropriate systems.
Work closely with our System Administrator to plan for and deal with any technical issues that 
might arise.
With assistance from interns and work study assistants, help to select and pull works of art from
storage for viewing in the study center; supervise visitors and ensure proper handling and other 
protocols.
Create and promote on-site and on-line educational opportunities to the community. Encourage 
public’s use of our collections.
Act as primary liaison to the educational community and the public regarding inquiries into Henry
collections.
Develop dynamic outreach for education programs centered on Henry collections to the UW and 
the 24 private and community colleges in the region. These may include Roosevelt High School 
peer tour guide program; Youth In Focus, an after-school program for at-risk youth; the annual 
University Art Institute seminar; and the newly-formed Visiting Fellow Program.
Coordinate activities for the Visiting Fellow Program.
Work with curatorial and marketing departments to increase number and relevance of collection-
related public programs.
Coordinate digitization of collection information and materials. 
Oversee, maintain, and update the Website, including the Digital Interactive Galleries and the 
creation of new on-line learning tools.
Recruit and supervise interns and volunteers
The Henry’s James Turrell Skyspace, Light Reign, was unveiled to the public in July of 2003. 
Combining architecture, sculpture, and atmosphere, the work is not only a spectacular addition to
the museum’s permanent collection, but it is also an important part of the building’s architecture. 

The position is .8 FTE (i.e., 32 hours per week), with full health care benefits and other 
perquisites, including discounted transportation options and access to some UW facilities (such 
as libraries and athletic facilities). 

Interested candidates should submit a resume and a letter describing why you want to work at 
the Henry to jobs@henryart.org. Please note how you learned about this job. The position will 
close May 15, 2009. PLEASE, NO PHONE CALLS!

HAVE IN MIND AT THE ANZA CLUB
FOR IMMEDIATE RELEASE
*
On May 17th, a new monthly event will debut at the Anza Club, organized by a
team of local figures in the Vancouver arts community. Headed by Leigh Christie of eatART, Have 
In Mind is Vancouver's first monthly art event, with all proceeds going towards non-profit arts 
organizations within the community.

Have In Mind features a rotating schedule of guest curators, artists, DJ's
and musicians, with all the proceeds going to
a non-profit arts organization. Each month, the lineup is arranged and
selected by a non-profit arts organization.

Hosted by Plank Gallery, the first Have In Mind will be curated by Zoe
Peled. The event features DJs Leigh Christie and
Matthew Johnson. Installations by Zee Kesler and Lindsay Hamilton.

Lindsay Hamilton¹s installation takes as a starting point the idea of a
future apocalyptic moment in a North American city where, after centuries of abandonment, the 
culture of street signage is viewed by a new race of creatures. At this future moment, our 
civilization is being reconstructed,investigated, as others seek to understand the history
of the place through traffic markings. By obscuring the clarity of the sign
through playing with scale, hybridizing, manipulating,pairing and arranging different symbols, new 
ways of viewing the standard symbols becomes articulated.

The profits from this event will go to Plank Gallery and eatART.

Any inquiries may be directed to Leigh Christie ( organizer) or Zoe Peled (
co-organizer/curator).

Zoe M. Peled
Freelance Curator, Writer & Photographer

David Wisdom: Vancouver 1970 - 1975
May 17 to August 29, 2009
Teck Gallery at SFU’s Vancouver Campus

Artist talks & ‘slide’ shows: Friday June 5 and Friday June 12, 7PM
Room 1600, SFU Vancouver Campus, 515 West Hastings

Reception for the artist: after the June 5 talk, in the Teck Gallery

A new exhibition by Vancouver-based broadcaster David Wisdom offers locals as well as summer 
visitors an extraordinary glimpse of the pre-history of today’s Vancouver. Drawn from his personal 
archive of thousands of slides shot in the first half of the 1970s, the 30 prints in this show reveal 
Vancouver’s strange beauty prior to the economic expansion that occurred with the ‘this town 
was ripe for the picking’ realization of the early 1980s.

These pictures began their public life in a series of ‘slide festivals’ held each year from 1969 to 
1979 with participants including Ian Wallace, Rodney Graham and Jeff Wall, among many others; 
by the time of the last slide show in 1979 the four of them were among the members of the 
legendary rock band the UJ3RK5.

Wisdom worked in colour at a time when 99% of street photographers world-wide worked in black 
and white. He realized that the scene one sees from the street is as important as any ‘street 
theatre’ that one might find in the street. For those working in colour, shooting slides rather than 
colour negative film was common in the 1970s; Fred Herzog, who is now famous as a key 
recorder of the Vancouver scene from 1955 right up to the present, was working almost 
exclusively in slide film for that entire time.

This is the first exhibition of David Wisdom’s photographs in print form.

David Wisdom is a well-known member of the Vancouver art scene and is also known for his thirty 
years of broadcasting on CBC Radio with shows such as Nightlines, RadioSonic and Pearls of 
Wisdom, which he hosted until 2007.

The Teck Gallery at SFU’s Vancouver Campus is on the main floor at 515 West Hastings.
Hours: Most days 7:30AM to c. 10:30PM; closes earlier on weekends; closed statutory holidays.
Tel: 778-782-4266 | Email: gallery@sfu.ca | Web: sfu.ca/gallery

<a href="/instant-coffee/listings/2009-05-14-10-surrey-art-gallery-michael-morris-in-conversation-may-23">10. Surrey Art Gallery | Michael Morris in Conversation | MAY 23</a>
------------------------------------------------------------------------

BC artist discusses the legacy of Pop art in British Columbia in the 1960s

Pop Art in BC: Michael Morris in Conversation
May 23, 2pm
Free admission

How did Pop art influence British Columbia arts and culture in the 1960s? What are some of the 
stories connected with the artists associated with Pop who came to this province at that time? If
the mid-1960s was a watershed moment in the development of contemporary art in BC, how has 
Pop specifically influenced work that has come after? On May 23 at 2pm, Surrey Art Gallery 
curator Jordan Strom will talk to artist Michael Morris about the impact of Pop on the BC art 
scene, and how Pop impacted the province’s media and culture more broadly. Morris will discuss 
his own art practice and those of his peers in relation to the broader Pop art movement in 
Canada and abroad. This event is presented in conjunction with the exhibition Pop Prints, 
featuring works by artists from Canada, the United Kingdom and the United States on loan from 
the Vancouver Art Gallery collection.

About Michael Morris

Since the early 1960s to the present Michael Morris has played a critical role in bringing 
international art ideas and influences to British Columbia. After his birth in Saltdean, England in 
1942, Morris immigrated to Canada where he settled on Vancouver Island. During the mid to late 
1960s, Morris’s painting forged new ground in the field of Pop and Op art. During the course of 
that decade he worked as a curator at the Vancouver Art Gallery and the Centre for 
Communications and the Arts at Simon Fraser University. He and frequent collaborator, artist 
Vincent Trasov, founded the Mail art archive Image Bank in 1970. Along with the likes of New 
York artist Ray Johnson and Toronto-based collective General Idea, Morris and Trasov fused a 
Pop sensibility with the legacies of Mail, Performance and Conceptual art. He co-founded the 
Western Front in 1973, and co-organized art projects there throughout the 1970s. Morris spent 
the 1980s producing work in Berlin, and returned to Canada in the 1990s. His work is housed in 
several major museum collections such as the National Gallery of Canada, Vancouver Art Gallery, 
Montreal Museum of Fine Arts, The Museum of Modern Art, New York and the Ludwig Museum, 
Cologne. He recently collaborated on an exhibition of Attila Richard Lukacs’s polaroids at the Art 
Gallery of Alberta. Morris lives and works in both Vancouver and Victoria.

About Pop Prints

The artists represented in Pop Prints are (from Canada) Greg Curnoe, General Idea, Betty 
Goodwin, Gary Lee-Nova, Michael Morris, N.E. Thing Co., Joyce Wieland, (from the United 
Kingdom) Peter Blake, Richard Hamilton, David Hockney, Allen Jones, Joe Tilson, (from the 
United States) Jim Dine, Robert Indiana, Jasper Johns, Ronald Kitaj, Roy Lichtenstein, Claes 
Oldenburg, Kenneth Price, Robert Rauschenberg, Ernest Trova, Andy Warhol.

Pop Prints is curated by Vancouver Art Gallery senior curator Ian M. Thom, and is organized and 
circulated by the Vancouver Art Gallery with the generous support of Spectra Energy. Pop Prints 
continues to June 14.
