ARTIST TALK: Hua Jin
DATE: Saturday, June 2, 1-2pm
LOCATION: Richmond Art Gallery

Vancouver artist Hua Jin will give a tour of My Big Family and will speak 
about the project and her family stories in relation to the Chinese One 
Child policy and contemporary China. 

The photographic and video works comprising the exhibition, My Big 
Family, are part of a long-term project by Vancouver based artist, Hua 
Jin, that explores a changing family structure in China as a result of its 
population control programs, particularly the One-Child Policy 
established in 1979. Jin, who was three years old at the time, belongs 
to one of the first generations affected by the mandate. The project, 
begun in the summer of 2010, documents over fifty relatives living in 
northern and eastern China as they attend to their daily tasks. My Big 
Family dually functions as a portrait of a specific family and as a 
cultural portrait of an evolving China, undergoing dramatic economic 
and social transformations. The exhibition continues until June 10.

Jin lived in Shanghai for more than 20 years before moving to 
Vancouver in 2007. She was trained as an animator and graphic 
designer and has worked as an artistic creative director for more than 
10 years. Her works have been shown in exhibitions in China, 
Netherlands and Canada since 2005. Her photo-based works are 
included in many publications and private collections, as well as the 
public collection of Museum of Contemporary Art in Shanghai (MOCA). 
Hua Jin is a recent graduate from Emily Carr University and was the 
winner of the 2012 Canon Photography Award.

The event is open to the general public and admission is free.

Angeles) is a two-channel dystopian road trip through America's

research for our immediate consumption.

viewer into a lush environment of scrolling film and image, playing on

preservation, a place to store the moment of the life of these films. With 
overt references to materials, evocative experiences are made to butt 
up against an intense interest in the textures of film's social history.

 Geng Jianyi 
Presented at the Belkin Art Gallery and Walter C. Koerner Library, 
"Excessive Transition" (2008) by Geng Jianyi is a series of large and 
small black-and-white photographs of everyday objects from the artist's 
life. He uses techniques to create semi-transparent, abstract, and eerie 
subtle effects and employs methods such as mark-making and frottage 
to manipulate the idea of what is evidenced in photographs. Though 
Geng's work is not easy to define, "Excessive Transition" follows his 
concerns of self-effacement and the dissolution and disappearance of 
identity in a culture that is undergoing change. Geng Jianyi (b. 1962) is 
a foundational figure in contemporary Chinese art and was part of the 
artistic collective known as the Pond Association, and a major 
participant in the '85 New Wave Movement.
 
Zhang Peili 
Zhang Peili's large-scale, multimedia installation "A Gust of Wind" 
(2008) is a meditation on the unpredictable forces that threaten ideas 
of the stability of middle-class domestic life. We see a living room in ruin 
and the process of its destruction is seen from multiple perspectives on 
large video screens. "A Gust of Wind" brings up ideas about the life 
cycle of disaster and renewal and the fickle nature of economic 
progress. Considered the most important video artist in China, Zhang 
Peili (b. 1957) was a member of the '85 New Wave Movement and his 
work has been concerned with prompting emotional responses from the 
viewer as a way to generate many interpretations.
