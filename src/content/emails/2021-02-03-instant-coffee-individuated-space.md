<a href="/instant-coffee/listings/2021-02-03-02-alternator-centre-kelowna-relief-push-woe-ryb-jan-29">02. EXHIBITION | Alternator Centre [Kelowna] | Relief, Push, Woe (RYB) | JAN 29</a>
------------------------------------------------------------------------
Exhibition: Relief, Push, Woe (RYB)
Artist: Connor Charlesworth
Date: 01/29/2021 - 03/13/2021
Location: Alternator Centre for Contemporary Art - Window Gallery

Connor Charlesworth explores the network of image, text, and object in a new
exhibition at the Alternator Centre for Contemporary Art

Relief, Push, Woe (RYB) is an exhibition by contemporary Canadian artist Connor
Charlesworth exploring his ongoing interest in the relationship between images,
objects, language, and representation.

Taking place in the Window Gallery of the Alternator Centre for Contemporary Art,
Charlesworth presents a body of paintings in combination with wall drawings and text,
creating a relationship between the different components. Charlesworth is interested in
the ways this
network informs, complicates, and influences one another to generate
meaning for the viewer.

The title of this exhibition derives from an archived list of words the artist used as
jumping off points for his work. As Charlesworth describes, these works are emotive
responses which reflect the urgency of a specific moment in time. The works also
reflect the network of emotions felt over a period of time; for example, how someone
can feel relief and woe simultaneously.

Connor Charlesworth received his MFA from the University of Victoria (2018) with a
specialization in painting, and his BFA from the University of British Columbia in
Kelowna, BC (2015) where he currently teaches drawing and painting for the Faculty of
Creative and Critical Studies.

Relief, Push, Woe (RYB) will be on view in the Window Gallery of the Alternator from
January 29 to March 13, 2021.

The Alternator Centre for Contemporary Art is an artist-run centre located in Kelowna
at the Rotary Centre for the Arts, 421
Cawston Avenue. The Alternator Centre for
Contemporary Art is a registered non-profit charitable organization dedicated to the
development of the creative community. Since 1989, the Alternator has shown the work
of emerging Canadian artists, focused on innovative and non-traditional mediums
engaged in social and cultural issues.

Alternator Centre for Contemporary Art
103-421 Cawston Avenue Kelowna BC

------------------------------------------------------------------------
Gordon Winiemko
They Came Together to Perform Heroic Gestures (in the manner that was meaningful
to them)
Exhibition Jan 30th, - March 06, 2021

"Ive never been so disappointed to find that my work is timely or relevant"
Gordon Winiemko January, 6, 2020

Through the juxtaposition of two milieus (which already have some points of overlap),
Winiemko frames political protest as a performative spectacle and the assumption of
the guise of a costumed hero as political. In this way, Winiemkos work presages and
illuminates the storming of the Washington Capitol by a costumed hoard of would-be
cospatriots.

Winiemkos practice is an exploration of the relationship of the personal to the
public, of
the construction of subjectivity in relation to the cultural framework " those things we
do whether they be institutionalized to the idiosyncratic, which can be so
commonplace that we often forget how they shape our lives, for better or worse.
Winiemko fluidly moves between mediums, often taking on the role of performer both
to use the figure the artist as a signifier of subjectivity and to perform those things we
do, often in ways that subvert those practices or re-present them in the form of
interruption or intervention.

Collaboration is an important part of Winiemkos practice as a means to foreground
and model social interaction, inviting the public at large to participate in the re-
examination of otherwise transparent social structures. The moving image and the
screen and the outsized role both play in the construction and dissemination of cultural
frameworks is a central focus of Winiemkos practice. By framing performative actions
and social interactions within the
screen and by appropriating and remixing tropes from
a variety of screen-based media, Winiemko sets the stage for a discussion and
examination of the social sphere.

CSA Space, 2414 Main St, Vancouver, BC
See Pulpfiction Books (2422 Main St.) for admission during regular business hours:
Mon-Wed 10am-8pm, Thurs-Sat 10am-9pm, Sundays and holidays 11am-7pm

20 Years On: New Drawings by Keegan Wenkman

February 5-28, 2021

Originally from California and now residing in Wisconsin, 38 year old Keegan
Wenkman is a unique artist for Outsiders and Others to be showing because we focus
on works by self-taught artists, and Keegan went to art school.

Wenkman was a young punk that hadn't gone to art school yet when Outsiders and
Others started working with him. His drawings were in the moment, immediate, and full
of heart. His loosehand and sparse choices with colour, because he is colour blind,
helped create his style of slightly messy and perfectly composed works.

Now, 20 years on, after formal education, his work really hasn't
changed much. Its
interesting how time and education hasn't had much influence on his illustration works.

Wenkman is also an accomplished oil painter and traditional printmaker.

This exhibition will feature over 100 original pen and ink illustrations, all created in the
last year during Covid.

<a href="/instant-coffee/listings/2021-02-03-10-reissue-online-speaker-series-ardele-lister-12pm-feb-5">10. TALK | ReIssue [ONLINE] | Speaker Series: Ardele Lister | 12PM | FEB 5</a>
------------------------------------------------------------------------
ReIssue - an interdisciplinary art writing platform by UNIT/PITT and VIVO
Speaker Series: Ardele Lister
Feb 5 at 12pm PST
ZOOM

RSVP: https://www.eventbrite.com/e/137050404449

ReIssue is kicking off 2021 with a series of free, virtual talks and presentations by
artists, writers, and artist-writers whose practices, though varied, meet at the
intersection of text, visual art, sound art, and teaching.

The Speaker Series will happen for five consecutive Fridays at 12:00 PST over Zoom,
the last being Ardele Lister on February 5.

The event will be a Zoom Webinar. Attendees will not be visible, but they may submit
questions to the speaker through the Chat function. Live Closed Captioning will be
available. For additional accessibility accommodations, please email
admin@reissue.pub

Bio:
Born in Calgary, Ardele Lister came to filmmaking in 1973 when she co-founded the
womens film collective, ReelFeelings, in
Vancouver. ReelFeelings' first film, So
Wheres My Prince Already?, a tragicomedy about love and marriage, directed by
Ardele, was selected for the International Festival of Womens Films in NYC in 76.
Later that year, Lister moved to NYC where she still resides.

From her days in ReelFeelings through the 80s Listers work focused primarily on
stories of women, historically under-represented as subjects in media. These works
include Sugar Daddy (1980) and Split (1981) with a sequel to Split entitled It Happens
to the Best of Us (1989)-both featuring Vancouvers Susy James. In 1986 she made
Zoes Car (shot at the Western Front), a humorous look at the American Dream of
receiving something for nothing.

Defining identities in our globalized culture and the role of media in shaping those
identities characterize other dimensions in her research. From her point of view as a
Canadian who has lived in the United States for many years, Behold the Promised
Land (1991) and Conditional Love (1997)
focus on nationalisms, provincialisms, at how
borders affect identities, at how we come to know who we are through the
media/propaganda of our places and histories.

After writing for The Province and for Vanguard, Lister founded and edited Criteria, a
critical review of the arts in Vancouver, which published from 1975 until 1978. In 1977,
while working for the Foundation for Independent Video and Film in New York City, she
founded and edited The Independent, the first monthly publication for independent
video and filmmakers, still in publication online.

At the School of Visual Arts, NYC, and at Rutgers, the State University of New Jersey,
Lister taught media for many years. She is currently Professor Emerita at Rutgers
University.

Her works are in the permanent collections of the Museum of Modern Art (NY), the
Centre Pompidou (Paris), the National Gallery of Canada (Ottawa), the Stedelijk
Museum (Amsterdam) and the Kunsthalle (Berlin). http://ardelelister.com/

About:
ReIssue is an
interdisciplinary art writing platform focused on shaping and sustaining a
contemporary west coast discourse rooted in critical engagement with experimental art
practices. ReIssue is a partnership between UNIT PITT Society for Art and Critical
Awareness and VIVO Media Arts Centre, funded through a Vancouver Foundation
Systems Change Grant. https://reissue.pub/

ReIssue operates on unceded Musqueam, Tsleil-Waututh, and Squamish homelands
known as Vancouver, British Columbia.

Dance Area Talk: Webster McDonald & Nikloli Attai
February 5, 2021 | 12:30 PM - 2:00 PM (PST) | ZOOM | FREE
Please join us for the second in a series of free talks presented by the SCA's Dance
Area.

Webster McDonald & Nikloli Attai: Gender Roles and Black Performativity in Jamaican
Dancehall

ZOOM LINK:
https://sfu.zoom.us/j/62264460726pwd=eE5LUzVrVjgvNXh3MXRWM3Vsb3Z1dz09

Webster McDonald is a Jamaican theatre practitioner and educator. He received a
B.F.A. in Theatre Arts from The Edna Manley College of the Visual and Performing Arts
and a M.A. in Theatre Education: Theatre and Community from Emerson College. He
has served as an adjudicator and performing arts consultant for speech, dance and
drama and has taught Theatre Arts and the indigenous cultural forms in Jamaica;
particularly Revival, Brukkins and Kumina. He is currently a PhD candidate in the
Department of Theatre and Dance at the University of Kansas.

Nikoli Attai is a Provosts Postdoctoral Fellow in Women and Gender Studies at the
University of Toronto, Canada. He is currently working on his first book manuscript
titled Queer Liberation? Interrogating Human Rights Activism and the Queer
Caribbean, which interrogates the work being done by activists and non-governmental
organizations in the Anglophone
Caribbean and Toronto, Canada, and theorizes that
current queer human rights interventions fail to adequately address the deeply
complicated ways that queer people negotiate and resist homophobia, transphobia and
discrimination in the region. His research and teaching focus on transnational
feminism, Black queer studies, transgender studies and transnational sexuality studies,
with a particular focus on the Global South. He has taught classes on transnational
sexualities and Caribbean women writers at the University of Toronto.

wielded against them since the devices inception. Ethnography's obsession with
preserving images of so-called vanishing cultures, through ethnographic films or,

a set of ever-evolving social practices whose continuity cannot be repaired by
preservation, only elaborated through struggle and, finally, achieved under conditions
