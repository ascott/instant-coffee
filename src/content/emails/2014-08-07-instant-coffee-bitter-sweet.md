Initial Gallery

Lincoln Clarkes
Giving Notice
August 7th - 30th, 2014

Opening reception
Thursday, August 7th, 6 - 9 pm

Artist in attendance

Initial Gallery is pleased to announce Giving Notice, a collection of
recent photographs by Lincoln Clarkes. The prolific photographer presents
candid images from his unique point of view, surveying moments of everyday,
capturing the in-between; Giving Notice is the activity of his curious eye.
The artist voyeuristically indulges his fascination with the unobserved,
ultimately treating his subjects with romantic reverence. Clarkes
transforms a glance into a scene to be noticed, to be savoured.

Lincoln Clarkes’ body of work spans four decades. After first emerging in
1980 as an infamous Vancouver street artist and designer/painter, Clarkes
abandoned the paint in favour of fashion and portrait assignments. His work
has been published extensively nationally and internationally including the
artists books Cyclists (2013), Views (2006) and Heroines (2002). Clarkes
currently resides between British Columbia, Ontario and England.

Artists' Talk with Cedric Nathan and Jim Bomford
Thursday August 7, 6 pm

Presented by Other Sights
at the Vancouver Maritime Museum
1905 Ogden Avenue, Vancouver BC

Join us for an illustrated talk with the Bomfords who will share the
backstory of the 3+ years leading to the launch of Deadhead, a floating
sculptural installation mounted to a WWII barge and towed by tug to
different locations. The team will discuss their unique collaborative
process and how distinct phases of research, salvaging materials, studio
production, and assembling on the Fraser River influenced Deadhead’s final
form. This informal presentation will provide opportunities for
conversation and discussion about the artwork’s references to British
Columbia’s west coast and its unique ecologies, economies, myths and legends.

An ambient improvised duet of horns will be performed at sundown aboard
Deadhead.
Karen Graves on saxophone and Vincent Mai on trumpet.

OPENING

Wood Carvings
John Ostenstad
August 9, 2014
Maillardville Cultural Appreciation Society

The Maillardville Cultural Appreciation Society is honoured to present a
retrospective of work by John Ostenstad. A Canadian airforce navigator,
Fraser Mills worker, artist and teacher, John Ostenstad made a lasting
impact on the community of Maillardville and Coquitlam at large. Over the
years Ostenstad meticulously crafted thousands of wood carvings from Fraser
River driftwood, found materials and various local woods, most of which now
exists in various private collections throughout the lower mainland. For
this posthumous retrospective a selection of John's substantial output will
be displayed along with two new pieces made in tribute to his work as an
artist.

The MCAS would like to extend a special thanks to John's daughters Chris
and Jane Ostenstad without whose support this exhibit would not have been
possible.

Maillardville Cultural Appreciation Society
227 Lebleu st
Coquitlam , B.C.

<a href="/instant-coffee/listings/2014-08-07-06-monte-clark-gallery-holger-kalberg-aug-16">06. MONTE CLARK GALLERY | Holger Kalberg | AUG 16</a>
------------------------------------------------------------------------
Holger Kalberg
THE FAMILY

Opening reception:
Saturday August 16th, 2pm to 4pm

Holger Kalberg’s exhibition THE FAMILY explores the legacy of high
Modernism and its related utopian models of thinking, presenting new
paintings and sculptures that examine and critique modes of Modernist
vocabulary.

Kalberg describes his own relationship with Modernism as “conflicted
appreciation,” often engaging with the aesthetics of the mode while at
the same time critiquing the model. The works presented in this
exhibition follow Kalberg’s desire for a renewed reflection of
Modernist formalism through an investigation into the artist's own
studio practice, motivation, and early influences, especially Joseph
Beuys and his socialist/utopian agenda.

Both the painting and sculptural works in the exhibition possess a
handmade quality, contrary to regular aesthetics of Modernist
formalism. The work demonstrates a self-referential language through
the use of fragmentation and collage, appropriating a Modernist
vocabulary and mixing it with elements of craft and hobby aesthetics
(which reflect Kalberg’s own process-oriented research and practice.)
In Kalberg’s paintings (pseudo-portraits each depicting one individual
that the artist calls “composite or alter-ego portraits”), he utilizes
craft and low-grade materials which reference Modernist utopian
ideologies in the ‘60s and ‘70s that promoted societal change through
individual action.

The sculptural objects included in the exhibition operate as models or
props for a Modernist formalism that has been disconnected from its
ideological ideals and has turned into a clichéd stylistic expression.
The artist says,

“The various pieces in the exhibition are assembled around a modular
retail structure, which is motivated by Modernist design. The display
structure acts as a platform or stage for the artworks to perform. The
theatricality and starkness of the display sets up a dichotomy with
the deliberately inexpensive materials used in the sculptural objects
and paintings. All materials are readily available craft materials
standing in for ‘proper sculptural’ materials. By creating a hybrid
language of craft/design and commodity retail display, I set up a
situation where the commodity art object that functions as the
supplier for the audience’s desire for meaning, is at the same time
resisting this very request. The objects on display are exposing a
sense of ineffectiveness in their role as idolized art objects, due to
the cheapness of materials and hand made characteristics of the
sculptures and paintings. I am interested in creating works that could
be described as being decorated with meaning by using
easy-to-manipulate materials that seem to stand in for something other
than what they are.”

Holger Kalberg was born in Germany and currently lives and works in
Winnipeg, Manitoba. He graduated from Emily Carr University (BFA,
2001) and the Chelsea School of Art in London (MFA, 2007). Kalberg has
been shortlisted for the RBC Painting Competition on three separate
occasions, and served on the jury for the 2013 award. Kalberg has
recently exhibited at the Vancouver Art Gallery, the University of
Manitoba, the Belkin Satellite Gallery, and the Agnes Etherington Art
Centre. His work is collected by the Royal Bank of Canada, the
Vancouver Art Gallery, TD Bank, the Belkin Gallery, and numerous other
public and private collections.

MONTE CLARK GALLERY
105, 525 Great Northern Way
Vancouver, BC V5T 1E1
www.monteclarkgallery.com

Guadalupe Martinez is an Argentine-Canadian artist based in Vancouver since

Her work fosters interactions that pay attention to the specificity of
place using found materials with the intention to transcend the (apparent)
static nature of sculpture. Embodiment is the lyrical potential to
interrogate and reorganize meaning.
Her practice combines three-dimensionality, performance, and
site-specificity creating work that mnemonically activates bodies and found
materials, reanimating their meaning into new forms of signification and
representation.

Martinez obtained her BFA at the Instituto Universitario Nacional del Arte
in Buenos Aires and is currently completing an MFA at University of British
Columbia.
In 2010 Martinez attended the Master Class Residency with Ken Lum at The
Banff Center for the Arts which significantly influenced the development of
her work. In 2013 she was invited to work at Dorchester Projects with
artist Theaster Gates, extending her practice into the realms of pedagogy
and engagement.

5pm (sharp) - The Sounds Of Your Ancestral Past

Lauren Marsden’s audio work, The Sound of Your Ancestral Past, produced
specifically for the exhibition, Behind the Wall /In Front of the Wall, is
composed as a sonic reflection of the each of the exhibition curators’
personal archives. Using a collection of family photographs and artifacts
as a guide, the soundtrack is comprised entirely of appropriated audio
clips including pop music, stock field recordings and Foley sound effects.
The Sound of Your Ancestral Past offers a drifting narrative through
imagined personal histories while taking its listeners through calm
pastoral settings, strange domestic scenes, and heated public protests.

The Sounds of Your Ancestral Past
Available for download -
https://soundcloud.com/behind-infront/the-sound-of-your-ancestral-past

Behind the Wall /In Front of the Wall is a consideration of two families'
recent past, against a backdrop of political change and economic
globalization. Minarikova and d'Angelo explore issues surrounding
religious, political, and personal freedom, through artifacts of their
families' lived experience.

Anezka Minarikova's exhibition is a self-reflection and contemplation of
the impact of mapping my family history in the context of the political and
historical events of the second half of the 20th century in the territory
of the Czech Republic. Personal objects become an imprint of my family
members’ lives. The items that no longer serve their original purpose are
witnesses of forgotten conversations and events and as such they become
artifacts. The single items are illustrations of memories and emotions but
together they form a whole containing several decades of my family’s
history. During long conversations and discussions I not only put side by
side the fate of my family with the fate of the nation, but I also
unintentionally plunged into the vortex of getting to know myself through
getting to know the past I did not personally witness.

Through a series of interviews Sylvana dAngelo got to know the last three
generation of her family and asked everyone to send items describing our
shared experiences. Despite our drastically different approaches to life
and styles of belief we all share the same types of responses. Our three
generation struggle with immigration and integration led to a strict
religious up-bringing and separation from the secular world. Creating a
warm pocket of the Canadian country side for us to grow up in; away from
the worldly energy of modern society. Despite our freedom to choose any
lifestyle the west has to offer we began living behind a wall of self
imposed separation. Told from the perspective of five family members, these
interpreted items describe our experience with restriction and separation.

<a href="/instant-coffee/listings/2014-08-07-10-dim-cinema-scenes-from-america-on-the-road-with-jrgen-leth-a">10. DIM Cinema | Scenes from America | on the road with Jørgen Leth and Robert Frank | AUG 18</a>

18 August

DIM Cinema

Scenes from America: on the road with Jørgen Leth and Robert Frank

Programmed by Michèle Smith

The Stars-and-Stripes are explored in two landmark works: Danish filmmaker
Jørgen Leth's travelogue 66 Scenes from America and Robert Frank's
single-take C’est Vrai / One Hour.

For his 1982 travelogue 66 Scenes from America, Jørgen Leth recorded a
more-or-less random cross-section of the country’s people and places in
postcard-like tableaux — most famously a four-minute homage to Andy
Warhol’s screen tests, in which the pop artist wordlessly eats a Whopper.
The Stars-and-Stripes fluttering across these archetypal images evoke The
Americans, Robert Frank’s iconic mid-century photo essay. Frank’s own
C’est Vrai/One Hour, his nearly unknown late masterpiece, is a perverse
buddy movie shot in a single take and a single hour on July 26, 1990,
during which the camera moves from Frank’s Manhattan studio onto the
street and into the back of a minivan that careens around the
neighbourhood, now and then stopping to jump out and capture a bit of
action: candid, staged, improvised — it can be hard to tell. “C’est Vrai
is a one-of-a-kind stunt, both street theatre and an urban road movie” (J.
Hoberman). “How much this is a tossed-off home movie about Frank’s
neighbourhood and how much it’s a contrived board game spread out over
several city blocks ultimately becomes a metaphysical question” (Jonathan
Rosenbaum).

<a href="/instant-coffee/listings/2014-08-07-11-concordia-university-montreal-akwiahre-we-remember-oct-16">11. Concordia University, Montreal | akwé:iahre [we remember] | OCT 16</a>
------------------------------------------------------------------------
akwé:iahre [we remember]

October 16 – 18, 2014

Concordia University, Montreal

The Aboriginal Curatorial Collective invites students, artists, curators,
academics and appreciators of art to the Iakwé:iahre Colloquium on the
traditional territory of the Kanien’ke:haka in Montréal, Quebec from
October 16-18, 2014. The colloquium focuses on an active and collaborative
act of remembering as applied to the contemporary idea of an archive from
an Indigenous perspective. “We remember” in culturally specific ways of
knowing based in orality and continuum of time. “We remember” this process
as a living and dynamic form of communication. The Iakwé:iahre Colloquium
brings together curators and artists to listen, share and discuss the
creation of an Aboriginal Art Living Archive, so that we all may remember.

The Iakwé:iahre Colloquium is the sixth in an ongoing series of colloquia
presented by the ACC/CCA, and the first to take place in the province of
Québec. The colloquium will bring together French and English-speaking
Aboriginal curators, artists, arts writers and researchers of all levels.
In order to enable a forum for critical exchange and help bridge the
linguistic divide, services in French and English, including simultaneous
interpretation, will be available.

All are invited to attend the Iakwé:iahre Colloquium. The majority of the
event will take place at Concordia University in downtown Montreal, with
adjunct programming taking place at the McCord Museum. All colloquium
participants will benefit from the engaging panel presentations and group
discussions, as well as opportunities to visit a number of exhibitions
throughout the city featuring the work of Aboriginal artists and curators.

We would like to thank our funders and partners for supporting this event
(more to follow): Department of Art History, Concordia University; The
Office of the Vice-President, Research and Graduate Studies (OVPRGS) Aid to
Research Related Events (AARE); The Canada Council for the Arts; Terres en
Vues.
