CSA Space presents:

Fountains
Zebulon Zang

Opening reception Thursday 31 July 2014, 6-9pm
31 July 2014 through 31 August 2014

It is obvious that what destroys the world will inherit it. A jab at
eternity ends with the most suspect form of existence. Culture grows in
unattended space, finding a means for propagation after, or despite,
destruction. These poetics will never render what is already understood
more comprehensible, they only allow additional marks to be made. You know
it when you see it.

Zebulon Zang is an artist and writer from Maillardville, BC

POETS THEATRE DOUBLE BILL

THURSDAY JULY 31, 8pm

"Rhythms and Forms: Artist Poetry"
Curated by Tatiana Mellema

Jack Spicer's "Young Goodman Brown" (1946)
Produced by Michael Turner

For the final event of our Poets Theatre Festival we are thrilled to
present this DOUBLE BILL of Poet Plays.

"Rhythms and Forms: Artist Poetry"

An evening of poems curated by Tatiana Mellema presents new works by
Vancouver-based artists Tiziana La Melia, Kate Moss, Donato Mancini,
Carolyn Richard and Johan Björk whose practices explore the intersections
between visual arts and poetry. The event will include performances that
respond to the open-ended, site-specific and provisional nature of Poets
Theatre.

Readings will take place inside of "Lot," a special installation by Tiziana
La Melia that acts as a theatrical environment to play in.

Tiziana La Melia will read from new works, including a text and sound piece
in collaboration with Julian Hou. Kate Moss, whose writings fold into her
studio practice will present "Studio Jams," a collection of rhythms. Donato
Mancini in collaboration with the audience, will read his poem
"Abattlehorseanudewomanandananecdote" from his forthcoming book Loitersack.
Carolyn Richard’s performance will consider the
military-industrial-media-complex and the strange terrain of the wrong or
right wars to wage, and Johan Björck will perform a musical piece that
responds to the festival’s scene of production.

Jack Spicer's "Young Goodman Brown" (1946)
Produced by Michael Turner.

Discovered amongst his papers in 2004, Jack Spicer's 1946 student
adaptation of Nathaniel Hawthorne's 1835 allegorical tale of Christians and
witches in New England is, according to the professor he submitted it to,
"the most successful dramatization of the story that could be achieved" --
"the form is almost exactly that of the formal stasimon-episode
alternation, and I think I detect the pathos scene, the catastrophe and the
lamentation."

Please join is for the final night of what has been exceptional coming
together of poets, artists and audience in celebration of Poets Theatre.

North Vancouver Community Arts Council presents

Urbanity

August 1- August 30, 2014

Opening reception:

Thursday, July 31, 7:00 - 9:00pm

Stop motion animation demo

Thursday, August 7, 6:30 - 8:00pm

Artists: Thompson Brennan l Mark Ollinger l Jon Shaw l Scott Sueme

This exhibition features paintings, sculpture and graffiti by four artists
who are inspired by the 'writers' who colourfully mark our urban landscape.
Each artist presents a unique approach to graffiti; an intrigue of urban
decay and beautification, the traces of presence left behind; and the
‘writers’ themselves who are compelled to leave their mark on an urban
canvas. Often controversial, graffiti can be observed as enhancing the
mundane, defacing the ubiquitous or making a thought provoking statement.
Undeniably, there is amazing artistic talent that exists in this realm.

Demo: Stop Motion Animation with Jon Shaw

Thursday, August 7, 6:30-8:00pm

Participants will learn various technical and artistic requirements to
transform their own drawings or paintings into an organic, stop motion
video. Jon will show a video that he created from the drawing process of
one of the pieces from the exhibition as an example. Participants will be
shown how to create similar animations using a DSLR camera, tripod, and
basic video software (Quicktime).

Thompson Brennan is a North Vancouver artist who has exhibited locally and
internationally. He holds a Bachelor’s Degree from the Goddard College,
Plainfield, VT and attended the Corcoran School of Art.

Mark Ollinger is a self-taught artist originally from Calgary, AB currently
residing in Vancouver. He is a graphic designer and designer/owner of
Duality Clothing and has been exhibiting his art extensively since 2008.

Jon Shaw is originally from the East Coast and currently resides in North
Vancouver. He graduated with a Bachelor of Fine Arts Degree from Mount
Allison University in 2008 which led him to a career in graphic design.

Scott Sueme grew up in Vancouver and attended Emily Carr University. He is
a graffiti artist, graphic designer and painter who has exhibited and been
commissioned for many projects and murals locally and internationally.

Jo Dunlop

Exhibition Coordinator

The largest Japanese Canadian festival in the country, the Powell Street
Festival returns for its 38th year from Aug. 1 to 3. This year’s festival
theme is “Roots” and the programming features an eclectic line-up of local
and international musicians, dancers, authors, filmmakers, actors and
visual artists, spanning the spectrum between those grounded in strong
traditions to innovators laying the foundations of a new vision.
Highlights of this year's Powell Street Festival include a launch party at
the Electric Owl (926 Main St.) on Aug. 1, featuring
California-by-way-of-Kyoto noise-pop artist GRMLN (aka Yoodoo Park) and

presenting their installation Music Temple at Centre A (229 E Georgia St.)

Leanne Dunic will curate new works by writers Lydia Kwa, Marie Mutsuki

Firehall Arts Centre (280 E Cordova St.)

Nakayama, contemporary dancer Erika Mistsuhashi and old-time string band
Shout!WhiteDragon (who will lead a square dance open to all guests), as
well as Sawagi Taiko, the first all-women's taiko ensemble in Canada, and
actor Kevin Takahide Lee, who stars as WWI veteran Zennosuke Inouye in an
instalment of the City of Surrey's The Re-Enactors series of historical
plays - and many more!

Also at the festival will be art installations, martial arts
demonstrations, the ever-popular Sumo tournament, Bonsai and Ikebana
displays, and activities at the Children's Tent ranging from origami to
Suika Wari, or watermelon-splitting), historical speakers and tours - as
well as over 15 food vendors serving delicious Japanese food and 23 craft
booths!

Evolution

Centre for Digital Media @ Great Northern Way

August 1-10, 2014

Presented at the Centre for Digital Media in Vancouver, Canada, Evolution
is an interactive exhibit exploring the evolution of gaming. Journey back
to revisit the classic games, systems, and characters that came to define a
culture and revolutionize interactive entertainment.

At their very heart, video games are interactive experiences that exist to
be played. Thus, whenever possible, EVOLUTION tells the tale of gaming
through playable installations. Each exhibit is designed to take people
back in time, creating an opportunity to relive these experiences together
and across generations.
Through the various installations, Evolution highlights the unique
challenges facing the young video-game industry, including limited
technology and designing for an audience that had never seen a video game
before. With both an eye to preservation as well as rediscovery, Evolution
is a chance to reflect on some of the amazing developments in gameplay,
technology, graphics, interfaces, music, and even the player.

EVOLUTION brings back the arcade and living-room hits (and misses) of the
‘70s and ‘80s, while exploring some the medium’s earliest influences. The
exhibition also features hints of the future with modern-themed tributes to
the classics. EVOLUTION shows not just how it was, but offers a lens
through which we might see how it could be.
The curatorial team, Isabelle Arvers, Dr. Kimberly Voll, and Malcolm Levy
welcome you to EVOLUTION. Please, join us as we revisit the strange, new
world of yesterday, merging pixels, polygons and gameplay into a nostalgic,
inspiring, and fun-filled exhibition.

Featuring consoles, games and plenty of hands-on interactive exhibits to
enjoy, Evolution of Gaming runs from Friday, August 1st to Sunday, August
10th, 2014.

<a href="/instant-coffee/listings/2014-07-31-09-the-queer-arts-festival-i-sing-the-body-electric-aug-1">09. The Queer Arts Festival | I Sing the Body Electric | AUG 1</a>
------------------------------------------------------------------------
Walt Whitman and the Beat Generation: they dared to be different,
expressing an individual language and lifestyle in the midst of the
conservative social mores of their times, changing our culture forever.

Just in time for Pride weekend, Erato Ensemble's I Sing the Body Electric
celebrates the queer spirit of Whitman and the Beat Generation. Whitman's
poetry is the basis for contemporary music about an emotional love story of
two men -- from meeting and falling in love, to separation by war and
death. Music by Kurt Weill, Charles Naginski, William George and world
premieres by Lloyd Burritt and Ben Schuman.

Whitman is the quintessential American poet: free-thinking,
individualistic, patriotic while being critical. He has become an American
institution and accepted as Poet Laureate. During his own time, Whitman was
no stranger to controversy: his poetry was decried as immoral for its
unapologetic depiction of homosexual desire.

A century later, Whitman's life and work provided spiritual guidance to a
new generation of American poets who became collectively known as "The Beat
Generation." Although one hundred years apart, Whitman and the Beats shared
literary and cultural DNA. Both extolled individual freedom, and protested
against tradition, conformity, materialism, militarism, industrialization,
and urbanization. Both portrayed nature as divine, and both appeared during
times of turmoil: Whitman during the Civil War, the Beats under the threat
of nuclear annihilation. Whitman and The Beats, many of whom were
homosexual or bisexual, celebrated homosexual encounters as both physical
and spiritual experiences - Whitman in the Calamus poems in Leaves of Grass
and Allen Ginsberg in "Howl" for two examples - and were censored for their
perceived sexual obscenity.

The Beat poets Allen Ginsberg, Jack Kerouac, Gary Snyder, Lawrence
Ferlinghetti and Diane Di Prima inspire colorful new works by David Del
Tredici, David Sisco, Jerome Kitzke, Steven Ebel, Anthony Ocaña, a “Beat
Madrigal,” and a world premiere by Catherine Laub.
Celebrate the queer spirit of Walt Whitman and the Beat Generation with
contemporary music inspired by their poetry.

Emi Honda and Jordan McKenzie present Music Temple at the 38th Annual
Powell Street Festival. Honda and McKenzie began Elfin Saddle after
migrating from Canada’s lush west coast to Montreal, transporting their
nature-inspired, ethnically ambiguous folk creations to the urban environs
of the east. Already seasoned collaborators in sculptural installation and
music, Elfin Saddle began as a more contained outlet for these
multi-disciplinary activities, using prepared acoustic instruments, found
percussion elements, and often mythological and pantheistic themes
delivered in a mixture of English and Japanese. Recently incorporating
video projection and audio installation elements into their live
performances, the resulting Music Temple presents a pseudo-narrative
culmination of all of the above.

<a href="/instant-coffee/listings/2014-07-31-14-grunt-gallery-epic-group-action-film-aug-6">14. grunt gallery | Epic Group Action Film | AUG 6</a>

Exhibition Title: Épopée – L’état des lieux
Artist: Épopée – Groupe d’action en cinéma (Epic Group Action Film)
Closing Reception: Aug 6, 2014, 7 PM
Exhibition Dates: July 21 - Aug 9, 2014

The installation L’État des lieux (The State of the Moment) and film
screening of L’État du monde (The State of the World) is co-presented by
grunt gallery, Queer Arts Festival and Dazibao.

Initiated by filmmaker Rodrigue Jean, Épopée is a collection of short films
written and made in collaboration with male drug addicts and sex trade
workers in Montreal. Set in the district known to residents as “the box,”
an area bounded by the streets St. Denis, De Lorimier, Viger and
Sherbrooke, the project was initiated following the shooting of the film
Men for Sale (2008), when participants expressed the desire to create
fictional works in addition to the documentary.

The project started with writing workshops that transformed into a website
(epopee.me) with short films written and acted out by the participants.
Made up of stories – 9 Fictions and 13 Trajets – written and filmed in
collaboration with male sex workers, Épopée uses cinema (writing, shooting,
editing and screening) as a gesture and an instrument of freedom and
community. These films are not portraits, whether real or fictional, of
their protagonists; instead they combine to paint a highly emotional and
political portrait of our first world, its structure and the segregation on
which it is based.

Punishment, directed by Lav Diaz. The film, set in the north of the
Philippines, explores the themes of crime, class, and family.

The lives of three people take a turn when one of them commits a crime.

when his money lender gets murdered. The crime is pinned on him. Misery and
solitude would transform him in prison.

Left to fend for the family, his wife Eliza (Angeli Bayani) pours all of
her strength to battling with despair and eking out a living for their
children.

with his country—its history of revolutions marred by betrayal and crimes
