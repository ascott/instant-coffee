Call to Artists: Downtown Kiosk and Storefront Artwork

Date: December 7, 2020 For Immediate Release

Building off the success of this years program, the City of Victoria is again inviting
artists to submit their artwork for downtown kiosks and storefronts for 2021. Creating
space opportunities for new and established artists and illustrators is essential to
fostering a city rich with everyday artistic encounters, especially during the pandemic,
said Mayor Lisa Helps. This years installations were wonderful and Im excited to see
what our local creative community comes up with next.

Call to Artists: Signage Kiosk Animation

Artists are invited to submit up to two artworks to be displayed on eight signage kiosks
in Victoria. Artists working in different types of media can apply " from poetry,
photography, sculpture, textile, painting, digital media and more, all are welcome. All
works
must be original to the artist.

Call to Artists: Storefronts Victoria

Designers, artists and illustrators are invited to submit vector artwork for display in
vacant storefront windows in Victoria. This opportunity will showcase the talent in the
local design community and temporarily animate vacant commercial spaces. The
artworks will be presented as vinyl wraps on the vacant storefront windows.

Artists must live in the Capital Region, including the Gulf Islands. The deadline for
submissions for the two Calls to Artists is Monday, January 11 at 4 p.m. Pacific
Standard Time. Interested applicants will also have an opportunity to meet with City of
Victoria staff to review the Calls to Artists at an online information session on Tuesday,
December 17, 2020.

Proposals must be submitted via email to
culture@victoria.ca. Hard copies will not be
accepted. For submission guidelines and more information.
http://www.victoria.ca/publicart

Exhibition Description

Bruce: Is fear for the fate of the world a
symptom of a mental illness?
Manny: Not at all
Bruce: All I know is that any doubts I have are only signs of initiative
Manny: It's a sign that we care.

In the midst of a global pandemic, an international uprising against anti-Black violence,
white supremacy, and police brutality, and urgent defense of Indigenous lands and
sovereignty, how do our relationships with the earth itself continue to ground our
struggles?
How do we begin to grapple with the existential uncertainty that our world faces? How
do we mitigate the emotional and traumatic experience of facing the realities of our
future existences? As we recognize the ways that white supremacy and racial/colonial
capitalism are entangled
with human domination over our kin to land, water, and air,
this exhibition weaves our valid fears and care to assert the future of our balanced
relationships to more-than-human* life. (* We engage the more-than-human value
system with reference to the research of Mtis scholar Zo Todd.)

Program Fear and Care for the Fate of the World begins on the eve of the winter
solstice, Sunday, December 20 at 4pm, with BUSH Gallerys online participatory
performance, Land Zoom. Stay tuned for registration details.

On Monday, December 21 at the Hives for Humanity Pollinator Meadow and
Therapeutic Apiary at 566 Richards (at Dunsmuir), the unceded territories of the
xwmkwym (Musqueam), Skwxw7mesh (Squamish), and Sl"lwta"/Selilwitulh (Tsleil-
Waututh) Nations, co-curator Manuel Axel Strain will gather and record artwork from
Afuwa, Chandra Melting Tallow, and Cecily Nicholson, Mercedes Eng, Hari Alluri &
Junie Dsil. aly de la cruz yip will install a sculpture at the garden. This day is part of the
artistic process and, due to COVID, not a public event. We invite you to download the
recordings on your own devices and visit the apiary on your own time to experience the
sculpture, sounds, and readings on-site.

On Tuesday, December 22 at 9am co-curator Manuel Axel Strain will broadcast the
recordings for the land in the forest at Alouette River, on the territory of the Katzie and
Kwantlen Nations. The broadcast will be livestreamed on Gallery Gachets Instagram
@gallerygachet

moment, while calmly acknowledging and accepting ones feelings, thoughts and bodily
sensations.

<a href="/instant-coffee/listings/2020-12-16-09-this-gallery-partially-online-vanishing-exhibition-explores-">09. EXHIBITION | THIS GALLERY [partially online] | VANISHING EXHIBITION EXPLORES THE ART OF LEAVING | DEC 31</a>
------------------------------------------------------------------------
The independent curatorial team at THIS GALLERY launches their first exhibition.

VANISHING, the debut exhibition from the curatorial team at THIS GALLERY, is
currently on view through December 31, 2020. VANISHING (thisgallery.org/vanishing)
is the combined artistic efforts of 45 artists from 17 countries and features videos,
paintings, drawings, installations,
as well as innovative uses of other media.

Artists from all over the world submitted original artworks that vanish, disappear,
evaporate, fade, melt, dissipate, diminish, drop out of sight, dissolve, or recede. Works
that referenced any thing or concept that is vanishing such as species, hairlines,
democracy and more were also submitted. Over 150 artists submitted works relating to
the theme of vanishing.

An award for artwork that vanished without a trace was given to Kellie Bornhoft of San
Francisco, USA, for her video, Portage, which tells the whispered story of a receding
glacier. Other works included an installation of 400 melting ice eggs, a photograph
exploring the ground after the departure of Brightons annual Pride celebrations, a
performance-to-camera of the artist repeatedly reciting a childrens rhyme in the
disappearing Irish language, an installation of a ghostly knob and lock vanishing into
thin air, a performance exploring an abandoned ancestral home built by the
artists
ancestors, and an installation of a variation on Peppers Ghost, an old stage magicians
illusion.

This exhibition is the first in a series of juried exhibitions to be hosted on the THIS
GALLERY website and in alternative art spaces around the Vancouver area. The next
exhibition is planned for Spring 2021.

About THIS GALLERY:

THIS GALLERY is an independent curatorial partnership
consisting of two artist/curators: Shannon Pawliw (based in Vancouver, Canada) and
Barbara Bryn Klare (based in the San Francisco area and Ohio, USA). Their vision is
to create a series of unique environments where artists can display and create their
work, emphasizing collaboration and the incubation of new ideas.

http://thisgallery.org/

------------------------------------------------------------------------
Call for Applications!

2021 YVR Art Foundation Emerging and Mid-Career Artist Scholarship Program

Application Deadline: Thursday, January 28, 2021, at 4pm.

Emerging Artist Scholarship Program

YVR Art Foundation provides $5,000 art scholarships annually for emerging BC and
Yukon Indigenous visual artists (age 17-29) to work with a mentor artist or attend a
school of art.

Who May Apply

To be eligible for a YVR Art Foundation Emerging Artist Scholarship applicants must:

-Be of BC or Yukon First Nations ancestry
-Reside in BC or the Yukon
-Be between the ages of 17 and 29 in 2021
-Create visual art that reflects BC or Yukon First Nations culture
-Have the goal of becoming a professional artist
-Have
been accepted to study/work with an artist mentor or at a school of art

Note: an Emerging Artist Scholarship can be awarded to an individual a maximum of
two times.

Mid-Career Artist Scholarship Program

YVR Art Foundation provides $5,000 art scholarships annually for BC and Yukon
Indigenous Mid-Career visual artists (age 30+) to work with a master artist, attend a
recognized school of art or work on a special project of cultural significance to the
artists community.

Who May Apply

To be eligible for a YVR Art Foundation Mid-Career Artist Scholarship applicants must:

-Be of BC or Yukon First Nations ancestry
-Reside in BC or Yukon
-Be age 30+ in 2021
-Create visual art that reflects BC or Yukon First Nations culture
-Wish to further their visual art career and/or extend their artwork into a new
scale or area
-Have completed basic art training including but not limited to formal education
in visual arts, worked with an artist mentor or a combination of similar experience
-Have
achieved local and/or provincial recognition through public presentation
of their artwork
-Be able to submit a portfolio of artwork that demonstrates the artists
commitment to their practice
-Be accepted to study/work with a master artist, attend a school of art or work
on a special project that is of cultural significance to the artists community

Note: a Mid-Career Artist Scholarship can be awarded to an individual one time only.

For more information on YVR Art Foundation and to apply for a scholarship go to:
www.yvraf.com/programs

Questions may be directed to: programs@yvraf.com or phone 604.276.6261.
