Ongoing SoEx Open Submissions

Southern Exposure is committed to supporting new, diverse and risk-taking forms of visual art and is 
accepting submissions for new work in all visual arts and related mediums. Southern Exposure 
reviews artists submissions for solo exhibitions, inclusion in group exhibitions, public art projects, 
public programs and events. A guiding principal of the organization is to offer the opportunity to 
artists to work in formats and contexts that extend and challenge their creative development. While 
we primarily focus on supporting local artists we encourage submissions from non-local and 
international artists as well. SoEx is interested in supporting a diverse population of artists and 
strongly encourages women and people of color to apply.
All submissions are reviewed by SoEx's Curatorial Committee, a volunteer group of 10 artists and 
two staff members. Submissions are reviewed on a quarterly basis. There is no deadline, 
applications are received on a rolling basis and are currently being considered for 2010 and 
beyond.
To read the full guidelines and download a copy of the application, please go to 
http://soex.org/exhibitsubmissions.html

http://soex.org/exhibitsubmissions.html

KIKA THORNE | THINGS vs PEOPLE – PEOPLE vs THINGS

JULY 2 2009: PUBLIC VIEW (limited space – please register)
6.45PM FOR 7.00PM ESCORT TO SPACE

JULY 4 2009: VIEWINGS BY APPOINTMENT, 2.00PM – 3.00PM
www.3084.ca – location / registration / appointments / further information

X marks the spot.
The heart of the matter.
Let us think with X.

X. A sign of negation; a sign of non-disclosure: singular relations between subject and object.
X. A model of inversion, of diametric binaries: the dynamics of bodily confrontation.
X. Crosshairs; the convergence of multiplicity into singularity: event horizon.

Between the surfaces of 3084, there is a logic of economized space. The space is greater than 
the sum of this logical frame. There is contingency. There is possibility. There is agency, energy 
and entropy. The potential value of these variables in relation to the space is X.

Things vs People
People vs Things

A sculptural response at the heart of the matter.

ABOUT THE ARTIST
Kika Thorne completed her MFA at the University of Victoria in 2007 where she studied with 
artists Lynda Gammon, Luanne Martineau and philosopher Taneli Kukkonen. She has 
participated in creating collaborative,
temporary, public sculptures to protest the erosion of democracy but only recently emerged as an 
autonomous sculptor since her return to the West Coast. Her large-scale, ambient abstractions 
are suspended in tension and appear to construct an agreement out of ordinary materials. The 
Pink Black Hole was included in Gasoline Rainbows, curated by Jenifer Papararo at the 
Contemporary Art Gallery. Octave was installed as a part of Enacting Abstraction, curated by 
Daina Augaitis at the Vancouver Art Gallery. She is currently exhibiting in The Cast of Shadows, 
curated by Jesse Birch at Access Gallery.

Other exhibitions include Zones, Art Gallery of Hamilton; The disOrder of things, Kino Arsenal, 
Berlin; Old Habits Die Hard, Berlin, Norwich, Oslo, Dresden, Istanbul; E Flux Video Rental, Berlin, 
Budapest, Boston, New York, Austin, Frankfurt, Lisbon, Paris; Canary Islands Biennale of Art & 
Architecture; State of Emergency, Open Space, Victoria; Street Scene, Murray Guy, New York; 
Elastic, The Apartment, Vancouver.

ABOUT 3084: SHORT-TERM STORAGE FOR MATERIALISED IDEAS / IDEATED MATERIALS 
3084 is a project space in a Self Storage unit. Complicating the
dialectics of waste and conservation, the commodification of storage space testifies to the 
enchantment of things, the multilateral power-relations of possession, and the tensions around 
questions of need, desire and responsibility. In this light, commercial Self Storage can be seen as 
a charged space through which to approach practices of material production and issues of 
materiality. 3084 is an example of such space and a facility for this approach.

Throughout June and July 2009, 3084 will facilitate the short-term storage of materialised ideas / 
ideated materials by a selection of artists based in Vancouver. These projects will be open to 
public encounter through a series of open storage events and viewings by appointment – please 
see www.3084.ca for details.


http://www.3084.ca

------------------------------------------------------------------------
Last Drop First Flame

Jessica Wozny

July 3 to August 1, 2009
Opening: Friday, July 3, 7pm

Deluge Contemporary Art
636 Yates Street, Victoria
Exhibition Hours: Wed to Sat, 12 to 5 pm

Wozny exhibits a series of site-specific drawings and sculptural installations which reference the 
gallery's history as Victoria's first firehall. The artist's work harnesses and considers the 
contradictory and ambiguous nature of fire and water to create an elemental metaphor for 
creation itself where the idea of the "Prozesshaftigkeit" of nature -- as something from which 
arises a constant state of becoming -- allows for an adaptive mutability of the mediums 
themselves.

Jessica Wozny is a German-born artist who has been living and working in Mexico for the last 13 
years. She has exhibited extensively internationally and throughout Mexico, most recently as part
of Paréntesis: 17 años de trabajo, Museo de Arte Contemporáneo de Oaxaca, (MACO), and 
NOW. Transformation. Spaces at Casa de Lago in Mexico City. With Luis Hampshire, she is the 
cofounder of Ediciones Plan B, a curatorial collective based in Oaxaca, Mexico.
Wozny is represented by Manuel Garcia Arte Contemporaneo, Oaxaca, Mexico.

Deluge Contemporary Art
636 Yates Street, Victoria, BC, Canada V8W 1L3
250-385-3327 delugeart@shaw.ca www.deluge.ws

<a href="/instant-coffee/listings/2009-07-02-07-pendulum-gallery-mapping-and-marking-vancouver-2010-jul-9">07. Pendulum Gallery | Mapping and Marking Vancouver 2010 | JUL 9</a>
------------------------------------------------------------------------
MAPPING AND MARKING VANCOUVER 2010
Exhibition at Pendulum Gallery, HSBC Building
July 6 – 18, 2009
Opening Reception July 9th, 6:00 - 9:00 pm

The City of Vancouver presents the concepts for eight public art projects
by artists that will celebrate, map and mark Vancouver during the 2010
Winter Games.

The exhibition of projects funded through the City’s Olympic and
Paralympic Public Art Program will run from July 6 to 18 in the Pendulum
Gallery of the HSBC Building on West Georgia Street at Hornby Street. A
public reception to launch the exhibition will be held on Thursday, July 9
from 6 to 9 p.m. at the Pendulum Gallery.

Mapping and Marking is part of the City of Vancouver’s new vision to
support and invest in the creative arts and culture of Vancouver over the
next decade. The program was born from one of the most innovative
recommendations Vancouver City Council adopted in 2008 to reinvigorate the
Public Art Program, namely, to encourage artists with strong connections
to Vancouver to propose works of art based on their own ideas and
practices. Mapping and Marking is the pilot project for what is hoped will
become ongoing opportunities for artist-initiated projects.

The overall intent of Mapping and Marking is to encourage exploration and
involvement in the City itself as the world turns its eyes on Vancouver
during the 2010 Games. More than 120 artists submitted to an open call for
proposals and eight projects were selected for presentation. The artists
for Mapping and Marking are:

Fiona Bowie
Geoffrey Farmer
Vanessa Kwan
Ken Lum
David MacWilliam
Project Rainbow
Anna Ruth
Paul Wong

The exhibition presents the artists’ initial concept proposals that will
evolve over the next few months and be presented during the Games.

On September 30, 2008, City Council approved a comprehensive Olympic and
Paralympic Public Art Plan to commission new works of art that reveal,
surprise, challenge and celebrate Vancouver, and the intent and spirit of
the Olympic movement. The plan includes a range of public art projects
from large-scale, site-specific legacy projects to temporary celebratory
projects.

Mapping and Marking Vancouver 2010 presents the first of a number of
projects in the Olympic and Paralympic Public Art Program. All projects
are commissioned by the City of Vancouver. For more details and updates
on the Olympic and Paralympic Public Art Program projects, visit the
Creative City section of the Host City website at vancouver.ca.

The Art of Taiko with Kenny Endo lecture/demonstration
Thursday, July 30, 2-4pm, 2009
National Nikkei Museum & Heritage Centre, 6688 Southoaks Crescent (Kingsway & Sperling),

Minimum donation of $10 per person. Please register by phone 604.777.7000 or email

Kenny Endo will perform and speak about the traditions of taiko as well as demonstrate his 
original work for this ancient instrument. He'll explain about the instruments, show how taiko is
learned and performance pieces are memorized. The historical and cultural context of taiko will 
be discussed. The odaiko (large drum), taiko set, tsuzumi, fue (bamboo flute) and various 
traditional percussion instruments will be featured. 

Kenny has been performing, composing, and teaching taiko for 34 years. He spent ten years in 
Japan (1980-1990) and received his natori (stage name and license to teach) in hogaku hayashi 
(Japanese classical music). Kenny is currently based in Honolulu, Hawaii where he is artistic 
director of the Taiko Center of the Pacific (a taiko school). He travels extensively around the 
world sharing his art and original compositions often collaborating with prolific artists of various 
genre. Kenny has a special affinity for the Vancouver area as his mother was born in Steveston.

------------------------------------------------------------------------
Balancing Acts

Surrey Art Gallery

June 26 to August 30, 2009
Opening June 25, 7pm

Brian Fisher, Antonia Hirsch, Richard Prince, Robin Ripley, Ruth Scheuing, Takao Tanabe and 
Diana Lynn Thompson

In the exhibition Balancing Acts, Robin Ripley transforms the mundane materials of sewing 
thread, pins, bobbins, into a three dimensional drawing. Diana Lynn Thompson’s installation 
challenges us to look again, we read the stones – some rock, some ceramic – as a form of 
language. The works of Richard Prince and Antonia Hirsch are concerned with systems of 
measurement and how our world is guided by these systems. The visual deconstruction of 
business suits (and by extension, the world of business and masculinity) is enacted in Ruth 
Scheuing’s work; while Brian Fischer’s prints explore definitions of mass and space through line.

Balancing Acts brings together a selection of artworks by BC-based artists made since the late 
1960s. Whether constructed of fabric, printmaking, clay, stone, or metal, there is a strong degree 
of control and attention to detail on the part of the artist in the creation of their work. These
artworks examine the role of measurement and pattern making in the creation of pictures, as well 
as in relation to the power structures found in the broader world.

The works presented in the exhibition – with the exception of Antonia Hirsch’s Average Country – 
are part of from the Surrey Art Gallery’s Permanent Collection.

Surrey Art Gallery

<a href="/instant-coffee/listings/2009-07-02-12-medea-collaborative-media-medea-is-hiring-at-malmoe-universi">12. Medea Collaborative Media | MEDEA IS HIRING AT MALMOe UNIVERSITY, SWEDEN | OCT 1</a>
------------------------------------------------------------------------

Medea Collaborative Media Initiative is a center for new media
research and development at Malmoe University, Sweden. Medea is
built around co-production with actors outside academia -
companies, organizations, artists, citizens, etc. A special
focus is put on the development of new creative practices and on
new public spheres.

Currently, three full professors, four associate professors and
six PhD students are employed in the two initial research areas
of Medea and closely related activities. In two years' time, the
plan is for Medea to comprise at least four major research areas
and employ around 30 staff members and PhD students.

We are now looking to expand our faculty with a full professor.
We also announce the opening of two programs for visiting
colleagues of exceptional standing: An artist-in-residence
program and an entrepreneur-in-residence program.

Professor of New Media with a particular focus on new media and
co-production.
The specific area of competence within new media is open (art,
design, public spheres, learning, economy, law, etc.) -- in a
way, we are looking forward to being surprised.
General criteria are scientific, collaborative and pedagogical
expertise.
Full announcement at
http://www.mah.se/templates/Job____98011.aspx
Application due on October 1, 2009.

Artist-in-Residence Program (Visiting Professor)
In 2010, Medea will launch an artist-in-residence program. The
program seeks artists working within the field of new media
interested in collaborative practices. Each year one or two
artists will be selected. The duration of the stay depends on
the kind of work to be carried out, but normally the stay will
be for either four or eight months. Each artist is granted
salary, accommodation and expenses for travelling to Malmoe.
The steering committee of Medea selects artists. For further
information, email Bo Reimer, Professor of Media and
Communication Studies and Director of Medea (Bo.Reimer@mah.se).

Entrepreneur-in-Residence Program (Visiting Professor)
In 2010, Medea will launch an entrepreneur-in-residence program.
The program seeks entrepreneurs highly skilled in working with
new media and new innovation models. Each year one entrepreneur
will be selected. The duration of the stay depends on the kind
of work to be carried out, but normally the stay will be for
either four months full time or eight months part time. Each
entrepreneur is granted salary, accommodation and expenses for
travelling to Malmoe.

The steering committee of Medea selects entrepreneurs. For
further information, email Bo Reimer, Professor of Media and
Communication Studies and Director of Medea (Bo.Reimer@mah.se).
