<a href="/instant-coffee/listings/2022-04-01-07-west-vancouver-art-museum-rabbit-lane-douglas-coupland-mar-3">07. EXHIBITION | West Vancouver Art Museum | RABBIT LANE: DOUGLAS COUPLAND | MAR 30</a>
------------------------------------------------------------------------
In 1998, Douglas Coupland published Girlfriend in a Coma, a work of fiction that traces
the lives of a group of friends from their teenage years through to middle age. Set in
West Vancouver, the story centres
around Karen McNeil, who falls into a coma that
lasts 17 years. When she awakens, the friends come together once again to face an
impending apocalypse. The novel, which weaves deftly between literary genres, comes
with a moral and a warning.

The book is set in the area of West Vancouvers Rabbit Lane, which Coupland chose
because he spent much time there growing up and he always saw it as a place that
time forgot.

Coupland has worked with the West Vancouver Art Museum to create a series of
staged photographic scenes that were inspired by his book. These photographs were
created in West and North Vancouver, featuring local homes, volunteer models, and
cars and clothes borrowed from the community. Girlfriend in a Coma specifically
resonates with multiple generations of people who grew up or live on the North Shore.
This project not only facilitates an exploration into the work of this West Vancouver
artist, but also furthers the Art Museums commitment to showcasing local residential
West Coast Modern architecture, which is swiftly disappearing. This project, like much
of Couplands written work, is both genre-bending and chronoclastic, exploring how
time can be condensed and contracted to change our perceptions.

Learn more: https://westvancouverartmuseum.ca/exhibitions/rabbit-lane-douglas-
coupland.

<a href="/instant-coffee/listings/2022-04-01-10-baf-gallery-spring-2022-exhibitions-apr-1">10. EXHIBITION | BAF Gallery | Spring 2022 Exhibitions | APR 1</a>
------------------------------------------------------------------------
BAF Gallery Spring 2022 Exhibitions

Sara Gulamali: [green heart emoji]
Jack Kenna: Tumbling Blocks
Karl Hipol: Tagpi-tagpi

258 East 1st Avenue
Apr 1 - June 11, 2022 (Tagpi-tagpi closes June 12)
Tuesday to Saturday 12-5PM
Reception: April 1, 6-9PM

On Friday, April 1, we will be unveiling new exhibitions from resident artists Sara
Gulamali and Jack Kenna, and a new Garage installation from Karl Hipol.

A public reception will be held on
Friday, April 1 from 6 to 9PM. Proof of vaccination is
required for the reception and masks are recommended.

Sara Gulamalis work explores a post-colonial navigation of the hyper-visibility or
invisibility of Muslim women using green screen technology. For the artist, green
screens and the colour green more broadly reference the notion of otherness and the
othered body within her practice"a nomadic space that can become anything or
transport you anywhere.

As we encounter the ubiquitousness of screens and technologies, Gulamalis interest
in digital spaces, and how screens can become proxies for the body, increases. The
green screen in "[green heart emoji]" becomes not only a tool but a material to perform
in and create with.

"Tumbling Blocks" by Jack Kenna takes its name from an age-old, tessellating pattern
of diamond shapes arranged to produce a three-dimensional effect"an illusion of light
falling on row upon row of stacked, angled cubes. Known in geometry as rhombille
tiling,
countless examples bearing the pattern range from ancient floor mosaics to
contemporary quilts.

Kenna applies the term to a new suite of sculptural works, produced during his winter
residency at BAF, that combine found plastic milk crates with abstract compositions in
stained glass.

"Tagpi-tagpi" by Karl Hipol is a constellated exploration of the artists Filipino heritage,
The title translates into English as patchy, from the root word Tagpi, meaning patch.
The installation combines handwritten Baybayin script, pasted manila paper and a
centralized painting of an abstracted bahay kubo, a type of pre-colonial Filipino
architecture.

Through the work, Hipol aims to comprehend a non-western perspective, both
practically and metaphorically. The installation patches lost and loose pages, aiming to
gradually rebuild broken Filipino identities. This exhibition closes Sunday, June 12,
2022, on Philippines Independence Day.

KHOSHGOZARAN | APR 2
------------------------------------------------------------------------

Royal Debris

GELARE KHOSHGOZARAN

April 2"May 14, 2022

Gelare Khoshgozarans film installation Royal Debris (2022) is informed by her decade-
long research about the shuttered former Embassy of Iran in Washington D.C. Located
at 3005 Massachusetts Ave N.W., the vacant building embodies a contradiction: it is a
ruin due to its deteriorating condition caused, in large part, by the ongoing US
sanctions on Iran, and it is privileged with an exceptional protection thanks to its
foreign mission status. Taking the buildings symbolic and material status as its
departure point, Khoshgozarans film is a rumination on borders, displacement and
ruination through excursions into family history, architecture, poetry and contemporary
art.

Vancouver, BC, V6B 2J2
