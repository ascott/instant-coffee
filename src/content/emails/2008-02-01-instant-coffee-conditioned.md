THE WORLD ETC... 
January 31 to March 1, 2008 

Opening Reception: 
Thursday, January 31st, 8-10 pm 

The Monte Clark Gallery Vancouver is pleased to present an exhibition of new work by Roy Arden. 

Following his mid-career survey exhibition at the Vancouver Art Gallery, Roy Arden’s exhibition will feature new graphic works and collages. 

The World etc… presents Arden’s latest graphic works (archival pigment prints) that are essentially digital collages, as well as recent small paper collages made from printed matter. 

Humans have a need to make images, which begets their collection, storage, ordering, interpretation, and re-use. Yet, there is always an element of folly to any archive, no matter how important or useful. What starts as a rational project invariably grows into a large problem if not an unruly monster. All archives are mirrors of human history. 

Arden’s digital and paper collages derive from his archives of images taken from books and magazines as well as his immense archive of images gleaned from the Internet. He mostly collects vernacular (non-art) photo images and since photography is largely synchronous with modernity, his archive and the collages he makes from it reflect modern history. Arden’s sense of history has always been critical in the dialectical sense that Walter Benjamin neatly described in his famous dictum; “There is no document of civilization which is not at the same time a document of barbarism”. 

In Arden’s new works, he plays with different ordering systems from quasi-scientific or simple scrapbook grids to complexly layered poetic collage. While each work focuses on a specific theme or subject, as with most of his art - history and modernity are the larger subjects. Many of the works pay homage to artists who have used retrieved imagery and so the history of collage is variously invoked from Picasso and Kurt Schwitters to Rauschenberg and Hans-Peter Feldmann. 

Roy Arden's ongoing web project, The World as Will and Representation - Archive 2007 (viewable at royarden.com) presents his personal archive of images gleaned from the Internet.

Cut and Please
Works by Peggy and Karen Ngan
Opening Friday, February.1  8-11PM
February.1- February.29


Lucky's Gallery
3972 Main Street
Vancouver, B.C.


Peggy and Karen Ngan
 This new series of collage and drawing is created with the still images from the video piece, “Dynamite” which was finished in 2007.  In “Dynamite”, they explore the identity of being twin sisters through a performative game between individuals.	While the idea of role-playing is being explored in the video, it is also performed through drawing and collage in this exhibition.
 The process of creating the collage or drawing recalls Ngan’s processes of video editing and shooting of “Dynamite”.  When bringing the two mediums together in the show, they not only mirror each other but also create different means within the medium itself.  
Peggy and Karen Ngan live and work in Vancouver.  They graduated from Emily Carr Institute of Art and Design in 2005.  They work on drawing, video and performance collaboratively.  They are also members of the Yac-Tac artists collective.

CANVAS I GASOLINE ROMANCE I FEB 7


CANVAS Gallery - Gasoline Romance Exhibition from January 19 - March 11

On Thursday, February 7th from 7-10 PM CANVAS Gallery (91 Powell Street)
is hosting the Opening Reception for the Exhibition Gasoline Romance
featuring the works of locally-based artists:

Stuart McCall - Discontinuous Spectrums
Katie Gravestock - Geo Metron
Peter Winnett - Brand Heresy
Joshua Van Dyke - Ritual de Habitual
Kate MacDonald - W and the Cityscape

Performances throughout the night.
DJ The Standard 9 PM

CANVAS Gallery has partnered up the Heart and Stroke Foundation and
will be accepting cash donations on the night of the Opening.

Seen from without, the Gasoline Romance is an imagined story; City
life permeates us. Its constancy, its information shrinks our scale of
meaning. Places we enter, objects we know suffer as well this
collective shift existing more as expressions of relativity then
expectations of being, as such. While some who pass through this
shifted landscape note its momentum as 'of the city', others imagine a
counter-melody.

Spaces want to be contemplated, symbols need their 'power' turned up,
objects have participatory expectations… through an innocent
engagement with the defiant re-imagining of a spectator, the duplicity
of acculturation is acknowledged. Its sudden absence, as an expression
of prior organization may well be the insinuation that a local
resonance holds the key to any larger understanding.

Seen from within, this referential idea, itself the vestige of an
earlier conceit allows an experience of the city-life that is not
beholden to its everyday reality. The Gasoline Romance, as such.
-r. meier

----------------------------------------------------------------
Western Front Media Arts 

Judy Radul
Artist-in-Residence: February 4 - February 29, 2008
In-Progress Presentation:  February 29, 2008, 8 PM

Celebrated Vancouver-based media artist Judy Radul will be developing the technical aspects of a major new video production project World Rehearsal Court (working title). The project is based on ongoing research into the performative and media aspects of the contemporary court of law. Radul's time at the Western Front will be spent developing new servo-controlled, programmable, repeatable camera movements for the series of live cameras that will be used in the installation. Specific trajectories will be designed for the cameras with the objective of providing viewers with an alternate perspective on the built environment in the gallery. This environment will refer to the theatre of the court, law, judgment, evidence, reiteration, and testimony. The completed work will be first shown at the Morris and Helen Belkin Gallery in the fall of 2009.

----------------------------------------------------------------
Carol Sawyer: Vacant Lot
an installation of video and photographs

Vacant Lot is a portrait of a large piece of land that was originally part of
Vancouver’s False Creek, filled in 100 years ago to make a railway freight
yard. The land has gradually turned into a meadow since the rail yard was taken

THOUGHT ON FILM 1
MONTHLY READING + DISCUSSION GROUP EXPLORING CINEMA + CINEMATIC PRACTICE THROUGH WRITTEN WORDS


Beginning in January, Cineworks will play host to Thought on Film. A monthly discussion + reading group, Thought on Film aims to promote critical thought around film product and practice through community-based discussion. Rooted in our membership but open to the public, Thought on Film will foster the close reading of texts confronting issues in contemporary, cutting-edge cinematic practice and philosophy.

But first, we will begin at a beginning. The initial text is a selection from Gilles Deleuze's Cinema 1. Deleuze was the first major philosopher to take on cinema, and so we begin our conversation by taking on him. Composed of Deleuze's three theses on the movement-image, the first chapter from Cinema 1 is just a small beginning. Bring ideas about your own cinematic practice and see what Deleuze's ideas look like up against them.

Join us! And please do bring your friends who have cinematic, philosophical or literary bents.
