<a href="/instant-coffee/listings/2019-03-21-03-conduit-gallery-lacuna-eva-bryant-7pm-mar-21">03. OPENING | Conduit Gallery | LACUNA | Eva Bryant | 7pm | MAR 21</a>
------------------------------------------------------------------------
LACUNA

EVA BRYANT --- SOLO SHOW

Conduit Gallery --- 165 E Hastings

Opening: March 21st 7-10 pm

Additional viewing Saturday March 23rd 2-8pm

Is blank space an invitation?

The physical body and the ephemeral mind enter the evocation of liminal space. A 
spectre of the sublime delicately caresses a pre-dawn lagoon. Standing on a precipice 
the landscape emerges filtered by environmental factors.

Lacuna, shrouded in night, a pool, a mirror. 

Eva Bryant is a interdisciplinary artist and tattooer who focuses on depicting the sublime 
through simplified natural forms. Her art ranges from semi-abstract landscape painting 
to meticulous botanical illustrations. Eva holds a BFA from Nscad university. She is 
originally from Vancouver, BC and currently resides in Montreal,QC. 

---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Conduit Gallery is an artist-run project founded in 2018 and directed by Allison Watters. 
It provides a platform for early career and local artists whose work initiates abstract 
experiences through performance and installation.

Conduit Gallery is located on the unceded, ancestral and traditional territories of the 
xmky"m (Musqueam), Skwxwu7mesh (Squamish), and sl"ilwta (Tsleil-
Waututh) Nations.

Accessibility: Wheelchair accessible, entrance is on street, main level with no steps.

That which identifies them, like the eye of the cyclops 

Opening Reception: Thursday March 21, 2019 from 7-9pm
Exhibition on view until May 4 

Beatriz Santiago Muoz's three-channel video installation follows a group women in her 
community in and around San Juan, Puerto Rico. She references Monique Wittigs 1969 novel 
Les Gurillres that envisions the aftermath of a violent war of the sexes in a future where 
women have toppled the patriarchy as a provocation of how an imagined future can be 
actualized in the present.

Franois Boucher and the LOdalisque brune: Revelations concerning the unknown 
origins of a secret and libertine work
A curatorial lecture by Guillaume Faroult

4:00 p.m., Friday, March 29, 2019
Room 102, Frederic Lasserre Building
6333 Memorial Road, University of British Columbia

This event is free and open to the public. Please join us for a reception following the 
talk.

The painter Franois Boucher (1703"1770) is known for an artistic career that was 
arguably one of most brilliant of the European Enlightenment. Called upon not only by 
the French court but also by many foreign governments, Bouchers artistic imagination 
provided many models and images that inspired artists throughout Europe. In 1765, he 
was named The First Painter of the King and thereby became the Director of the French 
Academy of Painting and Sculpture. During this same year, a highly suggestive 
engraving surfaced which allowed the French public to gain a glimpse of one of his most 
daringly erotic works, LOdalisque brune. Indeed, this work was part of a secret corpus 
of paintings for which libertine collectors clamoured throughout the Century. Painted in
