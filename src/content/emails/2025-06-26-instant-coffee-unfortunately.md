Voice as Practice
The Journeyman Sample Pack
Curated by Lillian Doyle

https://fillip.ca/voice-as-practice

Fillip is pleased to announce the launch of The Journeyman Sample Pack as part of
the series Voice as Practice.

Assembled by writer, poet, and musician Lillian Doyle, The Journeyman Sample
Pack is a project that amplifies the legacy of graphic designer and artist Emil
Antonucci (1929"2006). Antonucci was best known for his work with poet and mystic
Robert Lax (1915"2000) and for his project Journeyman Press (1959"74). He was
also a writer, painter, illustrator, and filmmaker.

Over the last two years, Doyle has digitized and documented Antonuccis archival
materials from The New School Archives and Special Collections and St.
Bonaventures Rare Books Collection. In these collections, Antonucci left a series of
reel-to-reel tapes that he used to record radio shows, poetry, live music, and
moments with friends and family.

Within the Sample Pack, this material is annotated and made into a publicly
accessible digital library of audio. The project explores Antonuccis personal history
and artistic philosophies and is supplemented by Doyles essay The Future is Not
the Enemy.

Voice as Practice is a new series of presentations of voice-centred works,
incorporating spoken word, song, live performance, broadcast, and recording. Voice
as Practice will investigate experimental modes of distribution for voice-centred
works.

Emil Antonucci (1929"2006) was a first-generation Italian American born and raised
in Brooklyn, New York. He was primarily a graphic designer, best known for his
project Journeyman Press (1959"74), which published poet and mystic Robert Laxs
(1915"2000) first poetic works, such as Circus of The Sun (1959). Together they
developed an aesthetic for abstract poetry that incorporated their Catholic
spirituality. Throughout his life, Antonucci explored many mediums, including film,
poetry, painting, woodblock printing, audio recording, and journaling. He was also
very dedicated to the Catholic Worker movement and wrote and designed for many
radical Catholic publications, such as Jubilee, Commonweal, and CHURCH
Magazine. He taught at Parsons School of Design at The New School in Manhattan
for forty years.

Lillian Doyle is a writer, poet, and musician based in Los Angeles, California. They
will be publishing a book on the life and work of Emil Antonucci with New Documents
in 2026.

Members receive all of Fillips titles published in the current calendar year, including
books released in the ongoing Supplement and Folio Series, plus full access to our
digital archives for twelve months and priority access to special events!

Protection Spells

Malcolm McCormick

June 5 - July 12, 2025

We are pleased to announce Protection Spells, a solo exhibition by Montreal-based
artist Malcolm McCormicks, with an accompanying text by Frances Williams.

Malcolm McCormick makes sculptural paintings in which multiple stretcher panels
make up a singular painting. Ranging from simple diptychs to complicated,
interlocking forms, his constructed compositions draw attention to the object-ness of
his paintings, while their mottled surfaces and expressive palette give them a unique
emotional character. McCormick likens the relationship between his paintings to
language, in which a row, or a room of paintings creates a rhythm or syntax similar to
words in a sentence or poem.

McCormick lives and works in Montreal and holds an MFA from Concordia University
in Montreal, and a BFA from the University of British Columbia, Okanagan. Recent
exhibitions include The Guard (Galerie Nicolas Robert, 2025), Shape (Hawkins
Headquarters, Atlanta, 2025), K Enters the Castle at Last (Galerie Nicolas Robert,
2024) and Les plus beaux cauchemars / Cruel to Be Kind (Le Livart, 2023), no
order, Joe Project, Montreal, 2023.

ARRAY
Eric Cheung
In collaboration with Cristian Gonzalez
Location: VIVO Media Arts Centre (2625 Kaslo St., Vancouver)
Friday, June 20 - Sunday, June 22
Friday, June 27 - Sunday, June 29
Hours: 4 - 8 pm
Closed on Monday-Thursday
More info: vivomediaarts.com/programming/array

ARRAY explores dimensionality to contemplate the layers of our existence, revealing
the abstraction between our physical and non-physical realities.

Multiple laser projections are generated in real time from body joint tracking data
captured during a trio choreographic score, composed of geometric, rotational arm
sequences and morphing poses. ARRAY translates this movement data into evolving
patterns, oscillating forms, and abstract geometries that emulate different
dimensional planes by integrating precise and randomly generated visualization
models derived from intra- and inter-body connections.

The laser projections are mapped onto 96 semi-transparent screen panels, each
representing a specific joint from a set pose in the choreographic score and
individually suspended in space according to each joints 3-dimensional positional
data. Select screens are coated with a thin layer of phosphorescent, UV-reactive
paint, capturing ephemeral traces of movement that slowly fade over time.

More info: vivomediaarts.com/programming/array

Part artist book, part project documentation and now an exhibition, Pedestrian
Protest chronicles the forty-three dancers, activists, performance and visual artists
who collaborated with Evann Siebens and Keith Doyle on their public art installation
at the Vancouver Art Gallerys Offsite in 2021. Texts in the newly-launched
publication include a blessing by Coast Salish Elder Kat Norris, a historical essay by
VAG curator Diana Freundl; an interview between Siebens, Doyle and Alejandro A.
Barbosa; and essays, text and excerpts from interviews with each of the participants.
The book features photographs and media collages made by Siebens in
collaboration with each of the participants.

From the lead artists:

"We acknowledge that we live and create on the unceded territories of the
xwmkwy" m (Musqueam), Swx w7mesh (Squamish) and sli" lwta (Tsleil-
Waututh) nations. As white artists, with settler origins of European ancestry, we hope
to humbly honour these lands with our creative endeavours. While working on
Pedestrian Protest, we were privileged to collaborate with extraordinarily talented
artists from many communities. We do not aim to claim their practices as our own,
rather to stand alongside, as allies, to give space and voice to the artists that make
up the layered project Pedestrian Protest. We asked our local community of dancers,
activists, musicians, and visual artists to respond to the concept of protest. Each was
asked to choose a site along Georgia Street, or beyond, and to create an action,
choreography or performance that would be documented though media and
photography. Created during COVID-19 through socially distant exterior film shoots,
the creativity and intimacy of the experience is palpable. Many spoke through
movement and interviews of how their existence embodies protest, that moving
through the world in their body is a protest. Still others spoke of their quiet activism
and how protest can be seen as healing and as medicine, particularly in the urban
environment.

Part of our own activism has been to bring attention to performers and performers
rights. Each artist or collective was compensated beyond CARFAC (Canadian
Artists Representation) rates, fully credited, and also holds shared rights to anything
involving their creative work and contribution to Pedestrian Protest. Collaborating
artists also receive master copies of media, to edit and to create their own ongoing
projects."

- Evann Siebens + Keith Doyle

Evann Siebens and Keith Doyle collaborated with forty-three artists for the making of
Pedestrian Protest, both for the original public artwork at Vancouver Art Gallery
Offsite in 2021 and now in book form. Siebens makes media, performance and
photos with movement. Her lens-based practice negotiates the human body as an
archival site and the politics of the female gaze. Recent exhibitions include Trieste
Contemporanea, Italy; Cloud Seven and Wil Aballe, Brussels; MACBA, Barcelona;
and Kunstinstituut Melly in Rotterdam. Evanns documentary work previously
screened at the Museum of Modern Art in NYC and was broadcast on PBS. Her
work from 'A Performance Affair' in Brussels, Belgium was featured on the front page
of the International New York Times in 2019. Keith Doyle is Associate Dean,
Associate Professor of Design + Dynamic Media at Emily Carr University of Art +
Design. He is a Co-Director of Material Matters, which was featured in the
Vancouver Art Gallery exhibition Fashion Fictions in 2023. Doyle will be releasing the
book Material Matters Lab: Here//Too//For in 2026, published by the Vancouver Art
Gallery.

images showing planets, moons, constellations in distant galaxies, and the light of
