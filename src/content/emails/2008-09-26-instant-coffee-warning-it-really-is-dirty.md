<a href="/instant-coffee/listings/2008-09-26-02-interurban-carel-moiseiwitsch-art-activism-and-censorship-se">02. InterUrban | Carel Moiseiwitsch: art, activism and censorship | SEP 26</a>

Carel Moiseiwitsch -- Talks on art practice, activism and censorship

Vancouver-based artist and actvist Carel Moisewitsch will be appearing at a
public forum at Rhizome Cafe on September 26 at 7pm to discuss her art
practice and activism in the context of media concentration, censorship and
SLAPP suits (Strategic Lawsuits Against Public Participation).

Moiseiwitsch has a long history of powerful work with challenging subjects.
Her art practice and activism often intersect, creating a feedback loop of
compelling images that do not pander to a comfortable view of our place in
the world.

Moisewitsch's current exhibition at the Interurban, A-POK'A-LIPS', both
engages and confronts the viewer with images from man-made catastrophes of
war, occupation, and the destruction of nature.

Her activism has also recently earned her a lawsuit from media giant
Canwest for "conspiring to publish and distribute" a parody of the
Vancouver Sun newspaper that satirised Canwest's anti-Palestinian bias.

"The mass media censors, but it also trivializes and commodifies the
shocking images it exploits of man-made catastrophes from Canada to Iraq
and Palestine," said Moiseiwitsch. "Apocalyptic events have become a daily
reality show."

The works in the exhibition reference stills and video clips from the mass
media -- of abattoirs, torture chambers and war zones. These disturbing
images were zapped into our lives for an instant by the mainstream media
but quickly buried and forgotten by the 24-hour news cycle.

"I believe these images deserve closer scrutiny and longer contemplation,"
explained Moiseiwitwch, "if only because of the personal tragedies involved."

The facile lure of electronic media encourages and assumes passive
consumption, dehumanizing both the subject and the voyeur. Images are
censored or are quickly discarded because they are deemed too politically
embarassing to the status quo or just not fresh and new enough for our
disposable, irony-saturated culture. In contrast, the hand made image
invites contemplation and engagement in an active relationship with the viewer.

"We are all implicated in and affected by the destruction of our world,
whether by war, exploitation, or neglect," said Moiseiwitsch, "and our
silence is complicity."

Contemporary Conversations: Monika Szewczyk

Saturday September 27, 2008

Contemporary Conversations. Each month this program will engage the

<a href="/instant-coffee/listings/2008-09-26-05-artspeak-carrall-street-althea-thauberger-sep-30">05. ARTSPEAK | CARRALL STREET | ALTHEA THAUBERGER | SEP 30</a>
------------------------------------------------------------------------
CARRALL STREET | ALTHEA THAUBERGER

Tuesday, September 30, 2008
8-11pm in the 200 block of Carrall Street, Vancouver

Carrall Street Public Forum
Thursday, October 2, 2008
7pm at 33 West Cordova Street

Althea Thauberger's one-night performance, Carrall Street, will present the
street (brightly lit like a film set at nighttime) as a stage, or zone of
illumination where the roles of performer and spectator blur. The
interweaving of organized performers, passers-by and audience members will
allow for unforeseen interactions to take place that reveal something of
the street's history, its current issues, as well as its future. Carrall
Street is planned in collaboration with local directors, performers and
community members.

Carrall Street is one of the oldest streets in Vancouver. It can be argued
that the entire history (and pre-history) of the city can be mapped along
its six blocks. Caught between urban gentrification and decay, the street
marks transitions from the most touristic parts of the city to what is
often described as the poorest neighbourhood in Canada. In ways that are
both unique and similar to other inner cities, it has been affected by
development, public policy neglect and polarized politics.

A publication accompanying the project will be available in 2009.

Althea Thauberger is an artist based in Vancouver. Her work involves
research and collaboration that result in performances, films, photographs,
audio recordings and books.

The performance and forum are free and open to the public.

This project has been supported by Arts Partners in Creative Development,
The Canada Council for the Arts, the City of Vancouver and the Portland
Hotel Society.
