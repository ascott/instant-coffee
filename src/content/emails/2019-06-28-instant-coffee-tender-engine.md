------------------------------------------------------------------------

The Vancouver Economic Commission and the City of Vancouver introduce the Activate 
Program. 

The Activate DTES & The Flats project seeks to address availability and affordability 
issues related to production space in Vancouvers Downtown Eastside, False Creek 
Flats and immediate surrounding areas. We are looking to generate effective change in 
the near term for groups that need light industrial space.

The Activate Program identifies underutilized spaces that can be transformed to provide 
social enterprises, artists and entrepreneurs with affordable spaces to launch and 
nurture projects or businesses that contribute to the Citys economic, social, cultural and 
environmental goals. This program helps landowners, tenants, and City staff work 
collaboratively to retrofit, permit, and fund affordable space projects. 

More info at: https://www.vancouvereconomic.com/industrial-concierge-service/

Ok, now the ask:

This project kicked off on June 14th, and we are hoping to quickly identify some key 
individuals who would agree to be interviewed and potentially take part in pilot 
project(s).

Please contact Matt Hessey at matt@hessey.ca and forward to folks in your network:

If you need space, particularly if:

You have been recently displaced from industrial space in Vancouver
You are looking to start-up or expand into industrial space in the next 6 to 24 months in 
Vancouver
Or if:

You own or manage underutilized industrial space in Vancouver

The timeline for the initial phase of work (interviews) is short, so please dont hesitate.

The Alternator Centre for Contemporary Art is a non-for profit Artist run centre in 
Kelowna BC. We are currently accepting submissions for our Main Gallery space for our 
programming in 2021/2022. Please visit www.alternatorcentre.com and follow the "call 
for submissions" link for more information and to access our online submission form. 

Alternator Centre for Contemporary Art
#103-421 Cawston Ave Kelowna, BC (inside the Rotary Centre for the Arts)

Since 1989 the Alternator has shown the work of emerging Canadian and international 
artists with an experimental approach, particularly those engaged in social and cultural 
issues. We foster energetic, creative, critical discussion on art & culture. We believe in 
the greater positive potential of our creative community and value our role as key 
contributors to the cultural life of Kelowna and beyond.

We envision our artist run-centre as a welcoming site for radical art-making and 
community-building. We have two primary goals:
First, we seek to provide support and resources to emerging and alternative artists 
in the Okanagan region;
Second, we seek to inspire our members as well as the broader community by 
showcasing local, national, and international work that is engaged in its social context 
and that disturbs and transcends dominant constructions of art, identity, and value.

We are committed to fostering artists professional autonomy and are proud to pay 
successful applicants CAR/FAC fees. The Alternator does have limited funds available 
to assist with exhibition production expenses. If you require assistance with production 
expenses, please include a detailed description and budget of production expenses as 
part of your programming submission as well as expected installation schedule/timeline.

artist mandate, floor plan, and access to our online submission form.

BENJAMIN LUMB - NEW LIFE - 
Hosted by Grosvenor Americas 
1370 marine drive, west vancouver
Opening hrs
11am-6pm Tues-Sat

Benjamin Lumb cordially invites you to his solo exhibition "New Life" located at 
Grosvenor Ambleside. 

Through creation of monumental sculpture transformed into conceptual photography 
and other supporting works Benjamin Lumb considers the memory, form and unrealized 
beauty of forgotten objects alongside the positive negatives people encounter when they 
are attracted by the promise of opportunity.

Artist Statement 

I have always been an artist but have only recently begun to embrace it. The work 
before you is what my practice has, at this moment, evolved into; The building of large 
sculpture that inspires further concept development and creation of supporting works 
that more deeply explore an idea. 

Observation of people interacting with and responding to my work has been incredibly 
defining to my development. The countless conversations had with new friends have 
been invaluable, allowing me to more deeply understand the people and world around 
me. Artist Lawrence Weiner once said "I make art to be a better person".

I am not connected to any particular idea, medium or process and do not consciously 
reference anyone's work. I work with many skilled artisans, craftspeople and fabricators 
to realize my concepts. If not for the people around me I could not produce any of my 
ideas.

I will continue to strive to create work that people can hopefully connect thier own 
metaphor to regardless of its meaning in my heart.

Hercules, 2019 - Steel, Water, Wood

Safe EXPLOSION, 2019 - Limted Edition Archival Inkjet Prints

------------------------------------------------------------------------

New Westminster Environmental Partners has a "Call for Artists" along with some 
criteria. The subject matter is the Brunette River and its tributaries which extends into E. 
Vancouver as well as part of Port Moody, Burnaby & Coq. If you would like a map of the 
Brunette Watershed, we can send that as well (it is set up to be printed in colour on 11" 
X 17" paper). As you will note there are a wide variety of mediums that can be done, 
including photography as well as quite a varied subject matter.

Note: Part of the Brunette on the North side from Coq. to Burnaby will be close to the 
proposed pipeline and in some cases will be less than 40 ft from where it will be laid. 
There are a number of streamkeeper groups who have been successful in keeping the 
Brunette viable to salmon for the past 35 years. (Members of the Sapperton Fish & 
Game Club started cleaning up this river in 1969 and it took them 15 years before the 
first of the salmon were able to return through semi-clean waters in 1984. SF&GC toiled 
hard to reduce the pollution and took out 10's of thousands of pounds of garbage which 
infested these waters. There are at least 5 more streamkeeper groups along the 
Brunette & its tributaries.)

If you have any questions, we will try to answer them to the best of our ability. Regards, 
Vic Leach, NWEP - Brunette River Projects Coordinator M: 778 - 237-0052

VANCOUVER ART GALLERY ASSOCIATION
1 PART-TIME POSITION AVAILABLE
AUDIO-VISUAL SYSTEMS DEVELOPER

DUTIES:
Reporting to the Audio-Visual Technician III, the AV Systems Developer performs 
specialized technical work in support of exhibitions, collections and events utilizing 
audio-visual hardware and software. 

Under direction of the AV Tech III the AV Systems Developer undertakes the following 
responsibilities:
programs and configures software/hardware systems for media playback to 
specification, from an artists specifications or from scratch.
designs and constructs custom electronics, primarily for interactivity.
edits and exports video and audio files for diverse use cases such as exhibition, event 
presentation, web-delivery, and archiving.
creates scripts and automation files for macOS playback devices and workstations.
setup and configuration of DMX and lighting control software.
manages iOS devices used in kiosks and audioguides.
provides research and input on equipment procurement and AV systems design.
creates and maintains digital file management structure and database systems for 
departmental and gallery media collections.
manages consumer-level network-attached storage for video editing and collaboration.
creates manuals and installation instructions, or audio/ video documentation as 
required.
assists in planning, installation, operation and maintenance of AV systems for 
exhibitions and Gallery operations.

In addition, the AV Systems Developer provides occasional support in the following 
areas:
setup and operation of Gallery events as required
fielding internal requests and providing AV support for internal meetings as required

As the AV Systems Developer you are tasked with ensuring safe and proper installation 
of equipment to precise specifications, ensuring reliable operation and presentation at a 
high aesthetic standard. Maintaining knowledge of current trends and advances in 
related technologies is required. 

The work includes computer operation, knowledge of low voltage electronics and basic 
electrical, cable pulling and termination, heavy lifting, basic construction tasks, ladder 
and lift work, cleaning and organization. The incumbent must be able to work in a safe 
and responsible manner, both individually and as part of a team. 

QUALIFICATIONS:
Completion of post-secondary technical courses in audio-visual or computer technology 
plus three years of experience working with computers in an audio-visual and/or 
programming field.

Experience and knowledge of the following equipment and systems, or applicable skills 
to familiarize quickly:
A proficiency in MacOS and Windows 10
A proficiency in Trimble SketchUp for exhibition layout and planning
Adobe Premiere Pro and Creative Suite for the production and editing of video content.
Programming languages in the C/Arduino family, Applescript, and/or node-based 
languages (Max, TouchDesigner)
NAS and simple network management, permissions and disk management, ability to 
communicate technically to IT team
Video playback and distribution, including synchronization on MacOS or dedicated 
players
Experience with FileMaker Pro or MS Access is beneficial 
iOS development and XCode experience is also beneficial

Ability to set up, operate and maintain a wide variety of audio-visual equipment and 
related systems for permanent installation and for live events. Ability to work safely with 
a variety of basic hand and power tools. Maintain excellent communication and 
interpersonal skills in a professional and friendly manner. The successful candidate will 
demonstrate a high attention to detail, effective problem-solving skills and the ability to 
work with colleagues in a team-oriented environment.

HOURS: 21 hours per week with additional hours as required

FEELING THE PULL

A new spin on gravitational art

Exhibition at Delbrook Community Center June 10 to August 8 
Edzy Edzed and Pierre Leichner

Gravity is a fundamental force for our existence. Yet its very essence remains a 
mystery.

Ripples in the fabric of space and time "gravitational waves " were first detected in 
2015.

They arise from the collision of extraordinarily large objects such as black holes and 
neutron starts colliding. These collisions generate heavy elements such as gold, 
platinum and lead. Converting the frequency of gravitational to sound waves create 
sounds that scientist have called chirps.

Many astrophysicists equate this discovery to that made by Galileo when he provided 
the evidence that the earth revolved around the sun. Gravitational waves could open 
doors to a deeper understanding of our universe, even possibly its very origin.

In this body of work both artists explore the force of gravity artistically. This mixed media 
presentation aims to engage viewers visually and physically in the experience. The 
works are of course imaginative representations as we only have limited knowledge of 
these events, however each of the works was made using gravitational force directly 
and some can be activated by it.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

The Bill Reid Gallery of Northwest Coast Art presents Language Matters, an exhibition 
that brings awareness to the importance of Indigenous language revitalization. Guest 
curated by Jordana Luggi (Dakelh and Wetsuweten) in recognition of the United 
Nations International Year of Indigenous Languages, this group exhibition features new

"Catching Butterflies" 
Fiona Zhao
Location: #5 - 115 E. Pender St., Vancouver V6A 1T6
Exhibition runs from June 21 - 30th
Gallery hours: June 22 & 23, June 29 & 30, 2-4pm

Chasing Butterflies"

I always dream of an empty landscape, so vast, with no definite edges. In the 
landscape, me, as tiny as a black dot, standing in the middle of the infinity. The machine 
inside me is pounding, quivering, the feeling comes still and tranquil.

Immersion Grade is a two-person exhibition at VIVO Media Arts Centre. It is a joint 
presentation with Tegan Moore (Ontario, Canada) and Ryota Sato (Okayama, Japan).

Immersion Grade refers to a type of protective coating that seals the surface from 
external conditions and weathering from the elements. This suggests the enabling of 
interaction with foreign objects without the loss of structural integrity and/or identity of its 
material construction. Through expanded thinking on cinematic environments, 
Immersion Grade addresses the materiality of a moving image, the climatic qualities of 
cinematic space, and the spatial contingency of the viewing experience.

A collaborative media installation involves crossover between Sato and Moores works. 
The installation looks to explore architectural air beams/streams that affect surface 
movement in the artworks that incorporate light refraction through the use of water 
vessels. Air Movers - fans specifically designed for streams of air maximizing 
movement and circulation in an indoor setting also works to agitate how people move 
through the space. 

Moores ongoing research involves the history of air conditioning and how it was 
popularized at the site of cinema, marketed as a climatic refuge which enticed people to 
escape to the theatre during the heat of summer. In reference to this early adoption of 
the concept of climate control, the installation places air circulating devices around the 
room to unexpectedly effect movements by way of the subjective thermal sensitivities of 
those who encounter it.

Immersion Grade plays with the position and proximity of the viewer, the atmospheric 
conditions of cinema viewing space, and the composition of the screen itself as a mode 
for liveness of both image and encounter. It brings up questions of collaboration and 
authorship, through the atmospheric play that affects and disrupts distinct artworks.

Space, and participated in residencies at Tokyo Arts and Space, Mustarinda, and 
Flaggfabikken (now Aldea). Her practice is informed by how objects and systems work 
invisibly in the built environment, especially concerning material properties, interior 
climates, sensory stimuli, energy, and fragility.

Book Launch and Reception 
July 28, 2019, 7 PM 
@ grunt gallery, 116-350 E. 2nd Ave

Over the past two years, grunt gallery has been at work on the legacy publication 
Wordless " The Performance Art of Rebecca Belmore, focusing on her remarkable 
performance career. 

Wordless features full-colour photos and stills, including the new photo series 
nindinawemaganidog (all of my relations) and essays by Richard William Hill, Curtis 
Collins, Kathleen Ritter, Wanda Nanibush, Jessica Jacobson-Konefall, Glenn Alteen, 
Jen Budney, Dan Pon and Florene Belmore. Edited by Florene Belmore. 

Presented by grunt gallery and the Audain Art Museum. Published by Information Office. 
Wordless was funded through Canada Council for the Arts 150 Program, New Chapter 
and the Audain Art Museum. 

"Wordless" is one of the 200 exceptional projects funded through the Canada Council 
for the Arts New Chapter initiative. 

Accessibility 
grunt gallery is accessed from the sidewalk via a 106 long, 64 wide concrete ramp that 
rises 12. The slope is 1 : 8.75. There are no rails on the ramp. The front entrance is a 
manually operated outward-swinging double door with a total width of 64. No stairs, 
inclines, or elevators are necessary to access the public areas once inside the gallery. 
grunt gallery has a single gender neutral washroom that is accessed via a 31 wide 
doorway with a sliding pocket door with a door handle that is 40 high. Please contact us 
at 604-875-9516 or email communications@grunt.ca with any questions.
