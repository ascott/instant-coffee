<a href="/instant-coffee/listings/2025-04-18-03-bag-offsite-natasha-katedralis-eyebat-the-cutting-floor">03. EXHIBITION | BAG Offsite | Natasha Katedralis: Eyebat, The Cutting Floor</a>
------------------------------------------------------------------------
Natasha Katedralis: Eyebat, The Cutting Floor

March 18, 2025-June 17, 2025

Bob Prittie Library

6100 Willingdon Ave, Burnaby

Eyebat, The Cutting Floor gathers 12 silver gelatin prints that incorporate
photograms, light drawing, contact printing, enlarged digital negatives and hand-
colouring techniques with oil pigments. Natasha Katedralis uses photographic tools,
as one move in a sequence of gestures, to emphasize, dramatize and recompose
the visual into a space of abstraction, flattening and association. These photo-come-
collage-come-drawings push the illusory qualities of the photographic medium into a
play of highly constructed and graphic surfaces.

Having recently learned photo-developing techniques under mentorship of artist and
darkroom technician Felix Rapp, the prints exist as an experimental extension of the
artists engagement with photography and images. Using the analogue process,
Katedralis combines digital photography with the physical ephemera that constellate
her wider practice such as drawings, collage materials and collected items of
significance. Fragments of computer desktop images, torn out magazine pages, ink
drawings, family heirlooms and renderings of the artists house keys, indiscriminately
converge in the material space of light-sensitive paper and the liquid chemicals that
develop them.

This exhibition is curated by Asia Jong, Burnaby Art Gallery's Curatorial Aide, and is
in participation with Capture Photography Festival's 2025 selected exhibitions
program.

<a href="/instant-coffee/listings/2025-04-18-08-bothkinds-project-space-life-force-apr-1">08. EXHIBITION | Bothkinds Project Space | life force | APR 1</a>
------------------------------------------------------------------------
Bothkinds Project Space

602 E Hastings

April 1-30

life force

an installation by Laura Clark

Life Force explores the subtle yet powerful space of waiting.

In these suspended moments of waiting, we confront the boundaries of our
existence, where the external world seems to fade and we become more attuned to
the present. Time stretches, appears to stand still, and yet creeps steadily forward
just beneath our awareness.

Perhaps we feel that life force is inextricably linked to our embodied awareness and
the rhythm of our breath.

The exhibit invites you to reflect on how presence and breath shape our experience
of life, and how the act of waiting can, paradoxically, become a powerful
manifestation of life itself like an involuntary meditation practice.

Gallery Hours; Saturday and Sunday 12-5, or by appointment

<a href="/instant-coffee/listings/2025-04-18-09-gibsons-public-art-gallery-man-made-cloud-by-carolina-franze">09. EXHIBITION | Gibsons Public Art Gallery | Man Made Cloud by Carolina Franzen | APR 3</a>
------------------------------------------------------------------------
CAROLINA FRANZEN | Man Made Cloud

Fine, detailed, colourful linework sensually evolves into expansive landscapes which
subtly engage with the beholder and their preconceptions. Pulp products are
reversed into tree, sky, and seascapes. Using colourful pencil and felt pen on found
and canvassed industrial packaging paper, Man Made Cloud is an ongoing series
first begun in 2021. The materiality of the work and the process behind each piece
shine through the finesse of the linework. The negative spaces of brown carton
papers are elevated to show growth as dense and vivid as the trees which make up
the paper may have once been.

Carolina Franzen holds an MA in History of Art and a PhD focussing on genocide
and philosophy. She has explored active and academic ways of living around the
Salish Sea since 2013. In 2024 Franzen exhibited her sculptural work, slyly swift
assemblages made of found objects, in GPAGs Joes Lounge; now, she returns
through this exhibition in the Main Gallery to works representing the forms of land.
Her drawings engage us with the quality and tranquility of a different pace,
reminiscent of the deeper sense of geologic time. She invites us to charmingly
clouded ice-berg tips, and to the beauty of a world void of human bodies.

https://gpag.ca/

<a href="/instant-coffee/listings/2025-04-18-10-surrey-art-gallery-a-tangled-thicket-apr-5">10. EXHIBITION | Surrey Art Gallery | A Tangled Thicket | APR 5</a>
------------------------------------------------------------------------
April 5-June 8

Enter the Connectome opening art party April 26 @ 6:pm

Surrey Art Gallery announces their spring exhibition A Tangled Thicket with Zinc
Artist Collective (Willa Downing, Lesley Garratt, Cora Li-Leger, and Claire Moore) at
Surrey Art Gallery from April 5 to June 8, 2025. Admission is free.

Zinc Artist Collective members are united by a profound passion for community,
craft, natural materials, and ecological consciousness. A long-standing presence in
Surrey art communities, the group has supported collaborative artmaking initiatives
with an extended network of creatives and the public at large for over a decade.

A Tangled Thicket captures the ethos of connectivity that lies at the heart of Zincs
practice. Drawing on theories of ecology, neuroscience, and ontology (the study of
being itself), each artist expounds on the interconnection between nature and the
human mind and how they are embedded within and shaped by each other.

Across painting, drawing, sculpture, textile, and handmade installations, each artist
alludes to the porousness of the supposed boundaries that separate humans from
other species, as well as the stories we tell ourselves about nature and our
relationship with it. Citing inspirations such as the neuroscientist Santiago Ramn y
Cajal, ecologist Suzanne Simard, and local poet and author Aislinn Hunter, Zincs
works vividly capture the resonance across all forms of matter: from the biochemical
schemata of plant cells and brain neurons to the mycorrhizal networks of fungi,
trees, animals, people, places, families, and memories.

Zinc constantly strives to push beyond the boundaries of their own practices,
inviting responses in the form of interactive installations. The collaborative Objects
for Pondering project encourages visitors to handle bespoke, handmade items"
books, miniature figurines, ceramic sculptures, memorabilia"that prompt personal
reflection and meaning-making. Made for the occasion of Surrey Art Gallerys 50th
anniversary, the Cultured:50 project includes 50 petri dishes filled with objects co-
created by other artists south of the Fraser.

Elsewhere, the exhibition includes artworks that celebrate the inherent craft of each
artists approach, emphasizing organic materials, bodily sensation, and
improvisation. Interactive sculptures by Claire Moore and ink paintings by Lesley
Garratt imagine vibrant worlds of anthropomorphic creatures that move between
realms, untethered by human categories, while artist books by Cora Li-Leger and
large-scale installations by Willa Downing draw attention to the synergy between
consciousness and history.

In the face of ever greater automization and social stratification, Zinc proposes a
radical creative togetherness imagined through the lives of artworks and the people
who make, use, and respond to them.

The opening of the Gallerys spring exhibits, Enter the Connectome, happens on
April 26 from 6:00 p.m. to 9:00 p.m. where Zinc members will discuss the
relationships between art, science, ecology, and consciousness. The evening will
also include collaborative artmaking activities and the relaunch of the saga shop.

Also in connection to A Tangled Thicket is a Slow Looking Workshop presented by
the collective on May 8 from 7 to 8:30 p.m. as part of the Thursday Artist Talk series
organized by Surrey Art Gallery Association. The artists will lead a guided workshop
of their Objects for Pondering collaborative art project.

On June 7, Assistant Curator Rhys Edwards joins Zinc Artist Collective for an in-
depth tour of A Tangled Thicket from 2:00 p.m. to 3:30 p.m. The event will conclude
with a reading by Dr. Aislinn Hunter, a faculty member in Kwantlen Polytechnic
Universitys Creative Writing Department.

BFA Graduating Exhibition 2025: Ill Meet You in the Space Between

April 17 - May 3, 2025

Audain Gallery at the School for the Contemporary Arts

Opening reception: Wednesday, April 16 at 6 PM

Open Critique: Thursday April 17 at 11 AM (TBC)

Audain Gallery

Emma Campbell, Shirley Chen, Caroline Chernega, Amy Dai, Abroop Dhami, Gracy
Singh Gandhi, Sana Goel, Luvz de Guzman, Thanyarat (Ocean) Hongphankul,
Julian Injeti, Lily Sng L, Daniel Molnar, Harvir Kaur Nahal, Harmon Nakajima,
Sarah Robinson, Renan Shao, Annie Yu

Ill Meet You in the Space Between presents the work of visual art students
graduating with a Bachelor of Fine Arts from the School for the Contemporary Arts at
Simon Fraser University.

The artists in the exhibition engage with the notion of space across multiple
registers, exploring its depth, fluidity, rhythm, and relational possibilities. Space is
considered via surface, material, or body; as dreamscape, landscape, environment,
and outer space; and as the place of connection, interconnection, and difference as
can be seen by way of cultural identity and popular culture. This notion of space also
embodies memory " memory that has life in the present and that is not relegated to
a past that is over and done with but that is recontextualized in a lived present.

Like memory, this space is ephemeral and shifting, and it can include notions of the
emotional, mental, and spiritual. It is an energy that exists outside and in-between
the parameters of artwork and acts as an interval for collaboration, conversation and
connection. Though the artworks in the exhibition take various forms including
painting, sculpture, photography, textile, and video to present a wide range of
subjects, there are rich spaces of relation and correlation of ideas, histories, and
identities.

genderqueer parody. The series uses self-portraiture as a means of character-
building, pulling from contemporary drag performance and reality TV personalities to

promotional content, once again disrupting the viewers expectations. The audience
