<a href="/instant-coffee/listings/2009-04-23-02-or-gallery-the-wild-so-close-apr-24">02. Or Gallery | The Wild so Close | APR 24</a>
------------------------------------------------------------------------
The Wild so Close 
Tacita Dean, Leslie Grant and Al Bersch, Jason Hendrickson, David Horvitz, Donald Lawrence
April 25 - May 30, 2009
Opening Friday, April 24, 2009 8PM 
Curated by Jennifer Cane
The Wild so Close presents photographic and video works by Tacita Dean, Leslie Grant and Al 
Bersch, Jason Hendrickson, David Horvitz, and Donald Lawrence.

The sites of leisure culture and recreation—within the construct of ‘nature’—are worthy of rigorous 
investigation. This exhibition brings together varying photographic and video works that 
investigate concepts of natural leisure environments and resources. In all cases, there is an 
underlying component of travel—the artist making a journey as part of the work’s production, and 
of that distant place becoming the subject. These leisure sites are often situated in opposition to 
other types of spaces; to the spaces of labour, the spaces of the domestic; some spaces 
showing the demarcations of gender and class constructions. In an interlocking manner, the 
works contain elements that trouble notions of the ‘natural’ that make up representations of such
sites. The exhibition raises important questions surrounding the spaces of recreation and tourism
in the wake of Vancouver’s Olympic venture. What is at stake is an opportunity to reconsider the 
economies of their use in both real and ideal terms.

The works, in turn, question specific values assigned to the category of leisure. Solitude, 
tranquility, masculinity and prosperity are just a few such qualities brought together in order to 
invoke a self-reflexive thematic that questions the motivations of recreational preoccupations. 
Looking more closely at the ideological implications of the province’s reputation, we see 
intersecting projections upon the sites of the so-called ‘wilderness’, evidencing different fantasies 
of space. Certain landscapes are hidden so that others might be highlighted. Through these 
spatial economies, we view complex and shifting, continually contested landscapes of leisure.

This exhibition is curated by Jennifer Cane, a candidate to the Masters Degree in Critical 
Curatorial Studies at The University of British Columbia, with support from the Killy Foundation,
the Alvin Balkind Fund for Student Curatorial Initiatives, the Morris and Helen Belkin Art Gallery, 
and the Department of Art History, Visual Art and Theory at The University of British Columbia.

<a href="/instant-coffee/listings/2009-04-23-03-republic-gallery-antonia-hirsch-apr-24">03. REPUBLIC GALLERY | Antonia Hirsch | APR 24</a>
------------------------------------------------------------------------
Antonia Hirsch

ANTHROPOMETRICS VOL. I & VOL. II

April 24 - May 28
Opening reception Friday, April 24, 6 - 8 pm

Anthropometrics Vol. I & Vol. II is a suite of colour photographs that operates as an inventory of 
'colloquial' methods of measurement using the human body as their base reference. The 
demonstration of these measurements results in the idiosyncratic gestures captured in the 
photographs. Anthropometrics Vol. I & Vol. II seeks to trace and document methods of 
measurement that remain in use parallel to standardized modes of measurement. These types of 
'biometric' measurements have a kinship to oral history that is passed on by generations of 
families or other social networks, rather than through official institutions. They require face-to-face 
exchange, rather than abstract or remote forms of interaction.

This is the first presentation of Vol. II of the series.

Antonia Hirsch is a Vancouver-based artist who has received critical attention for her work in 
Canada, Europe and Asia. Her work is currently on view as part of "How Soon Is Now" at the 
Vancouver Art Gallery and in the context of "awashawave" at the Blackwood Gallery in 
Mississauga. "Double Blind", a new public artwork commissioned for the Vancouver Community 
College, will be inaugurated in late April 2009. Additional information on the artist is available at 
antoniahirsch.com.

REPUBLIC GALLERY

<a href="/instant-coffee/listings/2009-04-23-04-vancouver-flying-university-artistic-strategies-in-the-publi">04. Vancouver Flying University | Artistic Strategies in the Public Sphere | APR 24</a>
------------------------------------------------------------------------
Vancouver Flying University event

Exhibitions / Projects / Distribution:
Artistic Strategies in the Public Sphere

Friday, April 24th - 7:00 - 8:30 pm 
Urban Subjects / Fillip Studio
305 Cambie St. (blue door at Cambie and Cordova)

Tim Sharp will present the /CD/DVD ROM /as an artistic strategy with examples from three joint 
projects: ImagiNative /Research as an artistic strategy; Hidden Histories: Remapping Mozart and 
Logbook 2006/2007: A Bulgarian Journey.
Lisl Ponger will talk about Hidden Histories: Remapping Mozart. A project which although it was 
part of the Austrian celebration of Mozart was focused on hidden positions, facts and structures 
that normally remain invisible and buried during national anniversary celebrations.
Their talk will be followed by a panel discussion with artist, writer and curator Kathleen Ritter and 
writer, editor and curator Jordan Strom.

To become immersed in Mozart, his time, his work and its reception 
means becoming involved in a confrontation with history. 
Even today, this history is mostly written from the perspective 
of power and the majority. Space and time, past and present are 
inscribed with powerful and historically developed images and 
geographical maps of that past—with the accompanying fading, 
ousting and negation of very many other perspectives and in the 
face of many other positions. There are, therefore, those who write 
history and those about whom history is written, the latter having a 
historiographical presence only as objects. 

Remapping Mozart consists of four exhibitions—called configurations—
that were realised in four locations in Vienna over a period of eight 
months from March to October 2006. The configurations are designed 
as committed, public, artistic and discursive presentations and will take 
place with the cooperation of scientists, theoreticians, artists and activists. 
Spatially and chronologically separate from each other, their order forms a 
structure that actively and critically examines historical and contemporary 
problems in art, politics and society, shifts the established perceptions and 
thus creates new meanings. The titles of all of the configurations are quotes 
from Mozart’s opera libretti. They give the four configurations a framework 
and, in this new conjunction, form a programmatic narrative.

Essential for the methodological way of approaching the configurations 
is an understanding of art exhibitions in an extended sense: research 
is regarded as an artistic strategy, the results of which are visualised 
in the form of documents, information and artistic works. 
The presentation of culturally and socio-politically relevant content, 
making the resultant knowledge accessible and distributing it, took 
place in the form of so-called “information exhibitions”. 
Accordingly, spatial and time-based artistic media are just as much 
a part of the exhibition as contemporary documents and objects or 
discursive events. In addition, the information zones are important 
elements of the presentation, enabling access to the relevant literature 
in the form of collections of writings and books that allow the work 
process to be followed, even if only in outline.

Lisl Ponger has shown widely in Europe including recent solo shows at the CUC, Berlin, 
Kunsthaus Dresden, and Charim Galerie, in Vienna.
In ArtForum, March 2007, Bridgitte Huck summarizes Ponger’s work:
"A visual artist, photographer, and filmmaker, Ponger is equally at home at Documenta (she 
participated in 2002) and at film festivals. Acting (often in the same work) as director, set 
designer, performer, and archivist, she investigates the interfaces between art and science, 
between sociology, art history, and political activism, moving obliquely through these disciplines to 
create compositions of explosive power and precise observation. Ponger interrogates the 
resonances of non-Western art within Western modernism."

Tim Sharp’s work is concerned with the construction of historical and personal memory. In his 
photo work he deals with the relationship between image and text while his films explore the 
changing relationship of image to sound and the mutability of the documentary assertion made 
by lens-based images. He has taken part in many international exhibitions and film festivals 
including Routes – Imaging Travel and Migration in the Grazer Kunstverein; Black Atlantic in the 
Haus der Kulturen der Welt in Berlin as well as Shake in the OK Centrum für Gegenwartskunst, 
Linz and Villa Arson, Nice. More recently he participated in TIEFENRAUSCH also at the OK 
Centrum für Gegenwartskunst, Linz. In 2005 and 2006 he took part in shows in the Kunsthalle 
Dresden where he will be showing new work in 2010 as part of the series of exhibitions Notes on 
the Empire.

http://remappingmozart.mur.at/
http://www.lislponger.com/
http://www.timsharp.at

Vancouver Flying University
Initiated by independent journalist Am Johal in collaboration with
US, Urban Subjects (Sabine Bitter, Jeff Derksen, Helmut Weber).

Based on the concept of mobile seminars and talks that Hungarian and
Polish dissidents used to skirt authoritarian state institutions,
Vancouver Flying University considers the current context of
gentrification and economic distortion of Vancouver’s inner city in
the lead up to the 2010 Olympics.

An active reframing of the political and social dimension of the civic 
dialogue has taken place to prepare the public for the coming of the
spectacle. Vancouver Flying University is looking to take a critical
view of displacement, private security expansion, changes in policing
policies and the development of concepts and positions which limit the 
right to the city and the processes by which they have become
normalized in the civic imagination.

<a href="/instant-coffee/listings/2009-04-23-05-sfu-gallery-kafka-in-the-penal-colony-apr-25">05. SFU Gallery | Kafka in The Penal Colony | APR 25</a>

The Insurance Man: Kafka in The Penal Colony 

April 25 – June 27, 2009, SFU Gallery, Burnaby Campus 

Three years in the making, this theatrical installation probes Kafka’s references in his story In The 
Penal Colony, where a researcher/explorer arrives at a South Seas penal colony and is blithely 
introduced to an strange apparatus designed for capital punishment. Anticipating the torture 
machinery of both the mid- and late 20th century, the story is a cautionary tale for our times, as is 
this exhibition. 

The combination of high seriousness and comic hyper-reality make Franz Kafka’s reality ours as 
well; his/our world is both surreal and subreal. His punishment instrument has been 
metaphorically recreated at SFU in a room filled with Kafka-era portraits, books, objects, sounds
and texts, blending the real with the uncanny, the archival with the contemporary. 

This is the first-ever Kafka exhibition in Vancouver, a city not entirely unlike Kafka’s Prague, as 
the full complexity of modernity has only taken hold here recently, a century after Prague 
endured its modernism-caused growing pains. Kafka knew Prague intimately; he knew its urban 
vistas, its offices and factories, as well as people of all classes, ethnic groups and language 
groups. Those people are the citizens in this installation, present as both participants and 
witnesses. The exhibition was conceived by Jerry Zaslove and co-curated by Bill Jeffries and Jerry 
Zaslove; it is accompanied by a brochure with a 3500-word text exploring the history and 
implications of Kafka’s colony. We hope to have a photograph of the installation up on our 
website by the day of the opening. 

Curators’ talk: on the origins and implications of the exhibition; at the Gallery, 2PM Saturday April 
25, followed by a reception from 3 to 5pm.

THOUGHT ON FILM XV
Monthly Reading + Discussion Group Exploring Cinema + Cinematic Practice
Through Written Words

Provoked by the presentation of Althea Thauberger’s Chelsea Girls at this
year’s Signal + Noise Festival, April’s meeting will feature J. M.
Coetzed’s 2003 Nobel Prize for Literature acceptance speech He and His
Man. Thauberger’s Chelsea Girls is informed by contemporary insights on
the legacy of Andy Warhol and his film of the same name, reflecting the
psycho-social issues of today. The final product is an exchange of
narratives, the distillation of a story passed back and forth from
resident to actor, director to camera person, and back again. In Coetzee’s
speech, you will find a confusion of author and subject, of quotation and
primary text, of master and slave going back to the “golden times” of
colonialism, and an examination of the sometimes brutal, sometimes
emancipating power dynamics of representation. Thauberger and Coetzee
share an interest in confusing traditional or easy conpartmentalizations,
each of them proposing re-readings that are derived from, but distinct
from, an inherited past.

Experimenting with the form of reading groups and their discussions, this
month’s selection will be read out loud by participants. No pre-reading
required!

If you have any questions about Thought on Film, please do not hesitate to
contact Programs Manager + Curator cheyanne turions at
cheyanne@cineworks.ca.

<a href="/instant-coffee/listings/2009-04-23-07-morris-and-helen-belkin-art-gallery-lorna-brown-and-laiwan-a">07. Morris and Helen Belkin Art Gallery | LORNA BROWN and LAIWAN | APR 27</a>
------------------------------------------------------------------------
Artist Talk: LORNA BROWN and LAIWAN

Monday, April 27, 6 to 7:30 pm
Lillooet Room - Rm 301, Irving K. Barber Learning Centre (formerly Main Library)
1961 East Mall, UBC

The Morris and Helen Belkin Art Gallery and the Walter C. Koerner Library at the University of 
British Columbia are pleased to announce an artists’ talk by Lorna Brown and Laiwan. This talk 
accompanies Brown's installation, "Threshold (cont.)" that is currently on view at Walter C. 
Koerner Library.

"Threshold (cont.)" by Lorna Brown is part of on ongoing inquiry into the topic of 'boredom' that
she began in 2000. Within various fields of research, boredom has been written about in diverse 
ways: as a condition that is dangerous to society, a precursor to positive radical change, and the 
result of becoming overwhelmed by tedious, repetitive activity. This video installation is made up 
of quoted texts from sources in literature, criticism, sociology and other discourses. Placed in the 
context of a university library where the published results of research are stored and accessed 
through different media, the work raises questions about how knowledge is circulated, the 
processes of engaged learning, and the weariness or lack of interest that can result from 
information overload.

Lorna Brown is a Vancouver-based artist, writer, and curator. She has exhibited her work 
internationally since 1984 and has taught at Emily Carr University of Art and Design and at Simon
Fraser University. Brown was the curator for "Group Search: art in the library," a series of artists’ 
projects at the Vancouver Public Library (2006-08) and is currently developing the Centre for Art
in Public Spaces at Langara College. She received the Vancouver Institute for the Visual Arts 
Award (1996) and the Canada Council Paris Studio Award (2000).

Laiwan is an artist, writer, and curator recognized for her interdisciplinary practice and for her 
activism in the gay and feminist communities. Her work is part of the Vancouver Art Gallery’s 
current exhibition, "How Soon is Now? Contemporary Art from Here" at the Vancouver Art Gallery 
(to May 3, 2009). In 2007, she created an ongoing, interactive website "Call Numbers: The 
Library Recordings", <http://thelibraryrecordings.eciad.ca/> a collaborative project with the 
Vancouver Public Library that was curated by Lorna Brown. Laiwan lives in Vancouver and 
teaches in the MFA Interdisciplinary Arts Program at Goddard College in Washington State.

as you enter the building from 7th Avenue, the reception will take place in the event space at top

Double Blind is a large-scale sculptural installation by Antonia Hirsch, located in the atrium of the 
new North Building on Vancouver Community College’s Broadway Campus that houses the 
School of Health Sciences. 
Spanning four stories of the building, Double Blind translates a Snellen, or eye, chart into braille 
by using dome mirrors in order to emulate the raised dots of braille script. 

This sculptural work is accompanied by a T-shirt multiple that echoes the installation’s oxymoronic 
premise; those who can see the work likely don’t read braille, and those who read braille can 
probably not see the work. The T-shirts will be available for purchase at the opening event.

<a href="/instant-coffee/listings/2009-04-23-12-caf-for-contemporary-art-navin-rawanchaikul-til-may-3">12. café for contemporary art | Navin Rawanchaikul | til | MAY 3</a>
------------------------------------------------------------------------

café for contemporary art proudly presents: 

WE LOVE YOU COMRADE NAVIN
An exhibition of works from Navin Party
March 23rd - May 3rd, 2009

(Exhibition has been extended!)

On March 21, 2009, café for contemporary art, North Vancouver’s newest contemporary art 
space and café opened its inaugural exhibition WE LOVE YOU COMRADE NAVIN. This 
exhibition presents two projects from Navin Party, a globetrotting narrative developed by artist 
Navin Rawanchaikul and café for contemporary art director Tyler Russell. 

A humorously hopeful reaction to the practice of nationalizing, ethnicising, and multi-ethno-
nationalizing of personal identity for the purposes of market and politics, Navin Party emerged in 
2006 out of a conversation Navin Rawanchaikul had with Tyler Russell in a Chiang Mai café. 
Navin had recently been invited to participate in “Diaspora”, a multimedia presentation featuring
“diasporic” artists of South East Asia, part of the cultural programme of the 2006 Board of 
Governors meeting of the World Bank and the IMF in Singapore. Playfully taking issue with being 
labeled a “diaspora artist”, Navin thought it would be fun to slightly destabilize the theme of the 
event and rather than offer a sentimental exploration of what it means to be a person of Hindu-
Punjabi descent growing up in Chiang Mai. His basic idea was to go to India, a country he had 
never been to, and interview people who shared his name: Navin or Naveen. He was hoping to 
somehow link this project to his long held desire to make a bollywood-style music video.

Tyler Russell was, as now, an aspiring cultural worker/curator primarily concerned with how we 
identify ourselves and relate with one another. He was well aware of North American 
multiculturalism’s habit of urging not-white and not-quite-white people to identify as hyphenated
creatures lurking somewhere between ethno-national categories. With this in mind, he proposed 
that it might be interesting to globalize the issue and initiate a campaign to unite the Navins of 
the world under the banner of Navin Party, www.navinparty.com. Navin completely concurred and 
immediately contacted his web guy to reserve the domain name. When the two later searched 
“Navin” on the internet, they discovered that it is not just an East Indian name derived from the
Sanskrit word for novel or new, but that Steve Martin sports the name in the 1979 film “The Jerk”, 
and among other autonomous origins, as a last name it has Gaelic roots. 

From there, Tyler and Navin began collaborating on the production of series of works based on a 
story where Rawanchaikul, a lonely son of diaspora, founds a global party for people who share 
his name. Traveling the globe in search of fellow Navins, the project has gone on to incorporate 
various cultural and propaganda forms into the ever-evolving narrative of the Navin Party. Navin 
Party projects have been developed in India, Japan, China, Korea and most recently for Dan 
Cameron’s Prospect New Orleans in the USA. Works from the series have been collected 
internationally including by institutions such as the UBS Art Collection.

We Love You Comrade Navin presents two works from this series Navins of Bollywood and 
Quotations from Comrade Navin. Navins of Bollywood, directed by Mumbai-based director Naren 
Mojidra, is a bollywood-style short musical film that recounts the founding tale of the Navin Party. 
It was filmed in Mumbai in June of 2006. Meanwhile, Quotations from Comrade Navin, complete 
with a ceramic bust of Navin Party Chairman Navin Rawanchaikul, a book of navinist quotes and 
a series of woodblock propaganda prints features the video of a mao-inspired propaganda 
campaign executed by the Navin Party in Beijing in the summer of 2007.

I am seeking paper proposals / presentations appropriate for the following panel to be held at the 
College Art Association next February:

The Age of Enlargement:
In a London newspaper in 1845 an anonymous author used the phrase Age of Enlargement to 
satirically critique the burgeoning industry and class segregation going on around him. This 
phrase also describes contemporary social and political conditions. Decades of trade 
deregulation have resulted in greater income disparities between the rich and poor, while the 
world's slums increase in size, and global building-booms and consumer demand for cheap 
goods requires unsustainable extractions of natural resources. Closer to home, calorie-counting 
restaurant signage in New York attempts to curb the growing obesity epidemic, and dangerous 
new weather patterns destroy homes in Louisiana and California. Are these extremes qualitatively 
different from those of the past, or is such thinking only a vanity in the minds of the living? This 
panel solicits paper proposals on subjects that address obesity, enlargement, gluttony, excessive
wealth or poverty, and similar or related themes. Paper topics can be historical or contemporary,
and interdisciplinary scholarship that considers art or visual culture in the context of current 
economic and/or political theory is of particular interest.

The Western Front Society seeks an outstanding, highly motivated arts professional to take on 
the role of Art Director / Co-Editor of FRONT magazine.

The Western Front is one of Canada's pioneering artist-run centres and produces and presents 
works in five programs: Exhibitions, Performance Art, New Music, Media Arts, and Publications 
(FRONT magazine). The Western Front was founded in 1973 by a small group of interdisciplinary 
artists, and has developed into an exemplary multi-disciplinary environment for experimental art 
practice and research. With a staff of ten, plus interns and volunteers, the Society collectively
produces over fifty events a year.

FRONT magazine has a mandate to publish contemporary works for the printed page. Through 
columns, studio profiles, interviews and theme-based artist projects, FRONT seeks to engage 
readers and serve as a launch pad for a diverse range of work by emerging and established 
artists. FRONT magazine is published four times a year.

Responsibilities

The Art Director/Co-Editor reports to the Board of Directors, and is an active participant in the
Western Front Management Team. Working closely with the Managing Editor, this position 
assumes co-leadership and responsibility of the publications program and for:

hands-on pre-press.

• Selecting and editing content

• Preparing the publication for mailing and distribution

• Preparing grant applications

• Attending and organizing events related to the publication

• Visioning and shaping the future of the Western Front Publications Program

• Collaborating with other Western Front programs

• Sharing responsibility with the Management Team for carrying out the policies of the Western 
Front and guiding staff and/or volunteers involved with the core operations.
