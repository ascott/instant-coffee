Bcc: A New Digital Art Subscription Program by Decoy Magazine
Curated by Maria Fedorova

http://www.decoymagazine.ca/bcc

Bcc: is a monthly, digital art subscription program that is designed to support new 
media artists and their audiences by delivering newly created contemporary 
digital artworks to subscribers' inboxes every month. The subscription fees we 
collect go directly into the pockets of the diverse media artists who are 
commissioned to create new digital works that will be released exclusively to Bcc: 
subscribers. The commissioned artworks have never been exhibited before, and 
each one will only be released once! We see this project as a gesture of 
collective patronage that supports the work and livelihood of digital artists.

The more subscribers we have, the more we can pay the participating artists.
It is only $3 per month to subscribe or $30 for the whole year. 

We are happy to announce that San Francisco-based Anthony Discenza will be 
the first commissioned artist for the project. His artistic practice is centred around 
the exploration of hybridity and fragmentation of digital content and imagery 
available online. Discenza works in a wide range of media including text, video, 
and sound. He has had exhibitions at SFMOMA, MOCA Cleveland, the Getty 
Center in LA, Aspen Art Museum and more. http://anthonydiscenza.info/

We believe it is very timely to revisit the idea of digital art ownership and to re-
imagine how we might define a culture of sharing creative work. Sign up by 
November 27th and receive the first artwork for free! Sign up here: 
http://www.decoymagazine.ca/bcc

A min. of silence, 2016 
HD video, 1 min loop
Jacob Gleeson 

November 10 - December 7 
5 pm - 12 am

'Clearing the mind and sliding in 
to that created space, 
a web of waters steaming over rocks, 
air misty but not raining'

'Who has come up with 
these miraculous forests and springs? 
Pale ink 
on fine white silk'

- Gary Snyder from 'Endless Streams and Mountains' 

For this iteration of After Hours in the READ Books window, Vancouver based 
artist Jacob Gleeson presents an intimate portrait of a natural hot spring in an 
undisclosed Pacific Northwest location. 
Within the image, moss thrives on fallen trunks and severed boughs as cedar 
and sword ferns frame the lush scene. Geothermal steam rises to meet the 
sunlight filtering down through tall firs. The illuminated clouds hang meditatively 
over the pool below ad infinitum. 
Gleeson shares an experience of transcendent repose in an undeveloped 
pocket of BC wilderness. An unexpected diversion from the quotidian lull for 
observant tourists and passersby. 

Jacob Gleeson is a Vancouver based artist and facilitator whose work has 
appeared in numerous galleries, festivals and publications including: SFU 
Gallery, Western Front, Belkin Satellite, Catriona Jeffries, Charles H. Scott, Helen 
Pitt, VIVO, Access, Open Space, Noorderzon Film Festival (NL), Nuit Blanche, 
Contemporary Magazine, Fillip, Pyramid Power and Vancouver Art & Economies 
(Artspeak).

------------------------------------------------------------------------
Website: terminal.front.bc.ca 

TERMINAL is a four-part project that examines single user interfaces, and the 
influence of technology on the adaptation of artistic forms. The project addresses 
itself to an idea of what different hardware units, operating systems, and user 
environments have offered to artists and to the viewers, readers or users of their 
work. Each iteration of the project considers a touchstone in computer interfacing. 
The second installation and website launch looks at graphical user interfaces, and 
takes up uses of the Commodore Amiga as a creative space for drawing, animation 
and contemplations of nostalgia.

Featuring:

An installation of Mark Pellegrino's video G.I.R.L., a humorous visual essay about 
early BBS culture; Three Cats in a Tree a digital painting by Barry Doup, made 
using Deluxe Paint; and (Untitled) a golden Amiga, made by Marisa Olson, as part of 
her series Time Capsules. 
The website includes Amiga Shorts by Amy Lockhart, Vhery, by Barry Doup, and a 
new essay by Clint Enns.

The On Edge Readings Series presents:

Jonina Kirton & Pamela Mordecai

The public is welcome 

In conjunction with Poets for the Peace, this night will be dedicated to send 
some love up to the Peace River in northern BC, in the spirit of solidarity with the 
Treaty 8 First Nations who are protecting the river valley from being destroyed by 
the Site C dam. The $9 billion dollar dam would flood over 100 km of river valley, 
destroying important fish habitat, hunting grounds, wildlife migration corridors, 
precious farmland and much more. The dam violates treaty rights, and has not 
been assessed with due diligence by the BC Utilities Commission. There is still 
time to stop the dam, which will take many years to build. 
http://www.poetsforthepeace.ca/

Author bios

Jonina Kirton Kirton, a Mtis/Icelandic poet, currently lives in the unceded territory 
of the Coast Salish people. She brings her experience as a graduate of the SFU 
Writer's Studio, a sacred circle facilitator, and a student of Continuum, to her 
writing and teaching. She remains curious about memory and the many ways 
one can access it. Her first collection of poetry, page as bone ~ ink as blood, 
released in April 2015 by Talonbooks, has been described as "restorative, 
intimate poetry, drawing down ancestral ideas into the current moment's breath." 
www.joninakirton.wix.com/poet.

Pamela Mordecai writes poetry and long and short fiction. Her first novel, Red 
Jacket, published by TAP Books, was shortlisted for the 2015 Rogers Writers' 
Trust Fiction Prize. Mawenzi House also published her sixth book of poetry, de 
book of Mary, in 2015. A former language arts teacher , she writes poetry, plays 
and stories for children and is committed to the use of heart languages in poetry 
and fiction. Born in Jamaica and educated there and in the USA, she and her 
family moved to Canada in 1993. She lives in Kitchener, Ontario.

<a href="/instant-coffee/listings/2016-11-17-09-emily-carr-university-of-art-design-alwyn-obrien-the-line-an">09. TALK | Emily Carr University of Art + Design | Alwyn O'Brien: The Line and the Fold | NOV 17</a>

North-West Ceramics Foundation presents:
Alwyn O'Brien: The Line and the Fold

Thursday, November 17, 2016 - 7:30 pm
Rm 245, North Building
Emily Carr University of Art + Design
1399 Johnston Street, Granville Island

All Welcome, free

The North-West Ceramics Foundation is pleased to present a lecture by Alwyn 
O'Brien. For O'Brien, the vessel is the "foundational metaphor" of her practice, 
but her vessels in no way resemble conventional containers. Consisting of 
assymetrical helps of lacy clay filaments elaborated to the point of near collapse, 
her ambitious yet fragile structures explore aspects of desire, entropy and loss. 
As a maker, she is interested in "the threshold where the immaterial and the 
material...fall into each other..." Incorporating historical references--in particular, 
to the Baroque--her vessels undermine notions of stability, embody fluidity, and 
celebrate the irregular and the possible.

Born and raised on Salt Spring Island, Alwyn O'Brien attended Sheridan College 
of Craft and Design, Oakville (diploma 2002), Emily Carr University of Art + 
Design, Vancouver (BFA 2004), and the University of Washington, Seattle (MFA 
2010). She has attended residencies at Pilchuck Glass in Washington State, 
Skowhegan School of Painting and Sculpture in Maine and the international 
wood-fired ceramics symposium in Kohila Parish, Estonia.

In 2012, she was nominated for the RBC Emerging Artist competition held at the 
Gardiner Museum, Toronto, and was awarded the North-West Ceramics 
Foundation Award for excellence in the field of ceramics. In 2015 O'Brien won 
the prestigious Winifred Shantz Award for Ceramics after which she studied 
Talavera Pottery at the Arquetopia Foundation in Puebla, Mexico. She has 
participated in numerous solo and group exhibitions in Canada and the United 
States, and her work held in public and private collections in both those 
countries. She is represented by the James Harris Gallery, Seattle, WA and is 
currently an instructor in ceramics at Langara College, Vancouver.

For more information see:

westceramicsfoundation.weebly.com

Alwyn O'Brien: http://jamesharrisgallery.com/artists/alwyn-obrien/

Is Crimson The Colour Blue?
Simone Jarvis
November 17th - December 17th, 2016
Opening Thursday, November 17th, 7:00 10:00PM

17 West Broadway
Vancouver, BC. Canada
V5Y 1P1
contact: info@field-contemporary.com
www.field-contemporary.com

Is Crimson The Colour Blue?, a solo exhibition by Simone Jarvis, presents new
paintings and sculptures that regard the presence of form and colour. The
exhibition gets it's disposition and titles from significant moments in the artist's
life. Representative emotive subjectivities similar to that of a conversation with a
friend, a witnessed happening, or a sighting on a walk or bike ride attest in
making her work autobiographical. Forms and gestures found in the paintings
reference spaces from flora to urban structures and domestic objects. Rich colour
and monochromatic hues are exalted, guiding the viewer through the numerous
spaces within the paintings.

Simone Jarvis is an artist currently working out of Vancouver BC. Originally from
Calgary, Alberta, she received her Bachelor of Arts from Emily Carr University. Her
most recent exhibitions include Towards a Fictional History of Colour at Burrard
Arts Foundation and How Long Can You Contain an Echo? at Field
Contemporary in 2015, Vancouver BC. Jarvis's painting practice explores formal
qualities and processes of the material. Her paintings, drawings, and sculptures
continue to be organized primarily by colour and form.
