------------------------------------------------------------------------
GREG GIRARD
Hanoi Calling / In the Near Distance

Monte Clark Gallery
February 17 - March 19, 2011
Opening reception: Thursday February 17, 6 - 8pm

The Monte Clark Gallery is pleased to present an exhibition of
photographs by Greg Girard.

The main gallery will feature works from Girard’s 2010 Magenta
Foundation publication "Hanoi Calling: One Thousand Years Now". 
For
this project, Girard explores the overlooked features that define life
for residents of Hanoi, Vietnam's capital, at their millennial
anniversary.

Works from Girard’s 2010 Kominek Gallery edition, “In the Near
Distance” will also be on display in the rear gallery space. A
collection of Girard’s earlier photographs circa 1973-1986, this body
of work follows Girard in his travels through Japan, Korea, China,
Vancouver, California, Las Vegas and San Francisco.

Greg Girard's photographs are collected by the National Gallery of
Canada, the Vancouver Art Gallery, the Canada Council Art Bank, the
Royal Bank of Canada, and more. His works have been featured in
National Geographic, TIME, and Newsweek, among others. 
Previous Greg
Girard exhibitions were reviewed by The New Yorker, The National 
Post,
TIME, and The Star. Greg Girard's book Phantom Shanghai is listed 
as
one of the top 10 photography books of all time by The Independent,
(UK).

Vector Association at Western Front

Matei Bejenaru, Dan Acostioaei, Florin Bobu and
Ayman Ramadan 

Opening: Thursday, February 17, 7 PM 
Exhibition: February 18 – April 16, 2011

Western Front is pleased to present an exhibition co-organized with 
Vector Association. Based in Iasi, Romania, Vector Association is a 
platform for contemporary art, which produces the Periferic Biennial, 
publishes Vector Magazine, commissions public projects as an 
initiate-member of the European Network for Public Art Producers 
and hosts residents in the Accented Residency Network. 

At the Western Front, Vector Association will showcase the range of 
its activities through an exhibition of works by Iasi based artists: 
Matei Bejenaru (RO), Dan Acostioaei (RO) and Florin Bobu (RO), as 
well as their current resident, Ayman Ramadan (EG). This 
collaboration with Western Front is the next chapter of Vector 
Association's year on the road, while they produce projects outside 
of their home city of Iasi. Previous to this stop, Vector Association 
was present at Frieze Art Fair 2010 (London, UK) as a Frieze 
Projects EU Partner.

The exhibition is intended as a cross section view of Vector's role in 
articulating a contemporary art scene in the city of Iasi, since the late 
1990s. As a group of works, the exhibition offers a survey of the 
current working methodologies of some of the city's artists and how 
the context of the city of Iasi, is used as a site of production by 
visiting artists.

W. www.front.bc.ca E. exhibitions@front.bc.ca
Gallery Hours: Tuesday - Saturday 12 - 5pm

NOMAD GALLERY

225 E. Georgia

SECOND OPENING

Friday, February 18th from 8pm - 12am

Show runs until February 28th, 2011

Actualized by a group of Emily Carr grads in January 2011, with the 
imperative participation of some forty graduating artists, the NOMAD 
Gallery is a self referential, not-for-profit organization which currently 
runs for one month at a time.

One of the prevailing characteristic of the project is its "nomadic" 
spontaneity spawning out of the participation and support of art 
communities not limited to city, country or continent. The gallery 
recognizes the obsolete definition of this term; to be a nomad, it is 
no longer imperative to travel in caravans, living off the plains of 
Tibet. A modern day nomad utilizes assets of modern technology, 
crosses oceans on a whim and calls the world his home. This 
product of globalization is reflected in the prerogative of Nomad. 
Dissipating as rapidly as it forms, the traveling gallery offers a 
glimpse of emerging artists in Vancouver with more cities to come.

<a href="/instant-coffee/listings/2011-02-17-06-open-letter-available-negotiating-the-social-bond-of-poetics">06. Open Letter | available | “Negotiating the Social Bond of Poetics” | FEB 18</a>

CFP for Open Letter

“Negotiating the Social Bond of Poetics”

Edited by Nancy Gillespie and Peter Jaeger

The idea for this issue of Open Letter developed out of a year long 
series of poetry readings and critical seminars run by Nancy 
Gillespie at the Kootenay School of Writing in Vancouver, under the 
title “Negotiating the Social Bond of Poetics.” The series drew on 
Jacques Lacan’s concept of the “four discourses,” in particular his 
Seminar XVII: The Other Side of Psychoanalysis (published in 
English for the first time in 2007). Delivered shortly after the May 
1968 events in Paris, this seminar engages with social interaction 
and its relation to language and power. Key to the seminar are 
Lacan’s four structures of discourses—i.e., the discourse of the 
Master, the University, the Hysteric, and the Analyst—and how these 
discourses form different social bonds for the subject.

We are looking for critical writing and poetry that responds to the 
questions below: Although Lacan’s seminar was first presented 40 
years ago, it is arguable that Lacan’s scepticism of the 68 events, 
and his assertion that the discourse of the University was becoming 
a new Master’s discourse, foresaw the dawn of a new ethos of 
capitalism that we now call Neo-Liberalism. Can returning to 
Lacan’s theory now offer new and useful methods of criticism? Is 
Lacan’s theory of the “four discourses” useful for rethinking how we 
engage with and disseminate poetry and poetics? Can poetics 
operate like the discourse of the Analyst to create a different social 
bond through language? Is Lacan’s concept of the structure of the 
four discourses useful for us today, particularly as we head into 
financial cuts in the arts and academia which may limit interventions 
in hegemonic discourses?

Please send critical proposals to gillespie.nancy@googlemail.com 
and poetry proposals or declarations of intention to 
p.jaeger@roehampton.ac.uk by March 15th, 2011. We are also 
interested in work that blurs these boundaries, which should be 
sent to both of us. Complete work will be requested by mid-August 
2011.

For more details about the KSW “Negotiating the Social Bond of 
poetics” series please see the website or write to Nancy Gillespie: 
http://www.kswnet.org/fire/announcementtargetpage.cfm?
showannouncement=NEGOTIATING.htm&announceID=315

<a href="/instant-coffee/listings/2011-02-17-07-republic-gallery-antonia-hirsch-feb-18">07. REPUBLIC GALLERY | Antonia Hirsch | FEB 18</a>
------------------------------------------------------------------------
Antonia Hirsch

STOPPAGES

Opening reception: Friday, February 18th, 6 - 9 pm
Showing: February 18th to April 9th, 2011 

"Stoppages" is the latest work in Antonia Hirsch’s ongoing World 
Map Project series, begun in 2000. "Stoppages" is designed as a 
portable unit that involves polyester straps of the type commonly 
used for the purposes of restraint or containment; these render the 
land borders of each sovereign country in the world at a scale of 
1:1,000,000. For the exhibition at Republic Gallery, "Stoppages" -- 
as a highly abstracted map -- is transposed into the real space of 
the gallery, thereby giving rise to a dubious alliance of states. 

--

Antonia Hirsch lives and works in Berlin and Vancouver. Her 
practice consistently engages with systems -- geographical, 
quantitative, syntactic -- that underwrite the most basic 
understandings of the world. She questions the often invisible 
hierarchies of these epistemological structures by relating them to 
more familiar territory: embodied experience. Her work has been 
exhibited at Program, Berlin, the Taipei Fine Arts Museum, Power 
Plant in Toronto, the Contemporary Art Gallery in Vancouver, and as 
part of Frieze Projects in London, UK, among others. Her work can 
be found in public collections such as that of the Vancouver Art 
Gallery, the Canada Council Art Bank, and the Sackner Archive of 
Concrete & Visual Poetry, Miami Beach. Her work is currently on 
view at the National Gallery of Canada as part of "It is What It Is, 
Recent Acquisitions of New Canadian Art"; at the Neue Sachsische 
Galerie, Chemnitz, Germany, as part of "Gullivers Sechste Reise", 
and at the Or Gallery in Vancouver with "Komma (after Dalton 
Trumbo’s Johnny Got His Gun)", starting February 12. More 
information on the artist’s practice can be found at 
antoniahirsch.com. 

--

"Stoppages" will be on show February 18th through April 9th, 2011 
with an artist reception on Friday, February 18th, 6 - 9pm. Regular 
gallery hours are 11am - 5pm, Wednesday through Saturday, and 
by appointment.

<a href="/instant-coffee/listings/2011-02-17-08-cag-catalogue-launch-an-invitation-to-an-infiltration-feb-19">08. CAG | Catalogue Launch | An Invitation to An Infiltration | FEB 19</a>
------------------------------------------------------------------------
An Invitation to An Infiltration publication launch

With special performance by Jonathan Middleton

Saturday, February 19, 3-5pm

Please join us at the CAG on Saturday, February 19, from 3 to 5 pm 
to celebrate our new exhibition catalogue An Invitation to An 
Infiltration. Jonathan Middleton will be in attendance to write 
fictionalized dedications to imagined recipients in your newly 
purchased and prized publication.

This timely catalogue launch comes one year after the exhibition 
and the 2010 Winter Olympics Games. An Invitation to An Infiltration 
was guest curated by Eric Fredericksen. He invited Fia Backström, 
Lucy Clout, Hadley+Maxwell, Jonathan Middleton, Dexter Sinister, 
Holly Ward and Jordan Wolfson to participate in an exhibition that 
explored the potential competitive nature of group shows against the 
background of one of the world’s major sporting competitions.

Points of conflict can often exist between artists, curators, and 
institutions and was brought to light in a variety of ways. For 
example, Jonathan Middleton’s proposal of a new title for the 
exhibition became a source of real antagonism, between artist, 
institution and guest curator. Funding from the Vancouver Olympic 
Organizing Committee (VANOC) presented opportunities for the 
artists to critique the acceptance of monies from a controversial and 
controlling source, as well as scrutinize the way the CAG handled 
the distribution of funds and the manner in which it negotiated with 
artists.

With, “the 2/4 beat of the stacking and restacking of old boxes of 
catalogues; moving in circles round the storeroom of a 
contemporary art gallery,” Lucy Clout incisively sums up the fate of 
many exhibition catalogues, relegated to the recesses of gallery 
storage spaces in unopened boxes while asking why produce a 
book when storage concerns outweigh its distribution? This 
pointed scrutiny itself is reflective of the aims of An Invitation to An 
Infiltration, to which this catalogue serves as both a document and 
as an extended forum for critical dialogue between the artists, guest 
curator and the CAG.

This new CAG publication is designed by participating artist 
collective Dexter Sinister, printed by Fillip, and edited by Jenifer 
Papararo. It features a transcript of renowned art historian Thomas 
Crow’s keynote address for the 2005 Frieze Foundation Talks, along 
side a series of blog entries and emails pertaining to the exhibition 
and the CAG’s relationship to VANOC and the 2010 Vancouver 
Winter Olympics. Fredericksen’s curatorial text and personal 
reflections on the exhibition, an audio piece by Fia Backström, and 
new artworks made specifically for the publication by 
Hadley+Maxwell and Jordan Wolfson, combine to make this an 
insightful and essential companion to the exhibition itself.

This publication was made possible with the financial support of 
VANOC and the British Columbia Arts Council under the Unique 
Opportunities programme.

Contemporary Art Gallery, 555 Nelson Street, Vancouver, BC, V6B 
6R5, Canada www.contemporaryartgallery.ca. Gallery Hours: Wed. - 
Sun. 12 – 6 pm. Admission is free by donation. For more 
information please contact: info@contemporaryartgallery.ca or 604-
681-2700.

VIVO MEDIA ARTS CENTRE
IMMINENT FUTURE
Feb 19 2011 12pm-1am
Organized by Cecily Nicholson, Althea Thauberger, Am Johal, 
Nicholas Perrin
1965 Main St. Vancouver BC www.vivomediaarts.com
info.imminentfuture@gmail.com

Imminent Future is a series of media art, poetry, performance, 
music and
cultural theory events programmed by Althea Thauberger, Nicholas 
Perrin,
Cecily Nicholson and Am Johal, that pose questions to how we 
imagine,
presume or deny the future.

If we no longer hold assumptions about The End of History, 
progress, or
the legitimacy of an avant-garde, then the question must be posed: 
Who are
we? What is the worth of the poet in a society that already knows 
better
than itself? Who can and who can't imagine a future? What is so 
terrible
about the Terror? Where's the comedy in apocalypse?

The series will commence on February 10 with a curatorial lecture by
Candice Hopkins (Elizabeth Simonfay Curatorial Resident in 
Indigenous Art
at the National Gallery of Canada ). Hopkins will discuss her 
collaboration
with Indiginous curators to mount Close Encounters: The Next 500 
Years at
the Plug In ICA in Winnipeg MB. Hopkins talk will be accompanied 
by the
screening of three videos from the exhibition: Tracey Moffatts Bedevil 
and
Night Cries: A Rural Tragedy (Australia); and Maria Thereza Alves 
Iracema
(de Questembert) (Brazil/France).

On February 19, Professor Joshua D. Goldstein from the University 
of
Calgary will facilitate a seminar titled Dancing with the Rose: Hegels
Philosophy of Right, terrorism, revolution, and finding satisfaction in 
the
present world. The seminar will be followed by short lectures by 
prominent
Vancouver writer and scholar Jerry Zaslove, SFU lecturer Fiona 
Jeffries,
and artist/curator Tania Willard, exploring notions of frozen time, fear,
and Indiginous Hip Hop.

After a performance at the Western Front by Romanian artist Matei 
Bejenaru,
comedian and writer Charles Demears will host Populist Myths of
Inevitability Cabaret. The evening will conclude with a revolutionary
dance party featuring DJ PunDit, the real featuring the unreal, and

I am a third year art student, studying photography. I am working on 
a project that examines the reaction and memory the viewer has to a 
single photograph.

If you would like to be involved please go to

www.thatlandscape.wordpress.com

The project examines multiple memories that can be evoked by one 
image.

This will be incredibly appreciated. I will be making a recording of all 
of the memories collected and will recite the memories while the 
photograph is being viewed.

I would be very grateful for those willing to participate and of course 
anyone participating will be left completely anonymous if the 
individual chooses.
