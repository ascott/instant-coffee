<a href="/instant-coffee/listings/2019-10-24-05-artspeak-hong-kai-wang-quivering-oct-25">05. OPENING | Artspeak | Hong-Kai Wang: Quivering | OCT 25</a>
------------------------------------------------------------------------

October 26 - December 7, 2019

Opening reception: Friday October 25, 2019, 8pm
Performance lecture: This is no country music, Friday November 1, 2019, 6pm

Hong-Kai Wangs exhibition Quivering is a continuation of her work through
experimental modes of sonic sociality. Wang describes Quivering as an
open
rehearsal, where the collective body becomes a conduit and site through which
knowledge is produced, and the aggregate of small affinities becomes consequential.
Operating on several different registers, quivering " bodily, socially, politically, and
geologically, Wangs works are propositions that offer possibilities for a choreography of
survival.

As an artist-in-residence at Artspeak, Wang will continue to build upon her research,
developing methodologies that will take into consideration our location on the unceded
territories of the Squamish, Musqueam and Tsleil-Waututh, and this region being
situated on the Cascadia Subduction Zone, Pacific Ring of Fire. Working with a choir
and members of the UBC Seismology department, Wang will trace points of
convergence, of histories, fault lines, through a collective embodied praxis examining
voice and vibration.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Opening Sat. Oct. 26, 5-7, Salt Spring Gallery, 135 McPhillips Ave., Salt Spring Island

Shadow Show

Melanie Thompson

We find beauty not in the thing itself but in the patterns of shadows, the light and the
darkness, that one thing against another creates. In Praise of Shadows, Junichiro
Tanizaki

Shadows, as an important visual element, have always been part of my work. From the
first lamps I made that cast their shadows up and out, patterning ceilings, walls, and
floors to the work of this exhibit I have
been intrigued by shadows, their beauty and their
impermanence. In Shadow Show materials were chosen specifically for their ability to
create shadows-a line becomes a shape becomes a shadow. The shadows created are
as important to the work as the work itself, the shadow becomes the work. One is
offered a new perspective, another way of seeing, another visual layer with the creation
of shadows. The way an object appears and the way it appears in the shadow object are
two perceptions, different yet similar, a dialogue between the real and the ephemeral, a
way to truly see the work.

Melanie Thompson 2019

'COPPER NUMENS'
by Hana Amani and Dariush Alexander

OPENING RECEPTION: October 29th, 7-11pm
OPEN GALLERY HOURS: October 20th - November 2nd, 12-4pm

Copper Numens is an exhibition of mixed-media and intaglio printmaking, works by
Hana Amani and
Dariush Alexander encompassing a variety of narratives.

Reimagining folklore and myths from the artists diverse heritages through a
contemporary framework, 'Copper Numens' presents the viewer a fresh take on non-
western stories and mythos.

These stories vary from the retelling of medieval Islamic tales of women, to reimagining
the visual world of Mexican Bollero. For many ancient cultures, copper was attributed a
sacred value, and was associated with the idea of eternal life. Numen is a divine
presence.

As the day of the dead approaches and Halloween is among us, the spirit realm is
drawn close. In honor of this time of year, the show will include a miniature tomb
encased in copper-gold leaf and mixed media. This tomb is to pay homage to the artists
recently deceased grandmothers, and they invite anyone who has lost a loved one to
write a note to the loved one and place it into a memory box, to then be incorporated in
the exhibition.

Bios

Hana Amani is a Sri Lankan visual artist
and storyteller, with Bachelor in Visual
Communication Design, from Emily Carr University of Art and Design. With a focus on
printmaking, a love for art history, and consistent curiosity for the future, Hanas work
follows themes of both historical and futuristic concepts with an emphasis on the state of
women. Hana works primarily in the print medium of Intaglio etching and linocut creating
images based on the folklore of her mixed heritage. She believes that myths and folklore
are an essential aspect of human culture that should be preserved and passed down to
future generations, especially in a time where life is so technology-driven and urban
concepts of time are fast and ephemeral. Hanas work pays tribute to recognize the
importance of our past while contemplating the state of the future. She will be published
in the upcoming Capilano review for her collection of prints, Scheherazades Dream
featuring series of prints exploring three women in Islamic mythology. She loves science
fiction, opera, fairy-tales, playing chess and listening to Amadeus at midnight.

Dariush Alexander Ghaderi Barrera is a Mexican-Iranian Visual artist. He has BFA from
Emily Carr University and is currently based in Vancouver BC. Dariush works with
diverse printmaking techniques, painting, and drawing. Dariush aims for a
multidisciplinary approach to his art.

<a href="/instant-coffee/listings/2019-10-24-13-grunt-gallery-a-sentimental-dissidence-gabi-dao-oct-31">13. OPENING | grunt gallery | a sentimental dissidence | Gabi Dao | OCT 31</a>

a sentimental dissidence by Gabi Dao
Opening Reception: October 31, 2019, 7 - 9PM
Exhibition Dates: November 1st - December 14th, 2019
@ grunt gallery
116 - 350 E. 2nd Ave
Vancouver, BC

How do you remember the past the most?

Equal parts family recollection, historical research and spectral diary, Coco Means
Ghost forms the moving-image focal point of Gabi Daos new installation.

Rooted in Daos research along the Mekong Delta and her own familys history between
cultures, a sentimental dissidence employs sculpture, video and sound to put imagined
contemporary and historical diasporic voices in conversation. At the centre of the
exhibition is a haunting: the eponymous narrator (a ghost in the form of a coconut)
resists a
singular place and time, moving freely, if not lightly, through personal
photographs, contemporary commentary and archival material. Other characters appear
" Lan, Ong Nam, Mr.Le, Quang, An, Nguyen and Dung " and together they tell the
fragmented story of Ong Dao Dua ( Mr. Coconut), a monk who founded a small, self-
sustaining, anti-war community in the late 1960s-70s on Con Phung, an island
colloquially known to Westerners as the Coconut Kingdom. Through the lens of Ong
Dao Duas oft-mythologized character, the work becomes an avenue to explore and
enmesh broader notions of memory, nationhood, belief, belonging and dreams for the
future.

Dao combines the single-channel video with sonically activated sculptures that transmit
her familys narrative in another form: excerpts from Foreign Accent Improvement
cassettes used by the artists parents in the 1980s. a sentimental dissidence points to
texture and poetics rather than conclusive fact, and creates a landscape that at once
immerses,
entangles and pushes back.

Gabi Dao is an artist and co-organizer at Duplex, a DIY project space + studio collective
based the unceded territories of the Coast Salish peoples, including the xmkwy"m
(Musqueam), Skwxw7mesh (Squamish), and Sl"lwta"/Selilwitulh (Tsleil-Waututh)
Nations. She is interested in the insistence of counter-memory and fragmentation
through the pursuit of sculpture, installation, sound, aural publishing, music and moving
image. She has recently shown her work in various contexts such as the Images
Festival, International Film Festival Rotterdam, Artspeak, Coop Radio, the internet, a
rootftop parking lot, Western Front, The National Music Centre and Unit 17.

Curated by Vanessa Kwan.

Niap: Reclamation and De-Categorization
opening Saturday, November 2 at the Marion Scott Gallery 1:00 - 4:00
The artist will do a performance at 1:30pm.
QUEBEC INUIT ARTIST SEES COLLABORATION WITH PHOTOGRAPHER AS AN
ACT OF RECONCILIATION

Vancouver, BC-When the Montreal-based Inuit artist Niap first saw photographer Robert
Frchettes black-and-white photographs of the northern Quebec landscape seven
years ago, she knew that she would one day want to do something with them. Many of
the photographs pictured the treeless terrain in and around Kuujjuaq, the small northern
community in Nunavik where Niap was born and raised. I knew right away I wanted to
work with him, she says.

At the time, Niap (who is also known as Nancy Saunders) was working at the
Avataq
Cultural Institute (ACI), the cultural organization of the northern Quebec Inuit, and
contemplating a career as an art historian. Frchette, a southerner with years of
experience travelling to Quebecs northern Inuit-occupied lands, worked at the institute
as its president. In the years since, Niap has emerged as a dynamic new voice in
contemporary Inuit and Canadian art, earning a reputation for working boldly across
disciplines (she eventually decided to give up art history training to pursue her artistic
interests full time). Although still only in her early 30s, her work has already been
acquired by the Montreal Museum of Fine Arts, and in 2018 she was the recipient of the
Inuit Art Foundation's Virginia J. Watt Scholarship to support Inuit artists and students.

Niap never forgot the landscape photographs or her desire to collaborate artistically with
her former colleague at the ACI. She recently approached Frchette with an idea for a
project. I thought about what I could do
with these amazing photographs, she recalls.
Niaps plan, as she outlined it to Frchette, was to paint over large format prints of some
of his photographs, using them as source material for her own painterly expressions.
Frchette agreed to the idea, and earlier this year Niap went to work, obtaining a
number of his images for the project (Niap purchased the images along with the
reproduction rights from Frchette).

A new show of this innovative body of work will open November 2nd at the Marion Scott
Gallery, the artists first solo exhibition in Vancouver. The exhibition comprises a total of
13 painted photographs, including two large-scale works (she will produce a third even
larger work during a public performance at the shows opening). Niaps stylistic
approach in these images ranges from bold abstract expressionistic painterly gestures,
complete with drips descending beyond the photographic image into the white borders,
to a variety of more delicate effects. The painted shapes and
designs generally play off
the forms in the photographed landscapes, resulting in a dynamic dialogue between two
different media and two different artistic sensibilities. Niaps decision to use mostly bright
neon and neon-like colours is a bold choice, and the resulting contrast between her
lively painting and Frchettes austere black and white images couldnt be more striking.

The title of the exhibition is Reclamation and De-Categorization, and one of Niaps
primary intentions in making this series of images is to address the issue of land
ownership in the North and to affirm the rights of Quebecs northern Indigenous people.
Like indigenous populations elsewhere in Canada, Quebecs Inuit people have struggled
to maintain their culture and identity in the face of the colonial policies of the provincial
and federal governments"policies that have produced a legacy of loss and youth
suicide rates that are 5 times the national average. Along with other members of her
community, Niap is
critical of aspects of the James Bay and Northern Quebec
Agreement (JBNQA), the 1975 framework that governs land management in region. The
agreement divides Quebecs northern lands into three different categories, with the Inuit
enjoying different levels of rights to their land depending on the category. Inuit leaders
and activists have been actively campaigning to revise this system of categorization,
which they regard as outmoded and restrictive, depriving them of their full rights to their
ancestral territories.

By painting directly over Frchettes photographs of some of these lands, Niap is, in her
own words, symbolically reclaiming the land for our people. At the same time, she
sees her work with Frchette, a non-Inuk white Quebecois, as more than just an artistic
collaboration between two practitioners. A passionate defender of the rights of
Indigenous people, Niap views her work with Frchette as a vital act of reconciliation
between two people and their respective groups. She
notes that there is much
miscommunication between Indigenous and non-Indigenous people in Quebec. There
is Inuit animosity towards the white Quebecois, while on the other side there is
discrimination and stereotypes, she says. Part Euro-Quebecois herself (her father was
a white French Canadian while her mother is Inuit), Niap finds herself sitting somewhere
between these two opposing camps. I am fighting for both, she asserts. I am for
eventual self-governance of our land, but we also need to take care of other things like
better education and more services for our youth.

Niap hopes her work with Frchette will be viewed as an example of how different
groups can work together in an environment of mutual respect and understanding. She
is especially grateful to Frchette for his gesture of trust. To lend me his photos and
allow me to paint them however I want takes a lot of generosity, she says. The
collaborative and cooperative spirit in which the work has been realized has extended to
other aspects of the project as well. As part of her public performances, during which
she paints the photographs in a live setting, Niap works to an audio accompaniment
consisting of sounds she has recorded in the North, including footsteps in the snow,
howling wind, people laughing and running streams. A southern Quebec-based sound
engineer edited the evocative sound montage, which also features some piano playing
by a non-Inuk musician friend from Montreal.

As for criticisms she has heard that she is showing a lack of respect for the beauty of
her northern homeland by painting over Frchettes black and white photographs with
dissonant florescent colours, Niap counters that she is simply expressing her identity as
an Urban Inuk. I think neon colours are cool. I love my land and I dont think Im
defacing it.

Niap: Reclamation and De-Categorization opens Saturday, November 2 at the Marion
Scott Gallery. A public opening will take place from 1:00 to 4:00 pm, with a performance
at
1:30pm.

Niap (Nancy Saunders) is an award-winning multidisciplinary artist whose work has
been collected by the Montreal Museum of Fine Arts and Avataq Cultural Institute
among others. Based in Montreal, QC, the artist divides her time between the city and
her home community of Kuujjuaq, Nunavik"a place that continues to deeply influence
her work. Working across media, Niap thoughtfully investigates her cultural heritage
through her practice. In 2018, Niap was the recipient of the IAF's Virginia J. Watt
Scholarship.
