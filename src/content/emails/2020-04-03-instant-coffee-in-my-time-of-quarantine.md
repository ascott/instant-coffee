T O N I C S - A Series of Daily Artist Features for the Socially Distanced | 2020
(ANYTIME)

QUARANTONICS
T O N I C S for What Ails
@quarantonics

QuaranTonics is a series of Daily Artist Features and Instagram-based curatorial project
organized and run by Toronto-based artist
/ activist / curator, Laura Hudspith. Created in
response to the spread of COVID-19 across the globe and subsequent necessary social
distancing and self-isolation measures for all, the platform seeks to provide artists with
an opportunity to create and maintain connections to larger artist/audience community.
While many groups are feeling the profound effects of these measures, artists are
among those who have been affected most acutely.

Artists working in any / all mediums, at any stage in their career, are invited to create a
submission package for a consideration to be feature on the platform. Features consist
of 2-3 images of your work, didactic information, statements and an artist biography
which each link to the artists' personal Instagram pages across three (3) posts in one
day.

See also Story Highlights for Submission Requirements, or follow the following
guidelines.

Instagram DM the following SUBMISSION Requirements to @quarantonics

2 - 4 Images of your work (Sized for
Web)
- Made within the last two years that represent your practice well
- The images must: speak to each other well side-by-side, both visually and
conceptually. (The QT feed is itself a unified visual 'Tonic' when viewed holistically)

As SEPARATE DMs:
1. Standard Didactic information for each of the works / images sent
2. Your preferred pronouns, City/Country your are currently based, Basic descriptor of
you/your practice (Sculptor / Interdisciplinary / Painter / etc.)
3. 80 word Max. Short Statement / Bio

About the QT Curator:
- Laura Hudspith -

Based in Toronto (Canada), Hudspith holds a BFA with distinction from Concordia
University (Montreal), and was the recipient of the Irene F. Whittome Prize in Studio
Arts. She has exhibited her work and participated in residencies in both Canada and the
United States, notably with solo exhibitions at This Month Only, and Project Gallery,
Toronto; group exhibitions at Kontort Production Space, Katzman Contemporary, and
InterAccess,
Toronto; and such residencies as the James Black Gallery, Vancouver;
Wreck City, Calgary; and the Red Lodge Clay Center, Montana. Hudspiths work has
received recognition from several Canadian granting bodies, and has been presented
with a Visual Arts Mississauga Jurors Award during the artists first museum show at the
Art Gallery of Mississauga, 2020. She will be pursuing a Masters in Fine Arts at
Carnegie Mellon (Pittsburg), Fall 2020/23.

(For more information: @laura.hudspith | www.laurahudspith.com)

If you were a pot, what would you look like?

We're inviting everyone, everywhere to create and submit their very own Self Portrait as
a Pot as part of this week's #CAGatHome art-making activity. Taking inspiration from
exhibiting artist Maura Doyle, whose Pot Experiments (2014-2019)
considers the
ceramic form as a mode of self-portraiture: Pots are like people. A pot has a neck, a
foot, a mouth. They have mysterious interiors and familiar exteriors.

HOW TO GET INVOLVED
Share your Self Portrait as a Pot with us on your Instagram feed or story by tagging
@CAGVancouver #CAGatHome and we'll repost our favourites every week across
Insta, Facebook and Twitter to our 33+ thousand followers.

Working from home with little ones? Find prompts, tips and easy-to-find materials on our
website here: https://www.contemporaryartgallery.ca/cag-at-home/art-making-
activities/self-portrait-as-a-pot/

Or do us one better and show us how far you can take each activity!

<a href="/instant-coffee/listings/2020-04-03-03-news-capture-photography-festival-welcome-to-capture-2020">03. NEWS | Capture Photography Festival | Welcome to Capture 2020</a>
------------------------------------------------------------------------

On behalf of everyone at Capture Photography Festival, I am
thrilled to welcome you to
the 2020 Festival!

During a time of great uncertainty, many of you may be asking why art, especially now?
In Robert Adams classic text Why People Photograph, he wrote "At our best and most
fortunate we make pictures because of what stands in front of the camera, to honor
what is greater and more interesting than we are." This idea of capturing something
larger and more profound than we are as individuals is particularly pertinent to this
moment. It is what gives us hope.

While we are all staying close to home, Capture has reconceptualised many aspects of
our programming to bring digital content directly to our audiences. I hope you will enjoy
our catalogue (capturephotofest.com/news), website (capturephotofest.com) and
Instagram (@capturephotofest) as we deliver virtual exhibitions, the Incubator silent
auction, filmed tours and talks, and other filmed content to you. It is a way for us to
celebrate the work of the incredible local and international
artists featured in this years
festival, and, importantly, to stay connected.

I want to extend my heartfelt thanks to the art community for the incredible support we
have been afforded both in our efforts to plan Capture in the first place, and then as we
responded to the current health concerns and planned alternative programming. The
way in which organizations, institutions, galleries and artists have collaborated with us
and supported our efforts was truly generous and heartening during a time that has
been fraught for so many. For me, it has been a great affirmation that it is through the
collective energy that so many people pour into Capture that we are able to realize this
magic annually in April.

All the best,

Emmy Lee Wall

Executive Director

<a href="/instant-coffee/listings/2020-04-03-05-publication-nikkei-national-museum-new-read-and-playthings">05. PUBLICATION | Nikkei National Museum | New read and playthings</a>
------------------------------------------------------------------------

New E-book and Infinity Flipper:
Thanks to funding from Metro Vancouver, the Nikkei National Museum has been able to
turn Ann Gomer Sunahara's seminal but now out-of-print book Politics of Racism that
helped support the Japanese Canadian Redress movement in the 80s, into an e-book.
Available for free reading soon on several platforms including
https://centre.nikkeiplace.org/research/, www.japanesecanadianhistory.ca, Amazon, and
Kindle.

And thanks to funding from Library & Archives Canada, we've been able to
complete a
Home Run on Powell Street digitization project for preservation and access to records
related to the Vancouver Asahi and Historic Powell Street neighbourhood. Partnered
with the Japanese Canadian Cultural Centre in Toronto, the completed project will make
2000 photographs, 40cm of textual materials and 12 audio histories available to the
public. Keep an eye out on www.nikkeimuseum.org for the collection. Our new promo
piece for the collection is an infinity flipper which will be available once we re-open to the
public. For a preview, check out the giffs below.

And if you live in the West-end of Vancouver and/or can get into Stanley Park on April 9,
please visit the Japanese Canadian War Memorial (behind the Aquarium) which turns
100 that day. Hopefully the 100year old ojochin cherry tree - the only one of its kind in
Vancouver - will be blooming. We've had to cancel our public commemoration for that
day, but will revisit on Nov. 11.

Effective Wednesday, March 18, the
Nikkei National Museum & Cultural Centre is
closed to the public until further notice as a community-based mitigation measure for
COVID-19. All public programs and events are cancelled at this time and staff have
transitioned to work remotely.

Please stay connected through our website: www.nikkeiplace.org and on Facebook,
Twitter, and Instagram.

A message for our community,

As our world changes wildly around us, we're focusing on what's happening in our
community, looking 'local' for inspiration, for strength and for community spirit.

In response to the novel coronavirus (COVID-19) as a precaution for our visitors, staff,
volunteers, and community, CityScape Community ArtSpace and North Van Arts offices
are closed until further notice. Staff are (mostly)
working from home -checking email and
phone messages on weekdays - and will respond to inquiries as best we can. We
continue to evaluate the situation and will communicate updates through our website,
social media and e-bulletin. Our current programme updates are listed below.

We understand that as a result of social distancing, many of our community members
are facing financial challenges and uncertainty, while others are looking for opportunities
to help. Therefore, we are offering our programmes on a 'pay-what-you-can' basis. You
decide what works best for you. Many artists have had events, performances,
presentations, and classes cancelled for months to come. For many this is their only
source of income. In response, our strategy is to 're-imagine' our programming for this
spring. We want to devise alternatives that maintain access for our community while
providing paid opportunities for our local artists.

For our members, we've been thinking of you and will check in with you
soon about how
you're doing, and what we can do to support you in the coming weeks and months.

On behalf of staff and our Board of Directors, we hope you and your families stay well
and encourage you to keep up your creative ventures, keeping healthy and strong.

Sincerely,
Nancy Cottingham Powell
Executive Director

-Art in the Garden, cancelled. Considering online options to keep the spirit alive.

SPARK BOX STUDIO DAWSON RESIDENCY AWARD & $1,000 BURSARY

supportive environment.

which Spark

artist Donald Dawson.

This residency opportunity is a unique way for established and emerging artists to
cultivate new ideas, access a rich library of arts resources, and focus on their career.

<a href="/instant-coffee/listings/2020-04-03-12-air-program-salt-spring-applications-for-artist-in-residence">12. CALL | AiR Program (Salt Spring) | Applications for Artist in Residence Program | APR 15</a>
------------------------------------------------------------------------


The Salt Spring Arts Council (SSAC) Artist in Residence (AiR) Program, now in its fifth
year, provides an opportunity for artists to work in the vibrant arts environment of Salt
Spring Island. The program is open to all professionals working in the arts, in any genre
or medium, at any stage of their careers. The intent of the program continues to be the
provision of time and space for creativity and productivity for the chosen visiting artists
and to introduce them to Salt Spring Island and its arts community. We welcome both
National and International applicants to the program.

All submissions are juried with residencies awarded based on artistic merit, available
facilities,
potential links to the Salt Spring community and associated collaborative
opportunities.

Our residents this year are Joyce Majiski from Whitehorse, working on a multi-media
installation "Song of the Whale, painter Claudia Wilholt from Hanover, Germany,
working with egg tempera on canvas, and writer Julie Burtinshaw working on her next
book: Petit Treason: One Women's Journey to the Gallows.

As in previous years, as well as working on their own projects, the artists will share their
expertise and enthusiasm through presentations, workshops, demonstrations and
exhibits, enriching our island with their work and ideas, interacting with Salt Spring
artists and enjoying our unique island lifestyle. For information about these artists and
events go to; ssartscouncil.com/artist-in-residence/residencies/

The AiR program invites artists to engage with the creative cultural community of Salt
Spring Island. Within the spirit of this framework, collaborative proposals by members of
the Salt
Spring arts community which involve off-island artists are actively encouraged.

In 2021 the annual SSAC presentation Easter Art Show will centre on an exploration of
colour, artists who feel their work, explorations, and timing might fit into this scenario
should make note of this on their application.

Applications for the AiR program can be submitted on or before April 15th. 2020 for
tenure between January 2021 and April 2021. Application information is available at
ssartscouncil.com/artist-in-residence/

For More Information:

Artist in Residence (AiR) Program, Salt Spring Arts Council

114 Rainbow Road, Salt Spring Island, British Columbia, Canada V8K 2V5,

Email: air@ssartscouncil.com

<a href="/instant-coffee/listings/2020-04-03-17-seymour-art-gallery-discovery-juried-exhibition-for-new-and-">17. CALL | Seymour Art Gallery | Discovery | Juried Exhibition for New and Emerging Artists | MAY 1</a>
------------------------------------------------------------------------

CALL
Seymour Art Gallery

Discovery:
Juried Exhibition for New and Emerging Artists

Deadline: Friday, May 1, 2020

Open to all artists in British Columbia, but local drop off and pick up is required. The
Discovery exhibition is looking to provide exposure to new and emerging artists;
therefore, eligibility is limited to artists who self-identify as emerging artists. Artwork may
be individually or collaboratively produced.

Criteria: Only original works created since January 2018 are eligible. We accept work in
all media. There are no size restrictions. Artists (or artistic collaborations) must submit a
minimum of three and a maximum of ten pieces. Between three and five pieces per
selected artist (or artistic collaboration) will be displayed. Works submitted should be
from a single series or body of work as it will be displayed together.

Availability: Work for exhibition must be available for drop off on Sunday, August 26 and
will remain on display for the duration of the Discovery exhibition: August 1 " September
12,
2020.

Presentation: Work must be suitably prepared for exhibition, ready for presentation. Wall
mounted work must be appropriately framed (or have a professional look with finished
sides) and must be wired for hanging. Work not properly prepared for exhibition may not
be exhibited.

Sales: Work is not required to be for sale; pieces not for sale (NFS) should be labeled
as such and should include a value for insurance purposes. Seymour Art Gallery will
retain 35% on all works (or orders) sold.

Awards: Emerging Artist Award $300

Membership: You do not need to be a member of Seymour Art Gallery to submit, but

wall hanging sculptures for a competitive purchase for the Citys Civic Art Collection.
These artworks will enter the SCL Portable Works Collection managed by ARTS. They
will be displayed throughout city galleries and offices.

For this call, maximum measurements for work are 48 x 48 x 5 inches and work should
not weigh more than 50 pounds. The

Seymour Art Gallery

2021 Exhibition Proposals
