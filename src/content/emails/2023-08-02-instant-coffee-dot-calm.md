Black Lives Matter Memorial Art Exhibition Debuts in Vancouver

The Black Lives Matter Memorial Project, dedicated to honoring Black lives lost to
racial injustice and police violence, will be held at Alternatives Gallery in Vancouver
from July 26 to August 2, 2023.

The exhibition features powerful artworks by renowned artists Dr. Syrus Marcus
Ware (Toronto), Maestro Jason Ikeem Rodgers of Orchestra Noir (Atlanta), and Toni
Latour (Vancouver). The exhibit aims to shed light on the systemic racism that has
led to the tragic deaths of Black individuals in both Canada and the United States.

Black lives are inherently valuable. We need to all fight to ensure that every Black
person gets to live long enough to become an elder. These incredible human beings
depicted here should be alive and with us today. We fight for abolition and Black
liberation everyday to help usher in a freer future " one where
Black safety and
Black life is assured, says Dr. Ware.

The project has been three years in the making, and is funded by the Canada
Council for the Arts, the B.C. Arts Council, and the Vancouver Biennale.

The exhibition comprises three components:

The first component showcases ten portraits drawn by Dr. Syrus Marcus Ware,
depicting Black individuals who lost their lives at the hands of law enforcement.
Accompanying each portrait is a text panel describing the person's full and rich life
and the circumstances surrounding their untimely death. The panels are
complemented by analog clocks moving forward and reverse, and screens
displaying digital life-long clocks counting forward from birth to death and backward
from death to birth. This poignant display emphasizes the injustice of lives cut short
by racist police forces and the oppressive systems that perpetuate them.

The second component features black stone slabs engraved with the names, birth
and death dates, and ages of over 400
Black people killed by police since 2012.

The third component of the exhibition is the video installation titled "black elegy."
Maestro Jason Ikeem Rodgers, founder of Atlanta-based Orchestra Noir, composed
an original piece of music specifically for this project. The video recording of the
performance by his all-Black orchestra, capturing the emotions of the composition,
will be on display during the exhibition.

OPENING RECEPTION
The Black Lives Matter Memorial Project offers free admission to the public, and an
opening reception will take place on July 29 from 3-5 p.m. with refreshments and
food from Riddim & Spice on Commercial Drive. The reception will feature guest
speaker Krystal Paraboo, lead curator for the Black Strathcona Resurgence Project
with Vancouver Mural Festival. Renee Austin will contribute insights on racial justice
and commemoration and Phabion Sutherland will offer an Indigenous welcome and
blessing.

FEATURED ARTISTS:
Dr. Syrus Marcus Ware (he/him)is a Vanier
Scholar, visual artist, activist, curator, and
educator living and working in Toronto, ON. Using drawing, installation, and
performance, Syrus explores social justice frameworks and Black activist culture. His
work has been shown widely, including solo shows at Grunt Gallery in 2018 and Wil
Aballe Art Projects in 2021 (Irresistible Revolutions). His work has been featured as
part of the inaugural Toronto Biennial of Art in 2019 in conjunction with the Ryerson
Image Centre (Antarctica and Ancestors, Do You Read Us? (Dispatches from the
Future)), as well as for the Toronto Bentways Safety in Public Spaces Initiative in
2020 (Radical Love). Syrus has participated in group shows at the Never Apart in
Montreal, the Art Gallery of Ontario, the University of Lethbridge Art Gallery, the Art
Gallery of York University, the Art Gallery of Windsor, and as part of the curated
content at Nuit Blanche 2017 (The Stolen People; Won't Back Down). His
performance works have been part of festivals
across Canada, including Cripping
The Stage (Harbourfront Centre, 2016 & 2019), Complex Social Change (University
of Lethbridge Art Gallery, 2015) and Decolonizing and Decriminalizing Trans Genres
(University of Winnipeg, 2015).

Jason Ikeem Rodgers (he/him) is a renowned Atlanta-based conductor, composer,
and arranger. He is the Founder and Music Director of Orchestra Noir, the Atlanta
African-American Orchestra. He began his conducting career as an apprentice to
John Mauceri, the founding director of the Hollywood Bowl Orchestra and protg of
Leonard Bernstein. Since then, Maestro Rodgers has achieved international
recognition, winning prestigious awards such as first prize in the London Classical
Soloists Competition and the International Conducting Competition in the United
States. He has conducted orchestras in various locations, including Europe and
North America, and made his debut with the Orchestra Di Toscana Classica in Italy.
His word has been exhibited at the Atlanta High
Museum of Art and he has gained
notoriety for his arrangements, including one for hip-hop artist 2Chainz's song
Forgiven featured on The Tonight Show Starring Jimmy Fallon. He has also
contributed to Oprah's OWN Network and the Amazon's series Harlem.

Maestro Rodgers holds degrees in Orchestral Conducting from the Cleveland
Institute of Music and the University of North Carolina School of the Arts, where he
studied under notable mentors such as Carl Topilow, Louis Lane, and Serge
Zehnacker. Additionally, he has a Bachelor's Degree in Piano Performance from the
University of North Carolina School of the Arts, where he was mentored by Clifton
Matthews.

Toni Latour (she/her) is a queer femme white settler and second generation Italian
Canadian artist. Toni is both a visual artist and professor. She works in
photography, installation, text-based practices, drawing, video, sound, public and
performance art.
Toni received her BFA from the University of Windsor in 1998 and her MFA from the
University of Western Ontario in 2000. Her work has included social commentary,
public art work, and large-scale installation work that concerns itself with
representation within the queer/trans/non-binary communities in Coast Salish
Territory (Vancouver). Toni has exhibited her work nationally and internationally in
both solo and group shows since 1994. Her work is held in private and public
collections, including the National Portrait Gallery of Canada, the Surrey Art Gallery,
and the Vancouver Biennale. She is the Non-Regular Union Executive
Representative at the University of the Fraser Valley where she advocates for labour
rights and equity. Toni is also an active member of UFVs RAN (Racism and Anti-
Racism Network) and the SOCA: EDI Committee (School of Creative Arts: Equity,
Diversity and Inclusion). Toni is also a single mother to an Autistic neurodivergent
daughter and they live together on the unceded territory of the Musqueam,
Squamish, and Tsleil-Waututh Nations,
colonially known as Vancouver.

The Black LIves Matter Memorial Project is grateful to its exhibition sponsors: the
Canada Council for the Arts, the B.C. Arts Council, and the Vancouver Biennale.

consciousness, and spirits in their physical relations to us, with the hope that our co-

<a href="/instant-coffee/listings/2023-08-02-06-gallery-gachet-kiskisiwin-7pm-aug-4">06. OPENING | Gallery Gachet | Kiskisiwin | 7PM | AUG 4</a>
------------------------------------------------------------------------
Morgan Possberg
August 4 - September 16
Opening August 4, 7-9pm, with artist talk at 7pm

In Morgan Possberg's "Kiskisiwin," the artist constructs conceptual
parallelisms between paskwaw mostos (buffalo) and the Indigenous
peoples of Turtle Island. The parallels drawn between the buffalos and
Indigenous bodies move beyond the disappearance of the buffalo from
the prairies as a convolution of ongoing colonization and exploitation
of the land by settlers, and ties to the spoor, sound and blood of
the buffalo, strongly linked to memories of the land and Indigenous blood knowledge.

Artist Biography

Morgan Possberg Denne grew up in Calgary, Alberta and spent a large
part of their childhood in Shediac, New Brunswick. They received a
Bachelor of Fine Arts Degree with a focus in Textiles /
Fashion from
NSCAD University in Halifax in 2019, and subsequently attended the
University of Calgary for a Master of Architecture Degree. Morgan has
most recently attended the Banff centre for an Indigenous Haute
Couture residency, taught traditional fish skin tanning classes in
Prince Edward Island / Epekwitk, and is currently working on a
commission for the Calgary Public Library. They are a millennial scoop
and foster care survivor; with settler, Cree, Metis, and Chippewa
familial connections. Morgan is both a writer and an artist with a
focus in writing about the modern Two-spirit Indigenous experience in
Canada, queerness, colonialism, institutional / systemic racism, and
uplifting BIPOC / Indigenous artists. Through their work, Morgan
explores alternate stories to fill gaps created by colonialism with a
focus on capturing inner truth and alternate possibilities.

Gallery Gachet is located in the Downtown Eastside of Vancouver on the
unceded and occupied territories of the
xmkwy"m (Musqueam),
Skwxw7mesh (Squamish) and Sl"lwta (Tsleil-Waututh) Nations.
Gallery Gachet has a mandate to support artists and offer art programs
addressing mental health and socio-political marginalization, while
promoting art as a means for survival, cultural participation, and
human rights.

<a href="/instant-coffee/listings/2023-08-02-08-richmond-cultural-centre-hot-pot-talks-queer-asian-storytell">08. TALK | Richmond Cultural Centre | Hot Pot Talks: Queer Asian Storytelling | 6PM | AUG 4</a>
------------------------------------------------------------------------
Hot Pot Talks: Queer Asian Storytelling

Friday, August 4, 6:00-9:00pm
Richmond Cultural Centre, Performance Hall

7700 Minoru Gate, Richmond BC, V6Y 1R9

What are the five elements to telling your own story? Join Love Intersections Co-
Artistic Directors Jen Sungshine and David Ng for an interactive film screening and
storytelling workshop on queer Asian futures. David and Jen will share their award-
winning documentary film, Yellow Peril: Queer Destiny (2019), and the sequel,
Yellow Peril: Queer Futures (2022), followed by an interactive storytelling workshop
on filmmaking methodologies and community care. Participants are invited to bring
stories to workshop with the group. Refreshments will be provided.

About the Yellow Peril films:

These two short films (35 min total run time) employ the five elements in traditional
Chinese medicine as a metaphor to explore queer Asian diasporic identity through
the narrative lens of Maiden China, a Vancouver-based intersectional feminist drag
artist. The creators reflect on making art in response to anti-Asian racism, post-
pandemic healing, and making room for joy through humour and love.

About the presenters:

Jen Sungshine is a queer Taiwanese-Canadian interdisciplinary artist, community
facilitator, and cultural producer based in Vancouver, BC. She is the Co-Artistic
Director of Love Intersections, a media arts collective producing intergenerational
and intersectional QTBIPOC stories through documentary film and artwork. Her
works include "Yellow Peril: Queer Destiny", winner of the Gerry Brunet Memorial
Award for Best BC Short; and "The House of 9 Dragons", an oral history exhibit in
Chinatown. She curates public programming at The Polygon Gallery, and co-
produces Hot
Pot Talks, CURRENT: Feminist Electronic Art Symposium, and Seize
the Means (of Production) Video Co-op.

David Ng is a queer, feminist media artist and Co-Artistic Director of Love
Intersections - an arts collective made up of queer people of colour. His current
artistic practices grapple with queer, racialized, and diasporic identity, and how
intersectional identities can be expressed through media arts. His interests
include imagining new possibilities of how queer racialized artists can use their
practice to transform communities.

Learn more and register: www.eventbrite.com/e/hot-pot-talks-queer-asian-
storytelling-tickets-662488359517

<a href="/instant-coffee/listings/2023-08-02-11-nap-morning-sun-garden-until-aug-5-aug-5">11. EXHIBITION | nap | Morning Sun Garden | Until AUG 5 | AUG 5</a>
------------------------------------------------------------------------
Morning Sun Garden
Aracha Cholitgul
June 15 - August 5, 2023

nap is pleased to present new works by Thai artist Aracha Cholitgul in her Canadian
debut solo exhibition, Morning Sun Garden. Through considering the ecology of the
house of which nap gallery is located in and its many human and non-human
relationships, Morning Sun Garden becomes an exploration into complex desires
that arise from loneliness. Guided by intimately honest conversations between the
artist and the resident of nap on personal
dilemmas surrounding endings, pets, and
companionship, the gallery is transformed into an offering for all neighborhood cats
who regularly visit, but also an invitation to consider the value of human/non-human
relationships, the complex dynamics of ownership, and of human/non-human needs
and of being needed. Featuring a giant litter box, a selection of drawings depicting
plants preferred by cats such as Thyrsostachys siamensis (otherwise known as

colonialism and capitalism, popular narratives of the enslavement of non-human

destruction of humankind by emergent technologies or extraterrestrial beings within

technologies such as artificial intelligence, digital surveillance, and data collection.

proposes an alternative system in which human relationships with other beings and
new technologies can exist in a democratic and transformational way to imagine a

The Artists in Communities (AIC) program is based on principles of community
cultural development. Its purpose is to encourage a wide variety of interactions
between artists and community members. The intention of an artist residency is to
create a lasting physical and/or social legacy, whether it involves a new
understanding of creative processes, the development of collaborative skills or an
artwork which remains in the community.

Community-engaged arts practices see artists invite community members (who may
not see themselves as artists) to work together over an extended period of time as
creators, producers, performers and active audiences. Contributing to the growing
vibrancy of our neighbourhoods, Artists in Communities seeks to inspire
participation, creativity and relationship building where art becomes a catalyst for
individual and community engagement.

NEW 2 Year Pilot Project: Youth Focused
Residency

In 2024-2025, two community centres will host an Artists in Communities project with
an intended focus on youth engagement.

This 2 year pilot will enable artists to establish deeper relationships within the
community and provide an opportunity for activities to evolve and new projects to
emerge over a longer time frame. While the project may involve other age groups, it
will prioritize youth involvement.

Work developed during these residencies will be featured in a public presentation for
BC Youth Week at the Roundhouse Community Arts and Recreation Centre in May
2025.

Artists in Communities advances cultural equity and accessibility for
underrepresented artists and communities, and prioritizes work that increases voice
and visibility of xmky" m (Musqueam), Swxw7mesh (Squamish) and
slilwta (Tsleil-waututh) arts and culture on the land.

Note: AIC no longer supports mural, tile mosaic or banner projects as there are
alternative funding avenues for these activities.
