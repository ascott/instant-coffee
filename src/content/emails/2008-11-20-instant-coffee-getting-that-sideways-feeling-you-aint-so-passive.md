Contemporary Art Gallery presents

Shannon Oksanen
Summerland

November 21, 2009 - January 18, 2009
OPENING RECEPTION: Thursday, November 20, 2008, 6-9pm

For Shannon Oksanen's solo exhibition at the Contemporary Art
Gallery, she will present a new body of work, including a 35mm
film and a painting series. In Summerland, Oksanen's remake
of the waterskiing scene from the 1965 film Viva Las Vegas, the
artist puts forward a compilation of visual estimations that
combine to represent a period in America just at a point when
a shift in consciousness was occurring. The film continues her
interest in showmanship sports following in the line of works
such as Spins, 2002 and Vanishing Point, 2001 that focus on
figure skating and surfboarding, using them to capture a time
period, and more specifically the ideals of an era as it is being
lost or defined. Here Oksanen uses waterskiing, saturated
colours, costume, choreography and music to set back time,
calling to mind an era and defining it stylistically. She will also
present a new painting and drawing series that brings together
her interest in portraiture, celebrity and nostalgia. For this new
body of work, Oksanen chooses Elvis Presley as her subject,
adding unabashedly to the already seemingly endless
assemblage of images of this pop king. The exhibition is
accompanied by an illustrated catalogue which will be
launched at the opening reception.

Oksanen has a growing international reputation and has
participated in group exhibitions at the CCA Wattis Institute for
Contemporary Art, San Francisco (2004), VTO Gallery, London,
England (2004), 303 Gallery, New York (2005) and The

Keep the IS in FEMINISM

In the CAG's street front vitrines, we will also open Keep the IS
in FEMINISM a collection of new Feminist slogans. For this
project we commissioned prominent feminist artists along with
emerging artists both female and male to reinvent the f word:
feminism, a directive taken from the Gorilla Girls.
We would like a new round of slogans to capture urgency by
inflecting the very notion of Feminism back into a
contemporary conversation. Its not only that women are under
represented in politics and over represented as objects, it is that
women, young and old, no longer identify themselves as
feminists. Even with the best of intentions there has been a
slow fading of feminists as we watch them slide into the
history books as something that is no longer relevant. How did
feminists and feminism get such a bad rap so quickly? In the
last two years there has been an active looking back to feminist
art practices of the 1970s. Two prominent references include
the traveling block buster WACK: Art and Feminist Revolution,
which is currently in Vancouver, and Frieze magazines 2007
feminist issue. The return of Feminist discourse to the
contemporary art table is not a surprise since we do understand
that the time is ripe to lay new attention on this now forty year
old practice, to rejuvenate the terms of feminism to better
understand what it has effectively achieved and lay out what
still needs to be done. The title of the exhibition is taken from
the list of slogans and is by Jeanne Randolph. Artists include:

<a href="/instant-coffee/listings/2008-11-20-04-monte-clark-gallery-karin-bubas-nov-20">04. OPENING | MONTE CLARK GALLERY | KARIN BUBAS | NOV 20</a>

KARIN BUBAS - Valley of the Heart's Delight
November 20th - Year end

Opening Reception: Thursday, November 20th, 6-8pm
MONTE CLARK GALLERY
2339 Granville Street

The Monte Clark Gallery is pleased to announce the solo exhibition
"Valley of the Heart's Delight" from Vancouver-based artist Karin
Bubas.

Partially inspired by early Technicolor films and hand-tinted
photographs, Bubas' large-scale digital photographs are presented as
subtly orchestrated scenes of colour and light. Each of the five
images presents for the viewer a carefully costumed figure in a
reminiscent landscape. The result is a mysterious and story-like
dynamic between the setting and the individual. Taken on a panoramic
camera, two of the images explore this format in a horizontal
configuration emblematic of the landscape genre, and for three Bubas
has aligned her images in a vertical format to dramatic effect.

The inherently cinematic narratives are derived from archetypal myths
and stories, creating for the viewer imagery both ominous and
enchanting, the figures appearing as apparitions in their mystical
settings. As Bubas carefully stages these five tableaux, allusions of
myths and new romanticism come together with cinematic sensitivity.
The end result is a series of supernatural costume scenes reminiscent
of Waterhouse's Pre-Raphaelite paintings as well as the films of
Alfred Hitchcock.

Karin Bubas was born in North Vancouver and currently lives and works
in Vancouver, BC Canada. Since studying at the Emily Carr Institute of
Art and Design, her work has been exhibited nationally and
internationally.

CSA SPACE
Presents: SAVE NOW
By Kelly Lycan
November 22 - January 10, 2008
Opening Reception: 7pm
Friday, November 21, 2008

The act of collecting, and the recycling of existing collections lies at
the conceptual and material core of Kelly Lycan's sculptural practice. Save
Now, Lycan's solo exhibition for CSA continues her examination of the
visuals of consumer culture, but more specifically addresses her material
pursuit and research of runaway consumption. This includes an investigation
of spaces that catch the overflow of abundant objects that are in a state
ofsuspension from appreciation to depreciation.

If the acquisition of things is both a palliative act and a stimulant,
what happens to these collections after they serve their therapeutic and
consumptive purposes? Whether it is the stuff of junk drawers, ordered
shelves or deep storage (self-storage is now a 17 billion dollar annual
industry in the North America), Lycan is interested in looking at the
configuration and negotiation of what has selectively been gathered and
saved, and not yet abandoned.

Collecting is a common social practice that reflects behavioral patterns
as it mirrors aspects of public and private life. It constructs a way in
which we relate to each other and instructs how we order the material
world. One main focus of Lycan's work has been the way that objects are
valued and devalued depending on their place of display. Save Now
materially stores, orders and reorders consumer culture and its
relationship to industrial design and contemporary art practices, creating
a link between market culture and visual culture in order to address how
aesthetic
values are created; how materials are used in the formation of identities
and ideologies; and how these forms take up space in her life.

LES THOMAS

Beare

Opening Reception with Artist

Saturday, November 22, 2008 between 2-4pm.

Les Thomas, was born in British Columbia in 1962. He earned his Bachelor
of Fine Arts at the University of Alberta in 1990 and then completed
Post-Graduate Studies at the Slade School of Fine Arts, University College
London, England in 1992. After returning to Canada he attended the
University of Calgary, gaining his Master of Fine Arts. Thomas has received
many awards for his work and has his paintings in public and private
collections in Canada, the U.S., The U.K., and Germany. He currently lives
and works in Alberta.

Les Thomas' dynamic wax and oil paintings play with the relationship
between culture and nature. The special encaustic wax finish he applies,
Les explains, may very well pertain to memory, and the manner in which we
hold visual experiences within the capacities for recollection.

Thomas sifts through a wide variety of visual sources for the imagery in
his work, noting that one of his major influences is culture, in its
broadest terms, including all aspects of our everyday life.

This exhibition will feature new works consisting of Thomas' recollection
of the female form, as well as other recollections.

<a href="/instant-coffee/listings/2008-11-20-09-teck-gallery-sfu-nick-danziger-blair-at-war-nov-24">09. OPENING | Teck Gallery, SFU | Nick Danziger: Blair at War | NOV 24</a>

Nick Danziger: Blair at War
November 24, 2008 - February 14, 2009
Teck Gallery, SFU Vancouver Campus
Artist talk on Afghanistan: Thursday, November 27, 7pm, SFU Vancouver
Artist walking tour of Blair at War: Saturday, November 29, 3pm, Teck
Gallery

Ever wondered what decision-making looks like when the participants know
that human lives will hang in the balance? This exhibition of photographs by
Nick Danziger is the result of a seemingly uncontroversial assignment to
document then-prime minister Tony Blair's 50th birthday for The Times
Magazine. Global events forced a metamorphosis of the assignment ­ it became
a month-long study of a British prime minister deliberating the pros and
cons of participating in the war in Iraq, during which Danziger, and his
collaborator on the project, Times Literary Supplement editor Peter
Stothard, had round-the-clock access to Blair's inner circle, often
wondering why they were not asked to leave.

The text panels in the show document who said what and are a record of the
process of the UK government assimilating information that often proved to
be unreliable. There is a film noir quality to the pictures even though the
scenes of dialogue look more like everyday considerations of whether to
order this or that type of wine for dinner.

Five years after the invasion of Iraq, these striking photographs do what
photography often does best ­ provide privileged access to inaccessible
people, events, and processes.

The Distinguished First Nations Artist Program is pleased to present a talk
by Dana Claxton. Dana Claxton is an interdisciplinary artist whose work
includes film and video, installation, performance, photography, and
curating. Over the past 17 years, her work has generated alternative,
subversive societal critiques that have informed the field of art and
social history.

She is currently part of a group show presented by the Contemporary Museum
of Photography at the National Gallery of Canada in Ottawa titled, Steeling
the Gaze: Portraits by Aboriginal Artists. From the whimsical to the
reverential, the poignant to the political, these artists refashion the
view of Native people not only by the way of the camera lens, but also
through their own cultural perspectives. The works also challenge the
stereotypes that have been previously constructed about them by others for
political, religious, or commercial purposes.

Gallery, Winnipeg Art Gallery and the Art Bank of Canada, National Gallery
of Canada, Eitlejorg Museum (Indianapolis) and has been screened

an Eiteljorg Fellow given by the Eiteljorg Museum of American Indians and
Western Art (Indianapolis). Claxton was the 2003 Global Television Chair at
the University of Regina, School of Journalism. She is a member of the
Aboriginal Educational Council for the Ontario College of Art and Design
and has taught at Emily Carr University and Simon Fraser University. Dana
Claxton is of Lakota descent and her family reserve is Woodmountain.

Western Front Media Arts presents:

Perspectives on an Archive

Perspectives on an Archive is a curatorial mentorship and community
outreach project initiated by Western Front Media Arts. From September 2008
to December 2008 three emerging curators from diverse backgrounds were
invited to curate programs of significant works of media art selected from
the Western Front Media Archive: Kemi Craig, Liz Park and cheyanne turions.
This project includes a screening series, a virtual exhibit at
www.perspectivesproject.ca and an accompanying catalogue distributed for
free at the screening events. Perspectives on an Archive marks an important
moment for Canadian media art, for the Western Front as a historic
interdisciplinary organization, and for these emerging curators.

The Media Archive is comprised of nearly 1000 historic video art works and
groundbreaking performance documents dating from 1975 to present. Most of
these are only available through the Western Front, and many haven't been
viewed by the public in over twenty years. These up-and-coming cultural
professionals have explored their own views on these essential documents
held in the Media Archive.

sense of disconnect as a result of running into physical, psychological or

divergent understandings of human movement and border crossings.

regulated.

as a link, Simcic considers the concepts of boundary and encounter,
material and surface, duration and the effects of time that are relevant to
both architecture and public art. Initially proposed in 2002, Park has
