<a href="/instant-coffee/listings/2015-12-17-01-publication-macaulay-co-fine-art-jeremy-shaw-variation-fq-12">01. PUBLICATION | Macaulay & Co Fine Art | Jeremy Shaw Variation FQ 12" vinyl release | DEC 16</a>
------------------------------------------------------------------------
Dear Vancouver,

On Wednesday December 16th, we will release 10 signed editions of the
Variation FQ 12" vinyl.
Stylus Records is located in the alley behind the gallery between 1st and
2nd Ave.
Hours 11am-6pm. Don't miss out!

Following the international success of Berlin-based artist and musician Jeremy
Shaw's 16mm film installation Variation FQ, The Vinyl Factory makes the
soundtrack available for the first time as a limited edition 12" vinyl.

No stranger to music production, Jeremy Shaw recorded under the name
Circlesquare from 1999-2009; releasing multiple records on Trevor Jackson's
now defunct Output Recordings imprint. Since ending the project indefinitely in
2009, Shaw's main focus has shifted to his visual art practice, yet his
film, video
and installation works almost always include a highly considered audio element.

Consisting of a repetitive, melancholy piano score combined with chopped vocal
samples and time-stretched tape effects, the soundtrack to Jeremy Shaw's
Variation FQ (2011-13) incites a hypnotic, mantra-like mood that accompanies
the virtuosic dance style of the film's protagonist - legendary voguer, Leiomy
Maldonado. Presented within the stark black and white aesthetics of Norman
Mclaren's 1968 ballet film Pas de deux, Shaw employs step-and-repeat optical
effects, slow motion and the original music to amplify Maldonado's unique,
cathartic movements that teeter between eloquence and violence. Variation FQ
is a study of the co-evolution of subculture, gender, dance, and special
effects.

Of the soundtrack itself Shaw says, "It alludes to classical piano scores
for solo
variations in ballet, without being as epic or dramatic - just a subtle
loop from a
moment in one that repeats with very slight changes. Recorded in mono to
adhere to the 16mm format, this spell is then broken by wide stereo-spread
clipped and pitched vocals samples from Leiomy's most-viewed YouTube
compilation video, as well as trance stabs and tape echo that illustrate
the film's
climax."

Editor's Notes:
* Limited to 300 copies worldwide
* Screen printed artwork by Jeremy Shaw
* 180-gram heavyweight white vinyl

About The Vinyl Factory:

Established in 2001, The Vinyl Factory Group encompasses a record label, vinyl
pressing plant, the record shop Phonica and music magazine FACT, in addition
to all of its visual arts activities. These include new arts commissions,
exhibitions
and collaborations between musicians and contemporary artists.

The Vinyl Factory record label releases limited editions made in collaboration
with musicians and artists, accompanied by events and performances. Releases
to date include: The xx, Christian Marclay, Massive Attack, Eddie Peake, These
New Puritans, Roisin Murphy, Dinos Chapman, Florence + the Machine, Maurizio
Cattalan, Pet Shop Boys, Martin Creed, Bryan Ferry, Keaton Henson and Trevor
Jackson.

<a href="/instant-coffee/listings/2015-12-17-02-csa-space-matter-trace-dec-17">02. OPENING | CSA Space | Matter-Trace | DEC 17</a>
------------------------------------------------------------------------
CSA Space presents:
Matter-Trace with Maegan Hill-Carroll and Brigitte Lochhead
Curated by Justina Bohach

December 17, 2015 - January 17th 2016

Opening reception: Thursday, December 17, 2015 6-9pm,

Maegan Hill-Carroll's photographs are full of materiality. Undecipherable
narrative remnants are detectable, culled together through her use of
objects within each photo. Various plots are often combined into a single
image. The next process is combining the images to make sets. Her
photographs leave reminisce of studio photography processes, staging, and
landscape. They are the formulation of experiments with functional
equipment used to perfect an image. Her arrangements move past the
technical; there is a relationship to nature, but it unravels and embodies
something that feels outside of this world. Somewhere in the vein of the
extra-terrestrial, or it could be a science-fiction of a wilderness and
solitude. Her photos have a mystique that lands in the esoteric and is
stabilized through her understanding and manipulation of historical and
contemporary photographic processes. They leave us reflecting on the
ambiguousness on interior and exterior worlds, and all that is in-between.

Brigitte Lochhead's sculptures speak through a material grammar. Part
tangible, part intuitive, they punctuate with a comma, exclamation and
question(s) marked within the vocabulary. Retract, insert, replace, join,
carve, clamp... The basis of this inner logic is the process of making. This
unfolding of a sculptural language is established within her repertoire.
Based in a material knowledge moves through an abandonment of expectation.
This does not come without particular guidance; the manipulations of
materials into unknown bodies of architecture. Working within a material
apparatus she subverts its logic and intention. Lochhead's method is only
enhanced by a challenge of material and labour. Her structural compositions
are embedded within a long relationship to wood and chainsaw carving.
Recently she takes her craft into a new realm, and references her own
proportion to the world. She comes at her sculptures with a bodily
response, a reminisce of performance.

<a href="/instant-coffee/listings/2015-12-17-03-field-contemporary-buy-us-for-you-by-us-david-roth-dec-17">03. OPENING | FIELD Contemporary | Buy Us, For You, By Us - David Roth | DEC 17</a>
------------------------------------------------------------------------
Buy Us, For You, By Us
David Roth
December 18th, 2015 January 16th, 2016
Opening Thursday December 17th, 2015, 7:00 10:00PM
FIELD Contemporary
17 West Broadway, Vancouver. BC

Buy Us, For You, By Us is a locally-inspired look at urban planning and
lifestyle marketing. The open-concept space features the debut of DRP, an
artisanally-curated unisex streetwear line featuring locally-sourced
organic materials. Buy Us, For You, By Us will also offer scheduled
barbershop services (trims only), allowing for community contributions
towards future editions of DRP designs. Visitors are also welcome to stop
by the official DRP workshop where lead designer David Roth will be
producing new works that are well-suited for both private collections and
the socially-conscious style hunter. All DRP originals will be available
for purchase both in-house and through our online shop, When I DRP, You
DRP, We DRP. Limited edition prints of DRP editorials will also be
exhibited and made available for photo and fashion enthusiasts alike. By
Us, For You, By Us is a community space, dedicated to conversations about
fashionable innovation, socially-progressive consumption and what quality
means to you.

David Roth's practice is the product of his economic surroundings. Raised
in an expensive city and surrounded by financial excess with limited
personal means, David decided to study the relationships between private
wealth, artistic worth and social value. David produces installations that
act as stages where he performs comic versions of achieving artistic and
financial success. As an economic comic, David wholeheartedly attempts
achievement to the point of absurdity, encouraging audiences to re-think
how they understand worth and value.

<a href="/instant-coffee/listings/2015-12-17-04-screening-the-cinematheque-traces-that-resemble-us-arndt-gra">04. SCREENING | The Cinematheque | Traces That Resemble Us: Arndt & Graham | DEC 17</a>
------------------------------------------------------------------------
Traces That Resemble Us

December 17, 2015

Robert Arndt / Pickpocket + Pursuit, Plunder & Fleece - 6:30pm
Rodney Graham / Dillinger is Dead - 8:30pm

The Cinematheque, in collaboration with Monte Clark Gallery, presents
Traces That Resemble Us, a screening series and art exhibition that
explores the intersections between visual art in Vancouver and cinema.
Motivated by Jeff Wall's history as a former film programmer at The
Cinematheque, Traces That Resemble Us invites twelve prominent
Vancouver-based artists to each program a film that has been influential to
his or her practice, and to exhibit artwork for a corresponding group
exhibition at Monte Clark Gallery.

On December 17 at 6:30pm, artist Robert Arndt will introduce his selected
film, Robert Bresson's immortal Pickpocket (1959), along with his own work,
Pursuit, Plunder & Fleece. "Robert Bresson's Dostoevskian Pickpocket is a
film that resists conventional filmic language. The editing,
cinematography, use of non-actors and their expressionless/non-acting
performances, duplications, choreography of shot sequences, music in a
way, it is Bresson who is pulling a sleight of hand on the audience and the
medium of film." (RA)

On December 17 at 8:30pm, Helga Pakasaar, Curator at Presentation House
Gallery, will introduce artist Rodney Graham's film selection, Marco
Ferreri's unsung masterpiece Dillinger is Dead (1969).

http://www.thecinematheque.ca/traces-that-resemble-us

Artspeak,

233 Carrall Street
Vancouver, BC, V6B 2J2
Canada

Artspeak purchased its space on Carrall Street in Gastown in 1999 for
$178,000. The expansion and inflation of Vancouver real estate demonstrates
the impossibility of this number in 2015, and outright ownership feels like
an insurmountable goal for an artist-run centre today. A long line of
dedicated board, staff, members, and donors allowed Artspeak to make
mortgage payments of $10,000 and $20,000 in 2007 and 2013, respectively,
and now, a final and most notable gift of $20,000 from Geoffrey Farmer, in
collaboration with Catriona Jeffries Gallery, has made us mortgage free
after sixteen years of ownership. Although it is meaningful for this
contribution to come from a past-president, it is even more of an honour to
receive this gift from an artist. It underlines the importance of the work
we do, and our shared investment in the future of Artspeak and all
artist-run centres.

Even though this city has shifted around us, from our changing neighbours
to the cost of land below our feet, our organizational makeup has not. We
remain an institution of two staff and nine board members, determined to
push Artspeak into an exciting third decade. Our plan is to embark on a new
impossiblethe purchase of a second unit in Gastownand have secured
$150,000 from the City of Vancouver and $17,000 from the BC Arts Council to
achieve this goal. By implementing a strategy of gradual growth, our aim is
to increase capacity, expand the publishing program, and improve our
ability to host readings, talks, and performances that serve the arts
community, our members, and our neighbours.

As we enter our thirtieth year, please consider BECOMING A SUBSCRIBER, a
sustained commitment of $20/month or an annual gift of $240. Subscribers
receive special edition mailers and publications throughout the year.
Subscriptions are an investment not only in the long-term growth of
Artspeak, but to our continued impact within this neighbourhood. Donations
made before December 31 are eligible for a 2015 charitable tax receipt and
can be made online at artspeak.ca/information.

We are fortunate. We are fortunate for the board and staff that had the
foresight to purchase our space, we are fortunate for the opportunity to
stay, and we are fortunate for all of you who continue to support us. You
give us possibility.

Call for Applications

Job Title: Development Specialist

Organization: Vancouver Access Artist Run Centre

Access Gallery is an artist-run centre established in 1991, with a vital
mandate to support critically-engaged visual artists at early points in
their careers. In the past two years, the gallery has undergone
transformational change, with a steadfast focus on artistic rigour and on
strengthening the long-term sustainability of the organization. Access now
seeks a limited-term Development Specialist to take a leading role in
stewarding the organization to its next stage of accomplishment and capacity.

Position Summary:
Reporting to the Director/Curator and working closely with the Board of
Directors, the Development Specialist is responsible for identifying,
implementing, and prioritizing new development strategies and revenue
streams, in order to further build the organization's breadth of
sustainable funding and long-term organizational and programmatic support.
With a clear view of the organization's mandate and mission, the Specialist
will work to define, build, and steward a community of gallery donors and
supporters. The Specialist will be tasked with successfully increasing
income garnered from development initiatives, so that the limited-term
Specialist position can transition into a permanent, part-time development
officer, and will educate Directors and staff so that these practices may
be institutionalized for the long term.

Qualifications:
Postgraduate degree in a related field
Minimum 5 years experience in a fundraising or communications position in
the visual arts or relevant non-profit sector
Proven success soliciting and securing financial donations within the
cultural sector and stewarding a donorship base

Outstanding writing, editing and communication skills and impeccable
attention to detail
Proven history of effectively functioning in a fast-paced team setting
yet also of successfully progressing projects independently
Highly proficient in Microsoft Word, Excel, Outlook, and relevant donor
management software

Must be available for occasional evening and weekend work

Knowledge of and experience with external communications methods,
practices, and techniques an asset

Experience with artist-run culture an asset

Essential Duties and Responsibilities:

Identify, develop, and implement donor acquisition strategy in line
with Access' vision, including the identification of donor/sponsor benefits
and providing measures for their fulfillment
Manage donor database and train gallery staff on database workings

Implement systems that enable and anticipate long-term event planning

Create and maintain a calendar of fundraising/development events, and
assist in their production
Oversee and continually develop donor related web content: website &
social media platforms
Work with Director/Curator to maintain and strengthen the
organization's brand through design, copy, and campaign-related materials
Oversee production and printing of direct mail and event-related
campaigns including flyers, invitations, newsletters, etc.
Work cooperatively with staff, donors and members of the community
Exercise good judgment and discretion with donors

If you meet the requirements listed above, please submit a cover letter,
resume, and writing sample to k.phillips@accessgallery.ca by January 20, 2015.

SALARY: $30.00/hour
Limited Term Contract (possibility of extension)
HOURS: 20 hours weekly for 48 weeks

Start Date: February 22, 2016

This position is made possible through the generous support of a British
Columbia Arts Council Capacity and Sustainability Grant.
