<a href="/instant-coffee/listings/2019-06-13-01-centre-a-call-for-volunteers">01. CALL | Centre A | Call for VOLUNTEERS</a>
------------------------------------------------------------------------
Founded in 1999, Centre A is Canadas only public gallery devoted to contemporary Asian 
and Asian diasporic art. Our mission is to build an inclusive community for contemporary 
Asian art and foster a greater understanding of evolving cultural experiences and identities. 
We are committed to creating mentorship opportunities for emerging artists and cultural 
workers, while facilitating collaborations across local, national, and international contexts. 
Our Reading Room is home to an extensive collection of books and research materials, 
including the Finlayson Collection of Rare Asian Art Books. 

Centre As volunteer opportunities are centered around 4 different roles: Gallery Attendant, 
Library Attendant, Installation Assistant, and Events Assistants. Additional Opportunities 
available. Each role requires different commitment levels! Help us!

Gallery Attendant

Stationed in our reception space, a gallery attendant will greet and introduce visitors to 
Centre As current exhibition, answer enquiries, issue memberships, and ensure a 
welcoming experience for all visitors. 

Commitment

One 5 hour shift per week

Reading Room Attendant/Library Attendant

Library Assistants will aide in the organization, cataloguing and maintenance of one of 
Canadas largest collection of literature dedicated to Asian art practices. In addition will be 
overseeing the maintenance and preservation of the Fraser Finlayson Collection of rare 
books on Classical Chinese and Japanese Art!

Installation Assistant

Installation Assistants will assist with the installation and take-down of exhibitions. 
Preference will be given to those who have previous art gallery experience, or is interested 
in gaining skills in visual art exhibit preparation and installation. Duties may include, 
painting, hang artwork, assist with labelling and didactic material, moving and building 
plinths, and using power tools. 

Events Assistant

Event Assistants will provide support for special events and public programming. Events 
may vary. Duties may include set up or tear down, greeting attendees, providing basic event 
information, managing the registration table, staffing the bar, and responding to general 
enquiries. 

Volunteer Skills/Qualifications Required
Enjoy working with the public.

Patience.

Work effectively independently and collaboratively within a team environment.

Successful candidates will serve without remuneration. We encourage all those who are 
interested to apply. We would love to have you in any capacity!

If you are interested in volunteering at Centre A, please fill out the form below or contact 
Emma at development@centrea.org.

<a href="/instant-coffee/listings/2019-06-13-04-gallery-jones-fiona-ackerman-herbaria-5-8pm-jun-13">04. OPENING | Gallery Jones | FIONA ACKERMAN: Herbaria | 5-8PM | JUN 13</a>
------------------------------------------------------------------------
Opening Reception: Thursday, June 13th, 5 - 8 pm.
Exhibition Dates: June 13 - July 13th, 2019

Gallery Jones is pleased to present Herbaria, a solo exhibition of new paintings by 
Fiona Ackerman. In this recent body of work, the artist explores her interest in botanical 
painting, a pursuit that had its heyday during the 18th century Age of Imperialism.

Inspired by prominent women of the time, notably Maria Sibylla Merian (1647-1717), a 
German-born naturalist and scientific illustrator, Ackerman takes art history and science "into 
the realm of abstraction and free aesthetic expression" through large oil and acrylic on 
canvas paintings. In the works, space is delineated and categorized with intention, revealing 
insects, birds and plants as curiosities and subjects of study.

Artist will be in attendance.

Rydel Cerezo is a Filipino artist residing in Vancouver, Canada. His work investigates 
the spaces between sexuality and religion, race and beauty, and identity and culture. He 
is interested in how these disparate themes metaphorically and visually coalesce.

Simon Grefiel is an artist born and raised in Tacloban City, Philippines and is currently 
living on the region south of the Fraser river, on unceded traditional and ancestral lands 
of SUTW (Tsawout), q"ic"y" (Katzie), qa:nan (Kwantlen), and sc"waena tmx 
(Tsawwassen) First Nations. Grefiel is currently exploring with language, history, and 
spirits. He does this through expressive digital gestures, performance, poetry, and 
engaging with the universal language of painting. Grefiel performed a reading at "Pa-
pag-page" publication launch at Richmond Art Gallery. He curated the show "twice the 
length as it is in width" at the City Centre Library, Surrey. He is working to finish a BFA at 
Emily Carr University of Art + Design. 

Rea Saxena is a South Asian video artist from New Delhi and was born in the small 
town of Hainburg an der Donau in Lower Austria. Her experiences as a South Asian 
woman in North America informs her practice as a video artist and narrative filmmaker. 
Saxena was raised around the world, as she moved to a different continent every 3 
years, this led to Saxena editing home movies, and making short horror films with her 
younger sister. She continues enjoying the process of completing her filmic puzzles in 
post production. Rea Saxena is an emerging artist and filmmaker currently working 
towards a Bachelor in film, video, and integrated media in Vancouver, at Emily Carr 
University of Art & Design. Her latest short film Rani won Best Directing award at Emily 
Carr University of Art & Design.

------------------------------------------------------------------------
Job Description:

Reporting to: Operations Director (grunt gallery) and General Manager (Other Sights for 
Artists Projects)

Hours: $27/hr 18 hours per week. Some evening and weekend work. Flexible schedule.

Start date: July 2, 2019

Project Description:

The Blue Cabin Floating Artist Residency (BCFAR) is a collaboration between grunt 
gallery Other Sights for Artists Projects and Creative Cultural Collaborations. The 
collaborating organizations have formed the Blue Cabin Committee to oversee the 
ongoing management of the BCFAR.

About the Blue Cabin:

The BCFAR provides artists with a unique opportunity to live and work in a completely 
restored and outfitted heritage cabin/studio with deep historical roots in both the 
foreshore past of North Vancouver on the West Coast of British Columbia and the early 
years of Vancouvers contemporary art scene. Situated on a floating platform and 
anchored in a marine environment, the residency features a state of the art, self-
sustaining tiny house as accommodation. Responding to the unique environment,

sentence.

Lou Sheppards work aims to disrupt systems of power, considering in particular how bodies

interrogate legibility. Through ritual, repetition, queer readings, and translation, Sheppard 
considers the illegibility of bodies and identities when they move outside of language. Taking 
as its structure the libretto of an opera, the installation at Access acts both a record and 
invocation of an action. My Fears of Tomorrow Are Melting Away is a score in five parts, 
situating the tasks of self-affirmation and self-actualization as the central labours of the 
individual in late capitalism.

IN CONVERSATION: Lou Sheppard and Katie Belcher
Saturday 29 June from 2-4pm

raised on unceded MiKmaq territory, and is based in Kjiputuk/Halifax. Sheppard graduated 
from the Nova Scotia College of Art and Design in 2006 and then studied English and 
Education at Mount Saint Vincent University. In their recent practice Sheppard uses 
processes of translation and metaphor to interrogate structures of power in data and 
language. Their work is evidenced through installation, performance and score and often 
leads them to collaborate with other artists, including musicians, visual artists, and 
performing artists. Sheppard has exhibited work both in Canada and internationally, and was

Fiona Banners alias The Vanity Press stems from The Vanity Press, an imprint she

become the mainstay of her practice, and is highly influential in the field of artists publishing.

This exhibition focuses on Banners Heart of Darkness, published in 2015 by the Vanity 
Press in collaboration with Four Corners Press. Banners remake of Joseph Conrads 1899 
novella takes the form of a glossy luxury magazine. It began to take shape when she 
accepted an invitation by the Archive of Modern Conflict to conduct research in the archive. 
Noting a lack of contemporary images, Banner commissioned Magnum photographer Paolo 
Pellegrin, who is well known for covering global conflicts, including in the Democratic 
Republic of Congo where Conrads narrator Marlow sets his story, to turn his lens on the 
financial district of London. These images form the illustrations that accompany the text. 
Alongside the publication itself, the exhibition includes related work such as Breathing Bag 
(2016), a small kinetic sculpture made up of a plastic bag printed with a misquote from the 
novella that reads Mistah Kurtz"He Not Dead and the film Phantom (2015) in which a 
drone Phantom camera attempts to read Heart of Darkness as the down draft from its 
blades continually blows the magazine out of reach, eventually destroying it.

Further, the exhibition reflects on earlier works such as The Nam and Trance (1997), 
including Banners verbal remake of Apocalypse Now, which in turn translates Francis Ford 
Coppolas redeployment of Conrads narrative framework from Heart of Darkness. Other 
works on display include a selection of Full Stop Inflatables (2018) and Full Stop Bean Bags 
(2015), which take the form of massive 3D period marks from various fonts. Also featured 
are the artists books Scroll Down and Keep Scrolling (2015), Font Book (2016), and select 
artworks related to them. This press release is set in Font, a typeface designed by Banner, it 
is an interpolation of all the fonts she has ever worked with.

Fiona Banner aka The Vanity Press is an English artist, who lives and works in London. In 
2002 she was shortlisted for the Turner Prize and in 2010, she was selected to create the 
10th Duveen Hall commission at Tate Britain. Other recent exhibitions include: Runway 
AW17, De Pont Museum, Tilburg, Netherlands (2017), Buoys Boys, De La Warr Pavilion, 
Bexhill, UK (2016), Scroll Down And Keep Scrolling, Ikon Gallery, Birmingham, UK (2015) 
and Kunsthalle Nuremberg, Germany (2016), Wp Wp Wp, Yorkshire Sculpture Park, 
Wakefield (2014).
