PLANS, DOLLARS, LISTS

Jill Henderson

Thursday, March 13th, 7-9 p.m.

March 13 - April 5, 2014

Wil Aballe Art Projects | WAAP
528-2050 Scotia St.
Vancouver, BC

Canada V5T 4T1

Buzzer 189
+ 1 778 229 3458
wil@WAAPart.com

Parking at rear of building
Hours: Tu 6-9 p.m., Sat 1-5 p.m

In this new exhibition by Vancouver-based, Glasgow School of Art-trained 
artist, Jill Henderson includes bodies of work, such as Hotel Rooms, Useless 
Money and List Paintings, will be shown salon-style within the WAAP space 
alongside two wall drawings. These series are assertions of our quotidian 
life; a candid approach to the banality of everyday codes and human notation.

Drawn from memory, Hotel Rooms are an ongoing series of paintings of hotel 
rooms stayed in by the artist over the last 20 years. These moments in time 
and experiences are revisited distantly with minimal notations, black paint on 
white canvas, describing these environments.

Meanwhile, the List Paintings are evidences of our productivity. Typically 
inert, abandoned pieces of paper hanging on fridges or desks are 
resurrected from the ashes of the useless; the contents of these scrawls 
have been rendered decisive with solid, incandescent colours as well as 
scale and composition decisions.

Similarly, Useless Money is a series of works, rendered candy-like with 
colourful enamels on wooden oval plaques, of reproduced handwritten dollar 
signs ‘$’ collected from restaurant and bar tabs. 

Another body of work, narrative numerical abstract paintings, feature 
repeated numbers, which are added, subtracted with allotted values related 
to the painting surface. The arrangement of colours and shapes on each 
painting have been assigned with their own internal set of rules, although the 
viewer may interpret another governing system of order within the works.

A further series of paintings of diagrams and graphs make use of the 
language of ‘assumed truths’ by diagrammatic display of ‘facts’ and ‘symbols’, 
analyzing the ‘now’ and the human condition.

Lastly, Jill Henderson’s wall drawings, which often feature humanized 
inhuman creatures and objects, project an unexpected humanity back at the 
viewer. They at first seem simple, then with later consideration, more 
complicated, a mirror of our own joys and fears. 

Bio

Since graduating from Glasgow School of Art, with a Masters in Fine Art in 
1993, Vancouver-based artist Jill Henderson (b. 1969, Tillicoutry, Scotland) 
has exhibited extensively internationally, notable exhibitions include; The 
National Review of LIVE ART, ICA, (London, UK), Beauty#2, Power Plant 
(Toronto), Art Gallery of Ontario (Toronto), Transmission Gallery (Glasgow), 
Art Metropole (Toronto), Catalyst Arts (Belfast), Hales Gallery (London, UK), 
Angelus Novus (Antwerp), Brasilica (Vienna) and Vox Populi (Philadelphia), 
Greater New York , PS1/MOMA, New York (2000), Orange Marble (2001), 
Taipei, Taiwan, Art Directors Club (NYC), Lothringer 13 (Munich), Morris & 
Belkin Gallery (Vancouver), Manif d’Art 2 (Quebec), The Americas Society 
(NYC) and solo shows at The Helen Pitt Gallery (Vancouver) and the 
Contemporary Art Gallery (Vancouver). She was also nominated for a Louis 
Comfort Tiffany award. Her work is also in the collection of The Robert 
McLaughlin Museum in Oshawa, Canada and the National Gallery of Canada 
as well as in numerous private collections. Henderson was also co-founder 
of ‘Free Parking’ an artist run gallery project in downtown Toronto.

------------------------------------------------------------------------
Emily Hill | Air Spill

Opening Friday, March 14, 2014, 6-8 pm

Macaulay Fine Art
293 East 2nd Avenue

Macaulay Fine Art is pleased to present Air Spill, a debut solo exhibition by 
Vancouver based artist Emily Hill. In a new body of work including large scale 
gestural paintings and a short performance, the artist explores material and 
colour as an immersive experience. Hill’s practice that oscillates between 
textile art and painting, highlights the dynamic tension of rethinking structure. 
Hill’s recent paintings are made with dye on ready-made wool rugs resulting 
in a complex array of effects that simultaneously refer to illusionistic space 
and to process-based materiality. Static colour is made flexible on an unstable 
fleece ground, producing distorted images. The work has a hazy internal 
logic, stirring up shared histories of tapestry, painting and the life of a 
domestic object. 

The exhibition will release a text by Tiziana La Melia in conversation with the 
new work.

Emily Hill is a recent graduate of Emily Carr University of Art & Design.

Exhibition/Opening UBC AHVA Gallery
Teaching Material
Teaching Material accompanies Shifting Pedagogies, the 37th annual 
Graduate Symposium of the Department of Art History, Visual Art and Theory. 
Runs March 14 to 29, 2014.

Teaching Material is an exhibition about art and education, whether the 
education of the artists themselves or their attempts to educate others. Each 
work considers the different sites of learning as an integral aspect of 
education. In some cases this means examining objects or spaces that have 
fallen into disuse or become outmoded, such as the paper encyclopedia or an 
abandoned schoolhouse. In others, institutionally organized education 
systems are questioned, or intervened with. For example, a UBC MFA 
candidate utilizing the structure of the Teaching Assistant system to examine 
her own position as both educator and student. Still other works turn their 
focus to the communal nature of learning and discovery, whether exploring 
the possibilities of a guided walk, or the limits of youtube art tutorials.

Teaching Material accompanies Shifting Pedagogies, the 37th annual 
Graduate Symposium of the Department of Art History, Visual Art and Theory; 
held March 14th and 15th in the Lillooet Room of the Irving K. Barber Learning 
Centre.

<a href="/instant-coffee/listings/2014-03-13-11-vivo-permanent-deviation-mar-14">11. OPENING | VIVO | PERMANENT DEVIATION | MAR 14</a>

PERMANENT DEVIATION
Opening: FRIDAY MARCH 14, 2014 / 8pm-12am (PST)
Website launch and live art coding

PERMANENT DEVIATION (http://permanentdeviation.com) is an online
Processing compiler and simultaneous participatory exhibition space
conceived by JULIE GENDRON in association with BRADY MARKS.

Permanent Deviation provides an online space that can be described as an
exquisite corpse, graffiti wall, training ground for making generative art
using the Processing* programming language. Coders must use the code of
the last participant in a set amount of time to generate a new art object.
In this way, each participant’s code acts as the baseline from which the
next coder has to work.

The site will be officially launched at VIVO on March 14, 2014 in the
Video Bar. JESSE SCOTT and JULIE GENDRON will co-curate an evening of 
live
art generation where any coders at the opening or around the world can
take over the site and change the canvas.

JULIE GENDRON is a designer and artist who works in the areas of
interactivity, access, playfulness and change. Julie designs and
facilitates experiences that allow people to explore and create their own
point of view, culture and communities through her participatory art
practice. This project is one in a series of structural artworks that is
intended to be completed by its viewers. Other works can be seen here:
http://desiringproductions.com

*Processing is a generative programming language typically used for the
purpose of art making. It is normally executed in a client-side
application but this project uses the processing.js library to allow it to
be used online.

This project was developed with the participation of Creative BC and the
British Columbia Arts Council.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

JOB POSTING: Programming Coordinator

Gallery Gachet is seeking a motivated, creative and organized individual who 
will work collaboratively with the staff/collective team to develop, support and 
implement the delivery of cultural services and operations related to Gallery 
Gachet’s mandate, while contributing to the financial health of the gallery.

Gallery Gachet is a dynamic collectively-run arts production, education and 
presentation space located in Vancouver’s Downtown Eastside (DTES) built 
to empower participants as artists, administrators and curators. We strive to 
provide a focal point for dialogue amongst outsider/dissident artists. Through 
artistic means, we aim to demystify and challenge issues related to mental 
health, disability issues and social marginalization in order to educate the 
public and promote social and economic justice. We see a time and place 
where people who have experienced marginalization feel fully supported in 
their struggle for a creative life and career, including access to adequate

Free Style Vision Boarding

Visualization is an important tool for achieving your dreams. 

Though the process begins in the mind, creating a physical presence for your 
ideas can be an empowering and uplifting experience. 

Begin by searching for things you find inspiring. Images are important, but 
often simply refer to someone else's vision. Scraps or samples of real objects 
and materials can serve as concrete fragments of the intangible thing for 
which you're searching.

Assemble these things in whatever way feels right to you. Place an object 
next to an image, take note of how it makes you feel, and try a different 
combination. You will know when the combination is right. 

Pay attention to the relationship between objects and their positions. Think 
about the psychology at play in your decisions. 

Embrace metaphor, and don't worry about cliches. Remember, this Free Style 
Vision Board is for you! Though there's nothing wrong with showing it to your 
friends or family, don't expect them to understand or appreciate the 
complexity of what you've created. 

Once you've invented your own language, you'll be able to reveal secrets to 
yourself you may not have known you were keeping.

Happy Boarding!

Stan Douglas: Synthetic Pictures

Presentation House Gallery is proud to launch Stan Douglas: Synthetic 
Pictures, an exhibition of new photographs by world-renowned Vancouver 
artist Stan Douglas. His complex artworks interpret the social impact of optical 
technologies. Frequently borrowing from popular culture and literary sources, 
Douglas examines the legacies of modernity and historical narratives of 
socio-cultural events through photographs, film and installation.

This exhibition premieres works that mark the artist’s first foray into abstract 
imagery. Corrupt Files is a suite of photographic scans extracted from 
Douglas’ film works, reduced to patterns of data. These synthetic images 
capture a sense a ‘ghost in the machine’ that is interfering with the electronic 
information. A large-scale panorama depicting postwar Hogan’s Alley in 
Strathcona is similarly distilled from his new online web application Circa 
1948, that provokes immersive experiences of Hogan’s Alley and the Hotel 
Vancouver during that period. The digitally reconstructed scene, rendered 
with intricate historical accuracy, is translated into a black and white 
photograph, and thus confuses distinctions between artifice and realism. The 
exhibition includes a documentary survey of this mixed race neighbourhood 
when it became slated for urban renewal with the construction of the 
Georgia Viaduct in 1972. Also included are photographs in the style of 
postwar news photography from Douglas’ recent Midcentury Studio series 
that relate to his cinematic stage production Helen Lawrence, a multimedia film 
noir thriller. Based on extensive historical research, Douglas’ new works 
provide insights into the fraught social tensions and corruption in postwar 
Vancouver.

Stan Douglas was born in 1960 in Vancouver, where he continues to live and 
work. He graduated from Emily Carr College of Art in the early 1980s. His solo 
exhibitions include: the Studio Museum in Harlem, New York (2005) and the 
Staatsgalerie and Württembergischer Kunstverein in Stuttgart (2007). He has 
participated in group exhibitions worldwide, including Documenta IX, 1992; X, 
1997; and XI, 2002 as well as the Venice Biennale in 1990, 2001 and 2005. 
He is represented by David Zwirner Gallery, New York/London and Victoria 
Miro, London. 

Two new books on Stan Douglas, published by Prestel and Steidl, will be 
launched in the coming months and the world premiere of Helen Lawrence 
launches in mid-March at the Stanley Theatre, Vancouver. Research for this
