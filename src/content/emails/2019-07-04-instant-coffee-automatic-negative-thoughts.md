Learn to make a traditional Coast Salish weaving featuring Kwantlen Elder Hazel Gludo 
on July 6 @ 11:00 am - 1:00 pm

Kwantlen Elder, Hazel (Fillardeau) Gludo has been weaving cedar for a number of 
years. From an early age, Hazels mother had taught her the importance that cedar has 
and how to embrace its values. Today, Hazel teaches and talks about how important it 
is in our lives and how years ago the people used it, and how we have great respect for 
our cedar tree and we too need to learn respect our trees that are around us, for the air, 
and how beautiful it is that we have trees.

Hazel is a prominent employee within the National Historic Fort Site in Fort Langley, BC. 
Visitors often come in just to visit with Hazel. Hazel uses the art of Cedar Weaving to 
share her stories.

Hazel will harvest the cedar she uses to weave hats, bracelets, medicine pouches, 
roses. One of Hazels Cedar Hats is on display with the We Are Kwantlen exhibit to 
show the importance that cedar has always had with the Kwantlen First Nation. Cedar 
has long been a valuable resource to the Northwest Coast First Nations people, but it 
also plays an important role in spiritual belief and ceremonial life.

Please join us at the Deer Lake Gallery for the workshop on July 6 @ 11:00 am - 1:00 
pm. Kits cost $5 each. Come on out and learn how to make a traditional coast salish 
weaving . Fun & interactive class. You must register early as space is limited!

$5 includes all supplies for the workshop required to participate. However, the 
demonstration is Free and open to all public to observe.

To purchase tickets please go to https://hazelgludocedarweaving.eventbrite.ca

<a href="/instant-coffee/listings/2019-07-04-04-kelowna-art-gallery-her-body-will-remember-jun-22">04. EXHIBITION | Kelowna Art Gallery | Her Body Will Remember | JUN 22</a>

Her Body Will Remember opens at Kelowna Art Gallery

Her Body Will Remember

June 22 to September 8, 2019

June 25, 2019 " Kelowna, BC: Her Body Will Remember is a contemporary art 
exhibition that features the works of three female indigenous artists who explore ways of 
remembering and reinterpreting their ancestors culture and practices.

It includes work by artists Mariel Belanger, Ts-"m- Igharas, and Tiffany Shaw-Collinge. It 
was guest curated by Erin Sutherland.

Each of the three artists were asked to consider the practice of making and their 
bodys memories of practice and technology through this process. The works make use 
of innovative materials and technologies to revisit the ingenuity of Indigenous arts 
practices, passed along through communities, families, and kin.

Visitors will encounter a variety of work in the two exhibition spaces that comprise the 
show. In T-t-n Singing Machine, Ts-"m- Igharas invites visitors into a karaoke booth 
where they can sing along in T-t-n. Tiffany Shaw-Collinges works are based on the 
handicrafts of her great grandmother. The mylar sculpture that is suspended in the air 
and black plastic 3D printed works are based on the floral (wild Alberta rose) motif 
pattern that her great grandmother drew and used. Mariel Belangers outdoor courtyard 
installation entitled tuktnix (Moon Lodge is a tule mat lodge, constructed using locally 
harvested reeds that were harvested, dried and woven together.

Her Body Will Remember will be on view for visitors to explore for themselves from June 
22 to September 8, 2019.

The Kelowna Art Gallery is located at 1315 Water Street in downtown Kelowna. For 
more information about current exhibitions, public programming or special events, 
please visit the Kelowna Art Gallery online at www.kelownaartgallery.com or call 250-
762-2226.

Mariel Belanger is a Vernon based artist, dedicated to contributing in the growth of 
interdisciplinary performance arts as a method to engage Indigenous community, 
language, and culture. As artist scholar, her MFA from UBC Okanagan researched 
identity through the lens of sqilxw ways of knowing and being, customary law, 
indigenous feminism, and performance theory.

Ts-"m- Igharas is an interdisciplinary artist and a member of the Tahltan First Nation. 
She has a bachelors degree from Emily Carr University of Art and Design (2011) and 
graduated from the Interdisciplinary Master's in Art, Media and Design program at 
OCAD.

Tiffany Shaw-Collinge is an interdisciplinary artist, curator and intern architect based in 
Alberta. She holds a BFA from Nova Scotia College of Art and Design (NSCAD) 
University, a masters in architecture from the Southern California Institute of 
Architecture (SCI-Arc) and is currently working at Manasc Isaac Architects.

Dr. Erin Sutherland is an Assistant Professor of Cultural Studies at the University of 
Alberta, independent curator and member of Ociciwan Contemporary Art Collective. She 
is of Mtis and settler decent and is from Grande Prairie Alberta, currently living in 
Edmonton.

Freedom-Hi! Zines from Hong Kong's Civil Movements

July 4 to July 20, 2019

Reception: 3 - 5 PM, Saturday, July 6 (artist in attendance, talk at 4 PM)

Hong Kong has been in the news! In the past weeks, the world has witnessed hundreds 
of thousands of its citizens taken to the streets to peacefully protest a controversial 
extradition bill
that could send the residents to Mainland China to be tried in court. The protests, in 
turn, question the human rights and freedom issues under the dictatorship of the 
Chinese Communist Party. The situation continues to escalate and create uncertainties 
and
unrests within Hong Kong as well as Chinas relationship with the world.

In this critical time, Hotam Press is pleased to present an exhibition of zines that will 
highlight and investigate the current social and political condition of Hong Kong. Titled 
Freedom-Hi!
Zines from Hong Kong's Civil Movements, the exhibition is a glimpse into the popular 
creative activity from a very focused lens.

Like in other parts of the world, the new generations in Hong Kong has taken up zine 
making and other DIY tactics as common practice and outlet in the expressing of ideas, 
spreading of
messages and voicing their concerns. Playful, creative and often humorous, these 
activist zines bring us closer to the milieu and the fundamental concerns of the makers.

FREEDOM-HI! is drawn from the rare collection of Ranee Ng and Zine Coop (Hong 
Kong). It is consisted of over fifty zines, printed matters and artifacts from the movement 
by different
artists, writers, activists, and everyday citizens.

Please note that some materials are in English or bilingual while some are in Chinese 
only, but with plenty of graphics.

In addition, in the afternoon of July 6 at 4 PM, artist Ranee Ng will share her insights into 
the background and culture of zine making, relating to the ongoing civil movements and 
protests
to Hong Kong.

Bookshop hours:
Thursday to Saturday, 12 to 6 PM

The Clock
Christian Marclay

Recognised as one of the most important contemporary artworks of our time, The 
Clock is an audiovisual tour-de-force. Presented in a custom-built cinema within the 
gallery, the work montages film and television footage from the last 70 years. The time 
represented in each clip is synchronised with actual time, allowing The Clock to 
function as a 24-hour timepiece. Christian Marclay won the coveted Gold Lion Award at 
the Venice Biennale in 2011 for this now-famous artwork, recently presented at the Tate 
Modern, London.

Intricately edited in both its visuals and sound, The Clock is created from thousands of 
film fragments collaged into a minute-to-minute tapestry of modern life, with 
appearances by many familiar movie stars. Pre-dawn scenes include dream sequences; 
morning scenes depict characters waking up, drinking coffee, or heading out to work; 
evening shots show parties, dinners, and theatre shows. The pace of The Clock 
reflects daily rhythms, building toward noon, varying in the afternoon, and utilising both 
images and soundtracks to herald the works dramatic midnight climax. The 
mesmerising experience of following various narratives, settings and moods within the 
space of a few minutes, makes time unravel in countless directions at once. 

The Clock will be on view at The Polygon Gallery from July 5 - September 15, 2019.
