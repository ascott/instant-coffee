<a href="/instant-coffee/listings/2019-02-07-03-conduit-gallery-considering-contraints-zandi-dandizette-7-pm">03. OPENING | Conduit Gallery | considering contraints | zandi dandizette | 7 PM | FEB 7</a>
------------------------------------------------------------------------
Conduit Gallery | 165 East Hastings

February 7-21st

Opening: February 7th 7pm

Open Saturdays 1-3pm and by appointment (email conduitgallery165@gmail.com to 
book)

When discussing tropes we can sometimes reinforce their structures while 
dissecting the binary aspects. considering constraints seeks to address this 
conundrum by breaking the binary with organic anomalies and a rotating loop of 
many faces without bodily intervention. Zandi frames the conversation with the 
material relationship to childhood utilizing puzzle foam mats. This time period is the 
foundation of our life as we are given names and identities to order the chaos in our 
existence. The absurdity being that weve created unrealistic expectations within 
these constraints.

Zandi Dandizette invites you to ascribe meaning and context to their body in a 
performance at the closing of the show on February 21st at 7pm. They will be 
wearing a blank canvas in which you may make marks over their entire being for an 
hour duration. They will continue to wear the created piece for the rest of the 
evening.

------------------

Zandi Dandizette is a interdisciplinary New Media installation artist and curator living 
and practicing in Vancouver, BC who originates from Portland, OR. They graduated 
from Emily Carr University of Art + Design with a Bachelor of Media Arts (2014). 
Demonstrating unwavering support for the arts in British Columbia, Zandi is the 
Founder and Director of the James Black Gallery (2014), Programs and Membership 
Coordinator for CARFAC, BC (2018) and member of the Board of Directors for VIVO 
Media Arts Centre (2018 " 2020).Zandi Dandizettes artwork has shown 
internationally in Australia, Canada, the United States, Iran, and the United 
Kingdom.

Zandi Dandizette likens their medium as space whether 2D or 3D. They use colour, 
queerness,gender, shape, and line to explore discourse around identity, through 
immersive interactions via the binary, absurdism, liminality, and fractal existence. 
They view it as a base code, and every medium that they learn is a tool to explore 
this identity-centric world building. Zandi most notably creates projection paintings 
and immersive installations, which have been displayed at VIVO Media Arts Centre, 
Richmond Digital Carnival WATER, Vancouver Arts and Leisure, Mount Pleasant 
Business Improvement Association, Satellite Art Show, and YVR Public Disco. They 
work with ideological tropes that are built in, rather than referencing contemporary 
Intellectual Properties and empowering them. Zandi utilizes colors like blue, pink, 
and purple, and shapes like circles, squares, and triangles for their binary and 
hierarchical aspects.

------------------

Conduit Gallery is an artist-run project founded in 2018 and directed by Allison 
Watters. It provides a platform for early career and local artists whose work initiates 
abstract experiences through performance and installation.

Conduit Gallery is located on the unceded, ancestral and traditional territories of the 
xmky"m (Musqueam), Skwxwu7mesh (Squamish), and sl"ilwta (Tsleil-
Waututh) Nations.

Accessibility: Wheelchair accessible, entrance is on street, main level with no steps.

<a href="/instant-coffee/listings/2019-02-07-04-job-griffin-art-projects-public-programs-and-residency-coord">04. JOB | Griffin Art Projects | Public Programs and Residency Coordinator | FEB 7</a>
------------------------------------------------------------------------
Public Programs and Residency Coordinator

PTE - 2 days per week, 9 to 5 pm

Griffin Art Projects is seeking an innovative and dynamic thinker who can help to 
build audiences and excitement through the development and delivery of public 
programs and residency programs that capture the publics imagination and expand 
the experience of art and art galleries.

Griffin Art Projects (GAP) is a non-profit art gallery and residency located in North 
Vancouver. GAP is a non-collecting institution that has quickly become a vibrant 
contributor to the North Vancouver cultural landscape.

Griffin Art Projects is introducing a new position that will assist in the coordination of 
its residency and public program. The successful applicant will assist in the design of 
programs that offer unique opportunities for visitors of all ages and levels of interest 
to engage with art and culture at GAP. This position will assist both with the 
development of adult public programming and youth engagement in order to 
encourage new audiences to participate and to generate new knowledge and 
innovative thinking on contemporary art and culture with the ambition of creating 
dynamic learning opportunities for a wide range of visitors whose familiarity with 
contemporary art may differ.

This role will engage with best practices in pedagogy and for the impact of learning 
and public engagement and offer a unique opportunity to create a meaningful 
program that integrates deeper learning and participation, involving high schools, 
higher education institutions, the local, national and international arts community, 
and the general public, and liaising with artists, thinkers, and innovators from various 
disciplines to support GAP in producing innovative and impactful learning and public 
engagement. 

Reporting to the Director, the Public Programs and Residency Coordinator will 
produce a schedule of public programs, events and community outreach activities 
that animate, complement and enhance our exhibitions and residency program, and 
GAPs exploration of art collecting and collections practices and methodologies.

Duties include: assisting with the conceptual development, administration and 
implementation of adult and youth focused public programs including: artists talks 
and tours, lectures, workshops, film series, symposia and performances. The Public 
Programs and Residency Coordinator will also assist with the development and 
coordination of special community-based partnership and education initiatives and 
related special projects.

It is a permanent part-time two day per week position, with scheduled work from 
Friday to Saturday, 9:00 am to 5:00 pm. This position includes some evening hours, 
as required to fulfill responsibilities and be on hand for gallery programs. 

Please submit a cover letter and current CV as one document in pdf format to: 
info@griffinartprojects.ca. We thank all applicants in advance. Only those selected 
for an interview will be contacted. Griffin Art Projects advocates equal opportunity for 
all employees and applicants for employment and is committed to diversity in the 
workplace.

..

QUALIFICATIONS

The successful candidate will possess a Masters degree in art or art history, 
museum or curatorial studies, thorough knowledge of Canadian and international art 
history and issues in contemporary art; 2 years working experience in a museum, 
public art gallery and/or artist run centre as well as demonstrated experience in 
program development and delivery. Exceptional written, presentation and 
interpersonal communication skills are required. Experience working successfully 
with internal and external partners including artists, educators, curators, producers 
and academics and the ability to problem solve and work independent and quickly, 
and manage the program on budget, while also keeping statistics, documentation 
and monitoring. Must be able to work with youth as well as adults, with the relevant 
clearances.

Candidates will have an ability to communicate effectively, both orally and in writing; 
strong organizational and administrative skills and the ability to work under general 
direction and within a small team environment. Fluency in French is considered an 
asset.

This will be a part-time position beginning in February 2019. The deadline for 
applications is February 7, at 5pm. To apply please send a cover letter, current CV to 
info@griffinartprojects.ca.

For more information on our organization, visit griffinartprojects.ca.

ARTIST BIOGRAPHY

Coming from a classical piano background of training, Kaifeng-born, Vancouver 
based Yu Su has arrived as a sound artist and composer corresponding audible 
elements freely between the cosmological Daoist mandate and the ecological 
property of the Pacific Northwest. Tapping into an expansive repertoire of New Age, 
library music, meditative jazz and house, Yu Sus emotion-packed compositional 
punches are best described as organically groovy jazz-bient expeditions between 
coasts. A proficient instrumentalist who conjures up moments of natural and 
synthetic beauty, her delicate, dub-inflected signature balances a variety of luminous 
and wistful downtempo textures. She is responsible for productions on DCs 
Peoples Potential Unlimited, NYCs RVNG Intl. & Arcane, and The Hague-based 
Wichelroede.

A lecture by Wu Hung as part of the Joan Carlisle-Irving Lecture Series

UBC Department of Art History, Visual Art and Theory

This event is free and open to the public. Please join us for a reception following the

Portraiture and self-portraiture are two standard genres in visual art, including 
photography. A self-portrait is a likeness whose subject is the artist him/herself; a 
portrait is a representation of a person made by someone else. This lecture 
investigates some photographs which disrupt this seemingly self-evident 
classification of images. These examples belong to a group of studio portraits that 
bear the sitters inscriptions. I suggest that when an inscription is imbued with a 
distinct I voice and expresses the sitters personal experience and aspiration, it 
transforms the anonymous portrait into an I-portraits. The main part of the talk 
discusses several images related to the queue-cutting movement in early twentieth-
century China, before and after the Republican Revolution in 1911 that ended the 
countrys dynastic history. The final section contemplates how photographys media 
specificity contributes to such transformation.

Wu Hung is the Harrie A. Vanderstappen Distinguished Service Professor in Chinese 
Art History at the Departments of Art History and East Asian Languages and 
Civilizations at the University of Chicago. He has published widely on both traditional 
and contemporary Chinese art. His interest in both has led him to experiment with 
different ways to integrate these conventionally separate phases into new kinds of 
art historical narratives, as exemplified by his Monumentality in Early Chinese Art 
and Architecture (1995), The Double Screen: Medium and Representation of 
Chinese Pictorial Art (1996), Remaking Beijing: Tiananmen Square: the Creation of 
a Political Space (2005), A Story of Ruins: Presence and Absence in Chinese Art 
and Visual Culture (2012), and Zooming In: Histories of Photography in China 
(2016). Several of his ongoing projects follow this direction to explore the 
interrelationship between art medium, pictorial image, and architectural space, the 
dialectical relationship between absence and presence in Chinese art and visual 
culture, and the relationship between art discourse and practice.

Wu Hung is also the director of the Center for the Art of East Asia, and consulting 
curator of the Smart Museum of Art at the University of Chicago. He will be

In this intimate investigation of the artistic process, Lezli Rubin-Kunda explores the 
nuanced path of creative work and the way artists make sense of home and place

role of women in creating and taking care of "home," ideas of home disconnected 
from place, and liberating concepts of "homelessness." Woven through these 
encounters with other artists are Rubin-Kundas reflections on her own artistic path.

ways that artists find and create meaning within a fragmented contemporary 
landscape.
