In conversation: Paul Kajander and Colleen Brown (moderated by Vanessa Kwan) 
Thursday, October 15th
7pm
Fillip 
305 Cambie Street
www.fillip.ca

Paul and Colleen will be talking of work, discussing ideas and chatting with you. Do come. Drinks.

Paul Kajander's cross-disciplinary works in installation, video, performance and drawing have 
been exhibited in Vancouver, Los Angeles and Tokyo. His activity with the bands The Emanglons 
and Kick Evrything--as well as the Directorship of the Helen Pitt Gallery Artist Run Centre-- each 
represent an important extension of his practice. 

Colleen Brown is an artist and writer living in Vancouver. Prior to her involvement with the art 
community, Colleen received her BA in Psychology and a diploma in Electrophysiology. After 
completing her BFA at Emily Carr Institute, Colleen worked as the Programme Coordinator at 
Artspeak for five years. She is currently studying sculpture at Bard College and will receive her
Master's degree in 2010. Her work has been exhibited in British Columbia and Quebec. 

Vanessa Kwan is a Vancouver-based artist and writer. Her work has been exhibited at a number 
of galleries, including solo exhibitions at the Or Gallery, Access, and the Art Gallery of the South 
Okanagan. She is a founding member of the arts collective Norma, and currently works as Public 
Programs Coordinator at the Vancouver Art Gallery. A public art commission for the City of 
Vancouver is forthcoming in 2010.

In association with the exhibition Black Hole is Also Supernova: Colleen Brown, Paul Kajander 
and Kara Uzelman
Curated by Vanessa Kwan
17 September - 1 November 2009
Richmond Art Gallery
7700 Minoru Gate Richmond BC

http://www.richmondartgallery.org

<a href="/instant-coffee/listings/2009-10-15-04-shudder-gallery-joseph-wesley-mcgonigal-holmes-peters-oct-15">04. Shudder Gallery | Joseph Wesley Mcgonigal Holmes Peters | OCT 15</a>
------------------------------------------------------------------------
call it something else.

new and discarded pieces from joseph wesley mcgonigal holmes peters

Opening Reception Thursday, October 15th 7pm to 11pm
Show Runs October 16th to 31st

Gallery Hours: Thursdays, Fridays, Saturdays 12:30pm to 5:30pm
433 Columbia Street @ Pender
(604) 488 5477
shuddergallery.com
jwmhp.com

Joseph Wesley Mcgonigal Holmes Peters describes his inspiration for his solo show with these 
words, "There are few utilities less appealing or relevant than those of objects forgotten. In July I 
fell out of love with my hands and by the end of September I had re-appropriated them. I fixed 
them to a board; tied strings to the tips. Sanded off the destiny lines and offset the composition 
with a few well placed pieces of metal I'd found in the street. When it was all done these hands 
were some other kind of contraption. They were a machine of different intention and something 
that I might fancy once more; despite being just as static, aesthetically awkward, and as 
purposeless as they'd always been. These pieces are much like those hands."

Joesph Wesley Mcgonicgal Holmes Peters will be displaying a series of new work in his first solo 
exhibition, taking place at Shudder Gallery on October 15, 2009. The artist will be displaying work 
in the multiple avenues that he explores through his medium of wood.

The artists' use of wood brings the pieces intentions to attention without becoming the focal point 
for the viewer. In his investigations of found work, Joesphs' use of wood is masked to the point of 
obscurity. The intent of this is not to fool but show the complexity of the subject matter, as well as 
serve as the allegory of falling out of love with his hands.

When the artist shifts his view to his furniture pieces, he approaches his ideas with out the veiled 
notions of his found work. The grain fully on display, and often made from one solid piece, this is 
a more sober statement of his medium as art with in functionality.

Satyagraha 
Yang Shu 

Thursday October 15-31, 2009 
Hours: Wednesday-Saturday noon-4pm 
Opening: Thursday Oct. 15, 2009, 4-7pm 
Gallery located in room 112 
Koerner Library 
1958 Main Mall, UBC, Vancouver 

The gallery located in Room 112, Koerner Library presents Satyagraha (October 15th – 31st, 
2009), the first Vancouver exhibition of Chinese artist Yang Shu. The word Satyagraha is a 
synthesis of the Sanskrit words Satya (truth) and Agraha (holding firmly to). It is a philosophy and 
practice of nonviolent resistance developed by Mohandas Karamchand Gandhi. For Gandhi, 
these two words linked together serve as a synonym for force, a united front representing truth 
and love. With that said, this exhibition presents Shu’s recent series of painting and paper 
collage works. These new works exude a forcefully raw and spontaneous visual power. His 
canvasses are crowded with superimposed scrawling, multiple layers of colour, abstract figures, 
and Chinese script. His impulsive brushstrokes and broken images walk a line between freedom 
and restraint. Yang Shu’s new works are explosive and provocative yet offer a subtle simplicity.

Since the early 1980s Shu has developed an extensive painting and installation based practice, 
showing extensively throughout China, and internationally (Shanghai, Beijing, Hong Kong, 
Amsterdam, New York, Sydney), including the iconic China/Avant-Garde Art exhibition at the 
National Art Museum of China in 1989. In excess of his individual artistic success, Shu is also a
professor at the Sichuan Fine Arts Institute and coordinates an independent artist-run space 
called Organhaus, both located in Huang Jiaoping.

Join us Thursday October 15th, 2009 from 4 – 7pm for the opening reception of Satyagraha, the 
artist will be in attendance.

More information: 604 822 4563 or 

http://www.ahva.ubc.ca/

<a href="/instant-coffee/listings/2009-10-15-06-or-gallery-brady-cranfield-and-kathy-slade-oct-16">06. Or Gallery | BRADY CRANFIELD and KATHY SLADE | OCT 16</a>
------------------------------------------------------------------------
Cranfield and Slade: 12 Sun Songs
Friday Oct 16 from 7 to 9pm
Zulu Records
1972 West 4th

The Or Gallery and Zulu Records are pleased to present the launch of artist/musicians BRADY 
CRANFIELD and KATHY SLADE's new work 12 SUN SONGS! Together with their crack band 
they will perform, entertain and provoke much debate... Drinks on hand!

12 Sun Songs is a yellow vinyl album made up of covers of pop songs about the sun. Aping a 
1970s concept album, Cranfield and Slade present 12 songs arranged to represent a day, 
beginning with songs about the sunrise and winding down with songs about sunsets. Tracks 
range from classics such as George Harrison’s Here Comes the Sun and The Kinks’ Waterloo 
Sunset to the lesser-known Sun by singer-songwriter Margot Guryan or Where Evil Grows by 
Vancouver ’s The Poppy Family. The album combines field recordings made in various Vancouver 
locations with electronic sound and acoustic and electric instruments.

Based in rainy Vancouver, Cranfield and Slade are made up of visual artist Kathy Slade and 
artist/musician Brady Cranfield, working with musicians including Larissa Loyva (Piano, Kellarissa), 
Johnny Payne (Victoria Victoria, The Shilos), and Chris Harris (Piano, Parks and Rec, The Secret 
Three, Womankind); and special guests John Collins (The New Pornographers, The Evaporators) 
and artist Rodney Graham (The Rodney Graham Band, UJ3RK5).

Admission is free

<a href="/instant-coffee/listings/2009-10-15-09-ecu-the-exchange-show-oct-21">09. ECU | The Exchange Show | OCT 21</a>
------------------------------------------------------------------------
The Exchange Show EMILY CARR UNIVERSITY 

21 October - 27 October 2009

Opening Wednesday October 21 7-10 pm, Emily Carr University Concourse Gallery.

What does it mean to displace one's self? To leave one’s routine and experience the new? In 
their third year, Emily Carr students have the chance to do just that, as a select number 
participate in the Student Exchange Program, traveling to various institutions across North 
America, Europe, and Australia. Here they have the opportunity to not only diversify their 
education, but also establish new connections while encountering a foreign place in a local way. 
Invaluable in it’s infinite contribution to what these students will produce in the future, the 
Exchange experience is one of great excitement and personal expansion; there is nothing quite 
so thought-provoking as stepping neatly out of one life and into another.

Upon their return, students from all faculties have come together to share a small part of their rich 
and varied journeys. The Exchange Show is a collection of these moments. Spanning multiple 
disciplines and medias, including industrial and communication design, drawing, painting, film, 
and photography, the student-organized Exchange Show presents work both inspired by, and 
created while, studying abroad.

See more at
http://exchangeshow.blogspot.com

The Grey Square Relay

Wednesday Oct 28, 2009

900h – 1600h, Downtown Vancouver

A minimum of 16 people, dressed in GREY, occupy a street corner, making a SILENT grey 
square.

Someone in that group will be designated corner captain. 

The corner captain keeps track of time and leads the grey square from one corner of the city to 
another.

After 15 minutes in a corner, led by the corner captain, they walk single file to another city corner 
and make another square.

This goes on all day, all around the downtown core.

People will be funneled in and out in hourly intervals so that no one except the most zealous 
need to commit more than an hour of their day.

At least 100m away, (this is important – so that people seeing the grey square have some time 
and space to go “what the hell?” and experience a bit of mystery in their lives before they are 
given an explanation) volunteers give out pamphlets that ONLY say:

The world without art is grey.

At the back of the pamphlet there will be an address to a blog:

http://worldwithoutart.wordpress.com/

This blog will tell them in greater detail what they are seeing and why.

Statistics, information, links to Alliance and other relevant sites etc.

Also, when there might be future grey squares.

(Future grey squares can be in front of performances, theatres, libraries etc and do not have to 
go on all day.)

It will eventually have videos and photos of past grey squares.

This grey square relay will also be documented – videos and photos.

This is not a protest. This is about art and artists taking their place in the world.

How to participate:

We need volunteers to:

- stand in the squares

- hand out pamphlets

- video the event and edit your version of the relay

- take photographs

Call for Submissions:

Eyelevel Galley Main Space Exhibitions

Deadline: October 31st, 2009.

Eyelevel Gallery is seeking proposals for the 2010-2011 exhibition season in our main gallery 
space. We are interested in progressive forward-looking practices; the fruition of innovative ideas; 
inventive approaches to artistic production; and work based on an articulate theoretical and/or 
critical and/or poetic thesis. We encourage and facilitate the presentation of artists and artworks 
that have not been exhibited in this locale. We are enthusiastic about artists' most recent 
endeavors and art forms that propel contemporary production and incite conversation.

Eyelevel Gallery accepts proposals on an ongoing basis. Proposals are reviewed by a peer 
committee once a year and reviews for the upcoming programming year will be accepted until 
October 31, 2009. Eyelevel Gallery pays fees that meet CAR/FAC guidelines. Special projects 
may have other deadlines throughout the year. These will be updated on the website.
