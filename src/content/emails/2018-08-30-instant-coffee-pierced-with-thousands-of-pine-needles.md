N E W S R E L E A S E

--------------------------------------

For Immediate Release
August 27, 2018

Victoria, BC " A multidisciplinary work by Vancouver-based artist David
Khang, La Monte Young Projects, will open at Victorias Open Space
artist-run centre on September 7.

In 1960, avant-garde composer La Monte Young produced Compositions 1960, a
series of ground-breaking conceptual compositions that play with ideas of
performance and audience. One piece, Composition #4, instructs the
performer to turn a butterfly loose in the performance area when the
composition is over, be sure to allow the butterfly to fly away outside.
The score for Composition #7 is two notes: B3 and F#4, with the sole
instruction: to be held for a long time.

Now, over fifty years later, artist David Khang has re-interpreted the
pieces from Compositions 1960 through installation, video, and performance
works to explore what they might mean to us today.

David Khang is a visual, performance, and biological artist whose practice
is informed by education in psychology, theology, dentistry, and law. Since
2003, Khang has used his own body, as well as other entities, such as raw
beef tongue, live monarch butterflies, and a Clydesdale horse, to
facilitate the performance of a non-verbal language and interrogate social
constructions of gender, race, and interspecies relations.

The gallery exhibition for La Monte Young Projects will run at Open Space
from Sept. 7-Oct. 20, 2018.

Join Open Space on Friday, Sept. 7 at 7:00 p.m. for the opening reception,
featuring a performance by David Khang and local musicians at 8:00 p.m.
Khang will also present an artist talk Saturday, Sept. 8 from 2:00 - 4:00 p.m.

Open Space, 510 Fort Street, 2nd floor

Free, but donations are welcome

THE ROAMING PERFORMANCE WITH DAVID KHANGS MARCH OF THE MONARCH

August 30, 2018

Vancouver B.C. " grunt gallery is presenting David Khangs moving
performance, March of the Monarch (How to Fly a Tank), at the end of
August. David Khang creates a new public performance from recurring tropes
in his art " monarch butterflies that camouflage a military soldier and his
bicycle-powered tank.

The public will be invited to form a migration of cyclists and
participate in a butterfly-themed bicycle ride through the city,
accompanying the tank along False Creek, to Granville Island. Accompanied
by the Korean music troupe Tazza, and with projected visual imagery as a
backdrop, the audience will be invited to help release live butterflies. As
part of Khang's ongoing work of being open to change, this multimedia
project looks to engage the audience towards effecting a social metamorphosis.

The performance will be comprised of a procession of bicycles. The meeting
place will be at Science World where the audience will gather, with their
bicycles, before departure. The group will then ride together at a
leisurely pace along the False Creek Bike Path for a total of 30 minutes or
almost 6 kilometres, with many stops along the way. The final destination
will be under the Granville Street Bridge for the main act of the public
performance. Bring your bicycles & your wildest butterfly costumes!
Children welcome!

Meeting Location: Science World at TELUS World of Science, 1455 Quebec St,
Vancouver, BC V6A 3Z7

Departure: 6:15 PM from Science World

Bike Route: We will ride together at a leisurely pace along the False Creek
Bike Path, with stops along the way.

Destination Point: ETA 7 PM Chain And Forge Granville Island, 1402 Anderson
St, Vancouver, BC V6H 4E5 (under Granville Bridge)

Performance: ETA 7:15 " 8:30pm

*Please note that all times are estimated, and subject to variances. The
total length of the bike ride is approximately 5.6 KM (30 minutes).

Different Ways

Lisa Birke and Terrance Houle

August 31 - September 1

10:00am - 9:30pm

Richmond Art Gallery

http://cinevolutionmedia.com/dc2018-featuredartists/

Different Ways is a collaborative project between Terrance Houle, an
interdisciplinary artist and member of the Kainai Nation (Blood Tribe) and
Lisa Birke, settler, video artist, and Assistant Professor of Digital and
Extended Media at the University of Saskatchewan. This project takes to
heart the call to action for all Canadians to begin a conversation"to
listen and to learn"in a bid for reconciliation.

Through ceremony, music, live performance, animation and innovative
projection, the slippery and unsteady understanding of our shared history
and stories will be explored at events and festivals across the country,
demonstrating how dialogue and reclamation can steady the course in
Indigenous and non-Indigenous relations. Live performance will be combined
with projection in a montage of video clips, underwater footage, 360 degree
video and live-capture animation created in community outreach workshops
remixed into an expansive experience. The artistic and poetic vision is one
that will allow the viewer to imagine a better future; a future of coming
together despite, and more importantly because of our different ways.

Co-produced by Cinevolution Media Arts Society and Curator Wynne Palmer, in
partnership with the Richmond Art Gallery.

Far Afield is pleased to partner with the Ranger Station Art Gallery to
present 'Rooted People', a solo exhibition of new work by Carrielynn
Victor. The exhibition explores the interconnectedness of plant and body

understanding of the connections between plants and the human body is
practiced by the St:l in traditional medicine and is also recognized in

ancestral relations.

three-part curatorial project featuring the work of artists Lexie Owen,
Carrielynn Victor, and Lianne Zannier. 'Touchstones' originated as an
investigation into a time capsule that was buried on a farm in Agassiz
thirty years ago. Throughout the month of September, these three artist
projects will address ideas of hidden or embedded knowledge, nature's
intelligence, and systems of value.

<a href="/instant-coffee/listings/2018-08-30-08-screening-dim-cinema-at-the-cinematheque-corin-sworn-the-spe">08. SCREENING | DIM Cinema at The Cinematheque | Corin Sworn: The Spectre of Breath | SEP 5</a>
------------------------------------------------------------------------

Corin Sworn: The Spectre of Breath
Wednesday, September 5, 7:30 pm
DIM Cinema at The Cinematheque

Fascinated by the erratic meetings and schisms of images, artefacts and
histories, Canadian artist Corin Sworn builds her research into scripts,
sometimes as objects and propositions in a gallery, sometimes as characters
and narratives in a film. The script for Lens Prism, a fragmented soliloquy
delivered by an unidentified man to an empty theatre, reflects on a common
paradox of representation: that we express ourselves more readily by
appropriating pre-existing narratives " here from literature, theory, and
film " than by finding the words for our own experiences. Prompted by a
collection of slides taken in Peru thirty years earlier by the artists
father, the script for The Foxes, first shown in the Scottish pavilion at
the 2013 Venice Biennale, leads to a consideration of the legibility of
photographs and the shifting nature of the narratives we create from them.
Finally, the feature-length script for The Coat, made with fellow Emily
Carr University alumnus Tony Romano, uses an ingenious subtitling strategy
to combine the directors response to interviews with migrants in Canada
and Italy with Sworns research on the commedia dellarte, resulting in a
loose adaptation of Aristophanes play The Birds filmed in Calabria.

Lens Prism | Corin Sworn/Great Britain-France 2010. 15 min. DCP
The Foxes | Corin Sworn/Great Britain-Canada-Peru 2013. 18 min. DCP
The Coat | Tony Romano, Corin Sworn/Great Britain-Canada-Italy 2016. 58
min. DCP

<a href="/instant-coffee/listings/2018-08-30-09-audain-gallery-sfu-mfa-graduating-exhibition-2018-sep-6">09. OPENING | Audain Gallery | SFU MFA Graduating Exhibition 2018 | SEP 6</a>
------------------------------------------------------------------------

SFU MFA Graduating Exhibition 2018: WE ALL COME OUT FROM BETWEEN OUR
MOTHER'S LEGS, NO ACTUALLY FROM INSIDE OUR MOTHER'S BODIES, OR WAIT HOLD
ON, WE ALL COME FROM BODIES...

September 6 - 22, 2018
Audain Gallery

Opening Reception
Wednesday, September 5, 2018, 7 - 9PM
Audain Gallery

The exhibition features graduating projects by MFA candidates Stphanie
Gagn and Maj Britt Jensen as well as ongoing research projects by Roxanne
Charles and Krystle Coughlin.

The exhibition of a graduating project represents the culmination of a
candidate's studies, and is presented in partial fulfillment of the
requirements for the degree of Master of Fine Arts.

As artists leaving the institution, we establish a playful and atypical
homage to the women that created us. We joyfully risk using the
overdetermined and even hyperbolic image of the space between our mother's
legs as an analogy provocative enough to hold our diverse work and to bear
relationships between creation, transformation, graduation, and reflection.

The title of our exhibition is taken partly from a comment by Pipilotti
Rist in a 2011 interview:

"We all come out from between our mother's legs. From there that we first
see the light of the world." The title also addresses the shared tendency
in our work to negotiate connections between personal, social and cultural
histories.

Is the gallery unimaginable as a womb?

Here inside, it is dark and warm. We don't have a name yet. No one has
started counting breaths. At this moment, in this place, we all know how to
swim. We are the centre. Although that's not the way it will be.

Now we are given a name!

Leah's daughter, Roxanne Charles focuses on the body as a primary mode of
communication and a vessel of living memory. Her ongoing material and
ritual research embraces many aspects of the celebrations that were once
banned on the lands we stand upon.

Brenda's daughter, Krystle Coughlin uses photography to consider the
ongoing influence of Canadian history on Indigenous presence and erasure.
Her photographs contemplate mundane scenes of interior spaces and domestic
life.

Brigitte's daughter, Stphanie Gagn displays a photographic and sculptural
installation emerging from her archive of family snapshots. In this work,
familiar experiences become strange incidents in which pleasure and
displeasure are difficult to distinguish.

Gertrud's daughter, Maj Britt Jensen presents an assemblage of her
interdisciplinary research on the physical brutality inflicted on female,
transgender and gender-nonconforming bodies. In this architectural
intervention, actual geopolitical events collide with images of
spectacularization.

We celebrate different ways of accessing and exiting this transitional
space. You too will soon be leaving the gallery, so what will you do outside?

Presented with the School for the Contemporary Arts at SFU.

<a href="/instant-coffee/listings/2018-08-30-11-chernoff-fine-art-celestial-bodies-sep-6">11. OPENING | Chernoff Fine Art | Celestial Bodies | SEP 6</a>
------------------------------------------------------------------------

Celestial Bodies - Laura Dutton, Matt Trahan, Chris Lindsay

Chernoff Fine Art
September 6 - October 11th
OPENING RECEPTION: Thursday, September 6th, 6-8pm

The artworks being exhibited in Celestial Bodies are all loosely connected
by either a direct relationship with a heavenly body or a suggestion
thereof. Each of the three artists is abstractly exploring colour and
incorporating an element of chance in the creation of form while using
alternative photographic processes.

Laura Duttons Sunspots & Horizons are a series of camera-less
photographic light experiments created in the colour darkroom using only
light sensitive C-print paper, chemicals, a flashlight, and laser pointer.
They may be most accurately described as light drawings. The process was
a continued attempt, and failure, to draw a perfect form in the center of
the paper while working in complete darkness. As these photographs are
the result of direct mark marking, they are each one-of-a-kind.

Matt Trahans Stool Tops are a series of photographs produced by scanning
stool tops from around the painting studio. They are printed large-scale
with flat backgrounds and start to look a little bit like painterly
interpretations of planets or moons given their shape and the curious
patterns of arbitrary marks.

Chris Lindsay has produced a series of silkscreened prints made from very
long exposures of the sun traveling through the sky using a purpose-built
pinhole camera. The line through each print was formed over the course of
several hours, sometimes interrupted by periods of clouds. The results are
printed in fifteen different hues that match the colours on a colour wheel.

Chernoff Fine Art
265 East 2nd Ave,
Vancouver, BC
V5T 1B8

A full-day workshop introducing participants to the Chromaflex experimental
colour film processing technique, using 16mm motion picture colour film.

Sunday Sep 30; 10.00am - 6.00pm
Cineworks Annex, 235 Alexander Street (driveway entrance),
Vancouver, V6A 1C1
$50-$80

$50 + taxes: Cineworks Members
$65 + taxes: Concessions and Community Partner Members*
$80 + taxes: General Admission

Prices include film stock and chemistry
No prior experience necessary
Instructor: Jack Beatty

REGISTER: https://bit.ly/2J24HVc

This full-day workshop will take participants step by step through the
stunning photo-chemical technique mastered by Australian experimental
filmmakers Richard Tuohy and Dianna Barrie, which allows selectively
controlled negative and positive colour and black and white images on the
same strip of film.

The technique allows us to make selection decisions in full light with the
images visible to the naked eye. Areas of black, clear, colour negative,
colour positive and glorious black and white can be created all within a
single frame of a camera-captured image.

The workshop begins with an introduction to the principles of the process,
and participants will then collaborate on shooting on colour reversal and
colour negative film stock outside, from which a freshly made colour print
will be made. Instead of processing this with conventional industrial
methods, a novel processing sequence is undertaken that will produce an odd
looking black and white image on the film. Participants then select various
areas on that print to end up as positive colour, negative colour, leave
black and white or process as black or clear film. Those areas will be
selectively covered with special chemical blocking tape. Then the film will
be selectively bathed in an assortment of colour chemistry before removing
the tape and bathing in more chemistry.

As well as engaging with a great experimental film technique, this workshop
is a great opportunity to learn in detail how colour film works. All
chemistry and materials are supplied, as well as full access to the
darkroom throughout the workshop. Workshop results will be screened on site
at the end of the session!

Instructor bio
Jack Beatty has been an experimental photographer for almost thirty years
and has extensive knowledge of analog technology. As the control analyst
for Technicolor (formerly Alpha Cine) he has known Cineworks since its
early days and assisted many members with their work. A long-time member of
the organization, he has served on the board several terms as Vice
President. As a Board member and volunteer, Jack has been instrumental in
the development of Cineworks Annex analogue film facilities.
