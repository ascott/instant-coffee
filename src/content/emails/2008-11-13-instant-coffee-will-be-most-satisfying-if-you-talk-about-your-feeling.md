Bio:

Poet and critic Stephen Collis is the author of three books of poetry, Mine
(New Star 2001) and Anarchive (New Star 2005), which was nominated for the
Dorothy Livesay Poetry Prize, and The Commons (Talonbooks 2008). His essays
on contemporary poetry and poetics have appeared in many journals, he has
edited Companions & Horizons: An Anthology of Simon Fraser University
Poetry (WCL 2005), and he is the author of two book-length studies, Phyllis
Webb and the Common Good (Talonbooks 2007) and Through Words of Others:
Susan Howe and Anarcho-Scholasticism (ELS Editions 2006). He teaches
American literature, poetry, and creative writing at Simon Fraser University.

***

coming up next:

Dec 4 - Susan Holbrook

Susan Holbrook is a poet and fiction writer whose first book, misled (1999)
was short listed for the Pat Lowther Memorial Award and the Stephen J.
Stephensson Award. Her long poem chapbook, Good Egg, Bad Seed, was released
in the Spring of 2004. An Associate Professor in the English Department at
the University of Windsor, Holbrook specializes in 20th-century poetry and
poetics in Canada and the U.S. Her critical articles on experimental poetry
appear in such journals as American Literature, differences, Essays on
Canadian Writing, and Open Letter. Her current research projects include
the edited correspondence of Gertrude Stein and Virgil Thomson and a
critical study of Stein in prescient relation to theories of performativity
evolving over the last fifty years. Other interests include women's
autobiography, queer theory, 19th-century American literature, theories of
translation, and creative writing.

Blanket Inc. is pleased to present Picasso’s Pink Vesuvius, a new body of
work by Brett Lund. Opening on Friday November 14th, the exhibition is the
artist’s first solo show with the gallery. Lund received an MFA from Art
Center (Los Angeles, CA) in 2006; he currently lives and works in Los
Angeles, CA. Lund’s work appeared in group exhibitions at galleries
including Patrick Painter Inc. (Los Angeles), Salon 94 (New York), Galerie
Christian Nagel (Berlin) and Foxy Production (New York) among others.
Earlier in 2008, Lund presented a solo exhibition at Daniel Hug Gallery (Los
Angeles) titled Brett Lund’s AlphaVille.

Picasso’s Pink Vesuvius follows Lund’s on-going audit of visual and semantic
morphology rooted in the ideas of early psychoanalysis. Exploring allusions
between art history and collective visual memory, Lund applies abstraction
to visual metaphors for the body to examine female form against its iconic
representations and specific histories. Often investigating universal taboos
in contemporary visual culture Brett’s work affects the beholder both
cerebrally and optically, demanding immediate attention to and understanding
of the work in various socio-historic contexts.

Lund’s sculptures often use the reconfigured body and incorporate geometric
forms commenting on his interest in modernist aesthetics. Being the primary
medium for the artist, Lund’s sculptural works take on a material logic,
referencing funk art and proto-pop of the late 50s albeit with
post-industrial twist. Incorporating shrink wrap, plaster, and fiberglass,
Lund liberates from too literal associations and adds a strong gestural
element to his subjects.

Lund’s two-dimensional work is inclusive of a similar to his sculpture
expressive methodology. The paintings are built out of their own titles,
two-dimensional anagrammatic structures and the use of stencil, offering
intentional linguistic conundrum. Resulting covert, nameless tensions
challenge notions of proximity, distance and the terror of flatness.

Centre A presents Toronto based Japanese-Canadian artist, Louise Noguchi,
in conjunction with WACK! Art and the Feminist Revolution at the
Vancouver Art Gallery. This first solo exhibition of Louise Noguchi in
Vancouver features two major works from her production of the last decade,
including a multi-video installation work entitled The Language of the
Rope, 1999, and her new installation works, Mirror, and Shanghai Dragon
(Moisture Forms), 2008. The Language of the Rope explores her interest in
wild-west performance skills. In the video installation, slow motion sounds
of knives swirling in mid-air and landing with a loud "thud" surround the
viewer. Visually, a number of video screens document different angles of a
traditional knife throwing performance, in which the artist, performing in
the role of the knife thrower's assistant, is attached to a revolving
target. In the new works, Mirror, and Shanghai Dragon, Noguchi examines the
role of the heroic landscape in Hollywood action films. She is interested
in the way these landscapes contribute to the narrative of the heroic
figure. The artist reconstructs artificial landscapes from popular films
such as Superman, Star Wars, Forbidden Planet, Enter the Dragon, the Lady
from Shanghai, using materials different from the original in order to give
the work a new context. Photographs representing the reflection of these
objects in a mirror will prompt viewers to question their own location
within the work. This is reminiscent of the cinematic experience in which
the viewer is an unseen participant in a scene. Overall, the exhibition
presents Noguchi's continuous exploration of social, physical and
psychological boundaries

<a href="/instant-coffee/listings/2008-11-13-06-ministry-of-casual-living-alison-mactaggart-victoria-nov-14">06. Ministry of Casual Living | Alison MacTaggart | Victoria | NOV 14</a>
------------------------------------------------------------------------
Alison MacTaggart
You and I
November 14 - 27, Opening Friday, November 14, 7pm

The Tuning-Fork-Like Apparatus and its embodiments in Prototype A and
Prototype B were developed to solve a particular conundrum: How does one
build an object that enables a person to talk back to another; and an
object, that in doing so, calls attention to both persons and the effects
of their words?

The conundrum in question arises from the permeating residue of real world
encounters in which individuals are subjected to other people's utterances.
Thus, in the real world, an invention that provides a resolution to said
conundrum has many practical applications.

Prototype A and Prototype B are the fruit of two years of rigorous research
and effort to devise satisfactory working examples of the proposed
invention. The exhibition and presentation of Prototype B represents one
potential resolution to the conundrum, which further promises features and
benefits that exceed its origins.

The artist gratefully acknowledges the support of the BC Arts Council.

Jeff Otto O'Brien is a Vancouver-based documentary photographer. He
photographs the everyday, and often mundane interactions between humans and
the environment.

Investigating how they each influence one another, O'Brien's work comments
upon how each affects one another and the subsequent synthesis created
through the captured photograph.

Despite this photography being of a conceptual nature, O'Brien maintains a
street photography ethic: he takes photographs that are often relatively
spontaneous, and of subjects that he ultimately finds interesting.

This new collection of photographs examines the interplay between humans
and the constructed environment.

ScareCity is a cinematic performance that fuses personally archived film
and video with live improvised storytelling and music. Berton’s
first-person recount of her return home to rural Southwestern Ontario to
care for her aging parents meshes with an emotionally charged personal
archive of childhood films, a time lapse of subdivision development around
her home, country landscapes, and portraits of economic castaways at the
local drinking hole. Her poignant reflections on aging and memory transform
into metaphors on the dwindling fuel economy, agriculture’s increasingly
violent use of antibiotics and pesticides, and the pandemic loss of wild
and rural land to urban sprawl. St. Aubin’s original live music score adds
lyricism and punctuation to Berton’s memories, often sustaining their
emotional reverberations long after their words are uttered. The Legend of
Buck Kelly tracks Berton’s obsession with the late Jeff Buckley across the
terrain of her own love life; from New York where she first met him, to her
home town of Dawson City where she imagines he went after faking his death.
She illustrates the serendipity that linked her to Buckley- from the
wilderness to the big city to boy friends with the names Buck and Kelly.

Instant Coffee Project for the Main Street Urban Transportation Showcase
Public Art Program Instant Coffee is an internationally recognized artist
collective based in Toronto and Vancouver. The collective developed, in
part, as a response to the division and exaggerated difference between
studio and exhibition practice. Through formal installations and
event-based activities, it builds a public place to practice, where ideas,
materials and actions can be explored outside of the isolated studio and in
a manner that renegotiates traditional exhibition structures, but is
still supported by them. For Instant Coffee, the triad of art practice
production, presentation and reception becomes jumbled, incorporating the
social as a priority.
Instant Coffee will discuss their newest project, due to launch in November
and running through to April, 2009. The first of a series of projects to
activate both the transit and civic infrastructure of Main Street, it
involves temporary and permanent artworks including exterior bus wraps and
panels, interior ad cards, and street signage. The project is supported by
TransLink, Transport Canad's Urban Transportation Showcase Program and the
City of Vancouver. Instant Coffee's most consistent members are Cecilia
Berkovic, Jinhan Ko, Kelly Lycan, Jenifer Papararo, and Kate Monro, with
the recent addition of Khan Lee. Instant Coffee has presented numerous
projects for public spaces in Canada, Europe, and the U.S. Learn more about
Instant Coffee at www.instantcoffee.org.

Marko Simcic
Park, a project for the Ontario Street Greenway Marko Simcic's Park will
activate the entire length of Ontario Street with a program of moveable
artworks beginning in November, 2008. Commissioned by the City of Vancouver
Public Art Program, Park involved extensive community consultations and
design reviews over a number of years, which informed the final forms and
method of delivery of the work. Park will consist of sculptural objects,
each approximately the size and weight of a small automobile. They will
temporarily rest in curb lane parking spots, and will be relocated from
time to time to various addresses along the route. Whether adjacent to
public sites, such as schools or parks, commercial properties to the north,
or permitted to occupy residential parking through their adoptionby

our most symbolic public space, is regulated. Simcic will discuss the

Gallery. Marko Simcic is an independent architect and artist with a history
of sited artwork commissions with the cities of Richmond and Vancouver.

found in social, spatial, political, temporal, bureaucratic and material
conditions. His architectural work has included several community projects
such as the Grace Church in Vancouver, the Cates Hill Church on Bowen
Island and several Gulf Island retreats. He has been a mentor for the
Architecture Institute of BC, and a thesis advisor and instructor at the
University of British Columbia. Awards include the Abe Roganick award
(1990) and a Canadian Architect award (2003) and a Lieutenant Governor's
Award in Architecture (2008). Recent public art projects include Light
Ring, a collaborative, sited work in Richmond. Future Speakers Our 2009

<a href="/instant-coffee/listings/2008-11-13-13-cineworks-after-school-special-nov-18">13. Cineworks | AFTER SCHOOL SPECIAL | NOV 18</a>
------------------------------------------------------------------------
AFTER SCHOOL SPECIAL

School, work, school. Alex Bag, Kika Thorne, Elisabeth Subrin. Cineworks
Independent Filmmakers Society presents After School Special, three videos
from the late nineties as an attempt to flesh out the gap between the
WACK! show at the Vancouver Art Gallery and contemporary feminist art
practice.

With music by Peaches, Kika Thorne's Work stars real life artist Shary
Boyle who plays an artist in a documentary that is not a documentary.

In Untitled Fall '95, Bag, the art student, "plays" Bag the art student.
Through a series of deadpan performances, interspersed with fragments of
pop detritus, Bjork, Hello Kitty, Killer Bunnies, Alex Bag successfully
maintains television's banal, static and endearing ineptitudes.

A cinematic doppelganger without precedent, shot for shot, Elisabeth
Subrin's Shulie reenacts a 1967 documentary portrait of a young Chicago
art student, who a few years later would become a notable figure in Second
Wave feminism and author of the radical 1970 manifesto, The Dialectic of
Sex: The Case for Feminist Revolution. Not a clone in the end, but a
brilliant rethinking of history. Subrin makes manifest the eternal return

KARIN BUBAS - Valley of the Heart's Delight
November 20th - Year end

Opening Reception: Thursday, November 20th, 6-8pm
MONTE CLARK GALLERY
2339 Granville Street

The Monte Clark Gallery is pleased to announce the solo exhibition
"Valley of the Heart's Delight" from Vancouver-based artist Karin
Bubas.

Partially inspired by early Technicolor films and hand-tinted
photographs, Bubas' large-scale digital photographs are presented as
subtly orchestrated scenes of colour and light. Each of the five
images presents for the viewer a carefully costumed figure in a
reminiscent landscape. The result is a mysterious and story-like
dynamic between the setting and the individual. Taken on a panoramic
camera, two of the images explore this format in a horizontal
configuration emblematic of the landscape genre, and for three Bubas
has aligned her images in a vertical format to dramatic effect.

The inherently cinematic narratives are derived from archetypal myths
and stories, creating for the viewer imagery both ominous and
enchanting, the figures appearing as apparitions in their mystical
settings. As Bubas carefully stages these five tableaux, allusions of
myths and new romanticism come together with cinematic sensitivity.
The end result is a series of supernatural costume scenes reminiscent
of Waterhouse's Pre-Raphaelite paintings as well as the films of
Alfred Hitchcock.

Karin Bubas was born in North Vancouver and currently lives and works
in Vancouver, BC Canada. Since studying at the Emily Carr Institute of
Art and Design, her work has been exhibited nationally and
internationally.

industrial

market

Nick Danziger: Blair at War
November 24, 2008 - February 14, 2009
Teck Gallery, SFU Vancouver Campus
Artist talk on Afghanistan: Thursday, November 27, 7pm, SFU Vancouver
Artist walking tour of Blair at War: Saturday, November 29, 3pm, Teck
Gallery

Ever wondered what decision-making looks like when the participants know
that human lives will hang in the balance? This exhibition of photographs by
Nick Danziger is the result of a seemingly uncontroversial assignment to
document then-prime minister Tony Blair's 50th birthday for The Times
Magazine. Global events forced a metamorphosis of the assignment ­ it became
a month-long study of a British prime minister deliberating the pros and
cons of participating in the war in Iraq, during which Danziger, and his
collaborator on the project, Times Literary Supplement editor Peter
Stothard, had round-the-clock access to Blair's inner circle, often
wondering why they were not asked to leave.

The text panels in the show document who said what and are a record of the
process of the UK government assimilating information that often proved to
be unreliable. There is a film noir quality to the pictures even though the
scenes of dialogue look more like everyday considerations of whether to
order this or that type of wine for dinner.

Five years after the invasion of Iraq, these striking photographs do what
photography often does best ­ provide privileged access to inaccessible
people, events, and processes.
