ROLLING SUBMISSION

Call for Applications: Similkameen Artist
Residency (SAR)

The newly launched Similkameen Artist Residency (SAR) is calling for applications
of interest for Artist Residents for one, two, or three-month residencies.

Deadline: We are accepting rolling applications for immediate availability of July
2023 through the end of 2024 until our spots are filled.

We currently have availability still in 2023 for July, August, September, and
December.

Please visit our website for application instructions:

simartres.com

About the Similkameen Artist Residency (SAR) :

Located near the Similkameen River, far away from the hustle of the big city, the
Similkameen Artist Residency is housed in a large newly renovated log building
surrounded by sage covered hills and provides an affordable, focused environment
for artistic development and exploration. The project space was initiated in the spring
of 2021 and offers artists the unique opportunity to live, work and cultivate new
ideas, while residing in the Southern Okanagan region of
British Columbia.
Enhanced by an ethos of community, curiosity, and creative exchange, our vision is
to create an engaging and supportive environment that fosters both productive solo
studio time with enriching group dynamics. As the residency is a communal
environment, we welcome artists who value collaborative and community practices.

Qualifications:

SAR welcomes emerging, mid-career, or seasoned artists and curators from across
Canada and internationally. As this is a self-directed residency, we encourage
applicants from a wide variety of artistic disciplines and backgrounds to apply. We
are seeking artists and curators with a BFA, MFA, or equivalent experience, who can
demonstrate a strong and committed practice.

We are ideal for a range of practices such as writing, sound art, film, video, digital
art, textiles, painting, drawing, photography, sculpture (with simple electric or hand
tools) and DIY printmaking. We are not suited for practices that require machinery,
welding,
kilns, access to a lot of water or an industrial sink, or practices with loud
noises or strong smells that infringe on the work of other artists working in adjoining
spaces.

For program guidelines, FAQs, and application instructions, please visit our website
at:

simartres.com

For questions or individual application support, please contact the SAR Admin
Coordinator at:

sar.info@simartres.com

We acknowledge that the residency rests upon the traditional, ancestral and
unceded territories of the Syilx Okanagan Nation, and are thankful to be able to
reside and create here.

Morning Sun Garden
Aracha Cholitgul
June 15 - August 5, 2023

nap is pleased to present new works by Thai artist Aracha Cholitgul in her Canadian
debut solo exhibition, Morning Sun Garden. Through considering the ecology
of the
house of which nap gallery is located in and its many human and non-human
relationships, Morning Sun Garden becomes an exploration into complex desires
that arise from loneliness. Guided by intimately honest conversations between the
artist and the resident of nap on personal dilemmas surrounding endings, pets, and
companionship, the gallery is transformed into an offering for all neighborhood cats
who regularly visit, but also an invitation to consider the value of human/non-human
relationships, the complex dynamics of ownership, and of human/non-human needs
and of being needed. Featuring a giant litter box, a selection of drawings depicting
plants preferred by cats such as Thyrsostachys siamensis (otherwise known as
Gamble Cat Bamboo), a video piece, and sculptures made with cats enjoyment in
mind, Arachas visual sensitivity comes together through Morning Sun Garden,
becoming an expression of longing across distances.

Aracha Cholitgul was born in 1988 in Bangkok, Thailand
(currently living and working
on Koh Phangan.) She has been writing, drawing, painting, and telling stories
relating to obscure feelings. Cholitgul has had high myopia since she was young (her
mother took her to see an eye doctor because she always walked into things.) As a
result, the idea of blurriness and uncertainty interests her. Her project is often an
attempt to observe and explore what goes on in the mental sphere.

Cholitgul received her Bachelor of Fine Arts from the Chulalongkorn University,
Bangkok, Thailand in 2010 with a concentration in ceramics. Cholitguls selected
solo exhibitions include The Study of A Long Distance Relationship (2021) and
Museum of The Swamp Eels (2018), both at Nova Contemporary, Bangkok,
Thailand. Her works have been exhibited in a number of galleries and institutions
mainly throughout Asia such as C U R RENT, UP at Vargas Museum, Manila,
Philippines (2022); PLANT TIME at 69 Campus Art Center, Beijing, China (2021);
They Have Brought Erasers With
Them at A+ Works of Art, Kuala Lumpur, Malaysia
(2020); Long March Project: The Deficit Faction at Long March Space, Beijing, China
(2019); Tree of Life, a solo presentation at ART021 Shanghai Contemporary Art Fair,
Shanghai, China (2019); The Last Two Digits at graw bckler garage, Berlin,
Germany (2019); and Art Jakarta with Nova Contemporary, Jakarta, Indonesia
(2019).

Please note the gallery is inside a home. Email haveanapinfo@gmail.com or dm
@haveanap.info on Instagram for the gallery address and accessibility information.

Juli Majer and Vladimir Majer: Sandbox
June 17 " July 22

Drawing from the pedagogy of sand play therapy and the embodied means by which
familial histories influence individual art practices, Juli Majer invokes themes of grief,
longing, remembrance and tradition by constructing intimate artifacts that act as
tributes in a posthumous collaboration with her late father, Vladimir Majer.

Artist Biography
Juli Majer is a sculptor and Illustrator living and working on the unceded lands of the
xwmkwy"m (Musqueam), Swxw7mesh (Squamish), and sl"ilwta (Tsleil-
Waututh) peoples also known as Vancouver, BC.

In their practice, Majer explores the ontology of materiality by investigating the
interrelations between 2D and 3D art objects and the possibilities that emerge when
2D art objects are transformed into 3D art objects and vice versa.

Thematically, Majers practice heavily incorporates the act of world-building. Majer is
profoundly inspired by the writings of Octavia Butler. Like Butler, Majer attempts to
utilize world-building as a form of escapism while also providing commentary on the
individual and societal state of the human experience.

Majers works fuses elements of play and mischief to further build on the
whimsical
aspect of her practice and, in an effort to utilize art production and encounters with
art as tools to problematize societal notions surrounding mental health and
encourage the creation of individual worlds within our own.

Gallery Gachet is located in the Downtown Eastside of Vancouver on the unceded
and occupied territories of the xmkwy"m (Musqueam), Skwxw7mesh
(Squamish) and Sl"lwta (Tsleil-Waututh) Nations. Gallery Gachet has a mandate
to support artists and offer art programs addressing mental health and socio-political
marginalization, while promoting art as a means for survival, cultural participation,
and human rights.

West Vancouver Art Museum | ORDER FROM CHAOS: JANE ADAMS AND B.C.
BINNING| June 21 - September 23

This exhibition explores the art of B.C. Binning and Jane Adams, and how the former
influenced the work of the latter. Adams is the daughter of Jean Coulthard, the
Canadian composer, and Don Adams, who ran a modern Scandinavian furniture store
in Vancouver. The Adams were close friends with B.C. Binning and his wife, Jessie. As
a child, Adams visited Binnings studio and became familiar with his paintings and
designs. As she developed her own artistic practice, she came to recognize the impact
of this early influence. This exhibition includes works by Binning from the Collection of
the West Vancouver Art Museum.
https://www.westvancouverartmuseum.ca/exhibitions/order-chaos-jane-adams-and-bc-
binning.

What Is Welcome? Exhibition (June 23-August 13, 2023)
Opening Reception: Thursday, June 22 from 6 to 8 pm
Join us for the opening reception of What Is Welcome? on Thursday, June 22 at 6
pm.
What Is Welcome? includes works from the Belkin's collection and long-term
residency that question the art institution's language, boundaries and potential for
change. From performance to works-in-process that effect institutional practices, the
artists included operate with, and at the same time counter, the institution to address
the what, how and the why of gallery operations.

As galleries and museums strive to make
space for new practices and ways of
knowing, artists are critical agents in the institution's evolution. In interrogating
institutional cultural systems, the artists included consider sustaining relevance in the
material of living ecologies through exchange, labour and stewardship. Collectively,
the works ask the question: What is welcome?

Artists include Allyson Clay, Claudia Cuesta, Andrea Fraser, ReMatriate Collective
and Holly Schmidt, as well as recent acquisitions of work by Skeena Reece, Kika
Thorne and Tania Willard as part of the Belkin's ongoing commitment to
diversification of the collection.

What Is Welcome? is curated by Barbara Cole and Melanie O'Brian and made
possible with the generous support of the Canada Council for the Arts, the Province
of British Columbia through the BC Arts Council, our Belkin Curators Forum
members, and our individual donors who financially support our acquisitions and
donate artworks to the collection.

Now Representing

Alex Tedlie-Stursberg

Monte Clark is pleased to announce the representation of Alex
Tedlie-Stursberg.

Stursberg is known for his sculptures and installations, which utilize found objects
and natural materials scavenged from the shoreline and urban landscape. His
material approach is inherent to a West Coast counterculture vernacular, while also
referencing the European aesthetic traditions of Dada, CoBrA, Nouveau Ralisme
and Arte Povera. Stursbergs bricolage sculptures, tableaus and assemblages
incorporate materials such as stones, coins and bottle caps as a means to explore
time, the value of materials and the ever-shifting meaning of objects. Degraded
styrofoam, salvaged from tire flotsams washed ashore are transformed into gleaming
bronze monuments to a modern existence marked by our debris. Bisected basalt or
granite stones reveal glittering automotive paint, reflective of muscle cars and
American guitars, associated with Southern California during the 1960s. Stursbergs
usage of detritus is subject to a material alchemy"from trash to brass, the mundane
to the
mythological, transforming our expectations of and in relationship to discarded
materials.

Stursberg will open a solo exhibition of his work at Monte Clark, Alex Tedlie-
Stursberg: Only the Stones Remain, on view July 1st through July 29th, 2023.
