Contemporary Art Gallery

Lawrence Paul Yuxweluptun

Neo-Native Drawings and Other Works

Guest curated by Petra Watson

March 19 - May 16, 2010

Opening reception: Thursday March 18, 2010, 6-9pm

Lawrence Paul Yuxweluptun refers to his drawings as preliminary studies
serving as background work and the measuring-stick for developing the forms
and ideas that have come to identify his style and distinguish his
pictorial inventiveness. Neo-Native Drawings and Other Works features three
decades of drawings extending from 1980 to 2009. The most recent tree
studies (2004 - 2009), as well as ovoid portraits (2002 - 2005), figurative
works (1985 - 2009), etchings (1993 - 2009), watercolours (1980 -1993),
and a number of sketchbooks comprise the first exhibition to focus on
Yuxweluptun's works on paper.

The figures of Coast Salish cosmology that frequent these works explore the
natural world as animate. By displaying indigenous forms of knowledge and
power that anchor indigenous culture to the land, these works on paper
scrutinize the course of actions that are destroying the natural world.
This strategy, which places aesthetic exploration against an ethical crises
that demands recording and change, is inseparable from the impact of
racism, beginning with early settlement and colonialism and continuing
forward within social and political regimes of oppression towards
indigenous people.

Lawrence Paul Yuxweluptun is best known for his large scale painting, which
he has exhibited nationally and internationally since graduating from The
Emily Carr Institute of Art and Design in 1983. Solo exhibitions include
Lawrence Paul Yuxweluptun: Colour Zone, Plug In ICA, Winnipeg (2001) and
Lawrence Paul Yuxweluptun: Born to Live and Die on Your Colonialist
Reservation, Morris and Helen Belkin Art Gallery, Vancouver (1995). Recent
group exhibitions include Challenging Traditions, McMichael Canadian Art
Collection, Kleinberg, Ontario (2009); Transporters: Contemporary Salish
Art, Art Gallery of Greater Victoria (2008); and Beyond Beads and Feathers:
Recent Work by Six Contemporary Native American Artists, Portland Art
Museum, Oregon (2002).
