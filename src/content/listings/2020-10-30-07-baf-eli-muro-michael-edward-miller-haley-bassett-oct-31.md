Two of the shows were produced by the latest participants
in BAFs Residency Program,
Michael Edward Miller and Eli Muro. During the program, the two artists worked side-by-
side in the two studios at BAFs purpose-designed facility in the False Creek Flats. Also
opening is new work from Haley Bassett in BAF's Garage; this street facing exhibition
window displays art to the public 24 hours a day and showcases early-career artists.

The show opens on Saturday, October 31 and closes on Saturday, December 19. The
show is free to the public during BAF Gallerys opening hours of Tuesday to Saturday,
noon to five.

With Michael Edward Miller's new site-specific installation, 'We were once here', he finds
a way to make interactive art COVID-safe: through light and shadow. Using pigments
that both absorb and emit light, he creates a blank, glowing stage for the viewer to
creatively project upon. Informed by a childhood spent feeling safer and more
empowered by the virtual realm than the material one, the new work brings the comfort
of virtuality into
meatspace.

In 'Genuine Fakes', Eli Muro investigates the nature of our reality using the very same
tools with which we construct it. Equal parts witty and dark"and often both
simultaneously"the exhibition points with one hand to technologies of hope, humour,
and possibility, and with the other to tools of social control, fulcrums of power, and
surveillance capitalism.

Haley Bassett's 'Snare' is an abstract, three-dimensional drawing created from
blackberry canes and steel wire. From a distance, the blackberry creates a composition
of brilliant red, sweeping marks in space. However, closer inspection reveals the thorny
and sinister aspect of these materials. 'Snare' continues Bassett's investigation into the
nature of trauma; she has chosen to use blackberry because of its aggressive and
invasive nature.
