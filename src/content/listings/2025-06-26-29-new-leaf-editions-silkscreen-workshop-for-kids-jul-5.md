August 19th, 2025

New Leaf Editions are excited to present a new show by Monique Martin.

Utilizing recycled and found materials, she will install 100 silk screened panels hung
from a fish net. This show was made possible by funding from the Canadian Ocean
Literacy Coalition.

Please stop by anytime between 12 to 6 PM on Saturday July 5th for a free
silkscreen workshop for kids!

All materials will be provided.

Location 3-1244 Cartwright St. Granville Island, Vancouver

Artist Statement

Our nascent attempts to change fate and reduce plastic currently in our environment
will never keep up with what is already in the world and being added daily. The
challenge is to discuss plastics to address what happened, but rather how and why it
happens so we can reduce the impact. Plastics in the ocean and on land are broken
down and have become smaller than the human eye can see and they are omni-
present, including inside our bodies.

The evincing nature of taking action creates more action by others. Our true worth
can be measured in how we help others and the planet. It is much better to
understand the challenges that plastics bring to our world than to accuse. The
impact of plastics is ubiquitous, perennial, never ending and a challenge for this and
the next generation. Everything we see and think about is filtered and sifted through
our memories and as I sifted sand on a beach collecting plastics for this project, it
was heartbreaking, tragic and something inside me broke. All tragedies, at least in
literature, have an ending, this plight on the environment will not end. I will never be
the same after creating this work. There are solutions and I will not despair as the
next time I am at a beach or walking on a city street I will pick up the plastics I see. I
am too small to change the big problems but this big problem is not going to change
me, I will still move forward in hope for solutions to the plastic waste.

Every sentence spoken to educate about plastics and every action taken to remove
plastics from our world makes a difference. Is it possible to normalize the act of
picking plastic from the beach rather than seashells?
