Exhibition Opening: 
Thursday, November 22
7-10 pm

Please join us for the launch of the first solo exhibition in North America of 
Turner Prize nominee, Nathan Coley. This major presentation includes 
Unnamed (2012), a new commission in the gallery and We Must Cultivate Our 
Garden, installed on the roof of the Pennsylvania Hotel in the Downtown East 
Side.

Unnamed forms the centerpiece to the exhibition, over 30 recycled 
headstones informally gathered together, supported on stout cedar batons. 
These ‘ready-made’ objects produce a powerful presence resonating with 
Coley’s ongoing investigations as to how our environment speaks of collective 
desires and beliefs through its embodiment of social histories.

Offsite project

Nathan Coley
We Must Cultivate Our Garden
The Pennsylvania Hotel,
Carrall Street at East Hastings Street

Presented off-site We Must Cultivate Our Garden is a large-scale illuminated 
text work held aloft on a scaffolding structure. Taken from the last line of 
Candide by Voltaire, the statement is powerful and complex; the use of the 
plural ‘we’ is inclusive, ‘must’ suggests an imperative and similarly ‘cultivate’ 
and ‘garden’ evoke metaphorical weight. We can cultivate our minds, our 
souls, our relationships as well as the soil.

Artist talk

Wednesday, Nov 21, 7pm
Emily Carr University
of Art + Design
Room 301, 1399 Johnston St, Granville Island

Coley will discuss his practice revolving around investigations into the social 
aspects of our built environment. Working across a diverse range of media, he 
explores how architecture comes to be invested — and reinvested — with 
meaning, and reveals that claims to public space in postmodern society are 
made by groups of people who have different ideas on how it should be used; 
the structures they erect manifest these desires, values and beliefs.

Coley lives and works in Glasgow, Scotland and was shortlisted for the 2007 
Turner Prize. He has exhibited internationally including The Australian Centre 
for Contemporary Art, Melbourne; The National Gallery of Modern Art, 
Edinburgh; Bergen Kunsthall, Norway; Tate Britain and Centre d’Art 
Contemporain, Paris. He is represented by Haunch of Venison in London and 
New York.
