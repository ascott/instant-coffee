ATMOSPHERIC FEEDBACK LOOPS, 2017, film, 17 min.
Susan Schuppli
The Cabinet, SFU School for the Contemporary Arts, Woodwards Building, 149 W.
Hastings St. Vancouver, Room 4390 (4th floor)
Sept. 3 to Oct. 31, 2025
Artist talk: Sept. 3, 4:30 pm, Opening Reception 5:30 pm Hours: Monday to Friday 8
am to 8 pm, Sat. 12 to 5 pm
Atmospheric Feedback Loops explores an open-air laboratory, a tall tower in the
midst of flat rural fields south of Amsterdam. The tower records the atmospheric
frequencies of nature to measure and monitor the changes taking place in the
feedback loops between land, sea and air. Since 1970 the Cabauw Experimental
Site for Atmospheric Research (CESAR) has been studying the ways in which the
complex behaviour of clouds, aerosols, radiation, and precipitation interact with
major land reclamation projects, global warming, and other terrestrial events.
According to the evidence collected at the site, the unique atmospheric properties of
light in the Netherlands, its refractive shimmer"famously captured in the 17th
century Dutch landscape paintings by Jacob van Ruisdael, among others"has been
lost due to the diminished number of water molecules carried inland by prevailing
winds.
Susan Schuppli will give a talk on her new research on the cryosphere and the
politics of cold, Cold Rights in a Burning World, in Room 4390, SFU, School for the
Contemporary Arts, from 4:30 " 5:30 pm. All welcome.

BIOGRAPHY
Susan Schuppli is Director of the Centre for Research Architecture, Goldsmiths,
University of London, and a Forensic Architecture Fellow and Advisory Board Chair.
Her artistic work has been exhibited at galleries and museums internationally.
Schupplis monograph Material Witness: Media, Forensics, Evidence was published
by MIT Press in 2020.
