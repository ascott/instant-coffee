How The Wet Sock Flower Revealed Itself
Sarah Davidson, Simone Jarvis, and Sonja Ratkay
closing reception: Dec. 27, 6-8pm

Dynamo Arts Association
Suite 103 - 30 East 6th Ave.

The wet sock flower gives direction, conducting a lyrical and lively dance across the 
stage. Still calling from its deflated horn it signals a movement. It narrates our stories 
offering suggestions and subtle indications. Perhaps we move this way or that way, 
perhaps we dont move at all. It either gets noticed or neglected, its calls are not 
always seen or heard. Moments of dissonance misunderstood or tremendous 
moments of harmony and empathy are what it offers. Currently it hangs from above, 
dry and draped over a wire. It began to play a part in the story after bellowing from 
its authoritative position above... Stay away from red it said. 

Delineation is about boundaries but in these works, boundaries are shown to be 
porous, confusing. Blobs suggest bodily limits, but shapes oscillate between the 
recognizable and the formal. This shifting recognizability is one in a series of visual 
tricks which these artists gestures perform, in their efforts to entangle viewing in the 
discourse of understanding. Their approaches overlap in using fragmentary shapes 
and gestures to embody relations between nested systems of bodies, organisms, 
and language. Certain types of shape-shifting can be recognized in depictions of 
similar forms, and where vocabularies differ they also converse: in Sonja Ratkays 
work, language and bodies are mutually suggestive, in Simone Jarvis, spaces which 
conflate micro and macro sketch out an ecosystem of gestures, in Sarah Davidsons, 
creatures and space collapse in upon one another, suggesting a porous 
biomorphism.

SARAH DAVIDSON works between drawing and painting to create compositions in 
which shadowy, biomorphic figures and delicate, foliated fragments mingle. Critters 
and space collapse in upon one another, suggesting a permeable web. Both the eye 
and the mind work towards the known--animals, plants, brush marks, lines--but are 
caught in a space of undoing. Making reference to a history of discourses 
constructing the natural world, her works investigate bodies, nature, environment 
and the tangled strings which often bind them together.

In SIMONE JARVIS work, formal and material qualities of painting - tone, color, and 
form - dictate spatial dispositions and relationships that occur within. The paintings 
forms and gestures reference partially recognizable objects and life forms; from flora 
to urban structures and domestic objects. Their unknown origins render these forms 
expatriate subjects, creating a new realm to exist in. Loose and thin brushstrokes 
paired with bold colors and hazy monochromatic hues form a depth of field that 
draws a gaze into the field yet returns it to the surface with primed areas and 
brushstrokes, creating a constant play between the interiority and exteriority of the 
picture plane. 

SONJA RATKAY is a practicing artist and poet. Through reliefs and paintings filled 
with poems, diagram-like drawings, and immersive installations, she researches 
topics of language and the body, forming of thoughts through known and unknown 
symbols, intuition, haunting and the haunted, states of being, and environmental 
triggers which lead to psychological transformation.
