Teplin gives us a peep show, with all its seductive promises, to the 
history of painting. She uses the language of painting as colour, and colour 
as form with out linear representation. In doing so, Teplin arrives at a 
space that brings together the vibration, the artificiality, the flatness of 
space/depth of field, and the observation of movement of colour in nature. 
There is no one system in place, but a conflation of moments, brought 
together to signify the feminine, the fantasy, and the subterfuges of the 
contemporary modern.

Alexis is currently teaching in the MFA program at the Art Centre College of 
Design in Pasadena. In September, she will be presenting an artists project 
at the Serpentine Public Pavilion in London. She currently shows with Hotel,

Frank Ramirez's new photo-based works involve a unique way of seeing
nature that incorporates aspects of pareidolia and the perspectives of
birds and other animals.

Frank started out in neo-dada, performance, photo-art, fiction, and
sonic experimentation. He moved over to music in the late 1970s,
fronting the legendary rock band UJ3RK5, which included local
luminaries such as Jeff Wall, Bill Gibson, Rodney Graham, Ian Wallace,
and others. Frank moved to Europe in 1980, and worked at teaching,
electronic music, and hosting an underground radio show featuring new
and live music heard in Germany, France and Switzerland.

While on holiday at Tofino in 1991, a friend loaned him a camera to
photograph natural phenomena there. The images seemed enigmatic and a
little "strange" in the central European context and were compounded
into tight triptychs. Further landscape works involved locations such
as CadaquÃ©s and the Balearics in Spain.

Frank returned to Vancouver a few years ago and eventually applied his
vision to the Gulf Islands and the parks of Strathcona: a new kind of
landscape photography influenced by the "isms" of art history and the
desire to see man as a part of nature.
