The Vancouver School of Art Presents:

NO PLATEAU: A NEW DECADE IN PAINTING

February 10th-20th, 2010
Opening reception Wednesday, February 10th, 6-9pm

Co-presented with WOO Gallery

VSA Gallery: Room 410 South Building & WOO Gallery: Room 145 North Building
Emily Carr University

TIFFAN BREEN MARK BUTLER, IAN EDMONDS, LIZ JACKSON, BRIAN KOKOSKA
RUPERT NUTTLE, MITCH SPEED, ANGELA TENG

We define the past through monuments of culture, decades lumped into
perfect packages easily referenced when need be. At the end of a
decade, much like the end of a long, late night of partying, we
cleanse, chugging lemon water and cyan pepper we wonder "What just
happened?" We intend to start fresh, remove the toxins and build-up
from the night before no matter how ludicrous the methods may be.
Let's Forgive and forget, or is it forget and forgive? Shitting out
the naughts of the previous decade, we take on the next a little more
light in the loafers, a little more ambiguous and a bit more
charismatic.

The recent arrival of the iPad can be thought of as one of many new
and shiny stones leading into our new century's teen years.
Technology's rapid development is no news, but the way in which it
defines our exponential future deserves consideration: a constant flux
of information, the speed of it's transfer, the nebulous stagnating
sea, are we pushing our selves off a cliff or up a mountain? As theory
suggests the Earth is being flung into (cyber)space, ever expanding,
growing and building on the past, if theory is right then how soon is
now to etch the graffiti into the bus shelter of the twenty-teens?
Ever since humans could gaze back into the primordial pool and
consider our own reflection we have been constantly defining
ourselves, we've been there and bought the t-shirt. So what's next on
the agenda, and where can we get the new shirt?

For the exhibition NO PLATEAU: A NEW DECADE IN PAINTING, the
collaborative curatorial team of VAN & Associates has considered the
history-burdened field of painting. Instead of continuing in an
endless cycle of death and ressurection, where the new is championed
over the old, we don't kill the father, we divorce him. Using the
format of the typical painting group show in which many painters are
shown with no commonality other than artist's chosen medium, NO
PLATEAU draws attention to the artists' insistence to navigate the
process of making a painting, developing in practice a model for
thought.

No style brings this work together, no slogans are proposed, nor
t-shirts to bear them. What these artists share is a knowing kind of
making, knowing of the past, boldly going where many have gone before.
Art has been digested by history into the phenomon of movements lumped
into decades: brackets of time in which favour was bestowed or
withdrawn. Looking around us, we see no consistancy, no movement
towards a common goal. While the present in its pluriformity may be
all the more difficult to summarize. Perhaps such an
old-non-progressive-non-technologically-advanced medium such as
painting stays open for a slower, more individual conversation.
Painting is as permeable to influence as any other medium, compiling
resources from the pool of information history, on consignment. There
is no high to come down from, no urge to make it new. Instead there is
a continual inquiry, a physical process in which the language of each
artist's commitment emerges; pulling the rigging, saying afloat,
facing the perfect storm. Without the burden of progress, without one
trajectory or another, the work stays in the present, out on a limb,
to be continued.

Curated by VAN & Associates
