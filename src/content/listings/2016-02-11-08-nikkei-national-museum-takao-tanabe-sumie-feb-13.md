Sumie is often translated as brush painting, ink wash painting, or Japanese
ink painting. In 1959, funded in part by a Canada Council artist grant,
Canadian-born painter Takao Tanabe visited Japan for the very first time.
For two years he studied with sumie and calligraphy masters in Tokyo.
Although celebrated across Canada for his seminal landscapes with low
horizons, primarily focusing on the west coast of Canada, Tanabe's sumie
paintings display a confident delicacy of technique, exploring both
traditional Japanese subject matter and abstraction.

This is the first extensive display of Tanabe's sumie works. The exhibit
and companion catalogue were made possible by generous private donations
and support from the BC Arts Council, Province of BC, and the Deux Mille
Foundation. We thank the Vancouver Asian Heritage Month Society for
additional marketing support.

Nikkei National Museum

The artist will give a brief overview of his time in Japan as an
