E.J. Bellocq
Storyville Portraits
May 10	June 14, 2008
SFU Gallery, Burnaby Campus

Film screening/Panel Discussion/Opening: Saturday, May 10 from 2 to 5pm
Lunchtime talks: 12:05 and 12:35pm on May 27, 28, 29

E.J. Bellocq¹s Storyville Portraits, made in the eponymous red-light district in New Orleans around 1912, are known the world over but have never before been exhibited in Vancouver. The SFU Gallery will show 49 of the 89 Storyville images. The prints were made by the American photographer Lee Friedlander, from glass plate negatives discovered after Bellocq¹s death in
1949. Ever since the first exhibition of the Portraits at the Museum of Modern Art in 1970, Bellocq¹s insider view of Storyville¹s brothels has been praised by many for its humanity, and criticized by others for its perceived exploitation of the subjects.

Prostitution is a complex issue that is rarely out of the news. This exhibition shows us not the act of prostitute sex, but the atmosphere surrounding it in one auratic incarnation. Many of the myriad issues linked to the representation of women are captured by Bellocq¹s project. This exhibition is about Bellocq and his subjects, and is set in a specific moment in the history of prostitution. But the show is also about the preservation of stories through photography, and about photography¹s ability
to tell those stories.

The Storyville Portraits exhibition comes to SFU through a generous loan from Carole and Howard Tanenbaum, Toronto.

Publication: The SFU Gallery is publishing a brochure on the Storyville Portraits with a 3000-word essay by Bill Jeffries and Veronika Klaptocz, available free of charge. The text is attached with this email (the brochure is still at the printer¹s!).

Discussion/screening: Saturday May 10, 2 3:30pm
Please join us for a panel discussion with Mary Lynn Stewart (Chair, Women¹s Studies, SFU) and Priya Ramu (Host, CBC Radio¹s On the Coast). Drawing on research from several of her recently published books, Mary Lynn Stewart will discuss photographs of women in fin-de-siècle France, including
representations of women in French bordellos. Priya Ramu will screen the documentary she co-produced for VSO Canada, called Brothel Justice, about a group of sex workers in Bangladesh. Followed by a reception, from 3:30 5pm.
