March 5, 1819
Reception 7-10PM, grunt gallery
Tuesday, March 5, 2019
Curator: Glenn Alteen
Artist: Rebecca Belmore

350 E 2nd Ave, Vancouver, BC

March 5, 2019 marks the 200th anniversary of the capture in Newfoundland of the Beothuk 
woman Demasaduit and the murder of her husband Nonosabasut by colonialist settlers. 
Later their skulls were taken from their gravesite and sent to the University of Scotland 
where they have been since. It was announced earlier this year that their skulls will finally be 
repatriated to Newfoundland.

In 2008 Rebecca Belmore produced the video installation March 5,1819 recreating the 
scene of the colonial capture and murder. But this is not a historical reenactment. The actors 
are in modern dress and Belmore questions what has changed over the past 200 years. 
March 5, 1819 was commissioned by The Rooms in St Johns and has also been exhibited 
in Ottawa and Toronto. Set at Red Indian Lake in central Newfoundland, it was shot in 
Vancouver at Mount Seymour in 2008 by videographer Noam Gonick. This will be the first 
screening of the installation in Vancouver. As we move forward with reconciliation, it is fitting 
to remember the history that brought us to this place.

We will also use this opportunity to celebrate the launch of a completely new website 
documenting Rebecca Belmore's career over the past 35 years. The new site features 
content that spans Belmores full career in all media taking the user deep into the heart of 
her artworks.
