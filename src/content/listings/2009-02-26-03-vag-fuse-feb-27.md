FUSE THIS FRIDAY

Vancouver's Most Artful Evening Out Happens February 27th at the

Vancouver Art Gallery from 6pm to Midnight

VANCOUVER, BC Some of Vancouver's most innovative musicians will perform
at the Vancouver Art Gallery's FUSE this Friday February 27th. The evening
will offer a performance of improvised ambient music by DJ Michael Red, a
Smiths-laced set by Vancouver new wave pop-rockers Call the City, musical
'Encounters' throughout the Gallery by some of Vancouver's top improvisers
(elevator music will never be the same), and live alternate voiceovers for
movies by comedian Riel Hahn.

Vancouver-based DJ, producer and promoter Michael Red will present his
ambient music project Souns. Ranging from hyper-minimal tones to
soundscapes supported by dub bass-lines, Red's live performance will weave
pre-programmed structures with the live looping and processing of bells,
chimes, glockenspiel, trumpet, samples, the immediate environment, found
objects, field recordings, static and hiss. Heavily improvisational, Souns
finds its roots in the elemental songs of nature and beyond.

Fronted by the mesmerizing Vanessa Sorenson, Call the City is leading a New
Wave revival in Vancouver. The band, rounded out by guitarist Julian
Growcott, formerly of Upper Levels, bassist Evan Lee and drummer Ben
Wilson, formerly of heartwarmongering, will play two sets. The first will
mimic a rehearsal within the confines of Samuel Roy-Bois' installation Ugly
Today, Beautiful Tomorrow, 2009 soundproof recording studio included in
the exhibition How Soon Is Now: Contemporary Art From Here where visitors
can play instruments and create music that is piped through speakers into
the Gallery's lobby. The group will also perform original songs and a
Smiths cover in a forth-floor gallery space backed by How Soon Is Now
artist Khan Lee, who will design a live video and lighting display.

Musicians will create instant music throughout the Gallery through a series
of 'Encounters' Vocal artist Carol Sawyer, whose video-based work is
included in the current exhibition How Soon Is Now: Contemporary Art From
Here, and pianist Lisa Miller will disintegrate a song based on
19th-Century art in the ground floor of the Gallery's rotunda. Clarinetist

place on for all four floors of the Gallery's rotunda.

For FUSE, comedian Riel Hahn is joined by her band of hilarity, Toby
Berner, Tallulah Winkelman and Thomas Jones for an innovative adventure
into the Neo-Benshi tradition. Neo-Benshi is the practice of producing live
alternate voiceovers for movies. Originating during the silent movie era in
Korea, Japan, Taiwan and other East Asian nations, it is experiencing a
resurgence with poets and performance artists from Los Angeles to
Vancouver. Hahn will also lead FUSE-goers on a tour of the Gallery,
blending fact and fiction, with the serious and the not-so-serious.

Held three times annually, the Vancouver Art Gallery's FUSE puts the city's
leading visual arts destination in an entirely new light at night. FUSE
tickets are $17.50 for adults. Members of the Vancouver Art Gallery receive
free admission.
