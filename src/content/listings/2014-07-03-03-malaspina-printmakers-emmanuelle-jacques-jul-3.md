Malaspina Printmakers | The Creation of the Universe: Emmanuelle Jacques |
JUL 3

The Creation of the Universe, which opens on Thursday July 3 at 6pm,
explores the generative nature of print media and its transposition into
video animation, then photography. Based on a series of 25 linocuts
featuring celestial imagery, this experimental project developed around a
creative process inspired by The Library of Babel, by Jorge Luis Borges,
which details an endlessly vast library containing all possible books
written with an alphabet of 25 characters. The 25 lino blocks used to
compose the prints stand in for the alphabet found in the library – their
space motif symbolic of its infinite possibilities. As with ancient books
comprised of individual lead characters and printed on a typographic press,
Jacques uses combinatorial processes to generate (in theory) all the prints
possible using a 25-block “alphabet”, like so many imaginary celestial
landscapes, viewed through the lens of a telescope.

What resulted are 1250 unique images out of the 15,625 possible
combinations. Each print is comprised of 3 of the 25 blocks, printed in the
3 primary colours. Though varied in combination, the colour is consistently
layered in the order of: yellow; magenta; and cyan. Each block was assigned
a number to identify its place within the gradual transformation of this
motif. Using a typographic press, each block’s corresponding number was
printed in the bottom right corner of each piece, revealing the code of its
composition, authenticating its uniqueness. While Jacques was printing, the
thought of God creating the Universe crossed her mind. She pictured him,
like her, randomly combining the elements of the periodic table, getting
sometimes a fruitful assemblage, sometimes a series of insignificant
amalgams. Hence the tongue-in-cheek title: The Creation of the Universe.

The prints will be presented in the order implied by their numbers,
demonstrating the gradual progression and rhythm of the image in its
successive combinations. The installation will be accompanied by a video
animation made with the prints, using the same combinatorial process but
revealing this transformation over time rather than within space. The
exhibition will also include a series of photographs Jacques is currently
working on. With a slightly long exposure, the camera can capture several
frames of the video animation on the same image. Digitally printed in large
scale, these images will have depth, nuances and subtleties the linoprints
don’t have. It’s like a way to layer images as with print media, but using
the property of photography to capture time and movement in a still image.

Emmanuelle Jacques lives and works in Montreal. She completed her Bachelor
of Visual Arts at Université du Québec à Montréal in 2004. She exhibited
her work, did artist residencies and lead workshops across Canada. She
recently took part in Eastern Edge’s Art Marathon Festival (NFLD), had a
solo exhibition at Martha Street Studio (MB) and was on the shortlist for
the Art Gallery ofYork University’s Artist Book of the Moment competition
(ON). Emmanuelle Jacques is interested in issues of territory: urbanism,
astronomy, great explorations, psychogeography, toponymy. By pursuing
endless tasks, such as the mapping of vast; continually transforming
spaces, she challenges the absurdity of the human condition. Her practice
is based on drawing and print media, although it often borders with other
disciplines. The language of cartography, with its pictorial and semantic
qualities, is the basis of her research on territory — an open notion that
she addresses freely, as a dilettante.
