from the river to the sea invites you to their next screening event, a presentation of a
classic, rarely seen feature gem, "Leila and the Wolves" by Heiny Srour (1984, 90:00),
that speaks to the intersectionality of womens struggles in occupied Palestine and the
surrounding region and their often hidden roles in history.
At the Reliance Theater, Emily Carr University of Art + Design, 520 E. 1st Ave,
Vancouver, Fri., March 15th
Doors open at 5:00, screening starts at 5:30pm
Admission by cash donation to Palestine relief in Gaza.No one turned away.

The film delves into the collective memory of Arab women weaving together re-
enactments, archive footage and fanciful sequences to create a testament to the
history of Palestinian and Lebanese womens political struggles over the past
eight
decades. It was filmed in often dangerous conditions over a period of seven years. In
the film, the protagonist Leila, a Lebanese woman living in London, time travels
through the 1900s to the 1980s, with each trip focusing on the centrality of women in
Palestinian and Lebanese resistance movements.

Following the screening, stick around for an engaging discussion.
Food graciously donated by Mazahr Lebanese Kitchen (mazahr.ca)
Childcare will be provided.
This event is being hosted by the BIPOC Creative Collective as part of Apartheid
Awareness Month @ECU.

Let's come together in these difficult times and share our presence in solidarity!

----
Bio: Born in Beirut, 23rd March 1945, Heiny Srour studied Sociology at the French
University of Beirut and read Social Anthropology at the Sorbonne in Paris. Without film
training she became a script writer, director and producer in documentary and feature
films. Innovative in form
and content, in Arab and Third World Cinema, her films
often shot under danger, deal
with social justice and womens issues. First woman of the Third World selected at
Cannes Film Festival in 1974 with her documentary THE HOUR OF LIBERATION,
she continued pioneering with LEILA AND THE WOLVES (1984), THE SINGING
SHEIKH (1991), RISING ABOVE : WOMEN OF VIETNAM (1995), whilst writing and
painting.
