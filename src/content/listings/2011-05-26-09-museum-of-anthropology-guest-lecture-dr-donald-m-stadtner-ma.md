Renowned Asian scholar, Donald Stadtner, will give an illustrated lecture
exploring how sacred sites come into existence, prompting questions that go
right to the heart of religious belief and devotion. His lecture will draw
from his most recent book Sacred Sites of Burma. Dr. Stadtner was the
recipient of a Fulbright grant and two Smithsonian Institution fellowships.
This illustrated presentation, hosted in partnership with the Canadian
Society for Asian Arts, the Stronck Family Foundation, and the Tung Lin Kok
Yuen Canada Foundation.

May 29th at 1PM

Free with Admission / Admission $15.68 Adults; $13.44 Students & Seniors
65+ / 6393 N.W. Marine Drive, Vancouver BC V6T 1Z2

More information can be found at [http://www.moa.ubc.ca/events](http://www.moa.ubc.ca/events).


Museum of Anthropology | Inuit Prints (Exhibition Opening) | JUN 19

This exhibition features exquisite and extraordinarily rare prints from
Japan and Cape Dorset, Nunavut, from the late 1950s and early 1960s. It
also tells the little-known

story of how, fifty years ago, the Canadian artist and “discoverer”  of
Inuit art, James Houston, travelled to Japan to study printmaking with
Un’ichi Hiratsuka.

In conjunction with this exhibit is: Faces and Voices of the Inuit Art
Market – A UBC Student Exhibit

Admission $15.68 Adults; $13.44 Students & Seniors 65+ / 6393 N.W. Marine
Drive, Vancouver BC V6T 1Z2

More information can be found at [http://www.moa.ubc.ca/exhibits](http://www.moa.ubc.ca/exhibits).
