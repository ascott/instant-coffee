AMAN AHEER: Are You My Mother?
AUGUST 4 - AUGUST 17, 2018
Opening Reception: Saturday, August 4, at 3 pm
279 E 6th Ave, Vancouver, BC

This body of work, created between 2017 and 2018, began as a way to address questions 
about sacrifice and revolution, but ultimately grew into a series of intimate meditations on the 
metaphysical significance of martyrdom.

Painted arches and frames set the stage and direct the gaze towards ghostly figures 
hovering alongside jerry cans and oranges; these symbols, taken together, call subtly upon 
traditions of religious painting, yet they also derive and draw upon my own understanding of 
self-sacrifice and contemporary revolution. What is the role of sacrifice in a revolution? What 
does one see or hear at the moments before abandoning ones body? What is experienced 
by those who bare witness to such performance? Where do they go, or where do they return 
to?

The Sublime Gate series, for example, invokes the liminal mental space that exists 
between the decision to sacrifice ones body and its final realization. At this moment, what 
might at first seem to be a mere hallucination becomes tangible and real, and that which 
may have previously seemed real becomes more fluid and more distorted. The outline of 
the gas can emit an ethereal neon glow, while the hyperreal citrus and poppies are 
dispersed along a warped, unstable plane. Shifts in depth and perspective that might 
otherwise only be experienced through the digital are evoked here using its conceptual 
inverse: Islamic geometry. Encased within painted lapis and emerald arches, the scenes !2 
might also be placed within the long painterly tradition of religious iconography. As in that 
tradition, the distinction between the real and the unreal is equally as indecipherable.

The painting My Son My Son What have you Done? offers a similarly volatile experience, 
but this time from the moment following a sacrifice. Though in deep mourning, and weighted 
down by thick oil, these witnesses remain determined. They are synchronized and deliberate 
while nonetheless turning their back to the viewer. Here, texture and textile work to bring the 
event out of the painting and into a third space where the viewer is forced, perhaps, to 
mourn as well. Also at this moment, the surveillant, chaotic, and at times condemning gaze 
of Witness invokes the distant satellite as well as the eyes of nearby onlookers; both, 
however, are equally prone to distorted visions. The -yat an-nr (signs of light) series, in a 
different vein, presents instead the instruments of sacrifice in their most disembodied form; 
outlines, spectres, projections. Taken together, this body of work implies a single moment or 
a series of moments, a single space or a series of spaces; distorting and bringing together 
the often disparate elements of a sacrifice, no matter its spatial or temporal existence.

Throughout my work, I try to explore the problem of ambivalence. That is, the paintings 
intentionally question the viewers confidence in the paintings, questioning their validity as a 
painting at all. The figures are familiar and convincing, and often explicitly foreign; they are 
neither here nor there. Pattern and the determined repetition of disturbing and often comical 
motifs amplify and indeed underscores this ambivalence. The concurrent notions of fear and 
acceptance, the tension of resistance and retreat that the works evoke, are, I believe, deeply 
resonant with the present moment. 

- Aman Aheer
