Grunt gallery presents at HIVE2 at The Centre for Digital Media Great Northern Way Campus 577 
Great Northern Way

The grunt gallery will be participating in HIVE 2, flying at you June 5-14th. This new event was 
inspired by the visual art community’s SWARM event in which several Vancouver art galleries 
open their doors on the same night. So its a natural fit for the grunt gallery Director of 
Programming Glenn Alteen to curate local performance artists @ HIVE. Alteen was one of the 
founding Board of Directors of LIVE - Vancouver’s International Performance Art Biennalle and 
has been curating performance art for 25 years. Alteen comments that “ My contribution will be to
provide a series of performance artists performing durational work that will create small vignettes 
within the wider frame of HIVE 2. These artists will explore the issues that have framed 
performance art throughout its history including the body, the situation and site. The focus will be 
to blend among the other works in discreet and subtle ways that bring new relationships to the 
forefront. Using processes of intervention, spectacle and ritual the artists will provide new ways for 
the audience to experience the event and view the proceedings.”

The performance run for 3 hours each from 7-10pm.

June 5 Paul Wong – Mainstreet. This 4 projector montage encapsulates new work on Wong from 
his perch on Southern Main Street. The live mix of video produced recently looks at A series of 
Main Street characters and situations. VJ Wong is in the hood!. He is known particularly for his 
tough engagement with issues of race, sex, and death. He has been awarded the Bell Canada 
Award (1992) and the Governor Generals Media Arts Award (2005). 
www.hungryghosts.net www.video.virtualmuseum.ca

June 6 Skeena Reece - Nurse Shaman. This Tsimshian/Cree Nurse Shaman is sure to ease your 
modern ailments from white guilt, to stress about the end of the world. This savvy nurse will 
prescribe music, books and other fabulous remedies to the shitty situation we call ‘the present’!
Make an appointment for 5-15 minute sessions! Remember, healing starts with you! Skeena is 
the girl pacing in the hallway at an aboriginal economic development conference rehearsing 
roasts on aboriginal leaders, or making a potlatch of 600 people sigh from hearing about child 
abuse in her stand-up comedy routines or excite elders watching her sing the blues on national 
television. 

June 7th Bobbi Kozinuk – Fitting in. Bobbi will enliven the holding space with micro scenes 
created from images of Vancouver and of scenes from movies. The projections will be “moved” 
around the space with the help of a mirror. The viewers will be led to consider /question the roles 
laid out by our society, as well as how we are altering our own environment. Bobbi is a Vancouver
based media artist, curator and technician. Her work spans installations, performances which 
exam cultural roles and community building workshops. Host of the show Soundscapes on Co-op 
Radio, Bobbi has produced workshops on low powered FM transmission. Kozinuk is the 
InterMedia studio technician at the Emily Carr Institute of Art and Design.

June 8 2008 Archer Pechawis - Shoot the indian. In this new work the audience is invited to 
“shoot the Indian” in his natural habitat, using paintball guns. All audience members can view the 
Indian as he performs traditional quotidian tasks, and for a fee rent the use of a paintball gun for 
five minutes. Pechawis is a media-integrated performing artist, New Media artist, writer, curator, 
teacher and dad. His practice investigates the intersection of Plains Cree culture and digital 
technology. His current fascination is what he calls ‘transitional Cree culture’, the place where
Cree culture meets the onrush of millennial technology.
http://www.apxo.net/home.html

June 10 Marlene Madison – Close to me. Madison will engage the viewer in readings of short text 
pieces addressing notions of intimacy, reflexivity, and authenticity. This performance will be used 
to create a new work for exhibition. Marlene Madison is a multi-disciplinary artist and writer. Since 
studying at the Emily Carr Institute of Art and Design she has performed and screened her work 
in festivals across Canada, the US, Mexico and Europe. For the past few years, Madison’s work 
has focused on mainstream media culture and the effects of its intrusion upon cultural a, public 
and private space.

June 11 Margaret Dragu - LADY JUSTICE GOES BUZZ-BUZZ is a 3 hour durational performance 
about public/private grief and unspoken desire for justice/revenge. She will perform with an 
ironing board and steam iron and immerse hankies in dishpans of salt water (representing tears), 
wring them out, iron the wet/salty hankies, then hang/drape them on a chair. She will perform 
yoga-warrior poses with her Sword of Justice and Scales of Justice. Margaret Dragu is celebrating
her 37th year as an artist. She has presented her work in galleries, museums, theatres, 
nightclubs, libraries, universities and site-specific venues including parks, botanical gardens, and 
public parade routes across Canada, the west and east coast of the United States, and in 
Western Europe. 
http://ladraguasladyjustice.blogspot.com/

June 12 Cheryl LHirondelle - êkâya-pâhkaci [don’t freeze up] is an interdisciplinary and 
exploratory process-based performance piece: an intersection of nomadic site-specifity, visual 
patterning, language, narrative, movement and rhythm. She will construct a lodge (a white 
canvas tent) and sew herself inside and slowly paint herself white. The project references the 
temporary structures that house many of Vancouver’s homeless and is a continuation of her site-
specific performative activities. L’Hirondelle’s practice is an investigation of the intersection of 
Cree worldview – nêhiyawin - and the inter/multidsciplinarity of creative expression inherent in 
other indigenous, world and youth cultures. Cheryl is part of the group M’Girl, Aboriginal Women’s 
Ensemble and Saskatchewan duo Nikamok. 
www.ndnnrkey.net

June 13 2008 Norma – Warm up Act. The performance will consist of 4-6 performers acting out, 
one by one, an introductory speech, over and over again. With both original and copied scripts 
(from online sources-- YouTube footage of speeches and weddings, mostly), the performers will 
introduce each other ad infinitum with a generic, but laudatory, intro speech. The effect of the 
work will be both irritating and humourous-- as patrons come in and out of shows, they’ll be stuck 
in a perpetually looping, skipping record-like introductory moment, which we hope will add to the
“many shows under one roof” Norma consists of seven artists of diverse backgrounds and have 
produced installation and performance works that employ absurdity, physical endurance and 
repetition in an exploration of collective identity and cultural anxiety.
www.normasite.com.

June 14 Rebecca Belmore – “,,,,more” This new performance by Rebecca Belmore comes on the 
heels of her solo survey exhibition at the Vancouver Art Gallery. Born in Upsala, Ontario and 
currently living in Vancouver, Belmore has exhibited her work both nationally and internationally, 
including two solo touring exhibitions, The Named and the Unnamed, Morris and Helen Belkin Art 
Gallery, Vancouver, 2002 and 33 Pieces, Blackwood Gallery, University of Toronto at 
Mississauga, 2001. She was Canada’s official representative at the Venice Biennale in 2005 and 
has participated in the Sydney Biennial and the Havana Biennial. Belmore’s current exhibition 
Rising to the Occasion is currently running at the Vancouver Art Gallery. 
www.rebeccabelmore.com.
