GRUNT GALLERY SPECIAL PROJECT

SERIES 2 is now installed -- March 11 to 25, 2011

who is that body in the window?
coordinated by Laiwan

Temporary exquisite corpse windows at 1965 Main Street, Vancouver
A special project to celebrate grunt gallery's presence at our temporary
location at 1965 Main Street, while engaging local artists in our community
with busy Main street traffic!

grunt gallery is very pleased to announce of the second in our series of
'exquisite corpse' displays, with the surprising consequences. In a blind
collaboration, the Series 2 players are: Merritt Johnson, Sepideh Saii and
Sonny Assu. The windows are viewable from Main Street, between third and
fourth avenues - drop by and say hello!

For more information on the grunt gallery exquisite corpse windows please
visit the flickr collection.

STATEMENT & BIOS FROM THE ARTISTS

Merritt Johnson
title: Hides his body (Buffalo Bull)

Buffalo are not known to have a knack for camouflage, or to show an
interest in stealth, but recent events in our collective history have
required changes in the Buffalo Nation's approach to everyday living.

Disguise has become a keen interest among the Buffalo, and despite their
size they are quickly becoming adept at camouflaging themselves so as to
move about without false charges of spreading brucellosis or
inconveniencing motorists being levelled at them or their family members.
While Buffalo respects Crow, Rabbit, Fox, Coyote and Raven, he requests
that he not be labelled a trickster, or a shape-shifter - despite his
mastery of disguise.
Born on Turtle Island (North America), Merritt Johnson is a
multidisciplinary artist of Mohawk, Blackfoot, and Non-Indigenous heritage.
Her work critiques the enforcement of division, as an exertion of power,
over stateless nations, individuals, and land without consent. Johnson's
practise is informed by a Fourth World perspective on land, competition,
and control of resources. The works imagine and enact challenges to
pervasive systems of division, ownership, and force as they are applied to
the land, sky and everything in between. Johnson exhibits, performs, and
speaks in traditional and nontraditional venues throughout North America.
She currently lives and works in Vancouver, British Columbia, where she is
a member of the Faculty of Visual Art and Material Practice at Emily Carr
University of Art and Design.

Sepideh Saii
title: Half-Assed

Sepideh Saii is a multi-disciplinary artist, working between Vancouver, BC
and London, UK. Her work has been exhibited extensively throughout Europe,
Canada, the United States and Japan. In her current practice, she
appropriates popular feature films by inserting herself literally into the
big picture in place of characters that are projected onto her body as she
acts out the scenes with them. For Sepideh the simple intervention of
standing in between the projector and the image represents a movement from
absorption to interaction. She views this gap as a stage where she can
perform her desired fantasies, enter into the film and be seen and heard on
screen. The fact that this is impossible separates her from the image and
disrupts the illusion, making her attempts more personal and theatrical.
She received her BFA in Integrated Media from Emily Carr University in 2003
and an MA in Fine Art Media at the Slade School of Fine Art, University
College London in 2009. www.sepidehsaii.com

Sonny Assu
title: Disconnected Confusion

"My series of disconnected paintings (panels and drums) explores notions of
disconnecting from traditional narrative and has become a study on
abstracting abstraction. Inserting the ramifications of colonization that
First People of North America have endured as the conceptual narrative, I
use this as a way to discover new directions for perceiving Aboriginal art
and how Northwest Coast form-line created a character based language that
was never discovered."

Sonny Assu pushes the boundaries of contemporary art by challenging the
perception of Aboriginal art. A multidisciplinary artist, Assu merges
Northwest Coast Aboriginal iconography with the aesthetics of popular
culture to challenge social and historical values that we, as a society,
face on a daily basis. His work is an exploration of his mixed ancestry and
creates a discourse on how and why we use items of consumer and popular
culture to define our personal lineage. [http://sonnyassu.com](http://sonnyassu.com)
