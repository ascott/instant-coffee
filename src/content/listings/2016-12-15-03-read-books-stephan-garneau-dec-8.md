Stephan Garneau
Destar, 2016
7:00:00
Dec 08 - Jan 05, 2017

"For each person there are two worlds, the 'idios kosmos', which is a unique 
private world, and the 'koinos kosmos', which literally means the shared world. No 
person can tell which parts of his worldview is idios kosmos and which is koinos 
kosmos, except by the achievement of a strong empathetic rapport with other 
people."* 

Stephan Garneau looks to the space between the private world and our 
collective consciousness to consider the effect of technology on our faculties of 
cognition, notions of harmony, and our relation to the natural world. His new 
work, Destar is composed of a series of slow-moving sequences produced in 
Vancouver during the fall of 2016 and is made for the window of READ Books

Forms of colour, shadow, line, movement and errors in processing gather as a 
collection of "digital biotics", sequences of phenomena resulting from digital 
translation. Recognizable environments appear in the sequence, while the 
effects of artifacting and discontinuity are also familiar. Varying speeds and focus 
exercise temporal agency and consequently bear the marks of the recording 
technology; the digital effects become symbols themselves. The duration 
conforms to the limit of the screening time and functions as a counter-balance to 
normal usage of pacing. It unfolds as a fragmented experience, creating a level 
of distanciation that invites personal encounters each night.

Stephan Garneau is a designer living in Vancouver, BC. In his work he is 
interested in the two-dimensional visual representation of information through 
textual, quantitative, graphic and photographic mediums. Consistent in his 
process is the mindful experimentation of colour and symbolic formalism, the 
pursuit of technique and a belief in the ethical dimension of graphic 
communication. In addition to freelance projects, he works as an instructional 
designer and maintains painting and writing practices. He holds a BDes from 
Emily Carr University and a BCom from the University of Alberta.
