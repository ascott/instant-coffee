Chris Gallagher
Around Here

April 11 - April 28, 2016
Reception Thursday April 14, 6-8pm

Chris Gallagher is an artist and filmmaker, whose background includes a
decades long position as an Associate Professor of film at UBC. Gallagher
creates vibrant photographic images from controlled time-based events,
utilizing a self-developed technique termed the radial pan which creates a
centrifuge-like effect.

Around Here combines motion picture duration with the stasis of the fixed
image. This work explores the dynamics of temporal media expressed in the
static structure of the photographic image, questioning the underlying
concepts of both film and photography. During exposure, the camera rotates
precisely around the lens axis, bending the linear exposure time into a
circle forming a closed loop reminiscent of the film loop. A circle is
understood with just a glance, it is narrative free and is immune to
temporality. The image smear created by the radial camera's movement shifts
the subject's representation from the real to the abstract, suggesting
temporality as the dominant element of representation, rather than spatial
depiction.

These images are an instant imbued with duration, a continuous moment to
which one brings a sense of self and seeks a point of identification. The
viewer oscillates between positioning themselves in cryogenic stasis (the
photograph) or whirling in the circle (the movie), yet finds themselves
continuously drawn back to the transcendental stillness of the centre.
