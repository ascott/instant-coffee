Everything is in the language we use
Mercedes Eng, Emma Hedditch,
and Lis Rhodes

Today, the gallery at Western Front re-opens to the public. Adjusted opening hours and
monitored capacity will ensure safe physical distancing for visitors. Originally scheduled to
open in March, this exhibition has been re-imagined as an experiment in hybrid ways of
engaging publics during the current conditions.

The artists works in Everything is in the language we use interrogate power, revealing its
underlying structures through the use of language, image, and action. While the pieces
selected have remained the same, the context in which they are now read has shifted
considerably in recent months. COVID-19 has highlighted labour precarity, disparities in
resource distribution,
and conflicts around the economic value placed on life. The murder
of George Floyd, and the ensuing global protests against systemic racism, further
resonate with such issues. Through an engagement with these artists ideas, in this
exhibition we hope to offer new models of learning and being that can further destabilize
and deconstruct these hierarchies.

On site, visitors to the gallery can view Lis Rhodes 80 minute film Journal of Disbelief
(2000-2016) and a series of printed excerpts from Mercedes Engs book Prison Industrial
Complex Explodes: A Poem (2017). Online at front.bc.ca, Rhodes film can also be
streamed by viewers, alongside other content that will be uploaded over the course of the
exhibitions six week run. This includes programs spotlighting Eng and Rhodes writing,
and proposals by Emma Hedditch that engage with the labour of exclusion and inclusion.
