CSA space presents:

Tegan Moore
Subtraction Inventories
June 9th - July 10th 2016
Opening Reception: Thursday June 9, 6-9pm

Fossilized air, secret vacancies, and a breeze.
A plenum pusher. A space full of matter
An interior weather. And a calculated unbuilding.

In her recent book Subtraction, architect and theorist Keller Easterling
advocates for architecture and building practices that value subtraction,
understanding it not only as a destructive process, but as a productive
means that works interdependently with addition. Her analysis questions an
approach to design that too often begins with a "tabula rasa" or a blank
slate. With this comes the task of exposing and tracing infrastructure and
hidden systems, working conscientiously with what came before, and what is
covered up.

In Subtraction Inventories, an unseen architectural cavity is aired out,
and a non-structural ceiling is designed for its own collapse. Eroded
synthetic material removed from the commercial waste stream, is hollowed
out to vent untouched interiors on the surface of a storied floor.

Tegan Moore is an artist from Toronto, currently living in London, Ontario.
She is a graduate of Emily Carr University of Art and Design (2008) and the
University of Western Ontario (2014). Concurrent with this exhibition, her
project Semiopaque with Liza Eurich is on at G Gallery in Toronto.
