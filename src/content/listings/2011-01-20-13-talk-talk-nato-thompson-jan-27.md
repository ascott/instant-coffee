Emily Carr Guest Speaker: Nato Thompson
7pm, Thursday, January 27, 2011

Emily Carr Lecture Theatre (SB 301)
1400 Johnston Street, Granville Island, Vancouver

Free and open to the public.

Emily Carr University of Art + Design is pleased to present a lecture with
Nato Thompson, Chief Curator at Creative Time, New York's premiere public
art organization.

Seeing Power: Socially Engaged Art in the Age of Cultural Production
In this talk, Nato Thompson will discuss his upcoming book with Melville
House Publishing which looks at the effects on vast cultural production on
the how cultural organizations and artists must work. Both part political
diatribe and anecdotal journey, this lecture focuses on the intersection of
art and activism at the beginning of the 21st century.

Since coming to Creative Time in 2007, Thompson has organized large scale
social based projects including Democracy in America: The National
Campaign, Paul Chan's Waiting for Godot in New Orleans, Jeremy Deller's It
is What it Is: Conversations about Iraq (a project in collaboration with
the New Museum), and most recently the Creative Time Summit. Previous to
Creative Time, he served as curator at MASS MoCA. His writings appear in
numerous magazines on the subject of art and activism, and his book Seeing
Power: Socially Engaged Art in the Age of Cultural Production is due out by
Melville House Publishing in January 2012.

14. Shudder Gallery | DRIL | JAN 27
Shudder Gallery presents:

Move It On Over

DRIL

Opening reception Thursday January 27th, 7 - 11 p.m.

28 January - 19 February, 2011

A drift of tumbleweed are accumulating within the walls of Shudder, carried
in by the wind of DRIL, propelled by their collective interest in the
iconography projected onto these simple plants. Through Western film, the
North American conscious has come to understand the icon of the tumbleweed
as a metaphor for transience and inhospitable land. By transposing this
iconic symbol from the badlands of film into the heart of DTES, DRIL poses
how this device's currency contextualizes our understanding of the changing
social and economic landscape of the surrounding area. In removing our
vicarious understanding of this icon, viewers are no longer witnessing the
story of the wild west, they are directly implicated into this ambiguous
narrative.

DRIL is a Vancouver based art collective comprised of Dylan McHugh, Rachel
White, Ian Prentice and Leisha O'Donohue. The collaborative group crafts
their practice around the politics and poetics of space. Through Move it on
Over you will see a continuation of the collectives interest in developing
site-responsive installations that traverse the intersections between
geography, history and propaganda.

Node is launching a Curatorial Studies Residency Program for research and
practice in curatorial studies. The program understands curatorship as
exhibition praxis, allowing direct experimentation with the exhibition
space. Additionally, the program will be reinforced with activities such as
workshops, seminars, meetings with cultural agents, art shows, and visits
to exhibition spaces.

Moreover, participants are expected to take an active role in organizing
the program (i.e., managing seminars, workshops, guest lectures,

develop specific theoretical-practical research during their residency.

logic of the limit: transversal curatorial processes'. The different lines
of research explore the curatorial practices that expand their own
mechanisms beyond the normal 'taxonomic' processes of the art exhibition
(i.e., classification, study, and exhibition), generating new discursive
possibilities.

During that period, the residents will enjoy a shared office to develop

25. Morris and Helen Belkin Art Gallery | Faces: Works From The Permanent Collection | APR 10
Faces: Works From The Permanent Collection
January 14 - April 10, 2011

For more information visit www.belkin.ubc.ca

"Faces" is an exhibition featuring work from the collection of the Morris
and Helen Belkin Art Gallery, The University of British Columbia. The
exhibition explores the diverse ways faces are represented, looking
specifically at how ideas of gender, race, class, and historical contexts
affect our understanding of them.

This exhibition includes over 90 paintings, photographs, sculpture and
video from the collections and archives of the Morris and Helen Belkin Art
Gallery. In addition, research and travel photographs that date from the
1890s to the mid 1970s have been loaned by the Museum of Anthropology
(UBC), the Canadian Museum of Civilization (Gatineau), and the American
Museum of Natural History Library (New York). "Faces" includes works by:
bill bissett, Claude Breeze, Dana Claxton, Stanley Cosgrove, Kate Craig,
Lawren Harris, Ray Johnson, Ken Lum, Myfanwy MacLeod, Liz Magor, Al Neil,
and Andy Warhol, among others; and photographs by Charles Edenshaw, Dan
Jorgensen, Fred Rychman, Harlan Ingersoll Smith from museum archival
collections.

"Faces" takes place in three locations in Vancouver: the Morris and Helen
Belkin Art Gallery (UBC), Walter C. Koerner Library (1958 Main Mall, UBC)
and the Satellite Gallery (560 Seymour Street).

At Walter C. Koerner Library, a collection of portraits represents some of
the men and women who have been part of the history of The University of
British Columbia between 1913 and 1966. Many of the artists who created
these images are major figures in the history of Canadian art such as Peter
Aspell, Robert Harris, John Koerner, Lilias Torrance Newton, and Charles
Stegeman.

At the Satellite Gallery, Rebecca Belmore's "The Named and the Unnamed"
(2002) will be presented from February 4 - April 10, 2011. This powerful
installation confronts the viewer with images of loss, struggle, and
silence. It incorporates a video of "Vigil" that Belmore performed at the
corner of Gore and Cordova Streets on June 23, 2002.

We thank Walter C. Koerner Library and the Satellite Gallery for
participating in this project.
 "Faces" is co-curated by Scott Watson, Kate Barbaria, Adriana Estrada
Centelles, Jonah Gray, and Mohammad Salemy. An illustrated catalogue with
essays by the co-curators and by Karl Fousek accompanies the exhibition.

We thank Jerry Allen, Rebecca Belmore, Neil Campbell, and Dana Claxton for
lending works of art and our donors who have generously gifted works to the
permanent collection. We gratefully acknowledge the ongoing support of the
Canada Council for the Arts and our Belkin Curator's Forum members.
