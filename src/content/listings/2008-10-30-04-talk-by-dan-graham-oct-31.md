DAN GRAHAM
ARTIST TALK

31 OCTOBER 2008, 1PM
ECU THEATRE, SOUTH BUILDING, ROOM 301

Dan Graham is a New York based artist who continues to be one of the most
influential Conceptual artists since the 1960s. Much of his work is
situated outside the gallery and addresses recurrent themes that are rooted
in everyday urban life. Graham's early projects took the form of
instructional conceptual texts inserted into popular magazines. In the
1970s Graham worked primarily with performance, film, and video, using
loops and mirrors to examine relationships between the performer and the
audience. His pavilions and architectural installations deal with ideas of
public and private, and issues of social space. He has collaborated with
numerous artists including Vancouver's Rodney Graham and Jeff Wall. Dan
Graham is also an influential critic writing texts that span music, popular
culture, architecture, and art.

Dan Graham's talk is presented in collaboration with the Charles H. Scott
Gallery, the ECU Speaker Series, the UBC Department of Art History, Visual
Art, and Theory, the Morris and Helen Belkin Art Gallery, and the Rennie
Collection.

Erasure is much in the news these days - stock portfolio values erased, a
neighbourhood buried under water by storms, candidates for office learning
that the public chose someone else, or a Fortune 500 company ceasing to
exist. Erasure, however, has another side that deserves to be in the news:
the poetic and the critical. This is the side reflected in this 24-person
international exhibition, which includes the first-ever installation of the
entirety of Tom Phillips's book A Humument.

The poets, writers, and artists in Less is More have each responded to the
ironic, formal, political, and semantic possibilities that awaited
liberation from the source material they elected to use. The resulting
poetry can take many forms: paintings, modified books, vinyl lettering on
walls and floor, or blacked-out government documents.

Erasure is the most serious way that playfulness has emerged in recent art.
By modifying existing documents and artifacts in aid of reconsidering their
meaning, erasures provide an intriguing model for the ways in which meaning
is created in the first place; it is epistemology, with fun added.

My paintings depict how people occupy the fragile infrastructure of roads

by this increasingly complicated energy economy.

09. Shaq'sthut | Gathering Place Colloquium | NOV 6
Shaq'sthut --Gathering Place Colloquium
November 7, 8, 2008
Sty-Wet-Tan Longhouse --University of British Columbia
The Aboriginal Curatorial Collective (ACC) is pleased to announce its
forthcoming colloquium, Shaq'sthut - Gathering Place. The colloquium will
be the third of a successful series of gatherings initiated and
administrated by the board and members of the Aboriginal Curatorial
Collective and involves Aboriginal curators, art historians and artists
from across North and South America. The title, Shaq'sthut, refers to the
Coast Salish name for a site that was a gathering place in which it was
common for people to speak five or six languages because of trading,
intermarriage, and other transactions that occurred between groups. Taking
Vancouver as an equally significant space, the ACC Vancouver Caucus has
proposed this city as the site for the ACC/CCA colloquium, scheduled to
take place on November 7, & 8 (2008) at the Sty-Wet-Tan Longhouse at the
University of British Columbia.

"Auntie- Hero"

Merritt Johnson, Skeena Reece and Cheryl L'Hirondelle

Curated by Maria Hupfield

Friday November 7 at 7pm

Co-presented by LIVE Biennial and ACC as part of the Shaq'sthut - Gathering
Place Colloquium

Transcending the ordinary this trio of performance artists, as flexible and
fluid as water, defy legend and capture the essence of the heroic through
the unheroic. In the exhibit "Auntie- Hero", Vancouver based artists
Merritt Johnson, Skeena Reece and Cheryl L'Hirondelle individually exercise
their inherent powers to address issues of survival in the 21st Century.
Thematically linked through the shared use of narrative repetition
manifested in visual patterning, language, sound, action and movement,
these artists infiltrate the interstitial spaces of the physical and
mental. In true coyote fashion the performances crisscross territories
ranging from a border crossing sneak and lock-pick, a gender-bender
punk-rocker, to a nomadic multi- sensory guide. Balancing the extreme
dualities of life these performances parallel the everyday lived actions of
aunties, as aunties, unified through gender and all of our relations.

11. Shudder Gallery | Pushing up Daisies | NOV 7
Pushing up Daisies - Scott Moore and Rob Nicholls
Opening Nov 7th 7pm to 11pm
Show runs Nov 8th to Nov 29th

"Pushing Up Daisies," a painting show featuring new works from Rob Nicholls
and Scott Moore, serves as an exploration of the uncertain; an invitation
to revisit imagined spaces of the past and to trespass through
temperamental sites of the now.

Moore's work is comprised of a vibrant, hyper-real palette applied
assuredly in a fashion that capitalizes on reductive strokes and layered
transparencies defining his subject of memorized instances in landscape.
This reductive technique also functions to describe a type of editing, and
ultimately of Moore's use of memory. Nicholls, on the other hand takes a
more definite, more violent approach to his subject; there is a sense of
danger which radiates from his compacted spaces. By using a palette which
mimics colours of rust and other products of chemical decay, combined with
the language of abstraction, Nicholls expresses a desire to breakdown and
then reassemble otherwise forgotten environments.
