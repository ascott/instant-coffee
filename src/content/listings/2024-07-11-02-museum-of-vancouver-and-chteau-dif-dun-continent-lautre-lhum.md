Dun continent lautre : lhumain et la nature

From one continent to another: humans and nature

Museum of Vancouver and

Chteau dIf Partner for New School Program

Students will explore how location and culture influence ecosystems

For Immediate Release: July 11, 2024 " Vancouvers children will soon get a closer
look at water pollution in France while French children can learn about deforestation
and Musqueam, Squamish and Tsleil-Waututh teachings on stewardship as part of
an international collaboration between the Museum of Vancouver and the Chteau
dIf in France.

As part of French Immersion educational offers for elementary schools, the Museum
of Vancouver and the Chteau dIf in France have
developed a partnership to offer a
joint virtual school program titled Dun continent lautre : lhumain et la nature
(From one continent to another: humans and nature).

Managed by the Centre des Monuments Nationaux under the supervision of the
Ministry of Culture in France, Chteau dIf is a fortress located offshore from
Marseille in southeastern France famous for being one of the settings of Alexandre
Dumas novel, The Count of Monte Cristo. But its also home to protected flora and
fauna, including the European phyllodactyl, a small nocturnal gecko.

By exploring the topics of biodiversity, climate change and water pollution from
human activities at both sites, students will be asked to compare how geographical
locations and culture have influenced human choices in both ecosystems and
discover solutions towards a more sustainable future.

The Museum of Vancouver is excited to be a part of this new collaboration with
Chteau dIf and the Centre des Monuments Nationaux. This program uses
digital
tools to extend remote learning while enabling students in both France and Canada
to explore sustainability in a cross-cultural way. It's also exciting to connect the
content of museums across the globe through education and learning. "Mauro
Vescera, MOV Chief Executive Officer

Students in both Canada and France will participate in activities centered around an
immersive 360 guided remote visit of Chteau d'If and modules from the MOV
exhibition That Which Sustains Us"both of which have the goal of raising
awareness among future generations about environmental protection, the
conservation of natural resources, and the challenges posed by current urban
management. Both portions of the program are offered in French, with an English
option for the Chteau d'If portion.

For students in Vancouver, the MOVs activities can be held in-person or online.
Local schools can visit the Museum for a scavenger hunt in That Which Sustains Us
while the virtual option can be done from the
convenience of the classroom.

About the Museum of Vancouver: The Museum of Vancouver (MOV) connects
Vancouverites to each other and connects Vancouver to the world. The Museums
programs, exhibitions and collections bring people together and inspire conversation
about the future. The Museum is an independent non-profit organization dedicated to
inspiring a socially connected, civically engaged city. The Museum of Vancouver and
the city of Vancouver is located within the shared, unceded, ancestral territories of
the xmky"m (Musqueam), Swxw7mesh (Squamish) and slilwta (Tsleil-
Waututh) Nations.

About Chteau dIf: The Chteau dIf is a monument built in the 16th century situated
offshore from Marseille at the request of Francis I, King of France.
It is managed by
the Centre des Monuments Nationaux, a public establishment, under the supervision
of the Ministry of Culture. The Centre des Monuments Nationaux has a mission to
restore and maintain a hundred monuments across France, ensuring a wide public
access.
