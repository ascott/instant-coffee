Special Exhibition: Otherwise Disregarded " Capture Photography Festival
Featuring the work of Adad Hannah, Jake Kimble, Michelle Sound, and Jin-me Yoon
Opening April 21, 2024 at the Audain Art Museum in Whistler

In a time of profound change, the four artists featured in Otherwise Disregarded
harness the transformative power of art to nurture creative expression, challenge
notions of justice, and foster a deep connection between the mind, body, and society
at large. They use visual tools for self-reflection, addressing complex social, political,
and environmental issues that should not be dismissed.

This Special Capture
Photography Exhibition showcases compelling photographic
and video works that encourage viewers to contemplate deeply the current social,
political, and environmental state of the world. It brings attention to the painful past
linked with colonial injustice, social discrimination, intergenerational experiences,
and environmental devastation, calling for justice and a sustainable future.

Otherwise Disregarded is co-organized by the Audain Art Museum and Capture
Photography Festival. The exhibition is curated by Kiriko Watanabe, Gail & Stephen
A. Jarislowsky Curator, Audain Art Museum and Emmy Lee Wall, Executive Director
and Chief Curator, Capture Photography Festival as a Featured Exhibition for the
2024 Festival.

Learn more: https://audainartmuseum.com/exhibitions/otherwise-disregarded-
whistler/

Public Artist Discussion " Otherwise Disregarded

Sunday, April 21 from 11:30am " 1:00pm
Audain Art Museum (4350 Blackcomb Way, Whistler)

The public is warmly invited to join the
Audain Art Museum for an informal discussion
with participating artists in What Fools These Mortals Be, the video installation
directed by Adad Hannah that is featured as part of the Otherwise Disregarded
exhibition.

What Fools These Mortals Be reimagines Shakespeares A Midsummer Nights
Dream as a series of tableaux vivants co-created and performed by 14 formerly-
incarcerated women.

This work was commissioned by The Circle Project, an evolving collaboration of
artists and formerly incarcerated women in the Vancouver region dedicated to
producing provocative art together.
