Tribute to Elisapee Ishulutaq, CM 

Join us as we pay tribute to the late Elisapee Ishulutaq.

Saturday, March 9, 1:00 to 3:00 pm. Remarks at 1:30

On Saturday, March 9th from 1-3 pm, the Marion Scott Gallery will host 
a special tribute to Elisapee Ishulutaq. Remarks will start at 1:30 pm.
The Marion Scott Gallery is pleased to present an exhibition featuring 
the printed editions of the late Elisapee Ishulutaq. Focussing on works 
produced between 1996 and 2018, Stories of My Life: Print Editions by 
Elisapee Ishulutaq will comprise 16 images, showcasing the artists 
deep commitment to the medium of etching in the last decade of her 
life. Produced in collaboration with Montrals Studio PM, many of the 
works are monumentally scaled and innovatively conceived.

Ishulutaqs images are based on her memories of growing up on 
southern Baffin Island at a time when the regions Inuit inhabitants still 
lived according to their seasonal traditions. Several works in the 
exhibition portray forms of marine transportation, a theme that was 
always important to Ishulutaq. On My Fathers Kayak shows a man 
paddling a traditional skin kayak while a second smaller figure lies face 
down just behind. Other prints show different kinds of wooden boats 
with multiple passengers. These vessels, with their wooden 
construction and modern features had been introduced to the Inuit by 
the traders and whalers who began coming to the Cumberland Sound 
area in the early 1990s and were successfully adapted for indigenous 
use.

Other images in the exhibition fall into the category of portraits of family 
life. Ishulutaqs large-format monochrome family triptych series 
presents her childhood memory of her father, mother and sister, each 
portrayed as a powerful single silhouette. Playing Inside the Igloo, a 
monumental print comprised of four separate panels, portrays 
Ishulutaq and her sister at play indoors while their mother tends to the 
cooking. Other works show people carrying gear by foot and 
performing various tasks associated with life on the land.

In a 2014 filmed interview, Ishulutaq stated: I want people to know 
what we used to do in the olden days, how we used to live back then. 
Thats what Im drawing now. Picturing the life and memories that she 
knew from her childhood, the works in Stories of My Life constitute the 
late Pangnirtung artists boldly expressive and deeply personal tribute 
to the traditions of Inuit culture and their ongoing relevance in todays 
world.
