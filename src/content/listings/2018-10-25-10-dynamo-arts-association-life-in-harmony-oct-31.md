Life In Harmony
Lyndsay Pomerantz, J.J.H.Lee, Nick O'Connell
Organized by Michael Lachman

Dynamo Arts Association
November 1-5, viewings by appointment
Opening Reception: October 31, 6pm

This is an appropriated excerpt from an original text. The italicized words have been 
replaced from the original.

Along these lines, it seems as though life is a construct of various placeholders that fuel 
the habitual practice of banality as meaningful. Its like an empty container that could 
change the read of the content within the vessel simply by altering its facade. If the 
content within is equal among many different products, then the labeling of the product 
functions as a heuristic, in order to further enjoy its contents. To further the argument 
around festivities, one must distinguish the function of this placeholder. 

Social practice and culture isnt a singular notion; therefore, the relationship between the 
providing and the receiving end should be defined. The enjoyment and the duty of 
preserving festivities do not necessarily lie on the subject who is receiving the gift. 
Rather it is those who are gifting that continue the desire to pass on the faith of 
festivities. There is the contradiction of continuing this falsehood when the giver also 
acknowledges its folly. Such contradiction of belief is inherited by the next generation 
but perhaps it is precisely how we enjoy these events..

We know exactly what were doing"know what it is not"nevertheless we continue, 
steadily.

Despite such logical discord, the will to maintain festivities is woven throughout our 
immediate surroundings. This is the issue of faith. With a leap, one is either a believer or 
the other (non-believer or the other). This does not mean that neither the act nor the 
subject of festivities is separated by their strength in belief. An individuals decision in 
their belief or non-belief is both true; however, the elements that make up festivities are 
composed of a series of fictitious narratives. These fictitious narratives are widely 
accepted as such. Ironically, truth in festivities is a collection of pre-acknowledged 
bogus; its almost perverted to question its legitimacy. But their specificities demand the 
labeling of the containers, which then illustrates the particular festivity. And particularity 
is important because without this labeling, the particularity cannot be established for its 
unique enjoyment. 

But what is this particularity actually doing? The argument serves that all festivities are a 
kind of placeholders in life, like an empty container. Different containers carry the same 
values but their systemic labeling differentiates them. Locating the particulars of 
festivities portrays that such events are grounding points in a day, a month, a season, or 
a year. They start to become hallmarks of time that serves the gamut of experience in 
life. Festivities only serve as a ground to the ordinary and reproduce banality for its own 
consideration.

About the Artists

Lyndsay Pomerantz is a visual artist and curator living in Vancouver, Canada. Her 
practice fluctuates between art making and curating and often the two roles converge. In 
the past, she has made artworks that serve as curated sites, inviting additional artists to 
participate. Most recently, she has been making soft sculpture assemblages which 
contain digital files of her poetry, drawings and photography and highlight the fabrication 
of meaning and value within everyday objects. She often uses biographical anecdotes 
as a starting point for her work. 

In 2017, she was selected to be a resident along with collaborator Michael Lachman at 
the Institut fr Alles Mogliche in Berlin, Germany where they mounted a group exhibition 
titled "Brot Crumbs". Other exhibitions include "One on One: Nachspiel" (2017), 
Koreanische Kulturzentrum, Berlin, DE, "Loving it is Easy, Thats Why So Many People 
Do" (2016), Concourse Gallery, Vancouver, BC, "I was Trying to Describe you to 
Someone" (2015), Aceartinc, Winnipeg, MB, and "Scarecrows Steal Soft Sandwiches" 
(2015), Avenue, Vancouver, BC. Published works include, Pragmatics (2018) and 
ISSUE 404 Magazine, No.7 (2017) 

Nick OConnell was born in Downey, California. He has received his BFA at Cal State 
Long Beach and was one of the selected artists for Virginia Commonwealth Universitys 
Summer Studio Program in 2018. His works are developed through iterative and serial 
practice as a formal drive to some larger recurring interests. They are widely referential 
to histories within art, to systems and ideas outside, and to work produced at an earlier 
time in his practice. OConnells work has been recently exhibited at the Torrance Art 
Museum and is currently working towards opening an artist residency / retreat in the 
Majave Desert, California. He currently lives and works in Lucerne Valley, California. 

Jacob, Jae Hyun, Lee was born in Seoul, South Korea. He has received his BFA at 
Rhode Island School of Design and was one of the selected artists for Virginia 
Commonwealth Universitys Summer Studio Program in 2018. His art practice is a 
constant search for an appropriate medium and research for a conceptual exercise. 
Collaging language and image, Lees interests lie on parafictional mimesis and its 
absurdity that is intertwined in art history. His work was recently featured at the CICA 
Museum in Gimpo, South Korea and juried exhibitions at the Gelman Gallery in 
Providence, Rhode Island. Moreover, he has curated artist-run exhibitions in Korea and 
the United States, engaging in community based works and conversations. He currently 
lives and works in Lucerne Valley, California.
