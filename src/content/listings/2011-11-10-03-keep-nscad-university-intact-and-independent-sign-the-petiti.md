The Nova Scotia College of Art and Design - NSCAD University - is Canada’s 
oldest fine art degree granting university, and the only independent institution 
offering a full range of undergraduate and graduate programming in visual art 
and design east of Ontario.

As you may know, Howard Windsor, the former Policy Analyst with Labour and 
Advanced Education for the province of Nova Scotia, has been appointed by the 
Minister of Advanced Education to examine the future of NSCAD University. Mr. 
Windsor has been given an open mandate by the Minister to propose a fate for 
the school which may include the termination of programs to wholesale or 
fragmented mergers with other provincial institutions. 2012 marks NSCAD 
University’s 125th birthday, and we need your support in ensuring another 125 
years for one of North America’s leading autonomous art and design institutions.

By signing this petition your are communicating to the Province of Nova Scotia 
why a strongly independent NSCAD University is important to you, to the 
province of Nova Scotia, to Canada, and to creative culture far and wide.

(ddexter.mla@gmail.com or premier@gov.ns.ca), and the Minister of Labour and 
Advanced Education, Marilyn More (marilynmoremla@ns.aliantzinc.ca or 
min_lae@gov.ns.ca), on how important NSCAD University has been in 
contributing to the vitality of visual culture locally, nationally and internationally are 
also wholeheartedly encouraged!

ARTS NOVA Bulletin
1 November 2011

The Nova Scotia College of Art and Design is in serious trouble.

Thanks in part to the Port Campus expansion, the Great Recession of 2008, and 
provincial government cuts to university funding, NSCAD has been sliding into a 
deep financial hole for the past few years.

NSCAD is not alone. In an attempt to deal with ballooning university costs, the 
provincial government commissioned the Report on the University System in 
Nova Scotia. Released in September 2010, the report included the following 
recommendation:

Recommendation 5(d): Explore both merger and internal restructuring options to 
address future financial challenges at the Nova Scotia College of Art and Design.

Possible mergers were discussed, and Dalhousie University was identified as 
the best fit.

One year later, on 23 September, as the NSCAD crisis deepened, the Minister 
for Advanced Education issued a press release. Howard Windsor, a former 
provincial deputy minister, was being appointed to work with NSCAD “to ensure 
a strong and sustainable future for fine arts education in the province”.

Noting the appointment, the Canadian Press wrote as follows:

Department spokesman Kevin Finch says Windsor will work with the school's 
board of governors to examine options to help alleviate "financial pressures" at 
the university, including a possible merger with another institution or a 
reorganization of course offerings.

Once again, the specter of merger was evoked.

Three days later, on 26 September, CBC reported that NSCAD “welcomes a 
review of its financial future, but it's rejecting talk of a merger”. The school's 
board of governors discussed the issue and “voted unanimously to remain 
autonomous”.

Serious concern over the fate of NSCAD has led to the formation of an ad hoc 
group, the Friends of NSCAD, with 1768 members as of this writing. An open 
letter on their Facebook page states that:

Mr. Windsor has been given an open mandate by the Minister to propose a fate 
for the school which may include the termination of programs to wholesale or 
fragmented mergers with other provincial institutions.

Clearly, there are a growing number of people who are seriously concerned 
about the future of NSCAD. Are we going to end up with a technocratic fix that 
kills the institution in order to save it?

I’ve been intensely involved in Nova Scotia’s culture sector for 40 years – as 
artist, activist, administrator, researcher, and consultant. Several years ago, I 
asked myself why it is that this small province, with its consistently dismal 
record for funding culture, has such a kick ass creative community. Based on 
many years of observation and a fair bit of research, I came to the conclusion 
that there are two main factors: (1) relatively high levels of federal funding, and 
(2) the Nova Scotia College of Art and Design.

Anyone who has more than a passing familiarity with this province’s creative 
community knows that it’s chock-a-block with NSCAD grads, and not just in the 
obvious fields of visual art and design. Root around in film, theatre, dance, 
music, architecture, writing, and beyond, and you’ll find a host of NSCAD alumni. 
That’s because NSCAD doesn’t impart just content and technique – it teaches 
broad creative skills that readily transfer to other fields.

Nova Scotians aren’t the only ones who benefit from NSCAD; its pervasive 
influence doesn’t stop at our borders. Canada and the world are full of highly 
accomplished graduates of this small but mighty academic institution. Indeed, it 
can fairly be said that this exceptional school has an impact way out of 
proportion to its small size and relatively isolated location. (I could have said that 
NSCAD punches way above its weight, but I’m not really big on crude, macho 
clichés).

NSCAD is unique … unique in Nova Scotia, unique in Canada, unique in the 
world. The roots of this exceptionalism can be traced back to the presidency of 
Garry Neill Kennedy when NSCAD became an early epicenter of conceptual art. 
The school gained an international reputation, and the highly regarded magazine, 
Art in America, suggested that NSCAD might just be “the best art school in North 
America.”

Without NSCAD, the creative sector, the creative economy, and most of all, the 
cultural life of Nova Scotia would be greatly diminished.

Given its unique mandate, history, and institutional culture, merger with a much 
larger institution, especially Dalhousie University, would signal the diminution, if 
not the death, of NSCAD. Let’s not forget that Dal tried to shut down its art 
gallery a few years backs, but the arts community pushed back hard, and the 
gallery was saved. Indeed, it has flourished.

Much the same thing needs to happen to ensure the future of NSCAD. Howard 
Windsor’s report is due at the end of this month. Now is the time to let the 
politicians, the bureaucrats, the technocrats, and the bean counters know that 
they (we!) have a unique resource in NSCAD, and that it must be preserved and 
enhanced as a gem in the crown of Nova Scotia’s postsecondary institutions.

Make your thoughts known by writing to Premier Dexter (premier@gov.ns.ca) 
and Minister of Advanced Education, Marilyn More (min_lae@gov.ns.ca). And be 
sure to become a Friend of NSCAD on their Facebook page. That’s where you’ll 
find Nicole Dixon’s über clever send up of the Ships Start Here sign.

Make art, not war, eh?
