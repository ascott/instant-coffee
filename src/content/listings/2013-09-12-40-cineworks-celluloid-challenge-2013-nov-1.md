Cineworks Celluloid Challenge 2013

Year of Disorder

Cineworks Annex is becoming Vancouver’s go-to artistic destination for all 
things analogue. As a shared studio environment and experimental laboratory, 
The Annex is one of the few artist-run film labs operating in North America and 
is largely run by a dedicated community of celluloid artists and enthusiasts. 

To celebrate the Annex, Cineworks is hosting its first ever Celluloid Challenge! 
As a series of workshops designed to produce a new film work from end to 
end along with screenings, Cineworks Celluloid Challenge 2013 is open to 
anyone interested in expanded cinema. Participants are encouraged to bring 
their own Super 8 cameras or work purely on expanding cinema techniques 
that do not require a camera at all.

For $50 (Members), $100 (Non Members), registrants will receive:

- One free workshop on hand processing (Either Saturdays, Oct 5 or 19 led 
by cj brabrant)

- One roll of Tri-X B+W reversal film stock

- 3 hours of complimentary darkroom time or in-house Cramer processing 
service*

- A public screening of your film on Friday, November 1

- Free entry to a special screening selected by Process Reversal on 
Saturday, Nov 2

- Members rate for Masterclass workshop by Kevin Rice on Saturday, Nov 2

Open to all Vancouver residents. Limited number of registrations.

Bio: For the past decade, film artist cj brabant has explored the regeneration 
and reformatting of the small-gauge film image. Working to make the familiar 
unfamiliar, brabant creates visual abstractions that yield the unseen and unfelt 
by means of mechanical and DIY processes that fracture, disassemble, and 
reassemble isolated moments. 

Bio: Kevin Rice is a ‘film archivist’ whose practice focuses on the study of 
photochemical theories, the development of lab resources for filmmakers, and 
the documentation of various darkroom odysseys on motion picture film. He 
has worked with several artist-run film labs including no.w.here (London), 
L’Abominable (Paris), Klubvizija (Zagreb), and LaborBerlin (Berlin). In 2012, he 
helped found Process Reversal, a film collective dedicated to producing 
resources for filmmakers and film labs. His most recent work includes the 
engineering of a black and white reversal process based on the properties of 
seawater for an adaptation of Homer’s Epic Cycle.
