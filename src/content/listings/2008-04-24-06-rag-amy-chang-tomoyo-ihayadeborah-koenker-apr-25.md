The Richmond Art Gallery presents:

Amy Chang: Donated Organ  +  Tomoyo Ihaya: Water, Rice and Bowl  (in gallery 1)

Deborah Koenker: Missing / Las Desaparecidas  (in gallery 2)

Exhibition Dates: Saturday 26 April - Sunday 6 July 2008
Opening Reception: Friday 25 April  6:30 - 9:30pm
Artists' Tours: Saturday 24 May 2 pm
Exhibition text available


In recognition of the City of Richmond's Asian Heritage Month, RAG presents 3 exhibitions exploring aspects of our world's ever-changing conditions. The work presented reflects on our responsibilities as global citizens, as the flow of people, goods, and information becomes ever more rapid.

Amy Chang's Donated Organ and Tomoyo Ihaya's Water, Rice and Bowl consider the implications of a world in which elements of basic human survival have become commodities.

Chang's ceramic objects represent severed human organs. Evoking the anonymous and often destitute people preyed upon to supply a constant demand for organ transplants, they are also signifiers for life, the life-cycle, and social relations: the tubes connecting these organs point to the interconnectedness of human lives.

Ihaya's combination of mixed media works on paper with a video focuses our attention on the process whereby water is collected, drip by drip, in Ladakh, India, for use in daily cooking and washing rituals. This work reminds us of the preciousness of clean water, an increasingly scarce but essential resource.

Deborah Koenker's Missing / Las Desaparecidas memorializes hundreds of young female factory workers who have disappeared from in and around the US/Mexico border town of Juarez. In solidarity with the families of the disappeared, the artist and 84 people from the village of Tapalpa embroidered images of enlarged fingerprints representing the missing women. Including photographs and the names of the missing along with the embroidered images, the installation points to and questions the lack of apparent interest from the Juarez authorities who represent and are responsible for justice and well-being in a town where tax and duty free factories needing cheap labour have radically disrupted societal structures, created danger, and not been held accountable.


Amy Chang received a Bachelor of Business in Taiwan in 1980, worked for the Cloisonné Company for eight years, received a Diploma in Studio Art from Capilano College in 2003, and completed a BFA from Emily Carr Institute in 2007.

Tomoyo Ihaya received a BA in German Literature at Rikkyo University, then attended Mount Allison University's Fine Arts Program, and received an MFA from University of Alberta in 2002. Tomoyo Ihaya wishes to acknowledge the support of the BC Arts Council.

Deborah Koenker received a BA from the University of California, did post-graduate work at Central St. Martins, and earned an MFA from Claremont Graduate University. She is an Associate Professor at Emily Carr Institute. Deborah Koenker wishes to acknowledge the support of the BC Arts Council, the Canada Council for the Arts, and Emily Carr Institute of Art + Design + Media.
