MirNs

March 13 - May 31

Mario Klingemann, Random International, Daniel Rozin, Louis-Phillipe Rondeau,
Kimyonghun Shinseungback, Klaus Obermaeir with Stefano D'allesio + Martina Menegon

Using complex technologies artists from around the world explore the social, physiological
and psychological aspects of mirroring, machine learning, interactive portraiture and
surveillance.

Masks Required

All sorts of things
in this world behave like mirrors (Lacan)

Nine international artists & artist-collectives have created six interactive, technology-based
installations that consider mirrors and mirroring in a changing world of technology. This 'no-
touch', interactive portrait exhibition explores how machines see, reflect and interpret our
biological bodies and faces. And how we, in turn, perceive and interpret the actions,
behaviour and output of these mirroring machines. Through interactive robotics, artificial
intelligence, proximity & motion sensors and facial recognition these works reflect how it is
to be human in a world of technology.

MirNs refers to Mirror Neurons, which are of increasing interest in biology, psychology and
physiology as well as robotics, AI, and coding. Intriguing and mysterious, mirror neurons
have been debated for years. Scientists theorize that Mirror Neurons allow imitative
behaviours, leading to empathy and the capability for aesthetic experience. It has been
stated
that Mirror Neurons are responsible for making us human. And it is mirror neurons
that are at play as we interact with these mirroring works.

The question of what holds and generates value in a world of technology is becoming
increasingly important. The human body and human face remain exemplars of what is
humanbut is that changing? As the boundaries between biology and technology become
blurred, how will our perception and interpretation of what is human change the world we
live in? How will we see ourselves reflected in future?
