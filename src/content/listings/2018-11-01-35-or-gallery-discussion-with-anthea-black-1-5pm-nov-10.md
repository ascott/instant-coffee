In Session // 
Discussion with
Anthea Black

Saturday, November 10, 2018
Discussion Group: 1pm- 3:30 pm
Public Talk and Launch 3:45pm - 5pm

Or Gallery
555 Hamilton Ave
Vancouver 

This call-in is an invitation to queer and transgender Art and Design students to join 
Anthea Black, co-editor of HANDBOOK, for a dialogue and workshop. 
Undergraduate and Masters students from institutions across Vancouver are invited 
to attend. In this intimate session, queer and/or transgender students are invited to 
discuss their lived experiences and current conditions within art and design 
academia. This session aims to serve as a space of exchange, discourse and 
strategizing for a more inclusive and generative learning environment. 

This session will open up to a public talk given by Anthea and reception for the 
Vancouver launch of HANDBOOK: Supporting Queer and Trans Students in Art and 
Design Education. 

Limited space, RSVP to info@artsassembly.ca 
A light vegan lunch will be provided, gluten free options will be available. 

We welcome faculty, arts educators, and cultural workers of all orientations and 
genders to attend the public talk and reception. 

Anthea Black is a Canadian artist, writer, and cultural worker based in San Francisco 
and Toronto. Her studio work addresses feminist and queer history, collaboration, 
materiality, and labour and has been exhibited in Canada, the US, France, Germany, 
The Netherlands, and Norway. Black is co-editor of HANDBOOK: Supporting Queer 
and Trans Students in Art and Design Education with Shamina Chherawala and 
Craft on Demand: The New Politics of the Handmade with Nicole Burisch, and the 
co-publisher of The HIV Howler: Transmitting Art and Activism with Jessica 
Whitbread. Black is an Assistant Professor in Printmedia and Graduate Fine Arts at 
California College of the Arts.

HANDBOOK: Supporting Queer and Trans Students in Art and Design Education is 
a collaborative intervention in art and design pedagogy. It offers faculty a radical 
rethink on how to work with queer and transgender students on their path to 
becoming artists and designers " from the first day of school through 
to seminars, studio classes, and critiques. HANDBOOK draws directly from student 
experiences to help faculty of all orientations bring equitable teaching practices and 
queer curricula into art and design classes. Queer Publishing Project was a working 
group of over 100 students, alumni, staff and faculty at OCADUniversity and beyond 
who identify as queer and/or transgender, active from 2014-2017.
