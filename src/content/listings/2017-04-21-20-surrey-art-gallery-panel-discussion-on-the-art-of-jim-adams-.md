Sunday April 23, 2:30-4pm

Of Milestones and Myths

Panel Discussion on the Art of Jim Adams

SURREY ART GALLERY

On the occasion, of Jim Adams - The Irretrievable Moment, a survey exhibition of 
Jim Adams 50 years of making, the Surrey Art Gallery is presenting a panel 
discussion on the work of this significant British Columbian artist. Of Milestones 
and Myths will feature presentations and discussion by exhibition co-curators 
Jordan Strom from Surrey Art Gallery, Laura Schneider from The Reach Gallery 
Museum, Ontario-based art critic Yaniya Lee, and Vancouver-based artist Sylvia 
Grace Borda. The event is free.

The panel will trace Jim Adamss artistic trajectory, from his early artist books and 
prints from the 1960s and 1970s to paintings on flight cultures, skyscapes, and 
suburban landscapes of the 1980s and 1990s, to his powerful portraits that 
address identity and selfhood, myth and memory from the past two decades. 
With humour and critique, Adamss work poignantly addresses our human 
condition by drawing from world myths and allegories that address themes, such 
as, jealousy, longing, betrayal and anger rooted in contemporary everyday 
reality. Also relevant is his portrayal of Black Canadian and American experience 
from the past half century, especially at a time when racism and xenophobia are 
on the rise across North America. Adams has created a voluminous body of art 
that demands viewers close attention and speaks to our increasingly fragmented 
present, through a collective and shared past.

About the Artist

Jim Adams was born in Philadelphia in 1943 and earned his bachelors degree in 
fine arts from Temple University and his masters degree from the University of 
Pennsylvania. At twenty-four, he moved to California and taught printmaking at 
California State University in Long Beach and drawing at the Laguna Beach 
School of Art and Design. He then moved to Vancouver, British Columbia, where 
he took up a position in the Fine Arts Department of the newly created Douglas 
College in 1970 and stayed with the department when it became part of what is 
now Kwantlen Polytechnic University. He remained there until his retirement in 
2000, whereupon he has continued to make his paintings and sculpture to the 
present.
About the Panel

Yaniya Lee is an art critic based in Kingston Ontario. Her research draws on the 
work of Black Studies scholars to reconsider black art histories in Canada. Her 
writing has appeared in C Magazine, Magenta, Adult, Fader, and Motherboard. 
From 2012 to 2015, she hosted the Art Talks MTL podcast, a series of long-form 
interviews with art workers in Montreal. Last fall, with members of the 4:3 
Collective, she organized the MICE Symposium on Transformative Justice in the 
Arts. Her chapbooks include In Different Situations Different Behaviour will 
Produce Different Results (2013) and Troubled (2014). She is an Editorial 
Advisory member of C Magazine and a founding collective member of MICE 
Magazine. 
Sylvia Grace Borda is an artist and Research Associate at University of Stirling, 
Scotland, and a former, Associate researcher at Emily Carr University of Art and 
Design. Her ability to think laterally across media in regard to how conceptual 
strategies can be introduced into art, photography, and installation were heavily 
influenced by her tutors at UBC Jeff Wall, Ian Wallace, and Ken Lum. Her work 
has been exhibited and published internationally, with group shows in London, 
Taipei, Montreal, and Los Angeles and solo exhibitions in the UK, Canada, and 
Italy. She continues to lecture widely on photography, new media, and imaging in 
Canada, USA, the UK, and the Baltics.

Laura Schneider is a curator and arts administrator with a track record of 
energetic leadership and insightful curatorial programming. She has been the 
Executive Director & Curator of The Reach Gallery Museum since 2015. 
Previously, she held the role of Director/Curator of the Cape Breton University Art 
Gallery (Sydney, NS), and was co-founder, chairperson, and artistic director of 
the Lumire Arts Festival. She holds two degrees in Art History from Queens 
University (Kingston) and Carleton University (Ottawa), and has additional 
academic qualifications in Education and Collections Management. 
Since 2009, Jordan Strom has worked as Curator of Exhibitions and Collections 
at the Surrey Art Gallery. Recent exhibitions include Mimetic Workshop: Kelly 
Lycan and Fiona Ackerman (2016) and Nep Sidhu: Shadows in the Major 
Seventh (2016), and the sound art group exhibitions Sonorous Kingdom (2014) 
and Sound/Tract (2013). From 2004 to 2008, Jordan was co-editor of Fillip 
magazine. He holds an MA in Critical and Curatorial Studies from the University of 
British Columbias Department of Art History, Visual Art and Theory.
The first part of the exhibition Jim Adams - The Irretrievable Moment is taking 
place at the Surrey Art Gallery, where it will continue until June 11. Part two of 
the exhibition launches at The Reach Museum and Gallery Abbotsford on May 
25th and runs until September 3rd.
