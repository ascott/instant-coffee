CSA Space presents:

Tegan Moore
Subtraction Inventories
June 9th - July 10th 2016
Opening reception Thursday June 9, 6-9pm

Fossilized air, secret vacancies, and a breeze.
A plenum pusher. A space full of matter
An interior weather. And a calculated unbuilding.

In her recent book "Subtraction", architect and theorist Keller Easterling
advocates for architecture and building practices to value subtraction not
only as a destructive process, but productive and interdependent with
addition. Her analysis questions the approach to design that so often
begins with a "tabula rasa" or a blank slate. With this comes the task of
exposing and tracing infrastructure and hidden systems, working
conscientiously with what came before, and what is covered up.

"In Subtraction Inventories, an unseen architectural cavity is aired out, a
non-structural ceiling is designed for its own collapse, and eroded
synthetic material removed from the commercial waste stream is hollowed out
to vent untouched interiors on the surface of a storied floor."
Easterling, Keller. Subtraction: Critical Spatial Practice 4. Sternberg
Press: Berlin, 2014.

Curated by Steven Tong
