CALL FOR SUBMISSIONS:
Produce/Produce: examining urban sustainability

Arnica Artist-Run Centre is considering creating an edible landscape in
2010. Arnica is partnering with Interior Health's Food Policy Council as
well as other organizations.
Background: Artist activists, interested in edible landscapes, are now
recognized by art institutions. In 2007, Fritz Haeg, an architect, created
an edible landscape for the Tate Modern in London. WORK Architecture
Company won the commission from MoMA for a temporary installation in
P.S.1's courtyards for that institution's summer concert series. The
installation proposed is a fully sustainable and productive urban farm,
complete with roaming chickens. Cities are also recognizing this green
revolution -Artist and architect John Bela along with the organization
Slow Food Nation planned and built a Victory Garden on the front lawn of
the city San Francisco Civic Centre in 2008.

Arnica is also considering holding a parallel exhibition in Arnica's
gallery of work that addresses the notion of food, nature, science,
sustainability, genetics, politics, history and/or ecology. This is a call
for submissions to this parallel exhibition. All media will be considered.
Arnica will also consider work that might be placed in public spaces as
well as performance work.

If Arnica's application for funding grants is successful, artist fees will
be paid.
At this point Arnica is looking for expression of interest. Please send an
artist proposal/statement to stephfarrell@shaw.ca by February 21, 2009.
