Western Front

The final stop on a three-part exhibition tour, I Dont Get It assembles a new body of 
work by Aleesa Cohene. This exhibition grows out of a co-commission and media 
arts residency that sought to effect a collaborative inquiry into racial subject matter, 
constructions of identity, and the role of the producer as interpreter, narrator and 
intermediary of images. As a body of work the show pushes at the limits of what is 
legible within the homogeneity of contemporary white Hollywood cinema to take up a 
persistent view and review of whiteness.

Using her established manner of working with existing movie footage as an 
opportunity to incite critical self-reflection in the viewer, Cohenes project offers 
rhetorical politics as a contribution to what is happening around her. In service of a 
targeted dialogue about white supremacy, Cohene points to all white bodies, 
including herself as she asks: How do I make room for other systems of 
representation to come into being? What is our personal role in maintaining a status 
quo that may be inherently racist?

Visit the exhibition throughout the summer to pick up a series of critical texts 
selected by the artist. New texts will be available bi-weekly on Friday afternoons. 

I Dont Get It is co-commissioned by Gallery 44, The Rooms and Western Front. The

About the Artist:
Aleesa Cohenes work with video, sculpture, and scent reconfigures and reframes 
cinematic material to investigate the clichs, codes, and stereotypes that make up 
dominant cultural narratives. Her work has been exhibited in festivals and galleries 
internationally, with recent solo exhibitions at Gallery 44 Centre for Contemporary 
Photography (Toronto), Gallery Suvi Lehtinen (Berlin), Glasmoog Gallery (Cologne), 
and Reykjavik Photography Museum. She holds a Masters of Visual Studies from 
the University of Toronto and completed a fellowship at the Kunsthochschule fr 
Medien (Cologne) under German experimental filmmaker Matthias Mller. She has 
participated in artist residencies in Canada, Denmark, and the Netherlands, and has 
won grants and prizes in Canada and Europe. Her work is part of the permanent 
collection of Oakville Galleries and numerous private collections.

I Don't Get It is on view until July 27, 2018.
