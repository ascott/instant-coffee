Richard Ibghy and Marilou Lemmens 
The lights constellating one's internal sky

Opening Thursday, November 17, 7-9pm

November 18, 2011 to January 15, 2012

Just as the “Occupy" movement is taking place in cities around the world, the 
Richmond Art Gallery is presenting an exhibition that examines how we 
participate in economic systems – not just in buying and selling – but in how our 
choices implicate us in the global economic network. The lights constellating 
one’s internal sky consists of new and recent works by artists Richard Ibghy & 
Marilou Lemmens. Ibghy and Lemmens address underlying political and 
economic issues that are particularly relevant in the current state of our global 
economy and question the systems and values that have translated to dramatic 
imbalances of economic wealth worldwide.

Employing strategies and forms collected from sources as diverse as scientific 
modeling, telemarketing, the food industry and mainstream economics, the 
works in the exhibition combine text and schematics to make visible the 
multiplicity of practices that constitute economic behaviour and the forces that 
determine and transform value. The works function to disarm and disrupt the 
prevailing dominance of the market economy and question what it means to be 
an economic agent in the 21st century; in doing so they broaden the way we 
conceive economic institutions, laws and values. 

Their work is characterized by a minimalist approach to form and construction 
and their use of materials as a way to make ideas visible. For this exhibition 
works include a mural, video, photography, and works on paper. An 
essay "Copernican Skies" by Antonia Hirsch will be available at the Gallery. Both 
artists will be in attendance at the opening reception.

Richard Ibghy & Marilou Lemmens have been working in collaboration since 
2002. Their solo exhibitions include The Promise of the Promised Land, Station 
Gallery Whitby, Ontario (2011), Demand Curves for Transcendence, Galleria 
Alkovi, Helsinki (2011), Horse and Sparrow, The New Gallery, Calgary (2010) 
and Vernon Public Art Gallery, Vernon (2010), The afterlives of books that set 
about changing society, VU, Quebec City (2010), and Sesame Street 
Economics, Ministry of Casual Living, Victoria (2008).

The Richmond Art Gallery gratefully acknowledges the support of the City of
