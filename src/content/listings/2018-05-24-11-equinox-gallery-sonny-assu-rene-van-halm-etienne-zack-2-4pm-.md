Sonny Assu, Rene Van Halm, Etienne Zack: Works on Paper
May 26 - June 30, 2018

Sonny Assu
Sonny Assu is Liwildaxw of the Kwakwakawakw Nations. Through a variety of 
mediums, Assu merges the aesthetics of Indigenous iconography with a pop art 
sensibility in an effort to address contemporary political and idealogical issues. 
Assus work focuses on Indigenous rights, consumerism, branding and technology 
as totemic representations of identity. Within this, he addresses the ways in which 
traditions of the past have come to inform contemporary ideas and identities, 
particularly as related to the effects of colonialization, and the loss of language and 
cultural resources in indigenous culture. 
Sonny Assu lives and works in Campbell River, BC. 

Rene Van Halm
Van Halms practice is underlined by her interest in the ways that individuals define 
and negotiate private experience within architectural and social spaces. Through 
various modes of painting, including still life, landscape, and abstraction, Van Halm 
considers how architectural space articulates and frames cultural values and social 
interactions. Her work often pulls from mass media sources such as decor and 
fashion magazines, incorporating the often-overlooked fragmentary elements 
present in everyday images: furniture, shadows, vessels, and elements of 
architecture. 
Rene Van Halm lives and works in Vancouver, BC.

Etienne Zack 
The works in this exhibition continue Zacks interest in the relationship between art 
and text. As an additive series, the works are complex considerations of 
architecture, institutional power, redacted history, and the role of language and text 
as intrinsic in the support and applcation of these mechanisms. In this exhibition, 
Zacks images are achieved through a gel transfer process - in which a collage of 
images is printed onto paper using an acrylic gel medium. The works embrace 
diverse media and materials: books, documents and files are stacked, slotted 
together, and carved into letter-like forms. Zacks imagery raises very pertinent 
questions about written histories, enlightenment, censorship, erasure, data storage, 
control, and collective knowledge. 
Etienne Zack lives and works in Point Roberts, WA.
