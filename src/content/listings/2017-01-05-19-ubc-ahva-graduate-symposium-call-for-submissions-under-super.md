Under Super Vision
40th Annual AHVA Graduate Student Symposium
March 9 & 10, 2017
Exhibition March 10 - April 2, 2017
Deadline for Submissions: January 15, 2017
AHVA GalleryAudain Art Centre
6398 University Blvd.
University of British Columbia
Vancouver, Canada

In conjunction with the 40th Annual Art History, Visual Art and Theory (AHVA)
Graduate Symposium at the University of British Columbia, the curators of the
accompanying exhibition invite local, national and international artists to propose 
work for inclusion. Works should address the theme of the symposium:

Surveillance and security are undoubtedly entwined, the need for one rarely 
mentioned without recourse to the other. The rise of global data collection 
machinery can be seen as a technical response to the flood of digital information 
we are all learning to manage. While security at the physical borders between 
countries is ever more stringent, the borderless nature of the Internet provides 
opportunities for the collection and sharing of information across national 
boundaries. Whether it is the use of drones in foreign war zones, or the 
harvesting of personal data via social media, issues of surveillance are both
urgent and historically resonant. As such, artists, scholars and activists are 
responding to these asymmetrical power relations through research and practice, 
documenting and revealing the structures of surveillance while posing critical 
questions and exploring sites of resistance. Considering the importance of 
freedom of expression for contemporary and historical art discourse and practice, 
the 40th Annual AHVA Graduate Symposium and exhibition seek to examine 
issues of surveillance specifically as they relate to creative practices. The 
symposium programming will include an exhibition of contemporary art, a seminar, 
a keynote address, and potentially a film screening. Together, these events will 
provide a unique platform to question what we, as art history students, artists, 
and scholars, can contribute to current debates on the dynamics of an increasing 
hypervisibility.

Artworks may respond to a range of topics and issues, such as:

x Ethical issues of surveillance vs. security-national and personal privacy

x Histories of surveillance

x Surveillance and ideology/hegemony, surveillance, and capitalism

x Surveillance technologies: social media, bio-surveillance, global imaging

systems, border controls, drones

x Policy and law, especially as it affects creative cultures (e.g., Canada's Anti-
Terrorism Act, Bill C-51), profiling, censorship

x Counterintelligence tactics, whistleblowers, disclosure archives (e.g. WikiLeaks)

x Works of art that distort, deny or disrupt surveillance

x Science fiction in art and visual media as an exploratory platform for anxieties

about surveillance (i.e., surveillance and the lens - photography, film, and video)

x Architectures of surveillance

x Sound surveillance - recorded conversations, coded messages

SUBMISSION GUIDELINES:

1. Artist's name, email address, telephone number, and mailing address. For 
artist

collectives of collaborative projects please include the aforementioned

information for each participant.

2. An artist statement no longer than 400 words containing the title of the work 
and

a description of the work. If the work has been previously exhibited, please

include the date and location of all showings.

3. A detailed list of the resources necessary to carry out the work: such as 
materials

and tools; electronic, audiovisual and lighting equipment; and expected

installation time.

4. Design and/or floor plan, including specifications regarding spatial 
requirements,

physical layout, and installation, as required.

5. A maximum of 12 JPEG-format images, or 5 minutes of DVD-quality 
audiovisual

material in MPEG or AVI formats, or 5 minutes of sound material in MP3 format.

Weblinks of the same quality are also acceptable.

6. A brief curriculum vitae (2 pages maximum). Should work be submitted by an

artist collective, please include a brief history of the collective and a CV of each

of its members.

*PLEASE NOTE: Although we welcome submissions from outside of Vancouver, 
we are unable to provide travel funding or assist with the shipping of artworks. 
There may be a small honorarium offered to each participating artist, depending 
upon the scope of the exhibition and funding available.
