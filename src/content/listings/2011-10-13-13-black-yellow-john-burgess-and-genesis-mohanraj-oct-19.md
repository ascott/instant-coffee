"Solos", New wors by John Burgess and Genesis Mohanraj 

At Black & Yellow, Room 106, The Waldorf Hotel. 1489 East Hastings street 
Vancouver, BC

Opening 19th of October, 7pm to 10pm 
“Solos”, explores Black & Yellow’s propensity to heterotopia. For this exhibition, 
the non-hegemonic condition of the gallery as a space within other spaces (the 
Waldorf, A Hotel, a Hotel’s room,) is further complexified by John Burgess and 
Genesis Mohanraj’s site specific installations.

The two artists offer contrasted readings of the site through the use of distinct 
material and conceptual intervention. John Burgess’s “Vertical Developments“, 
utilizes the history of the Waldorf Hotel as a departure point, incorporating the 
various entities the hotel has encapsulated over the years. The structure 
references each entity in chronological order placing value on historical narrative 
as well as questioning the connectivity of the space through temporality. The 
prolific content and immediacy of Burgess’s practice in contrast with the less 
conspicuous aesthetic of Mohanraj’s asks for a drastic change in how the work 
is considered. Working with the idea of template and minimal aesthetic, 
Mohanraj employs structural or formal elements already present within the 
space. "Things aren’t always what they seem/ Skim milk masquerades as 
cream/ A man with a dream with plans to make C.R.E.A.M.", addresses the void 
spaces within the gallery while also questioning formal decisions from an 
architectural standpoint.

While assimilar at first hand, John Burgess and Genesis Monhanraj’s practice 
share a same phenomenological approach to art making. Their interest in space 
intervention is contingent upon a desire to subjectively redescribe places. In this 
way their works can be viewed as an allusion to the impossibility of 
acknowledging the presence of place outside of the “here is now” affirmative and 
intallation art as a means of connectivity between elements and multiplicities.

Curated by Kelin Kaardal & Marilyne Blais

About the Artists: 

John Burgess

John Burgess' work incorporates the passage of time and its socially connected 
history in relation to a space. Commonly working with building materials and 
construction methods to make large 'artifices' that integrate into the architecture 
of the gallery. Burgess has been working in Vancouver for the last 7 years, 
where he attended the Emily Carr University of Art and Design. His work has 
been shown in Canada and internationally.

Genesis Mohanraj

Working across sculpture, where physical things in relation to physical things 
carry on a discourse of recoding previous gestures. Where design flaws and 
architectural facades (scale, lighting, color, décor and so on) become a template 
for investigation. At times, process and spatial alliances evince aesthetics, at 
other times; structures acquire their full significance post-factually (where the 
original is nothing without its repetitions). There is no room for change, only room 
for effect. Where the viewer discovers what I have also discovered, I can ask no 
more from my work than for one thing to increase in one place, and another to 
decrease in another. The void is real.
