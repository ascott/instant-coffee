Writer Carmella Gray-Cosgrove and interdisciplinary artist Jessie McNeil present Out
of Reach Projects first collaboration Tractor in the Birkenstock. The exhibited zine
and wall-mounted photography captures remnants of play and the beautiful absurdity
that surrounds the curious caregiving parent.

With an accompanying essay, the zine Tractor in the Birkenstock revisits a collection
of photos shared by Gray-Cosgrove on her Instagram page during her early
matrescence. A potato peeler in a rubber boot, migrating plastic dinosaurs, or an
abandoned parfait of egg, carrot and apple juice are a few examples of miniature
worlds or the geography of childhood we can witness but cannot gain access to.
During the first weekend of March, the publication will be launched alongside limited
edition prints from the zine as well as a small collection of new photography by
McNeil.

March 1 + 2, 2025, 12-5pm

Studio 8FiveSix

Laneway entrance of 856 East Georgia St., Vancouver BC

Kids welcome

Carmella Gray-Cosgrove is a writer and editor living in Vancouver after fifteen years
in Newfoundland. Her debut short story collection Nowadays and Lonelier (Arsenal
Pulp Press, 2021) won the BMO Winterset Award. Her work has appeared in Prism,
The New Quarterly, THIS Mag, and elsewhere. She is the
Managing Editor at Riddle
Fence Magazine. @carmellaprecarious

Jessie McNeil is an interdisciplinary artist and mother living and working in
Vancouver BC. Through collage, performance, book art, video and painting, McNeil
has been studying the built and invisible boundaries of urban space. Since the birth
of her first daughter, she has focused more intensely on how architecture can exist
for the caregiving body. Her role as an artist-parent has led to co-creating the
Vancouver-based Motherload
Collective and Out of Reach Projects. @jessieavm

Out of Reach Projects mission is to bring artist/(m)others together in a low-pressure,
anti-patriarchal and decolonized space to contribute to the community of democratic
multiple-makers. We make art because of our caregiving responsibilities, not despite
them.

We acknowledge the support of the Canada Council for the Arts.
