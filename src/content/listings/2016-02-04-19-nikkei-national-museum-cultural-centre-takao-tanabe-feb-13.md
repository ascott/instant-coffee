TAKAO TANABE
SUMIE : INK BRUSH PAINTINGS circa 1959-1960

Opening Saturday, February 13, 2016, 3-5pm with artist in attendance
Exhibition February 13 - May 6, 2016

Takao Tanabe is arguably one of Canada's most significant landscape
artists. His works grace the collections of the National Gallery of
Canada, the Glenbow Museum, the Art Gallery of Ontario, the Art
Gallery of Greater Victoria, and the Vancouver Art Gallery, as well as
many private collectors. He is best known for evocative, large-scale
paintings with low horizon lines, primarily depicting the Prairies and the
British Columbia coast . By comparison, this exhibit of 39 sumie, or ink
brush paintings, invites us to experience a delicacy of technique
hitherto unkown to Tanabe's contemporary art fans.

In 1959, Tanabe visited Japan for the very first time on a Canada
Council artist's grant. The sale of a few paintings allowed him to remain
there for two years, during which time he studied with calligraphy
masters in Tokyo, creating an extensive body of work that has never
been shown in full. Travels through the country brought him to his
ancestral village of Shingu, where he painted en plein air. He
describes Shingu as "...foggy...mystical... lovely landscapes" - a telling
prelude of things to come.

This exhibition and companion publication are made possible by the
generous assistance of the BC Arts Council, the Province of British
Columbia, the Deux Mille Foundation, Vantage Art Projects, the West
Vancouver Museum, and private donors to the Nikkei National Museum
& Cultural Centre. Special thanks to the artist, the Nikkei National
