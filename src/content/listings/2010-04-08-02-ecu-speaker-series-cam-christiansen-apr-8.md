Emily Carr University of Art + Design
Spring 2010 Speaker Series

Cam Christiansen

April 8, 2010 at 6pm
South Building Lecture Hall, Room 301

The Emily Carr caps off this year’s Speaker Series with Calgary-based animator and filmmaker, 
Cam Christiansen. Cam's presentation, "Adventures in Indie Digital Film Making," will trace the 
evolution and making of anlanda digital studio. He will look at past work and share a behind-the-
scenes look at one of his latest projects in development and share the challenges and 
technological benefits of being a director/animator in 2010.

Cam has produced animated short films notably for Bravo!FACT and the National Film Board of 
Canada. These shorts have been screened internationally at numerous festivals and had the 
distinction of having been official selection to the Sundance Film Festival in both 2008 and 2009
and recently selected as a Top Ten Canadian Short film of 2009 and previously in 2007 by the 
Toronto International Film Festival. Cam was also short-listed for an Academy Award for I Have 
Seen The Future (2008). Cam’s films have won numerous awards, including Winner of Best 
Animated Experimental Short at the Los Angeles Film Festival 2008, Video of the Year award 
from the Western Canadian Music Awards 2007, 2008 Alberta Centennial Award 2007, 6 AMPIA 
awards for The Real Place, the single most awarded production of 2008 Including Best director 
and Best short. Cam was recognized as an innovator in the technical aspects of animation and 
digital film making by Calgary Inc magazine Top 40 under 40 issue.

Each semester, Emily Carr’s Speaker Series brings to campus the unique and diverse 
perspectives of world-renowned artists, architects, designers, curators, scholars, and writers. Each 
speaker presents their work and invites the audience to participate in a critical discussion. The
primary mission of the Speaker Series Program is to foster cross-disciplinary discussions around 
topical issues in contemporary art, design, and media.

Lectures are free and open to the public. Visit www.ecuad.ca/events for more information or email
azion@ecuad.ca.
