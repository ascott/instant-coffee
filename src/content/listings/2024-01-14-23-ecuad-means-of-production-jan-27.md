Means of Production: Indigenous Patterns, Forms, and Letters with Kathleen and
Christopher Sleboda

January 27th, 2024, 11:00 a.m.

Emily Carr University of Art + Design

Register here: fillip.ca/vkjp

Join Fillip and design educators Christopher and Kathleen Sleboda (Nlakapamux) of
Draw Down Books on Saturday, January 27, in the COMD Studio, Emily Carr
University of Art + Design, for a typography workshop presented in collaboration with
the Jake Kerr Faculty of Graduate Studies.

Christopher and Kathleen Sleboda will guide participants through a hands-on program
intended to deepen understanding of the profound connection between
language,
culture, and design, with a particular focus on the nuances and cultural specificity of
Indigenous patterns from Native North American communities. The workshop will
encourage individuals to consider the stories and symbolism behind each stroke and
curve, and explore the intricate relationship between cultural heritage and the visual
language of typography.

Entrance is free but space is limited, and registration is required. All are welcome;
priority registration will be provided for Indigenous participants.

This workshop will take place over six hours, with a lunch break midway. Refreshments
and a light vegetarian lunch will be provided with registration. Participants are
encouraged to bring a laptop computer. Please contact us with venue accessibility
inquires and we will make every effort to accommodate specific needs.

About the Presenters

Christopher Sleboda and Kathleen Sleboda currently split their time between Boston
and Connecticut. Together, the pair have run
Draw Down Books, publishing and
curating printed matter, since 2012. They also create illustrations (as Gluekit), design
for clients in the arts and cultural heritage fields, and teach at Boston University and
the Rhode Island School of Design.

Christopher was previously Director of Graphic Design at the Yale University Art
Gallery where he worked closely with graphic design students in the Yale MFAprogram.
In 2022, he launched Multiple Formats, a yearly symposium and book fair focused on
artist books, graphic design, and pedagogy.

Kathleen, an enrolled member of the Coldwater Band of Merritt, BC (ne"kepmx),
worked as an archivist at the Beinecke Rare Book and Manuscript Library at Yale. For
the past fifteen years she has designed books and printed materials for cultural
institutions while lecturing and writing about graphic design, independent publishing,
Indigenous knowledge systems, and the preservation of cultural heritage.

Both Christopher Sleboda and Kathleen Sleboda have
been active in the artist book
space for over a decade, organizing and curating fairs (the Otis MFA GD Fair, Odds
and Ends at the Yale University Art Gallery, and Multiple Formats at Boston University)
and participating in over fifty book fairs around the world.
