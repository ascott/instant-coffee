The On Edge Reading Series invites you to hear:

Damian Rogers 
Thursday Oct 18 - 6 pm
at the library, Emily Carr University of Art & Design,
520 East 1st Avenue

Everyone is welcome!

Bio:

Damian Rogers is a poet based in Toronto. Her second poetry collection, Dear 
Leader, came out in 2015 with Coach House Books. She is currently working on a 
memoir about her relationship with her mother and works as the co-artistic director 
of the performance series the Basement Revue.

We gratefully acknowledge that these readings take place on unceded Coast Salish 
lands and thank the Canada Council for the Arts for its support.

Here's the schedule for this fall's On Edge Readings Series:

Thurs Oct 4 - Chris Bose
Thurs Oct 11 - Marusya Bociurkiw & Jennifer Zilm 
Thurs Oct 18- Damian Rogers 
Thurs Oct 25 - Saleema Nawaz
Thurs Nov 8 - Christy + Margaret Fenton
Thurs Nov 22 - Garth Martens 

Bios:

Chris Bose is a writer, multi-disciplinary artist, musician, curator and filmmaker. He is 
a founding member of the Arbour Collective, an Aboriginal arts collective based in 
Kamloops, with a national membership. He is also a workshop facilitator for 
community arts events and projects and does curatorial work for Aboriginal 
exhibitions, projects, and more. His latest book is A moon made of copper, 
published by Kegedonce Press. He is of the Secwepemc and Nlakapamux Nation 
in BC, and currently spends his time in Kamloops B.C.

Jennifer Zilm is the author of two chapbooks and Waiting Room (BookThug, 2016) 
which was selected by the CBC as one of the most anticipated collections of poetry 
by the CBC and the 49th Shelf. A second collection is forthcoming from Guernica 
Editions in 2018. She is presently working on a collection entitled First Time Listener 
and a mash up of Proust and Surrey Girl jokes.

Marusya Bociurkiw, is touring her new book, Food Was Her Country: The Memoir of 
a Queer Daughter from October 8th to 12th in Vancouver and the Lower Mainland. 
Marusya is the associate professor of media theory at Ryerson University, and 
Director of The Studio for Media Activism and Critical Thought as well as an award-
winning and accomplished author and filmmaker. Food Was Her Country is being 
called an un-put-down-able memoir by Cynthia Flood, author of What Can You Do, 
emblematic of a specific Canadian generation by Sarah Schulman, author of 
Conflict Is Not Abuse, and "a powerful whole that left me sad, yearning and satisfied 
all at once by Anne Fleming, author of poemw.

Saleema Nawaz is the author of the short story collection Mother Superior and the 
winner of the 2008 Writers Trust of Canada/McClelland & Stewart Journey Prize for 
her story "My Three Girls." Her first novel, Bone and Bread, won the Quebec Writers 
Federation Paragraphe Hugh McClennan Prize for Fiction and was a 2016 Canada 
Reads selection.
Christy Jordan-Fenton is the co-author of four award-winning childrens books about 
Inuvialuk elder Margaret Pokiak-Fentons time at Indian Residential School. Fatty 
Legs and A Stranger At Home have been added to numerous Canadian school 
curriculums, staged as musical productions, featured in a music video, and 
translated into French and Korean. 

Margaret Pokiak-Fenton, or Olemaun, is the best known as the indomitable subject 
and co-author of four award winning childrens books about her time at residential 
school in the 1940s, the best known being FATTY LEGS (2010). Being Inuvialuit, 
she was born on Baille Island in the Arctic Ocean, en route with her nomadic family 
to their winter hunting grounds on Banks Island. 

Garth Martens won the 2011 Bronwen Wallace Award for Emerging Writers. His first 
book Prologue for the Age of Consequence (House of Anansi Press) was a 
Shortlisted Finalist for the 2014 Governor General's Literary Award in Poetry.
