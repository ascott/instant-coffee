Hank Bull presents
One of Many
(France, 2004, English with French subtitles)
A film by Jo Béranger, Doris Buttignol and Sally Tisiga


Screening and DVD Launch at Pacific Cinémathèque
1131 Howe Street, Vancouver
Thursday, May 1, 2008, 7:30 pm
Tickets: Adult $9.50, Student/Senior (65+) $8



With the gracious support of the Consulate General of France in Vancouver.
A reception will follow the screening.

For more information www.cinematheque.bc.ca
"My name is Sally Tisiga. I am a member of the Kaska Nation, a member of the Wolf Clan, a survivor of the Canadian integration policies enforced towards Canada's First Nations. I am one of many removed"

Sally Tisiga, mother of two teenage boys and a member of the Wolf Clan, Kaska Nation, sets out on a voyage of personal and collective discovery that takes her through the magnificent landscape of the Northern Rockies to Alberta and saskatchewan. She is looking for her own story, for her mother, and a way to understand what happened to herself and her people under the Canadian integration policies that for many decades separated thousands of First Nations children from
their families. The voices of women, healers, and kids take us through the sharp clear cold of the north and eventually to Sally's childhood home at Lower Post. Archival footage illustrates the unfolding of a cultural genocide. We witness confrontations with the truth and the birth of hope, as political organization leads to a quest for autonomy and reconciliation. By 2003, 28% of First Nations children are attending schools controlled by their own people.

Shown in 35mm, this powerful film, exhaustively researched and shot over ten years by two French documentary film makers, Jo Béranger and Doris Buttignol (Zapata's Girls, Rififi à Seattle) was the first to tell the story of Canada's residential school system for theatre and television audiences in Europe and Québec. It situates the consciousness of Canada's First Nations clearly within the streams of capitalism and globalization.

While One of Many has been shown on the reservations where it was shot, this will be its first large scale Vancouver showing.

Doris Buttignol will be present to discuss the film. The screening will be introduced by Hank Bull, Executive Director of Centre A.
