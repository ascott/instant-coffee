Isabel Nolan
The weakened eye of day
July 29 to October 2, 2016
Opening: Thursday, July 28, 2016

Artist will be available for interview.
INVITATION: Press preview: Thursday, July 28, 6-7pm 
Refreshments served courtesy of Jameson Irish Whiskey.

Taking its title from the English novelist and poet Thomas Hardy's 
The Darkling Thrush (1899), the exhibition examines how the light 
of the sun, physically and symbolically, manifests or resonates in 
our thoughts, preoccupations and pursuits. In Hardy's poem, the 
sun, described as "the weakening eye of day" is a dismal star 
drained of its force by a gloomy pre-centennial winter afternoon. As 
its gaze weakens, so flags the spirit of the poet who, until 
interrupted by birdsong, sees only the inevitability of death in the 
cold world around him.

Evolving out of consciously erratic enquiries into the aesthetics of a 
wide range of disciplines, from a scrutiny of individual literary or 
artistic works to cosmological theories or the writings of a 
Renaissance theologian, narrative is an important tool for Nolan.

Across the spaces of CAG, discrete works trace different attempts 
to understand 'our place' in the universe. Inspired by the 
Nuvvuagittuq Greenstone Belt of Northern Quebec, Rock founded 
place, a text written by Nolan, concerns geological time by 
contemplating the oldest rock formation on earth. In contrast we 
might consider a small-scale, abstract painting such as Dreams of 
no thing, no time (2014) that appears to be dissolving before our 
eyes. Combined with other pieces such as the constellation of 
brightly coloured ceramic vessels, Nothing new under the sun 
(2014), these reflect Nolan's interest in the way colour, form and 
materials are used to express mythic, spiritual or scientific 
sensibilities.

Through their subjective nature, the works in The weakened eye of 
day reveal, as with much of Nolan's oeuvre, the inherent and 
beautiful absurdity of attempting to tackle the enormity of such 
subjects through a quixotic process of making. Whether 
monumental or intimate in scale, they are presented to us as 
tentative and precarious markers of the experience of our place 
beneath the sun.

Originated by the Irish Museum of Modern Art, Dublin, this 
exhibition presents a new selection of work in collaboration with 
Mercer Union, Toronto. The exhibition is supported by the Culture 
Ireland programme for 2016 celebrating Ireland's Centenary. We 
also acknowledge the generous assistance of Kerlin Gallery, 
Dublin.

BIO: Isabel Nolan lives and works in Dublin, Ireland. Recent solo

York; Kerlin Gallery, Dublin (2015); Sean Kelly Gallery, New York;

Douglas Hyde Gallery, Dublin; Artspace, New Zealand (2008); 
Glasgow International, (2006); Project Arts Centre, Dublin (2005). 
Nolan represented Ireland at the 2005 Venice Biennale in a group 
exhibition, "Ireland at Venice 2005" and work has been featured at 
numerous institutions, biennials internationally, including LIAF 
biennial (Lofoten International Art Festival), Norway, (2015); 
Artspace, Sydney, (2015), Salzburger Kunstverein, Salzburg, 
(2015; Wallspace Gallery, New York (2014); Palais de Tokyo, Paris,

Modern Art, (2010); SMART Project Space, Amsterdam (2009); 
Beijing Art Museum, (2008); The Mediation Biennale, Poznan, 
Poland (2008), Stroom Den Haag (2007); and The Yugoslav 
Biennial, for Young Artists, Serbia-Montenegro (2004).Work is held 
in public collections including the Irish Museum of Modern Art; The 
Hugh Lane Gallery, Dublin; Allied Irish Bank; Arts Council of 
Ireland; and various private collections in Europe, Asia, the USA 
and Canada.
