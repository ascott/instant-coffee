12. café for contemporary art | Navin Rawanchaikul | til | MAY 3

café for contemporary art proudly presents: 

WE LOVE YOU COMRADE NAVIN
An exhibition of works from Navin Party
March 23rd - May 3rd, 2009

(Exhibition has been extended!)

On March 21, 2009, café for contemporary art, North Vancouver’s newest contemporary art 
space and café opened its inaugural exhibition WE LOVE YOU COMRADE NAVIN. This 
exhibition presents two projects from Navin Party, a globetrotting narrative developed by artist 
Navin Rawanchaikul and café for contemporary art director Tyler Russell. 

A humorously hopeful reaction to the practice of nationalizing, ethnicising, and multi-ethno-
nationalizing of personal identity for the purposes of market and politics, Navin Party emerged in 
2006 out of a conversation Navin Rawanchaikul had with Tyler Russell in a Chiang Mai café. 
Navin had recently been invited to participate in “Diaspora”, a multimedia presentation featuring
“diasporic” artists of South East Asia, part of the cultural programme of the 2006 Board of 
Governors meeting of the World Bank and the IMF in Singapore. Playfully taking issue with being 
labeled a “diaspora artist”, Navin thought it would be fun to slightly destabilize the theme of the 
event and rather than offer a sentimental exploration of what it means to be a person of Hindu-
Punjabi descent growing up in Chiang Mai. His basic idea was to go to India, a country he had 
never been to, and interview people who shared his name: Navin or Naveen. He was hoping to 
somehow link this project to his long held desire to make a bollywood-style music video.

Tyler Russell was, as now, an aspiring cultural worker/curator primarily concerned with how we 
identify ourselves and relate with one another. He was well aware of North American 
multiculturalism’s habit of urging not-white and not-quite-white people to identify as hyphenated
creatures lurking somewhere between ethno-national categories. With this in mind, he proposed 
that it might be interesting to globalize the issue and initiate a campaign to unite the Navins of 
the world under the banner of Navin Party, www.navinparty.com. Navin completely concurred and 
immediately contacted his web guy to reserve the domain name. When the two later searched 
“Navin” on the internet, they discovered that it is not just an East Indian name derived from the
Sanskrit word for novel or new, but that Steve Martin sports the name in the 1979 film “The Jerk”, 
and among other autonomous origins, as a last name it has Gaelic roots. 

From there, Tyler and Navin began collaborating on the production of series of works based on a 
story where Rawanchaikul, a lonely son of diaspora, founds a global party for people who share 
his name. Traveling the globe in search of fellow Navins, the project has gone on to incorporate 
various cultural and propaganda forms into the ever-evolving narrative of the Navin Party. Navin 
Party projects have been developed in India, Japan, China, Korea and most recently for Dan 
Cameron’s Prospect New Orleans in the USA. Works from the series have been collected 
internationally including by institutions such as the UBS Art Collection.

We Love You Comrade Navin presents two works from this series Navins of Bollywood and 
Quotations from Comrade Navin. Navins of Bollywood, directed by Mumbai-based director Naren 
Mojidra, is a bollywood-style short musical film that recounts the founding tale of the Navin Party. 
It was filmed in Mumbai in June of 2006. Meanwhile, Quotations from Comrade Navin, complete 
with a ceramic bust of Navin Party Chairman Navin Rawanchaikul, a book of navinist quotes and 
a series of woodblock propaganda prints features the video of a mao-inspired propaganda 
campaign executed by the Navin Party in Beijing in the summer of 2007.
