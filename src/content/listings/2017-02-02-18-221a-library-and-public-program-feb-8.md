A Parallax Course - The New Romantics: Aesthetic Autonomy in the Age of the 
Wage

This semester at the University of British Columbia, Dr. Jaleh Mansoor has 
introduced a new course examining the role of romanticism and autonomy across 
art and politics. These discussions will be expanded to 221A's program through 
the hosting of four sessions at 221 East Georgia Street on these topics that 
draw from the course syllabus and beyond. Each session will be hosted by guest 
contributor.

"The problem of autonomy has long held a central position in the history of 
Western art; indeed it may constitute "Art," as distinct from craft and technical 
skill, beginning with Kant's Critique of Judgment (1790). No other category has 
been as central or as contested within the period known as Modernism (1860-
1973). And yet when Kant formulated the category "Art," it was understood to 
have a secular and ultimately political value. Paradoxically (or historically? 
Dialectically? The course posits the question), numerous collective struggles and 
political movements of the Nineteenth and Twentieth Centuries, and of the 
present, elaborate themselves under the rubric of autonomy - autonomy from 
the state, or autonomy entities such as political parties or unions. This course will 
explore the antinomic coupling of autonomy aesthetic and political through 
case studies of practices and moments that draw the question to the surface 
most vividly' 

-From the syllabus, The New Romantics: Aesthetic Autonomy in the Age of the 
Wage, Art History 440B; Professor Dr. Jaleh Mansoor, University of British 
Columbia
