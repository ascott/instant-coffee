UNREAL PANEL II / BETWEEN CONCRETE AND ABSTRACT

In the Gallery

In conjunction with the exhibition UNREAL, poet Roy Miki, visual artist
Sandra Meigs and mathematician Izabella Laba come together to 
explore
alternative languages of abstraction. How do abstract ideas become 
concrete
form, and how do abstract forms engender concrete ideas?

Roy Miki a Vancouver-based poet, editor and activist. He received a 
BA from
the University of Manitoba, a MA from Simon Fraser University and a 
PhD
from the University of British Columbia. In July 2006, Miki was 
named a
Member of the Order of Canada for his contributions in voluntary 
service
and arts-writing. Miki's work investigates the interrelation of 
home, race,
poetry, and internment in the formation of his identity as a writer.

Sandra Meigs is a Victoria-based artist working predominantly in 
painting.
She received her BFA from Nova Scotia College of Art and Design 
and her MA
in Philosophy from Dalhousie University. Since 1993 she has taught 
at the
University of Victoria, and has exhibited widely across Canada, in 
Europe
and in Australia at the Fifth Biennale of Sydney.

Izabella Laba is a mathematician in the Department of Mathematics 
at the
University of British Columbia. She holds a MSc (Honours) in 
Mathematics
from Wroclaw University and PhD from the University of Toronto. Her 
main
research areas are harmonic analysis, geometric measure theory 
and additive
combinatorics. She has also worked in mathematical physics.

Tuesday March 29, 7-9pm

OPEN CLASS: HUMANITIES 101 COMMUNITY PROGRAM (HUM)

In the Gallery

Humanities 101 Community Program (Hum) is an educational 
opportunity
available to residents of Downtown Eastside and Downtown South 
who live
with material poverty. Led by Academic Director Margot Leigh Butler, 
this
class invites the public to join in for a chance to listen and engage in
discussions.

Humanities 101 is part of an international movement and the oldest 
program
of its kind in Canada. It runs free courses at UBC and free public
education in the Downtown Eastside and the Downtown South with 
residents
who are passionate about learning and live on low incomes. This 
Faculty of
Arts program offers practical support in a responsive environment to
students and alumni who give their encouragement and acuity freely 
to each
other and to everyone involved in the programme. They offer new 
insights
into the relevance of the arts and humanities.

Open Class is organized in conjunction with WE: Vancouver - 12 
Manifestos
for the City.
