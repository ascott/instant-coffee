A Canoe is an Island: Lydia Brown, Simon Grefiel, davi de jesus do nascimento,
Valrie Walker, and Spepll Tanya Zilinski
Running at Gallery Gachet until March 22, 2024 / Tue"Sat / 12"6
Closing Event: March 22, 4-6pm, with performance by Simon Grefiel and song by
Spepll Tanya Zilinski

'A Canoe is an Island' is a group exhibition of photographs, tapestries, cotton scrolls,
sculptures, and a canoe that explores the multifaceted concept of Vessel". Defined as
a craft for traveling on
water, a container for holding something, and a person into
whom some quality is infused, the vessel is utilized as a unifying idiom to describe
the multiplicity of experiences, realities and relationships that bodies of colour situated
in a post-colonial world have with bodies of water and formation of place.

Addressing topics such as maritime violence, colonization, migration, and folklore, the
exhibition, curated by Olumoroti Soji-George, transcends individual narratives,
showcasing how the Vessel becomes an object, person, condition, and gesticulation of
necessity. The artworks by Lydia Brown, Simon Grefiel, davi de jesus do nascimento,
Spepll Tanya Zilinski, and Valerie Walker emphasize the significance of innate
embodied knowledge, repelling depoliticized definitions. Inspired by the Hawaiian
adage "He wa'a he moku, he moku he waa," the exhibition ties the vessel to the
voyage and place, speculating that bodies in the post-colonial condition are vessels
seeking to make sense of
their realities, recover lost knowledge, intervene in histories,
and foster new relations with place. The Vessel becomes a living embodiment of place,
a voyage, the people, and a physical or spiritual return, envisioning resistance, re-
emergence, and societal reconfiguration.
