Renowned Japanese Sweets Master's
Canadian Teaching Tour

The creation of visually stunning Japanese sweets is a trend that attracts new
enthusiasts daily. Traditional confections known as "wagashi" are undergoing a
transformation led by Eijun Mitsubori, an artist transitioning from music to artisanal
craftsmanship. Mitsubori, known for his silver hair and black kimono attire during
performances, has garnered an international following with his unique approach to
wagashi creation. After successful appearances in Paris, New York, Hong Kong,
Toronto, Sydney, and Shanghai, Mitsubori is returning to Burnaby for the second
time.

As an advocate of "the way of wagashi," Mitsubori will perform as well as teach
classes. His Canadian tour begins with the opening of the "The Superlative Artistry
of Japan" exhibit, in collaboration with the Japan Foundation, at the Nikkei National
Museum & Cultural Centre on Saturday, November 4. Following the exhibit's launch,
Mitsubori will perform his signature wagashi act in the evening.
Three masterclasses
will be conducted in the days that follow. Before his Vancouver engagement,
Mitsubori will also conduct masterclasses at Gusta Cooking Studio in the Greater
Toronto Area.

About Eijun Mitsubori
Eijun Mitsubori, formerly known as Junichi Mitsubori, is the third generation of
wagashi artisans. He also served as the president of the Japanese Sweets
Association Board. As a proponent of the concept "Kado" " the Way of Wagashi "
he views confectionery as an artistic and cultural pursuit rather than mere consumer
goods. With his unique approach to wagashi art, he pioneered the use of a pair of
needles to create shapes and contours that were otherwise unattainable. His work
was featured in the trendsetting So Good magazine in 2017, marking the
introduction of traditional Japanese confectionery art to the Western world of sweets.
Website: [https://www.ichi-ka.jp/en](https://www.ichi-ka.jp/en)

About Wagashi
Wagashi are traditional Japanese sweets that come in a range of shapes and sizes;
some seasonal,
some available all year around. Often simple but intricate in their
design, they are morsels of edible art that channel the beauty surrounding culture
and nature.
