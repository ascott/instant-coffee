Vancouver based multi disciplinary artists Lee Hutzulak and Aaron Read will be showing 
new paintings on paper at Lucky's Comics. While "Let's Happy" (the artists' 2011 show) 
featured grey pencil drawings and sparse colours, "Showrooming" delves into a rainbow 
array of hues, full page compositions, and experimental techniques.

Lucky's Comics: 3972 Main St. (back room gallery)
Reception February 2nd 7-9pm 
Show runs February 2nd-16th 
Gallery Hours @ [http://www.luckys.ca/](http://www.luckys.ca/)

About the Artists: 

Lee Hutzulak pursues several avenues of creative expression including music, song 
writing, video, graphic design, and painting / drawing. He has exhibited his work 
internationally, but mostly in and around home town Vancouver. Current and ongoing 
fascinations include the material nature of paint, an exploration of techniques and the 
aesthetics of composition, centred around a singular subject matter: the human figure 
and relationships. This work reflects an interest in ink, acrylic paints, brushes, dip pens 
and spray paint as the medium and tools of choice. In 2011 he became a father and 
again in 2015, shifting his focus to more immediate demands. Recently though, his 
energy and interest in painting has returned with renewed vigour and intensity. In 1993 
he graduated with honours from the drawing program at the Alberta College of Art. 
Check out www.leehutzulak.com.

Aaron Read is a visual artist and comedian working out of Vancouver BC. He spends his 
time creating album and poster art, making comics, and most recently attempting to 
create full page paintings on paper. Using gouache and gelly roll pens that mostly teens 
and scrapbookers utilize, he attempts to create colourful, underdeveloped spaces that 
exist somewhere between a room and a floating plane. Cartoon bears, circus elephants, 
and vomiting cavepeople are some of the recurring characters used to explore themes 
of isolation, human relations, and comedy/ tragedy. The Paintings are created by 
drawing and redrawing rough ideas on paper, allowing floating thoughts and free 
association, as well as a confined relationship with the act of drawing to produce images 
that are somewhere between a joke and a dream, or a poorly painted story. 
Check out his work at cargocollective.com/aaroncread.
