NATURE MORTE
Karin Bubas, Douglas Coupland, Owen Kydd, Alison Yip
May 6 to June 5, 2010

Opening reception: Saturday, May 8, 2pm

Plainly stated, the genre of still-life is the study of inanimate
objects. Still-life in French is referred to as Nature Morte, which
translated literally means dead nature. However, the innate
qualities of objects (symbolic, cultural, individual, or otherwise)
can fill a subject with personality, lending it human presence and
character.

In her 2003 photographic series Ivy House, Karin Bubas presents
intimate scenes from a Victorian English row house. Furniture, decor,
and items from the interior are captured in soft natural light.
Showing space that is devoid of the figure, her documentations
nevertheless can be viewed as portraits of the former inhabitant.

Douglas Coupland urges the viewer of his Philosopher's Stone works to
consider the stark contrast between the everlasting and the ephemeral.
Of these sculptures, Coupland explains within a series of closed bell
jars the eternal and the ultra-short-term collide and sit locked in a
pas-de-deux in the form of meteorites and ramen noodles. Placed upon
ornate tables designed and built by Allan Switzer of William Swtizer
and Associates, these works take on an air of sacredness.

For Nature Morte, Owen Kydd has re-created a striking still from
Mission, one of his best known photographic / video installations.
This candid examination is a portrait of a lived-in space, the tableau
showing a modest suburban table setting.

Alison Yip's series Parfum presents small paintings of perfume
bottles. These intimate works display her deliberate and observational
style. As the items in each picture are borrowed from different
individuals and arranged by the artist, Yip's paintings suggest
personal representations.
