SCA Studio Series #1

Please join us the evening of Monday, September 28th, 8-10pm, for a 
free event at Interurban Gallery featuring dance works by three recent 
MFA graduates, Daisy Thompson (2013), Luciana D’Anunciação 
(2014) and Emmalena Fredrikssen (2015), from SFU’s School for the 
Contemporary Arts.

In celebration of SFU's 50th Anniversary, the School for Contemporary 
Arts is hosting six evenings of dialogue and performance over the next 
academic year. The SCA Studio Series showcases past, present, and 
in-progress work by a diverse and interdisciplinary cross-section of 
SCA alumni. Taking place at the Interurban Gallery on the last Monday 
of every month between September 2015 and March 2016, the SCA 
Studio Series provides a unique opportunity to participate in / view a 
scope of work by artists from the SCA community since the program's 
conception in 1975.

Interurban Gallery
1 East Hastings St
September 28, 8 - 10pm

Performances:

8:30pm | Daisy Thompson | “The On-going Wonder of…”

This work treads through a collective and individual experience of past, 
present(ce) and future, through combined scores created from their 
lived experiences. Each time performed, the rhythm of their individual 
journeys meets another differently, sometimes in sync, sometimes not. 
The moments of meeting brings them into the present, as they enter a 
dialogue with what the others have to propose.

Concept & direction: Daisy Thompson
Performers and collaborators: Katie DeVries, Natalie Tin Yin Gan, 
Michelle Lui, Ashley Whitehead & Alex Mah

9:00pm | Luciana D’Anunciação | “The Sound Between”

What does exist in the space our bodies occupy? How do we 
experience it? In the performance “The Sound Between” I investigate 
the role of the human body’s perceptive system through the acoustic 
phenomenon known as feedback. Holding a microphone I capture the 
sounds from the room that are amplified by the speakers. These 
sounds provoke movement response onto my body (and the audience 
member’s bodies), which on their own turn provoke noise in the room 
again, thus creating a constant cross-feeding cycle between my body, 
the sound and the audience. I use my body as a vessel for these sound 
loops, as a sentient entity that is compelled and repelled by sounds 
that are never predictable or repeatable. Similarly my performance is 
driven by chance and the ever-changing conditions of the space. 
Overall, I intend to make visible and audible some of the vibrating 
energy that exist between us.

9:30pm | Emmalena Fredrikssen | “Dance Work / Work Dance”

Exhibited as 4 distinct dance solos at the Audain Gallery in May 2015, 
“Dance Work/ Work Dance” explored aspects of dance and visual arts 
practices. In this excerpt I will dismantle my own work and turn a solo 
into a group piece, exhibition dance into performance, movement into 
text and vice versa.

Danced by: Ashley Whitehouse, Layla Mrozowski, Erika Mitsuhashi, 
Alexa Mardon, Isabelle Kerouac and Emmalena Fredriksson

Artists:

Daisy Thompson (UK) trained at Laban Dance Centre, London, and in 
2013 she completed an MFA in Interdisciplinary Arts at SFU. As 
performer she has worked with Trisha Brown, Eva Kargzac, Angela 
Bruguera, Katie, DeVries and Emmalena Fredriksson amongst others. 
As maker she has presented her work locally and internationally, most 
recently at SFU Dance Rep show, Art for Impact, Quest University, 
Firehall Arts Centre, Scotiabank Dance Centre, Interurban Gallery and 
the Anvil Centre.

Luciana D’Anunciação has invested her career in body training from 
butoh to contact improvisation, somatic dance practices and physical 
theatre. Her works have a strong dialogue with video, installation, and 
sound. She has performed and exhibited in festivals and venues in 
Canada, Brazil, Chile, Poland, Germany and US.

Born in Sweden, I am an artistic nomad enjoying movement in all its 
forms. In the past ten years I have lived and worked in seven different 
countries, curiously exploring dance in new contexts and 
unconventional frameworks. With an interest in choreography as a 
relational practice I am currently working with structured improvisations 
for performance, exhibition and social events.
