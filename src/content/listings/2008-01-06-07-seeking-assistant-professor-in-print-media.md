Emily Carr Institute, a leading Canadian institution for the education of artists and designers invites applications for a full time, tenure-track, Assistant Professor position in Print Media, commencing August 01, 2008. (Competition #F004-2007) 


Located in downtown Vancouver on the West Coast of Canada, ECI’s main campus is on Granville Island, an award-winning site of urban renewal. ECI has a culturally diverse student body of approximately 1600 students from across Canada and the world enrolled in studies leading to Bachelor’s and Master’s degrees in Fine Arts, Media Arts, and Design. 


Emily Carr Institute is actively searching for a full-time faculty member with demonstrated ability and experience in teaching both traditional and digital print media. The candidate is an active practicing artist able to work with a range of print media including strength and expertise working with book forms. The candidate is also able to consider and teach visual arts in its broad social and cultural contexts and with a demonstrated interest in collaboration, new technologies and critical approaches to art as a contemporary, cross-disciplinary practice. The selected candidate will provide instruction to students at all levels including graduate studies, be an active participant in the Institute community, and will be expected to take a leading role in developing the program and generating a vision for the future of the area. 


Candidates should have a Master of Fine Arts or equivalent degree, and a minimum of two years of related post-secondary teaching experience plus a distinguished record of artistic practice. This may include exhibition, production, publication, and other related activities. 


Letters of application should address the candidate’s expertise in the areas of teaching, pedagogical philosophy, professional work and community service, including a philosophy or approach to teaching within culturally diverse communities (maximum 500 word letter). The applicant should include a current curriculum vitae, images of work (20 maximum), and, if relevant, copies of theoretical writing or critical publications. Submissions should include the names, addresses and telephone numbers of three people from whom letters of reference can be obtained commenting on the candidate’s experience and professional status.

Works for the festival should examine relations of cross-cultural media - through video, sound, electronic music, internet, print, and writing.  A mosaic has traditionally referred to art which takes pieces of a part to create a whole.  The word has been used in multiple references across literature, media, science, software, video, and religion to describe different constellations of this phenomena.  To quote Sean Cubitt, ‘A newly global connectivity creates new arenas for interaction between science, art, and technology, but also creates the preconditions for global crises.'
 
In recent years many political scientists and economists have discussed the equalizing effects of the internet on global culture. Diaz Contemporary is pleased to present Vancouver-based artist’s Samuel Roy-Bois’ first solo-show with the gallery. 
 
Almost is a collection of Roy-Bois’ axonometric drawings of building structures which he has produced over the past five years.  Presented as an organized environment for the first time, Almost becomes “a substitute city” of office towers and apartment of fictitious origins.
 
Using graphite on paper, the highly rigid affirmation of Roy-Bois’ architectural lines evoke our built environment and consequently allude to the nature of our relationship with it. Challenging idealistic states of mind brought forth by utopian propositions, Roy-Bois’ drawings are rooted in the ordinary, banal and imitation and question how much we are living in failed ideals. 
 
The physicality of the drawing process is important in Roy-Bois’ works. The geometry of the simple structures, their formal plans and the utopian ideals from which they arise react against the work’s messiness and failures. Rendered faulty and slightly amiss, beyond the claim of the “hand-made” emerges a sense of self-appropriation of one’s immediate landscape.
 
Accompanying Roy-Bois’ drawings are two large-scale silver paintings by Canadian artist Elizabeth McIntosh. Complementing the architectural foundation of Roy-Bois’ work, McIntosh’s paintings formally explore elements of line composition and the building blocks of structural components. Rendered with a simplicity of abstract form, the rigidity of McIntosh’s lines and repetition of irregular shapes pronounce her ongoing investigation of geometric abstraction. 
 
Samuel Roy-Bois is a multidisciplinary Vancouver-based artist who is also an active musician and performer. A graduate of Laval University and Concordia University (M.F.A.), Roy-Bois originally explored painting before turning to the architectural dimension of installation. Roy-Bois has shown his work across Canada and abroad. Selected shows include: Burrow at Oakville Galleries (2007), Divertissements at Point Ephémère, Paris (2007), Improbable and ridiculous at Musée d’art contemporain de Montréal (2006), Faire l’indépendance at Montréal’s Quartier Éphémère (2005). 
 
Elizabeth McIntosh is a Vancouver-based artist who graduated with a BFA from York University in 1992 and received her MFA from Chelsea College of Art and Design in London, UK in 1996. McIntosh’s recent Canadian solo-shows include: Young Night Thought at Diaz Contemporary, Toronto, (2006), Parisian Laundry, Montreal (2007) and Fire at Full Moon at Blanket Gallery, Vancouver (2007). McIntosh was also included in the group exhibition PAINT at the Vancouver Art Gallery (2006/07). McIntosh’s work is included in notable collections such as: The National Gallery of Canada, The Perimeter Institute, The Bank of Montreal and The Royal Bank of Canada. McIntosh currently teaches painting at the Emily Carr Institute of Art and Design in Vancouver.
