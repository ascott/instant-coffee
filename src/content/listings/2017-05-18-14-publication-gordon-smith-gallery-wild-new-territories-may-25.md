Thursday, May 25, 2017, 6:30 - 8:30pm at the Gordon Smith Gallery, 2121 
Lonsdale Avenue, North Vancouver.

Invitation to a gala funding raising book launch event for Wild New Territories, 
Portraits of the Urban and the Wild, London, Vancouver and Berlin and Kick off 
of Coho Festival 2017 (one of Canadas oldest environmental conservation 
focused festivals in its 37th year) 

Coast Salish Welcoming Ceremony and Opening Remarks 7pm. This event will 
include a brief introduction to the Wild New Territories Book along with related 
displays featuring the Coho Festival Mural and Banner Projects and Project 
Partners.

This is part of an effort to engage all sectors of community both local and 
international in constructing innovative platforms aimed at creating an informed 
cultural / environmental conservation consciousness focused on negotiating the 
unprecedented urban and wild dynamic evolving in contemporary society.
The Book documents the Wild New Territories Exhibition featuring important First 
Nations Artists such as Dana Claxton and Edgar Heap of Birds, world renowned 
Turner Prize winning artist Gillian Wearing, prominent Canadian Artists Glenn 
Lewis and Michael Morris who both recently won the Governor General of 
Canada Award for Visual and Media art, Vincent Trasov who ran for Mayor in 
Vancouver in the 1970s as Mr. Peanut along with many other award winning and 
emerging artists from various geographical regions.

Part of the innovation of the exhibition (that evolved through three iterations in 
London, Vancouver and Berlin) were the exhibited works that were installed in 
outdoor locales as well as galleries and institutional spaces. Partner institutions 
included, London Wildlife Trust, The Foundling Museum, Museum Natural History 
London, the Linnean Society of London, Simon Fraser University, Vancouver 
Parks, Canada Place Big Screen, Stanley Park Ecology Society, The Botanical 
Gardens and Botanical Museum, Berlin among many others.

A primary goal of the exhibition and consequent book is to engage in a 
multigenerational, trans border, cross cultural dialogue exploring the urban and 
the wild with the expanded vocabulary contemporary art provides. Part of the 
initiative offered workshops to local youth generating art works that have been 
used to engage a larger audience in environmental conservation strategies 
through the Coho Society of the North Shore building on a many decades long 
effort to protect and enhance valuable wild salmon populations and related 
habitat on the North Shore.

Proceeds from the sale of Wild New Territories Text are dedicated to innovative 
art and science environmental conservation education offered to youth and 
community at large in the country of its sale.
To learn more about this project and view a short 5min doc featuring a 100 x 30 
mural that was created as part of this program in 2016.
