COYOTE COMFORTS
Lois Klassen & Maggie Winston

JUNE 18 2009: PUBLIC VIEW 
6.45PM FOR 7.00PM ESCORT TO SPACE (limited space – please register)

JUNE 19 – JUNE 21 2009: VIEWINGS BY APPOINTMENT

www.3084.ca – location / registration / appointments / further information

The coyote has rich symbolic value, more recently standing for westward urban migration, enticed 
toward an uncertain future through spaces opened by the infrastructure of human progress. 
Arriving in places where opportunism and adaptation can be aggressively demanding 
requirements of survival, migrants are confronted with a world of unsettling challenges. Many find 
themselves displaced from states of possibility into the struggles of a gutterscape existence.

Throughout the week of June 15, Lois Klassen and Maggie Winston will enact a transformation 
inside 3084, directly addressing the troubling and all too frequent outcomes of human 
displacement: maladjustment, dispossession and homelessness. Performing in seclusion, they will 
re-purpose hundreds of textile squares from the public art project Lines of Commitment (World 
Urban Forum, 2006), sewing them into a large pile of patchwork blankets. The squares bear 
distinctive coyote prints with color and text added by participants in the original public work – a 
Judith Marcuse Projects initiative designed to give young people a voice in dialogues about 
global sustainability and social justice. 

In what will be the final Coyote Comforts sew-in, following previous iterations at The Living 
Blanket (Centre A) and the Britannia Library, Klassen and Winston will conclude their 
collaboration, re-awakening the possibility in these materials and committing them to action for 
the comfort and shelter of Vancouver’s displaced. On completion of the blankets, the artists will
abandon 3084 as a site of production/performance, temporarily leaving the finished objects for 
open view, accompanied by images documenting their labor throughout the week. For further 
details visit www.3084.ca

ABOUT THE ARTISTS

Lois Klassen produces collective-action projects, performance, video and installation works. Since 
2001 she has hosted Comforter Art-Action, an ongoing material response to human displacement 
that has involved over 200 individuals and groups from over 20 countries, recently featured at 
The Glenbow Museum’s Sew City, Calgary (2009). Previous exhibitions/projects include: Banff 
New Media Institute Liminal Screen Residency (2009); Richmond Art Gallery (2008); CityScape 
(North Vancouver, 2008), VIVO Media Arts Centre (2006), The Western Front (2006), 
Transportale (Berlin, 2003), and aceartinc (Winnipeg, 1999). Lois is currently an artist in residence 
with the Means of Production Artists Raw Resource Collective at the MOP Community Garden in 
Mount Pleasant. 

Maggie Winston is a Puppeteer/ Performance Artist, originally from Baltimore and a graduate of 
Sarah Lawrence College, NY. Artistic Director of Lost and Found Puppet Co, Maggie presents 
stories through live object-animation. Her puppet shows and performance pieces have been 
featured in Earth(ling): A Festival for Youth, Activism, and Art, BC Buds, Vancouver Fringe 
Festival, Memelab, The Western Front, Blim, In the House Festival, Walking Fish Festival. Maggie 
is a participant in Comforter Art Action and other DTES projects such as Vancouver Moving 
Theatre and Downtown Eastside Music Theatre Society. 

ABOUT 3084: SHORT-TERM STORAGE FOR MATERIALISED IDEAS / IDEATED MATERIALS 

3084 is a project space in a Self Storage unit. Complicating the dialectics of waste and 
conservation, the commodification of storage space testifies to the enchantment of things, the 
multilateral power-relations of possession, and the tensions around questions of need, desire 
and responsibility. In this light, commercial Self Storage can be seen as a charged space through
which to approach practices of material production and issues of materiality. 3084 is an example 
of such space and a facility for this approach.

Throughout June and July 2009, 3084 will facilitate the short-term storage of materialised ideas / 
ideated materials by a selection of artists based in Vancouver. These projects will be open to 
public encounter through a series of open storage events and viewings by appointment – please 
see www.3084.ca for details.
