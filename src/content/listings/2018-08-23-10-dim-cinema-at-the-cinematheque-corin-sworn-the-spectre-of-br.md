Corin Sworn: The Spectre of Breath
Wednesday, September 5, 7:30 pm

Fascinated by the erratic meetings and schisms of histories, images and
artefacts, Canadian artist Corin Sworn builds her research into scripts,
sometimes in the form of objects and propositions in a gallery, sometimes
as characters and narratives in a film. The script in Lens Prism, a
fragmented soliloquy delivered by an unidentified man to an empty theatre,
reflects on a common paradox of representation, that we express ourselves
more readily by appropriating pre-existing narratives " here from
literature, theory and film " than by finding the words for our own, most
intimate experiences. The script for Foxes, first shown in the Scottish
pavilion at the Venice Biennale, was prompted by a collection of slides
taken in Peru 30 years earlier by the artists father and developed into a
meditation on the legibility of photographs and the shifting nature of the
narratives we create from them. Lastly, the feature-length script for The
Coat, made with fellow ECUAD alumni Tony Romano, uses an ingenious
subtitling strategy to combine the directors response to interviews with
migrants in Canada and Italy with Sworns research on the Commedia
dellarte, in a loose adaptation of Aristophanes play The Birds shot in
Calabria.

Lens Prism, Corin Sworn, UK/France 2010, 15 mins, DCP
The Foxes, Corin Sworn, UK/ Canada/Peru 2013, 18 mins, DCP
The Coat, Tony Romano and Corin Sworn, UK/Canada/Italy 2016, 58 mins, DCP
