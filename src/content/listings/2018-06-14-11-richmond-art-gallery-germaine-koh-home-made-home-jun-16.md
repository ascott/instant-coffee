RICHMOND ART GALLERY

Germaine Koh: Home Made Home

Exhibition Dates: June 17 : August 26, 2018

Opening: June 16 at 7:00pm & Symposium: Resistance Begins at Home, 2:00 -6:00pm

Vancouver artist Germaine Koh explores social relations and small-scale
dwelling in her solo exhibition Home Made Home. An advocate of creative
space design, accessibility, and sustainability, Kohs compact structures
probe the complex housing issues relevant to the Lower Mainland. Home Made
Home will include several of Kohs structures - a portable, convertible
telephone booth, a service kiosk, and a prototype for essential living
services.

Her newest construction, Home Made Home: Lululiving, is a 160 square foot
micro-dwelling built on wheels temporarily located outdoors near the
Richmond Art Gallery. A fully functioning small home, it is a platform for
public dialogue on the locally and globally relevant issues of housing and
accessibility.

Two separate displays are included in the exhibition that present a range
of housing forms that further contextualize Kohs works: historical images
of do-it-yourself shelters and vernacular architecture from the local
communities history, and contemporary small home alternatives to the large
conventional single-family home. A 240 square foot schematic model built
for the exhibition functions as a proxy living space and houses the
archival images depicting First Nations settlements, pole houses, and
squatter shacks in Vancouver and Richmond.

The second display, comprised of presentation boards featuring small home
alternatives, profiles small, prefab and mobile housing by designers,
builders and activists invited by Koh and include Tiny House Warriors,
Backcountry Hut Company and Lanefab. These projects, both conceptual and
realized, highlight an underlying social and political momentum toward
expanding conventional notions of housing and social responsibility. All of
the projects, local to the Pacific Northwest, innovatively respond to
circumstances of homelessness, eviction, housing affordability and
environmental threat. Home Made Home draws attention to the systems and
conventions operating within the present housing crisis, prompting
questions of sustainability and civic engagement.

Essay by Toronto-based architect Annabel Vaughan is included in the
exhibition brochure. The Richmond Art Gallery received a Canada Council
project grant in support of the exhibition. The Richmond Art Gallery
gratefully acknowledges the on-going support of the City of Richmond,
Richmond Art Gallery Association, the BC Arts Council, and the Province of
British Columbia. Home Made Home: Lululiving is presented in partnership
with Richmond Public Art Program.
