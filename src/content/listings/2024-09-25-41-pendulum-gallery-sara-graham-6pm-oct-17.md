Exhibition dates: October 7 - November 1, 2024
Location: Pendulum Gallery

Elissa Cristall Gallery /projects is pleased to present an exhibition by Port Moody
artist Sara Graham.

Sara Graham's work is primarily concerned with issues and ideas that surround and
make up the contemporary city. Her current
practice is predominately rooted in
drawing and one of her central activities is exploring different ways and means to
transform a viewers perception of place using forms of architecture, forms of
storytelling, forms of mapping or some or all the above. "The Spaces That Were
Missed Have Been Rearranged" brings together 10 years of Graham's studio
practice and her observations of the city. She writes, "The artwork in this exhibition is
informed by my daily observations of the urban form, and how spaces and places
interpolate as one moves through them and where points of views are constantly
shifting and revealing new perspectives".

Fata Morgana is a new body of work about Vancouver created for this exhibition.
Fata Morgana is a type of mirage that produces multiple overlapping images that
actively change and distort creating the deceptive appearance of distant objects. The
work in the exhibition includes photography, drawing, collage and maquettes.

About the artist
Sara Graham uses a
cross-disciplinary approach to her practice, often employing the
methods and materials of architecture and city planning. Her work proposes
alternative ways of looking at the city and its parts. Using the real to construct the
imagined. The results range from paper, sculpture, photography, to large scale public
artworks. Graham's online project, Sculpture a Day (SAD), relates and sometimes
complicates the relationship between encounters with unexpected objects within and
outside of an artistic practice. Sara Graham received an MFA, University of Guelph
(2006) and a BFA, Nova Scotia College of Art and Design (1997). Her works are
included in collections across Canada. Most recently Graham was commissioned to
create a public artwork for the Gordie Howe International Bridge Project connecting
Windsor ON and Detroit MI set to open in 2025.
