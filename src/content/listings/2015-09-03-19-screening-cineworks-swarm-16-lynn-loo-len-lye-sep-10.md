For SWARM 16, Cineworks presents a speculative interweaving of works by
London-based experimental filmmaker, Lynn Loo (Singapore), with those of
the late, pioneering polymath, Len Lye (New Zealand), exploring formal
resonance and interference in a speculative genealogy based on alliterative
coincidence and the pleasure of its incantation.

Lynn Loo made a transition from a music background to filmmaking in 1997.
She studied film and video at The School of the Art Institute of Chicago,
then film archiving in University of East Anglia, and currently works as a
Conservation Specialist at BFI National Film and Television Archive,
London. Loo describes her films as compositions of images and sound that
suggest narratives or convey an event without text or words.

In 2004, she was introduced to films made by makers involved in the London
Filmmakersâ€™ Co-op, particularly those active in the 1970s. This has
influenced her present work exploring the material capacities of celluloid
and the spatial and performance-based qualities and possibilities that
inhere in projection-based presentation. Since 2005, She has been creating
installation and performance works for multiple projectors in collaboration
with British experimental filmmaker Guy Sherwin.

Loo's films have been included in numerous international festivals and
galleries. Some of her works are in the collection of Asian Film Archive in
Singapore and recently in He Xiangning Art Museum in China.

Swarm is a 2-night event that marks the launch of Vancouver's artist-run
centre (ARC) programming season with exhibition openings, performances,
