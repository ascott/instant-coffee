10 November - 17 December 2016
OPENING RECEPTION: Thursday, November 10, 7-9pm

The drawings and paintings in this exhibition are an attempt to solve a problem that 
doesn't quite exist. The illusion is the illusion of painting or the illusion under 
investigation or the illusion of the mark. The paint brush is a magic wand, the mark 
maker deluded, the marks traces of a measured moment. The materials are literally 
the in-between. The paper is from the inserts that occupy the plastic sheets of 
ITOYA-Portfolio Presentation Display Books, before they are filled with 
presentations, or the protective tissue between clear film. The paint is mis-tint and 
epoxy. The problem is to make a hole on the surface of an illusion, a variation of an 
old trick.

Phillip McCrum is a lecturer in the Department of Art History, Visual Art and Theory. 
University of British Columbia. Research interests include the mediated images 
relation to the act of painting and the experience of painting. Current themes include 
the role of 'impoverished material in art making and an analysis of the illusion.
