Born in British Columbia, Tara Nicholson is a photo-based artist currently
living in Montreal. Her new series of photographs and short film
accompanied with a soundtrack continues to investigate her
connection to the Northern Landscape. In the production of this
new work, Nicholson made several trips to the Boreal Forest throughout
British Columbia, Alberta and Quebec, questioning her attraction to
wilderness and its mythical presence within Canada. Her images can
be seen as documents of her own desire to photograph the stillness
in this landscape and an attraction to something fleeting or ephemeral.
Through examining her own emotional connection to wilderness,
Nicholson has become increasingly aware of her desire to occupy a
space within her imagination, one existing between the physical and
physiological presence of the north.

Nicholson is an MFA candidate at Concordia University, and holds
a BFA from Ryerson University, School of Image Arts, and a Diploma
in Art History from the University of British Columbia.

Born and currently living in Halifax, Nova Scotia, Justin Tyler Tate grew
up in Cape Coral, Florida. His artwork combines elements of sculpture,
installation, and performance. Tate is concerned with the relationship
viewers have with art (particularly the position of viewer as the subject
of an art object), how the context of the gallery affects that connection,
and the way in which function is interpreted. This interaction with the
object results in an introspective examination of the body and the
surrounding space. Art Rover and 01 Tête are newer robotics based
installations that use the gallery, art and viewer as points of catalyst;
in the absence of those elements they cease to function as intended.
In the activated space, between object and viewer, the work is left
to create a myriad of associations and possible interpretations.

Tate received his Bachelor of Fine Arts degree from NSCAD University.

BALCONE is a not-for-profit artist society with a mandate to support

Solo show of Vancouver artist David Pirrie -"Codified Topographies" through July 25th - at the 
Douglas Udell Gallery - Vancouver

David Pirrie was born in 1965 in Vancouver, British Columbia. He studied at Capilano College in 
Vancouver and received a Diploma of Studio Arts in 1988. He furthered his studies at Concordia 
University in Montreal and in 1993 received his Bachelor of Fine Arts.

In David’s words…“I often think of isolation, but in a more conceptual manner. This in turn 
becomes a framework for ideas in my art production. Through imagery that has ranged from 
disembodied figures to car crashes to the more recent mountain peaks, it is the isolated subject 
captured in stasis that has become the recurring theme in my 15 years of painting. The subject 
matter often quite literally alludes to some form of impact or breakdown, be it social, 
environmental, or geological. However, my formally precise treatment is calculated to enable the 
imagery to act symbolically, freeing it up to suggest a broader interpretation. Like a symbol, the 
whole construction of the painting becomes a formal investigation into this stasis.”

His works are currently being shown at the Surrey Art Gallery in “Search/Research: Contemporary 
Landscapes. Recently, he had a solo show, “Western Drift” at Simon Fraser University’s Tech 
Gallery in 2006, been part of a group show “Road Runners” at the Kelowna Art Gallery, and has 
his works at Headbones, The Drawers in Toronto. Also, he is in the midst of having a 
documentary made on his work and process by film maker Greg Hewitt.

Exhibition continues through July 5th, 2008.
