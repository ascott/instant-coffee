PANEL DISCUSSION with CANADIAN FORCES ARTISTS

Dick Averns, Ho Tam, Jim Burant

Saturday, November 13th, 2pm

In the Gallery, 3rd floor

To mark Remembrance Day, the Vancouver Art Gallery is hosting a special
panel discussion on the theme how do artists bear witness to conflict?
Panellists include Canadian Forces Artists Program advisor Jim Burant and
two Canadian artists who have participated in the CF program, Dick Averns
and Ho Tam.

The Canadian Forces Artists Program was created in 2001 to record the
actions of Canadian soldiers in Canada and around the world. The program
builds on a rich Canadian tradition of war art dating back to the First
World War. Since its creation in 2001, nearly 40 artists have toured with
the Canadian Forces and produced a wide range of art, documenting an even
wider range of settings where Canadian soldiers are at work.

The panel brings together three cultural practitioners who explore the role

Jim Burant is an archivist with Library and Archives Canada and is a member
of the advisory committee of the Canadian Forces Artists Program. He will
be talking about the program, and the role art plays in shaping how we as a
society think about the military and conflict.

Dick Averns is a Calgary artist and writer who was deployed in August 2009
with Canadian troops serving as peacekeepers in the Middle East. His tour
with the Canadian Forces resulted in an artwork called War Art Now,
comprising photo-based artworks and non-fiction writing investigating the
role of art and military cultures in the context of the War on Terror.

Ho Tam is a Vancouver artist who travelled with HMCS Calgary from Pearl
Harbour to the Esquimalt Naval Base in 2005. His work, Romances, includes
drawing, painting, video and photography which explore the sea and the
Navy. The project continues Tam's long-time study of gender and race, and
examines masculinity, the military and nationality.

The panel discussion will take place in the Gallery's Bearing Witness
exhibition. Bearing Witness presents artworks exploring the theme of
conflict in a broad range of areas including military, economic, social
and environmental conflict - and includes works by artists such as Ed
Burtynsky, Magdalena Campos-Pons, Leon Golub and Pablo Picasso.

SPECIAL TOUR of BEARING WITNESS

Jim Burant

Sunday, November 14th, 2pm

In the Gallery, 3rd floor

For more than 20 years, Jim Burant has been a key figure in the Art and
Photography sector of the Library and Archives Canada. Drawing from his
expertise as an archivist, Burant leads a special tour of Bearing Witness
and questions the role of the artist as a documenter and a social
commentator. He focuses on photography as an artistic medium as well as a
recording device and speaks to some of the photographic works in the
exhibition.
