Emily Hermant: till your voice catches the thread
Scott Billings: Flat Moon
Chris Eugene Mills: a finely-tuned inference engine thwarted by a painting of an 
ouroboros (in thirty-six parts)

Opening Reception
Friday, June 14, 2019
7pm-11pm
258 East 1st Avenue

On Friday, June 14, three new art exhibitions will open at Burrard Arts Foundation 
(BAF).

Two of the shows were produced by the latest participants in BAFs Residency Program, 
Emily Hermant and Scott Billings. The two worked side-by-side in the two studios at 
BAFs facility in the False Creek Flats. Also opening is new work from Chris Eugene 
Mills in BAF's Garage; this street facing exhibition window displays art to the public 24 
hours a day and showcases early-career artists. The show opens on Friday June 14 and 
runs until July 27th, and is free to the public during BAF Gallerys opening hours of 
Tuesday to Saturday, noon to five.

Emily Hermant's recent body of work, 'till your voice catches the thread', was made 
using telecommunication wires that were stripped and glued together to form a surface, 
creating works reminiscent of textiles.

In 'Flat Moon', Scott Billings uses 3D printing, a meat slicer, lasers, relays, and other 
surprising materials in a show that speaks to the relationship between the physical world 
and the idealized digital one.

Chris Eugene Mills will exhibit an original generative software on 36 monitor screens in 
BAF's street-facing exhibition window. The program departs from found digital material 
such as diagrams and powerpoint slides to create something new and purely self-
referential in an endless cascade of digital mark-making. The work is titled 'a finely-
tuned inference engine thwarted by a painting of an ouroboros (in thirty-six parts)'.

More information about all three exhibitions can be found on BAF's website.

About the BAF Residency Program:
A cornerstone of Burrard Arts Foundation programming, the BAF Artist Residency 
Program offers creative support and professional development to qualified Vancouver-
based artists. Vancouver is a city where the space, time and financial resources needed 
to create art can be scarce, and these limitations can constrain artists, preventing their 
work from reaching its full potential. The BAF Residency Program helps artists surpass 
these barriers, providing studio space, a materials budget, and an honorarium to help 
artists realize new ideas that otherwise, may not have been possible.
