DOWNTOWNSPACE is a new exhibition space based in a street vending location in the 
800-block of Granville Street near Robson Street in downtown Vancouver. Programmed 
by visual artist Germaine Koh and multdisciplinary artist Evan Sahmet (Sky), the eight-
by-eight-foot sidewalk space will host a series of projects in a variety of mobile 
structures, ranging from vending carts or trailers to minimal architectural forms such as 
a small stage. Koh says, "DOWNTOWNSPACE is a site for creative propositions about 
public space and social engagement. In this street vending site, we will trade in objects 
and ideas that tackle the downtown commercial context. Both the commissioned 
projects and the presentation structures will confront urban issues."

For the first season of DOWNTOWNSPACE Koh, an artist known for work concerned 
with public engagement and the built environment, is developing a multi-disciplinary 
program that addresses issues of shared space, social exchange, urban planning, and 
the commercial site. Follow DOWNTOWNSPACE on the web at downtownspace.ca or 
on Facebook at www.facebook.com/downtownspace. Proposals for projects are 
welcome and can be submitted via the website.

The project launches on 29 and 30 December with "Loss Found," a proposition by co-
founder Evan Sahmet (Sky). A humble service cart built from construction scraps will be 
the site for a sort of lost-and-found booth in which visitors are encouraged to post and 
talk about their experiences of loss. Project description:

Loss Found

29 & 30 December 2016, 12 noon - 6 pm

Have you lost something precious?
I lost my koala.
It is the last few days of the year: time for remembrance of things lost. 
DOWNTOWNSPACE closes 2016 with a project that reflects on the passage of things, 
people, and time. At a small service kiosk built from scrap wood, visitors can write, draw, 
post, peruse, and talk about what they and others have lost. You can also send your 
LOST or FOUND notices to info@downtownspace.ca, to be posted on site December 
29 and 30. 
What have you lost?
Where might you find it?
Together, loss can be found or mourned.
