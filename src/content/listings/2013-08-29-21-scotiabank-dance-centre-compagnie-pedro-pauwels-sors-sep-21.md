The Dance Centre's 2013-2014 Global Dance Connections contemporary 
dance series opens with a vividly theatrical new solo performed by the 
inventive France-based choreographer Pedro Pauwels. Sors (Get Out) is a 
new solo inspired by the German expressionist pioneer Mary Wigman's 
legendary 1914 solo Witch Dance. Pauwels commissioned four very different 
choreographers of international renown - Japan's Carlotta Ikeda, South Africa's 
Robyn Orlin and France's Josef Nadj and Jerome Thomas - to create with him 
a striking contemporary vision of a present-day sorcerer, which conveys the 
passionate intensity of the original with style and unexpected humour.

Mary Wigman (1886-1973) is considered one of the most important figures in 
the history of European dance, and her work was hailed for bringing the 
deepest of existential experiences to the stage. Inspired by her vision of 
witches as masters of chaos, Pauwels seeks to explore the positive and 
negative forces of the creative process, delving into the roles of rhythm, 
energy and movement in relation to sorcery, spells and passion. Sors 
premiered in 2013 in France and has also toured to Germany.

Born in Belgium, Pedro Pauwels took his first dance lessons in Brussels and 
won a scholarship to the Centre Rosella Hightower in Cannes, where he 
studied classical, jazz, modern and musical theory. He joined the Jeune 
Ballet International de Cannes, where he worked with major choreographers 
including Dominique Bagouet, Mathilde Monnier and Viola Farber, and also 
collaborated with Karine Saporta, Odile Duboc and Alan Marty Company. He 
started his own company and began to present his own choreography in 
1990, and since then has created a large repertoire of works including pieces 
for the Opera of the Rhine, Young Ballet International Cannes, Opera Theatre 
Massy, Limoges Dance Biennale, and the Opera National de Paris. He has 
toured extensively around France, and to Seoul, Bangkok, Egypt, Tunisia, 
Morocco, Senegal, Madagascar and Mauritius. Pauwels collaborates 
extensively with other disciplines and fields including theatre, techno music, 
hip hop and technology and is highly regarded for his work in teaching, 
education and outreach. www.cie-pedropauwels.fr
