apparatus for the circulation of Indigenous voices and ideas into the air

Duane Linklater with Raven Chavon, Elisa Harkins, Layli Long Soldier and Laura 
Ortman

Opening Reception: Thursday, November 9 @ 7pm

Exhibition dates: November 10 - December 16
Gallery Hours: Tuesday - Saturday, 12-5pm

Free Admission 

Wawatay Radio has been broadcasting across the Treaty 9 area and beyond for many 
years, providing listeners and active users of the network access to Indigenous 
languages of Northern Ontario. Duane Linklater became inspired by the way in which 
the communities that use this network disseminate ideas, stories, and music, most often 
in the Cree and Oji-Cree languages. At the core of Linklaters new installation is a small 
AM radio station which has been broadcasting from his home in North Bay, Ontario and 
which will be moved to the Western Fronts gallery, where it will broadcast throughout 
the run of the exhibition. Linklater will use this platform as a space to give a series of 
invited guests a presence on the radio.

As part of apparatus for the circulation of Indigenous voices and ideas into the air, 
Linklater will launch Ehepik Records, a new label focused on releasing work by 
Indigenous artists. Ehepik will publish a set of four 7" vinyl records that act to extend and 
disperse the sonic space of the installation. The first three records feature poet Layli 
Long Soldier, violinist and composer Laura Ortman, and artist Elisa Harkins. The fourth 
EP includes a musical collaboration between Linklater and artist Raven Chacon.

This exhibition is commissioned by Western Front with support from the Canada Council 
for the Arts.
