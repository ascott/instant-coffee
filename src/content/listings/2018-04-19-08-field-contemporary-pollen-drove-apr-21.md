S.H.

Steve Hubert works in painting, sculpture, theatre, and video. These form a constellation 
around such notions as the studio, history, networks, chance, the shape of thinking, 
technology, and power. Influences include Philip Guston, Kippenberger, Jutta Koether, 
Betty Woodman, Winifred Nicholson, DFW, Venturi, Genzken, Hirschhorn, Bonnard, 
Borges, Annie Dillard, live theatre, hobbies, and the films of Jesus Franco.

Huberts recent play, The Chiropractor, follows a chiropractor named Settee who 
moonlights by sneaking into apartments and cracking people in a special way to prevent 
them from moving. Settees nimble movements, and poems inspired by Ode on a 
Grecian Urn, give way to ruminations on love, fate, theft, and entrepreneurship.

Huberts work has appeared in Akimbo, The Bartleby Review, Young Adult, SETUP, 
LAN: R-T, Decoy, Videographe, The Fillip Review, and Pyramid Power. He has exhibited 
in Vancouver Canada at Artspeak, DUPLEX, Unity, Field Contemporary, 221A, Sunset 
Terrace, MODEL (back patio), INDEX, CSA Space, Or Gallery, Helen Pitt Gallery, VIVO, 
Shudder Gallery, LES, Eyelevel Gallery (Halifax), Jeffrey Boone Gallery, WWTWO 
(Montreal), Ministry of Casual Living (Victoria), and 2of2 Gallery (Toronto). He teaches 
at SFU, Emily Carr, and Langara College.

J.M.

Juli Majer explores heightened emotional and psychological states, imagined worlds, 
and peculiar modes of existence. Investigating fictional microcosms through drawing, 
sculpture, and comics, Majer weaves together visceral abstractions, somatic sensations, 
and inarticulate textures which emerge from relationships between her characters, 
symbols, objects, and environments.

Majer works collaboratively in artist collective Puddle Popper, and is one out of three 
members of publishing group DDOOGG. Currently, as part of her fellowship at 221a, Juli 
has been running a series of reading groups, book library, screenings & events around 
the theme Science Fiction and the Other. Here she is exploring different articulations of 
alterity, other worlds, other beings, other ways of relating to ourselves, one another, 
other life forms & our environment.

D.M.

Deirdre McAdams current body of work considers the effects of digital technology on 
the medium of painting. McAdams is interested in how digital tools and an increasingly 
screen-based visual culture are informing current production in contemporary painting, 
and in elucidating the generative possibilities and philosophical implications of a 
technologically-aided approach. McAdams seeks to ask how does the meaning of a 
digitally-produced painting differ from one made through analog means? A digital 
approach to making painted images requires a shift in perception about the definition of 
the medium in that it brings into question the fundamental aspects of what a painting is 
considered to be and how it should be made. McAdams work is an exploration of how 
those fundamentals (i.e. gesture, colour, line, form, support) are expressed or 
represented through the filter of software and print technology, and how this filtering can 
offer artists new possibilities for image-making. McAdams is am interested in examining 
how the use of software can facilitate inventive perspectives and therefore progress for 
the medium, and in how new image-making modalities can evolve from expansive 
material perspectives.

Deirdre McAdams is a visual artist living and working in Vancouver, BC. She is a 
graduate of Emily Carr University of Art and Design (2008), and the Victoria College of 
Art (2003), where she studied painting. She was awarded an Honourable Mention in the 
2011 RBC Painting Competition, as well as a prize in 2010 from Canadian Art Magazine 
for her writing on contemporary art, and has received multiple grants from the BC Arts 
Council. Her recent exhibitions include solo shows at CSA Space in Vancouver and G 
Gallery in Toronto. She is currently a MFA candidate at the University of Guelph and 
was recently granted the Joseph-Armand Bombardier Canada Graduate Scholarship 
(SSHRC).

A.T-S.

Alex Tedlie-Stursberg is a multidisciplinary artist working across various mediums 
including sculpture, assemblage and video. His work has been exhibited in numerous 
galleries across North America and Europe; recent and upcoming exhibitions include 
Holy Wave as part of Glasgow International, Scotland and Everything Flows, with 
Burrard Art Foundation, Vancouver, BC. Currently, Tedlie-Stursberg is an artist in 
residence at the Glasgow Sculpture Studios.

Working with assemblage, cultural iconography and material appropriation, Tedlie-
Stursbergs practice looks at systems of value throughout society, demonstrating their 
interconnectivity and mutual dependency. Concerned with the nature of undervalued 
materials and the political economy of consumer resources his practice regularly utilises 
found objects of low material value, often acquired through thrift, salvage, or trade.

Tedlie-Stursberg continues to produce a range of group exhibitions, for organisations 
including Glasgow Open House Festival, Vancouver Art Book Fair, and Vancouver Art 
Gallery. The most recent of these was Micro-Wave, a group exhibition of artists working 
with found materials presented in re-purposed garage space in East Vancouver, BC.

D.T-S.

Dylan Townley-Smith is a multidisciplinary artist working predominantly in sculpture, 
web, video, and painting art. Recently he has explored creative writing in the form of the 
novel. The subjects of public relations and the semiotics of advertising, along with the 
fetishization of everyday commodities are a starting point for Townley-Smith's critiques 
on popular culture. His work has been acquired into several private collections in 
Vancouver, Los Angeles and New York.

Townley-Smiths work critiques North American social and political issues by 
appropriating and re-contextualizing elements of mass media and visual culture in a 
sincere manner. His paintings explore the profundity of seemingly banal everyday 
objects such as cigarettes and magazines that, despite their commonness, are often 
bearers of cultural significance. He uses these signifiers to centre his work in a relatable, 
cross-cultural framework, while simultaneously presenting more painterly formal 
abstractions to juxtapose and question the objects apparent universality. His physical 
treatment of paint functions as a self-aware separation from the real world that positions 
these images within the context of art history by invoking influential painters like Edward 
Hopper, Giorgio de Chirico, and Alex Colville. His sculptures, or holders, are non-
referential constructions that are formally reliant on the ready-made they are displaying. 
By using the idioms of modernism to engage in subjects of American politics, consumer 
culture and the everyday, he aims to subvert dominant perceptions of images in order to 
create counter-discourse while attempting to critically engage notions of desire that have 
been fundamentally implemented and destroyed through advertising and design.
