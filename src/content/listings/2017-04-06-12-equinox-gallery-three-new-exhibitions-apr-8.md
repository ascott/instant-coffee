Marten Elder: Retinex
Marten Elder sees that the digital camera sensor records the world in a fundamentally 
different way than the traditional emulsion-based film processes. It has only been 
through specific technological efforts that digital photography has come to replicate the 
look of film, itself an attempt to approximate human vision. The parallels and distinctions 
between the way that the human eye and the digital camera process information are of 
particular interest to Elder.
In previous exhibitions Marten Elders subjects have included window views and exterior 
architecture. Retinex sees the artist largely turn his interest to plants, vegetation, and 
the natural landscape, creating photographs that explore how colour is seen and 
understood by the human eye and the digital camera. 
Marten Elder lives and works in Los Angeles, California.

Fred Herzog: Selections from Modern Color
Born in Germany in 1930, Fred Herzog has been taking street photographs in colour 
since 1953, the year after he first moved to Canada. He remained virtually unknown as 
a photographer until 2007 when printing technology allowed him to make archival 
pigment prints that matched the exceptional colour and intensity of the original 
Kodachrome slide film. The exhibition features nearly 50 photographs on view, including 
20 previously unprinted images and a selection of rarely-seen black and white images.

Gordon Smith: With Photographs 
Born in 1919 in East Brighton, England, Gordon Smith has worked continuously to 
expand the dialogue between abstraction and representation. Over the course of his 
artistic career, Smith has become known for a generative practice that sees the act of 
picture making as one that is inherently investigative. At once an educator, collage artist, 
sculptor, abstract and landscape painter, Smith has also made use of photography over 
the years to reimagine the way that images can be constructed in relation to painting.
