David Wisdom: Vancouver 1970 - 1975
May 17 to August 29, 2009
Teck Gallery at SFU’s Vancouver Campus

Artist talks & ‘slide’ shows: Friday June 5 and Friday June 12, 7PM
Room 1600, SFU Vancouver Campus, 515 West Hastings

Reception for the artist: after the June 5 talk, in the Teck Gallery

A new exhibition by Vancouver-based broadcaster David Wisdom offers locals as well as summer 
visitors an extraordinary glimpse of the pre-history of today’s Vancouver. Drawn from his personal 
archive of thousands of slides shot in the first half of the 1970s, the 30 prints in this show reveal 
Vancouver’s strange beauty prior to the economic expansion that occurred with the ‘this town 
was ripe for the picking’ realization of the early 1980s.

These pictures began their public life in a series of ‘slide festivals’ held each year from 1969 to 
1979 with participants including Ian Wallace, Rodney Graham and Jeff Wall, among many others; 
by the time of the last slide show in 1979 the four of them were among the members of the 
legendary rock band the UJ3RK5.

Wisdom worked in colour at a time when 99% of street photographers world-wide worked in black 
and white. He realized that the scene one sees from the street is as important as any ‘street 
theatre’ that one might find in the street. For those working in colour, shooting slides rather than 
colour negative film was common in the 1970s; Fred Herzog, who is now famous as a key 
recorder of the Vancouver scene from 1955 right up to the present, was working almost 
exclusively in slide film for that entire time.

This is the first exhibition of David Wisdom’s photographs in print form.

David Wisdom is a well-known member of the Vancouver art scene and is also known for his thirty 
years of broadcasting on CBC Radio with shows such as Nightlines, RadioSonic and Pearls of 
Wisdom, which he hosted until 2007.

09. 221A Artist Run Centre | HALVING by Joji Fukushima | MAY 22

HALVING
Joji Fukushima

May 22nd - June
Thur & Fri 4pm - 8pm
Sat & Sun 12pm - 5pm

A sharp knife cuts through an apple. Scissors slice down the centre of a clean sheet of paper. 
This is halving; a simple activity that we engage in every day. Joji Fukushima, an industrial 
designer and artist, has developed the project entitled HALVING as a response to this daily 
process. This project explores how the basic act of cutting objects in half can yield new 
interpretations, understandings and possibilities.

A line starts everything in this simple process.

10. Surrey Art Gallery | Michael Morris in Conversation | MAY 23

BC artist discusses the legacy of Pop art in British Columbia in the 1960s

Pop Art in BC: Michael Morris in Conversation
May 23, 2pm
Free admission

How did Pop art influence British Columbia arts and culture in the 1960s? What are some of the 
stories connected with the artists associated with Pop who came to this province at that time? If
the mid-1960s was a watershed moment in the development of contemporary art in BC, how has 
Pop specifically influenced work that has come after? On May 23 at 2pm, Surrey Art Gallery 
curator Jordan Strom will talk to artist Michael Morris about the impact of Pop on the BC art 
scene, and how Pop impacted the province’s media and culture more broadly. Morris will discuss 
his own art practice and those of his peers in relation to the broader Pop art movement in 
Canada and abroad. This event is presented in conjunction with the exhibition Pop Prints, 
featuring works by artists from Canada, the United Kingdom and the United States on loan from 
the Vancouver Art Gallery collection.

About Michael Morris

Since the early 1960s to the present Michael Morris has played a critical role in bringing 
international art ideas and influences to British Columbia. After his birth in Saltdean, England in 
1942, Morris immigrated to Canada where he settled on Vancouver Island. During the mid to late 
1960s, Morris’s painting forged new ground in the field of Pop and Op art. During the course of 
that decade he worked as a curator at the Vancouver Art Gallery and the Centre for 
Communications and the Arts at Simon Fraser University. He and frequent collaborator, artist 
Vincent Trasov, founded the Mail art archive Image Bank in 1970. Along with the likes of New 
York artist Ray Johnson and Toronto-based collective General Idea, Morris and Trasov fused a 
Pop sensibility with the legacies of Mail, Performance and Conceptual art. He co-founded the 
Western Front in 1973, and co-organized art projects there throughout the 1970s. Morris spent 
the 1980s producing work in Berlin, and returned to Canada in the 1990s. His work is housed in 
several major museum collections such as the National Gallery of Canada, Vancouver Art Gallery, 
Montreal Museum of Fine Arts, The Museum of Modern Art, New York and the Ludwig Museum, 
Cologne. He recently collaborated on an exhibition of Attila Richard Lukacs’s polaroids at the Art 
Gallery of Alberta. Morris lives and works in both Vancouver and Victoria.

About Pop Prints

The artists represented in Pop Prints are (from Canada) Greg Curnoe, General Idea, Betty 
Goodwin, Gary Lee-Nova, Michael Morris, N.E. Thing Co., Joyce Wieland, (from the United 
Kingdom) Peter Blake, Richard Hamilton, David Hockney, Allen Jones, Joe Tilson, (from the 
United States) Jim Dine, Robert Indiana, Jasper Johns, Ronald Kitaj, Roy Lichtenstein, Claes 
Oldenburg, Kenneth Price, Robert Rauschenberg, Ernest Trova, Andy Warhol.

Pop Prints is curated by Vancouver Art Gallery senior curator Ian M. Thom, and is organized and 
circulated by the Vancouver Art Gallery with the generous support of Spectra Energy. Pop Prints 
continues to June 14.
