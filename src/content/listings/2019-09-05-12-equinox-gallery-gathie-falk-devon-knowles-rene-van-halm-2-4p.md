GATHIE FALK
September 7 - October 12, 2019

Equinox is pleased to present an exhibition of new paintings and bronze sculptures by
renowned artist Gathie Falk. Born in 1928 in Manitoba, Falk came to Vancouver in 1947
where she trained and worked as an elementary school teacher. In the 1950s, Falk began
taking painting courses, subsequently studying pottery and sculpture at UBC under the
tutelage of Glenn Lewis. In 1968, Gathie Falk was introduced to performance art through
workshops given by American dancer Deborah Hay. By 1976, she had created fifteen
meticulous performances, as well as many of her signature sculptural works including Home
Environment, Herd, Veneration of the White Collar Worker, Single Mens Shoes, and the
Fruit Piles. After many years of working as a sculptor and performance artist, Falk returned
to painting for nearly two decades, producing the renowned exhibitions Pieces of Water,
Night Skies, and Cement. Since the 1990s, she has skillfully alternated between two- and
three-dimensional work, creating her most significant papier-mch and bronze works, and
continuing her evocative exploration of paint.
Gathie Falk lives and works in Vancouver, Canada.

LEAVE THE WINDOW OPEN
BC Binning
Devon Knowles
Rene Van Halm

September 7 - October 12, 2019

BC Binning (1909-1976) was one of Canadas foremost modern artists, architectural
innovators and educators. His early work in the 1940s was characterized by elegant,
expressive yet controlled line drawings, using brilliant colour to express the paintings
flatness as a structural element and emphasizing a strong sense of order and composition.
His interest in architecture led to the design of large mosaic murals for public buildings,
including the British Columbia Electric Building (1955) and the Imperial Bank of Vancouver
(1958).

Devon Knowles lives and works in Vancouver, Canada. She has a material centred practice
that considers the production methods of both materials and objects and how together they
influence and form the language that surrounds them. Her sculpture, installation, public and
two-dimensional work expands on these subjects but is not limited to them.

Rene Van Halms practice is underlined by her interest in the ways that individuals define
and negotiate private experience within architectural and social spaces. Through various
modes of painting and sculpture, she considers how architectural space articulates and
frames cultural values and social interactions. Rene Van Halm lives and works in
Vancouver, Canada.
