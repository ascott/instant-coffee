Radical (Re)worlding: Breaking and making worlds through radical lenses
University of British Columbia
Department of Art History, Visual Art and Theory (AHVA)
44th Annual AHVA Graduate Symposium
March 4-5, 2021

Deadline for Submission of Papers: 11:59 PM PST Febuary 5th, 2021
Breaking and making worlds through radical lenses such as decolonization, abolition,
queering, feminisms (xeno-, cyber-, trans-), and destituency.
To change anything, start everywhere. - CrimethInc.

Radical (Re)worlding is a collaborative, interdisciplinary event, organized by current
AHVA graduate students that openly welcomes contributions on a wide variety of topics,
both contemporary and historical, by emerging academics, artists, curators, and
independent researchers and theorists.
We're thinking about radical ideas such as xenofeminism's post-gender world;
anarchists' dual-power projects; accelerationism's embrace of automation and
rejection
of folk politics; Agamben's destituency; and finally in the academy, the growing body of
thinking around the Undercommons.
Forty-plus years ago, Gayatri Spivak defined "worlding" as the transformation of the
colonized space by colonizers (e.g. through map making). Can we retake or remake that
term? Rather than hit , can we, in fact, do better?
...imagination is a contested field of action, not an ephemeral afterthought
that we have the luxury to dismiss or romanticize, but a resource, a
battleground, an input and output of technology and social order. In fact, we
should acknowledge that most people are forced to live inside someone
else's imagination and one of the things we have to come to grips with is
how the nightmares that many people are forced to endure are the
underside of elite fantasies about efficiency, profit and social control. - Ruha
Benjamin
Another world is possible.

The Department of Art History, Visual Art and Theory (AHVA) at the University of British
Columbia is pleased to invite submissions for the 44th Annual Graduate Symposium
Radical (Re)Worlding: Breaking and making worlds through radical lenses to be held on
Thursday, March 4 and Friday, March 5, 2021. One panel will take place on Thursday
evening, with the remaining taking place over the duration of Friday.
The symposium invites a variety of perspectives and investigations into art's purchase
on these conversations both contemporarily and historically. Questions might include,
but are not limited to the following:
- What are the ways in which art and art discourses respond to, intervene in, or
situate themselves within notions of (re)worlding?
- How does art and art discourse imagine refiguring power relations, social
relations, expanding or discarding restrictive notions of gender, feminisms, the
body?
- How are artists and organizers finding digital spaces productive for this
reimagining?
- What have art and technologies past to offer to the conversations today, or how
can we situate radical imaginings in their histories?
- How might (re)thinking pasts through contemporary discourses create
opportunity for (re)worlding our future(s)?
- How might art/art discourses engage in/with dismantling white supremacy and its
structures through (re)worlding?
- How might art/art discourses create and facilitate anti-racist communities?
- How does art break and (re)make pasts and presents?
- While a grounding in the pragmatic is to be expected, can an embrace of the truly
weird light the way?
In what ways can the explicit prioritization of marginalized epistemic positions, as
espoused by mechanisms such as Border Thinking, function against hegemonic
modes of knowledge production and management?
"If we want to escape from the present, we must first dismiss the settled
parameters of the future and wrench open a new horizon of possibility." (Srnicek
and Williams, Inventing the Future)

The AHVA Graduate Symposium organizing committee invites proposals for fifteen-
minute presentations. In the place of a traditional keynote presentation, this years

symposium will include a roster of scholars and artists who will participate alongside
student presenters on panels organized into thematic clusters. We are seeking forms of
radical (re)worlding from researcher-creators, historians, writers, poets, and emerging
scholars across all disciplines. All submissions must be original. Submissions may
engage with the questions above in a variety of formats.

For example:
- Essays or academic papers;
- Creative writing, such as a blog post, a letter, an interview, screenplay, script,
fiction or prose;
- Workshops, interviews, or presentations.

Applicants should submit an abstract of no more than 300 words in PDF format with the
title of the paper, along with a separate PDF document that includes a 250-word bio to
grad.symposium@ubc.ca by 11:59 PST on January 24th, 2021. Please include your full
name, affiliation (if applicable), and contact information in
your bio. As all submissions
will be kept anonymous and reviewed by a jury committee, please ensure that your
name and the title of your paper are included in your letter of introduction, but that your
name and other identifying marks do not appear in the abstract. Radical (Re)worlding
will include a concurrent exhibition, held both online and in-person, keeping with the
theme of the symposium from March 4"5, 2021.
