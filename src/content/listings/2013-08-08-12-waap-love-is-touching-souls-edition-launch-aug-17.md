Wil Aballe Art Projects | WAAP is proud to launch a photocollage 
edition with Vancouver-based artist, Roselina Hung. Entitled Love is 
Touching Souls, a large-scale version of the work will be installed as a 
piece of public art at Banff Town Hall for 18 months. The work was 
originally created in late 2011 from an old found photograph of 
Quebecois tourists in the Rocky Mountains. While taking part in a 
thematic residency about love and nostalgia at the Banff Centre, Hung 
used familiar imagery to engage viewers with their own personal 
experiences and memories, evoking a common nostalgia. 

Hung draws parallels between the cyclical nature of personal 
relationships and myths and stories that are repeated subjects of art 
history. Her interest in the recollection of memories and the cyclical 
nature of repeating stories throughout history has manifested itself in 
her work in the form of repetition and pattern.

Within this particular work she created a pattern that joins the 
individuals with their surroundings, thus addressing the affinity 
Canadians have with nature. The end result is both a visual love letter 
to the mountains and a diagram of how the sublime environment 
touches us and brings us together.

Roselina Hung lives and works in Vancouver. She received her MA 
Fine Art from Central Saint Martins College of Art & Design, London, 
UK and her BFA from the University of British Columbia. She also 
spent a year abroad at L’École Nationale Supérieure des Beaux-Arts, 
Paris, France. She recently had a solo show Pretty Boys Kill Me, 
Gallery FUKAI (2013), and group exhibitions include Scenes of Selves, 
Occasions for Ruses, Surrey Art Gallery, Surrey (2012); FLOE: New 
Work, SÍM, Reykjavik (2012); The Kingston Prize, Royal Ontario 
Museum, Toronto (2011). She has been an artist-in-residence at SÍM, 
Reykjavik; Ox-Bow School of Art, Saugatuck; and The Banff Centre, 
Banff.
