Terminal Creek Contemporary
569 Artisan Lane, Bowen Island, BC
www.tccontemporary.com

August 25
1-3PM

Serpentine Path is the inaugural exhibition at Terminal Creek Contemporary, an alternative 
art space on Bowen Island, British Columbia. Curated by Patrik Andersson of Trapp Projects 
in collaboration with artist Kim Kennedy Austin, the exhibition features sixteen artists whose 
works snake a tenuous path within the square space of the gallery, transforming it into a 
visual and conceptual labyrinth.

The title of the exhibition is derived from a postcard produced in the 1920s by The Union
Steamship Company of British Columbia. Throughout the 20th century, Bowen Island 
offered a nearby getaway from the hustle and bustle of the mainland. One could say that this 
has been Vancouvers Island of La Grand Jatte, a summer retreat for the rich and a 
relatively affordable and reachable pleasure island for the working class as evidenced in the 
many company picnics staged here. This is not to suggest that Bowen Island does not 
have a more bohemian and artisan history. Most notable is the artist colony set up on the 
Eaglecliff side of the island by Einar and Muriel Neilson which became vital for many famous 
Canadian writers, artists, musicians and intellectuals including Margret Lawrence, LeMoine 
FitzGerald, Alice Munro, Malcolm Lowry and others. In some ways, TCC has a similar 
motivation, albeit perhaps less romantic, to that of their Leiben artist colony " to create a 
relaxed setting for engaged artistic practices and critical dialogues.

Serpentine Path is also inspired by CoBrA, the international art movement that through the
1940s to 60s insisted on the centrality of the margin by establishing artist colonies such as
Bregnerd in Denmark, the Imaginist Bauhaus in Alba, Italy, and the Situationist Bauhaus 
farm, Drakabygget (Dragons Lair) on Swedens West Coast. A more contiguous example is 
also found in this exhibitions inclusion of Ingrid and Iain Baxters N.E.Thing Co., the 
conceptual art collective founded in 1966 (disbanded in 1978) that insisted that Art is All 
Over. From their office on the periphery of the periphery (a suburban house in North 
Vancouver), they created an interdisciplinary practice that ranged from photography, site-
specific performances and installations that has been credited as a key catalyst and 
influence on Conceptual practices globally and as seen in some of the work in this 
exhibition.

The artists featured in this exhibition are Vikky Alexander, Kim Kennedy Austin, Ingrid Baxter
(N.E. Thing Co.), Lyndl Hall, Jack Jeffrey, Andrew Kent, Ken Lum, Myfanwy MacLeod, 
Michael Morris, Ryan Quast, Kerri Reid, Sylvain Sailly, Dan Siney, Erica Stocking, Ron 
Terada and Neil Wedman.

Serpentine Path runs from August 25th to September 16. The gallery is open Fridays,
Saturdays, and Sundays from 12:00 to 4:00 p.m.during the dates of the exhibition or by
appointment.
