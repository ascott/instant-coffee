A full-day workshop introducing participants to the Chromaflex experimental
colour film processing technique, using 16mm motion picture colour film.

Sunday Jun 10; 10.00am - 6.00pm
Cineworks Annex, 235 Alexander Street (driveway entrance),
Vancouver, V6A 1C1

This full-day workshop will take participants step by step through the
stunning photo-chemical technique mastered by Australian experimental
filmmakers Richard Tuohy and Dianna Barrie, which allows selectively
controlled negative and positive colour and black and white images on the
same strip of film.

The technique allows us to make selection decisions in full light with the
images visible to the naked eye. Areas of black, clear, colour negative,
colour positive and glorious black and white can be created all within a
single frame of a camera-captured image.

The workshop begins with an introduction to the principles of the process,
and participants will then collaborate on shooting on colour reversal and
colour negative film stock outside, from which a freshly made colour print
will be made. Instead of processing this with conventional industrial
methods, a novel processing sequence is undertaken that will produce an odd
looking black and white image on the film. Participants then select various
areas on that print to end up as positive colour, negative colour, leave
black and white or process as black or clear film. Those areas will be
selectively covered with special chemical blocking tape. Then the film will
be selectively bathed in an assortment of colour chemistry before removing
the tape and bathing in more chemistry.

As well as engaging with a great experimental film technique, this workshop
is a great opportunity to learn in detail how colour film works. All
chemistry and materials are supplied, as well as full access to the
darkroom throughout the workshop. Workshop results will be screened on site
at the end of the session!

Instructor bio
Jack Beatty has been an experimental photographer for almost thirty years
and has extensive knowledge of analog technology. As the control analyst
for Technicolor (formerly Alpha Cine) he has known Cineworks since its
early days and assisted many members with their work. A long-time member of
the organization, he has served on the board several terms as Vice
President. As a Board member and volunteer, Jack has been instrumental in
the development of Cineworks Annex analogue film facilities.
