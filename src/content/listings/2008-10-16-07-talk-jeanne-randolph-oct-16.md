Public Talk

Jeanne Randolph
The Thrill is Gone

Thursday, October 16 at 7pm
at the Vancouver Art Gallery

Jeanne Randolph's copiously illustrated talk, The Thrill is Gone, will
explore the ironies, utopian remnants and dilemmas of representation
relevant to 21st century feminist art.

Since the publication of Psychoanalysis & Synchronized Swimming in 1991
Dr. Jeanne Randolph has continued to write about the intertwining of art
and such major themes as technology, advertising, psychoanalytic theory,
the politics of representation, and mass-mediated phenomena. Her most
recent book Ethics of Luxury: Materialism and Imagination was launches this
month at Artspeak Gallery on October 8th.

Free with Gallery admission.

       LANDON MACKENZIE – Neurotransmitter
 
From October 17 to November 8, Art45 is pleased to present recent paintings from Canadian artist Landon Mackenzie. Neurotransmitter brings together small oil paintings as well as Mackenzie's trademark large-scale works. The exhibition focuses on the artist's fascination with temporal networks and fictitious mapping of the nervous system. Skillfully using bold colors and complex patterns, the artist continues to link the intriguing spheres of landscape, memory and metabolic systems.
 
Landon Mackenzie studied at the Nova Scotia College of Art and Design and received her Masters of Fine Arts from Concordia University in Montreal where she also began her teaching career. She won First Prize for her "Lost River Series" in the Third Quebec Biennial of Painting. Her work is included in the collections of the National Gallery of Canada, the Vancouver Art Gallery, the Art Gallery of Ontario, the Montreal Museum of Fine Arts and the Musée d'art contemporain de Montreal among others. Mackenzie is on the faculty of the Emily Carr Institute of Art + Design in Vancouver.

The model is subject to series of definition and images. One can categorize
it in two ways: on one hand as a formal model based on the definition of
container space, fixed and autonomous, and on the other, the relative,
evolutionary model with coexistence of times and spaces. These two forms
are not necessarily in opposition: sometimes, they complement one another
or return one to the other. As a symbolic space, the model questions our
relation with reality. It projects us in a hypothetical space where time is
fixed. For the artists, it makes it possible to push back the material or
financial constraints of a project. It is also a platform for testing new
ideas just like a different way to present a work.

In this exhibition, Fraser-Dagenais has three artists explore the
possibilities of the model in a series of actions between himself and the
participating artists. Initially Fraser-Dagenais created 3 models that are
given to the artists along with the above mentioned phrase. These models
will give information on the form, the design, the colours and the
materials of the exhibition models. These models will then be reproduced in
1:5 scale. After having received and assembled their space, each artist
