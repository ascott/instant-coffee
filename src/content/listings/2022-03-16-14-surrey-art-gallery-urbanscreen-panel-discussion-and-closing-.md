Saturday March 19

1-3pm

Surrey Art Gallery (13750 88 Avenue, Surrey, BC)

After 12 years in operation, Surrey Art Gallery has announced that their programming
and operation of the offsite public art venue UrbanScreen, located at Chuck Bailey
Recreation Centre (13458 107A Avenue), will be concluding. To commemorate both the
final exhibition at UrbanScreen, Body as Border: Traces and Flows of Connection, as
well as the legacy of UrbanScreen itself, an artist panel with three of the currently
exhibiting artists will be held at Surrey Art Gallery
on Saturday, March 19 at 1pm. Proof
of vaccination with government-issued photo ID is required for this free event.

UrbanScreen was initiated in 2009 by a group of artists-in-residence at Surrey Art
Gallery: Sylvia Grace Borda, M. Simon Levin, Dennis Rosenfeld, and Jer Thorp. They
envisioned a large-scale, outdoor exhibition site to show works such as their Glocal
project, which invited participants all over the world to submit their own images to a
collaborative global database. Stewarded by them and other artists, along with
technicians, planners, and engineers, UrbanScreen was funded as a legacy project of
the 2010 Cultural Olympiad. When it opened, it was among the largest outdoor art
projects of its kind and has been recognized as a nationally award-winning venue by
the Canadian Museums Association for its innovation. Since then, many other
institutions have been inspired by UrbanScreens programming model: non-
commercial, site-specific, artist-led media visible to a mass
audience. Essays by
different writers of nearly every UrbanScreen exhibition are available in the companion
book Art after Dark: 10 Years of UrbanScreen (2019) found on the Gallery websites
publications page (free for download and available for hardcopy purchase).

Over the years, UrbanScreen has hosted dozens of artworks, all responding to the site
in unique ways. Some connected directly with the architecture of the recreation centre,
while others spoke to the sites history and geography, or even invited audiences to
interact with the work through social media, radio waves, or hand gestures (in 2016,
the site was upgraded with an interactive kiosk, enabling visitors to further interact with
certain artworks).

With an upcoming expansion of Chuck Bailey Recreation Centre, UrbanScreen
programming in its current format will finish at the end of winter 2022. UrbanScreen
may appear in another format in Surrey in the future, pending successful grant funding.

The last exhibition to appear
on UrbanScreen at its current location combines poetry,
artificial intelligence, and biology in an immersive outdoor art project. Artist team
pr0phecy sun, Freya Zinovieff, Gabriela Aceves-Seplveda, and Steve DiPaola"all
artists and academic researchers at Simon Fraser Universitys School of Interactive Art
and Technology (SIAT)"created a moving soundtrack set to imagery of the Fraser
River, the human body, bacteria, cells, and fragments of poetry. Body as Border:
Traces and Flows of Connection highlights the profound interconnection between
individual human beings and the broader ecological and digital environments in which
we live.

sun, Zinovieff, and DiPaola will convene in discussion with Assistant Curator Rhys
Edwards at Surrey Art Gallery on March 19 for an in-depth discussion of some of the
themes and ideas behind Body as Border, as well as its connections with other aspects
of UrbanScreens history. The artist talk will feature footage of Body as Border, as well
as the SIAT
student and alumni film showcase Boundless, originally screened at
UrbanScreen. These short films expand upon and amplify the themes of ecological
connection and self-identity captured in Body as Border. A companion essay about
Body as Border by curator, writer, and academic Shalon T. Webber-Heffernan,
published as part of the Surrey Art Gallery Presents publication series, will also be
available on the Gallerys website. Body as Border: Traces and Flows of Connection is
on display half an hour after sunset until midnight at UrbanScreen until May 1.
