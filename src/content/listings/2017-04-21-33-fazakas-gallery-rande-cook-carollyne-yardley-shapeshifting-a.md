RANDE COOK & CAROLLYNE YARDLEY
Shapeshifting

Reception: Thurs, April 27th, 5-8 p.m.
Exhibition: April 27 - May 27, 2017

Please join us for an exhibition of new collaborative works by Rande Cook and 
Carollyne Yardley. This collaborative effort is an aesthetic response to the 
relationships and exchanges between Native and non-Native communities, and 
how these relationships are healing and flourishing through reconciliation 
agendas. 

---

Rande Cook is a contemporary multi-disciplinary artist of Kwakwakawakw 
descent whose work has been acknowledged throughout Canada and the 
United States. From a young age, Cook was taught the values of art, culture, 
and ceremony from his grandparents in Alert Bay, which has greatly informed his 
artistic practice. His work raises awareness about current issues in First Nations 
politics, referencing movements like Idle No More and the REDress project. 
Through his work, Rande Cook encourages consideration of our relationships to 
First Nations issues and the examination of our roles within the larger architecture 
of contemporary Canadian society. 

Cook currently teaches at UVic through the Audain Professorship in 
Contemporary Art Practice of the Pacific Northwest, a program that brings a 
distinguished practicing artist to teach in UVics Department of Visual Arts. His 
work can be seen in galleries throughout the United States and Canada, as well 
as collections around the world. He currently lives and works in Victoria, BC. 

Carollyne Yardley is a visual artist and former creative director whose work is 
informed by both disciplines. She received a Bachelor of Arts from the University 
of Victoria, where she completed a Double Major in Psychology and History in 
Art. She also studied at the Visual Arts Department (Faculty of Fine Art, 
University of Victoria), Victoria College of Art, and The Ryder Studio School in 
Santa Fe, New Mexico. 

Yardleys self-coined term Squirrealism is used to describe her signature style of 
fine artwork employing squirrels, hybrid humans, and masks. Shape-shifting and 
humanoid forms are further examined in her work through collaborations with 
artist Chief Rande Cook (Kwakwakawakw), reminding viewers that art is alive, 
influenced, and constantly changing. 

Yardleys detailed oil paintings riff on neo-classical works by masters such as 
John Singleton Copley, who painted a portrait of his half-brother, Henry, playing 
with his pet flying squirrel, (transforming a species with little intrinsic monetary 
value, into a pampered pet with exalted status). Yardleys intricate portraits 
explore both therianthropy, (the ancient belief of shapeshifting), and 
theriocephaly, (animal-headed humanoid forms, such as the ancient Egyptian 
gods Ra, Sobek, and Anubis).
