Pegah Tabassinejad : Monitoring [ A Dollʼs House] 
October 19, 20, 21 2012 
Performances at 8pm in Studio T, SFU Woodwards

INTRO: Vancouver, BC - Pegah Tabassinejadʼs Monitoring [A Dollʼs 
House] resides somewhere between theatre and visual art. The piece 
will run as a combination of three scheduled theatrical
performances taking place on Oct. 19, 20 and 21 at 8PM, and then as 
a live installation which takes place between the theatrical 
performances. The whole project is an ongoing durational piece, so 
that the audience can visit at any time from 8PM on Oct. 19th to 10PM 
on Oct. 21st. The School for Contemporary Arts is honoured to present 
Pegah Tabassinejadʼs MFA thesis production.

Monitoring [A Dollʼs House] is a performance, interactive and multi-
channel video installation which explores identity, surveillance, 
technology, and privacy.

[Pegah]: “Since I am from Iran, the difference between the life one 
leads outside, in public, and the life she leads inside, within interiors 
(i.e., those places where she can hide herself from the outside world 
and feel safe) has always been very striking to me. What was 
unsettling, however, was this nagging idea that a personʼs identity 
could still be shaped within her private realm by certain external forces, 
by (as Paul Virilio might refer to) windows through which she observes 
the outside and through which she is also being observed (windows 
such as the internet [like Skype, various social networks, etc.] and 
television). What we see is mediated through these ʻwindowsʼ. What do 
we make then of a personʼs identity? Are we all just interchangeable in 
this society, ubiquitously observed and controlled from a distance, 
merely a small part in a homogeneous mass? Is anyone unique?” 
How a person constructs their identity is explored in this work, via 
another question, which is how does an actor create their character. 
Do ʻactorsʼ choices belong to them, or do they belong to something 
external? To explore some of these questions Pegah has adapted 
Ibsenʼs ʻA Dollʼs Houseʼ, a play that happens entirely within interiors. 
This project is a window into ʻA Dollʼs House.ʼ
