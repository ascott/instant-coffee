Audain Gallery SFU Woodward's Nov. 17, 2011 - Feb. 25, 2012: Mapping the 
Everyday: Neighbourhood Claims for the Future.

About the Audain Gallery

The Audain Gallery serves as a vital aspect of the Visual Arts program at Simon 
Fraser University's School for the Contemporary Arts.

The Audain Gallery's mission is to advance the aesthetic and discursive 
production and presentation of contemporary visual art through a responsive 
program of exhibitions in support of engaged pedagogy.

The Audain Gallery encourages conceptual and experimental projects that 
explore the dialogue between the social and the cultural in contemporary artistic 
practices.

The Audain Gallery is curated by Sabine Bitter, working with Gallery Assistant 
Brady Cranfield.

Mapping the Everyday: Neighbourhood Claims for the Future is a collaborative 
project between the Downtown Eastside Women's Centre (DEWC), visiting 
artist Elke Krasny, the art collective desmedia, the collective red diva projects, 
the collective Coupe, and students from the School for the Contemporary Arts at 
Simon Fraser University, and the Audain Gallery. It is realized with support from 
the City of Vancouver's 125th Anniversary Grants Program; the Austrian 
Federal Ministry for Education, Arts, and Culture; Am Johal, Vancity Office of 
Community Engagement as part of Simon Fraser University Woodward's 
Cultural Unit.

Marie Clements is currently The Ellen and Warren Tallman Writer in Residence 
at Simon Fraser University's English Department.
Hours
Tue.- Sat. 12pm - 6pm 

The Audain Gallery is pleased to announce a series of events featuring the work 
of red diva projects and their collaborators as part of the exhibition project 
Mapping the Everyday: Neighbourhood Claims for the Future.

red diva projects is a collaboration between veteran artists Marie Clements and 
Michelle St. John committed to deepening the vocabulary and aesthetic style of 
integrative Indigenous performance and producing new and innovative work.

February 7, 7:00 pm
Film screening and discussion of Jesus Indian, a short film by red diva projects, 
and The Language of Love, a new documentary short by Marie Clements.

Jesus Indian
(8 minutes, 2010)
Jesus Indian tells the "brown, down, and out" story of a jailed and dying "No-
Name Aboriginal woman" and her fellow inmates, both real and spiritual. 
Together they embark on a "lyrical journey" towards "ultimate resurrection," 
revealing and challenging the "experiences that continue to en-cage them."

The Language of Love (featuring Stephen Lytton)
(10 minutes, 2012)
The Language of Love is a ten-minute documentary featuring 
writer/actor/activist Stephen Lytton's raw and poetic articulation of the fourteen 
years he endured in the residential school system - a child's survival redefines 
itself as the artful embodiment of a man.

February 10, 7:00 pm
Jennifer Kreisberg
Jennifer Kreisberg, a Genie award winning singer, producer, composer, and 
frequent collaborator with red diva projects, will perform new songs and talk 
about her music-making practice which is rooted in Southeast Indigenous 
musical traditions.

February 25, 7:00 pm
red diva projects
An experimental theatrical musical performance by red diva projects and women 
from the Downtown Eastside Women's Centre, produced out of a series of 
workshops exploring the use of written words and vocables (non-lexical sound-
words) to address the themes of isolation and incarceration and the critical 
potential of fiction to express truth.
