The Organization

Established in 1980, in Vancouver, on the unceded territories of the xmky"m
(Musqueam), Swxw7mesh (Squamish), and Sel"l"witulh (Tsleil-Waututh) Nations,
Cineworks Independent Filmmakers Society is a non-profit artist-run centre, supporting
independent filmmakers, media artists, and arts audiences through facilities and initiatives
that encompass production, exhibition, consultation, outreach, and advocacy. Through
programs that foster experimentation with motion-picture arts, in dialogue with their historic,
current, and future cultural contexts, we engage our membership and wider communities in
the investigative, expressive, and transformative powers of the moving image.

Applications Welcome

Cineworks is committed to establishing and upholding values of equity, diversity, and
inclusion. We strongly encourage applications from peoples of underrepresented groups
and those who have faced systemic barriers at institutions, including cis, trans, two-spirit
and non-binary, and BIPOC. We also recognize that diverse lived experience and
knowledge can support a candidates eligibility outside of formal educational and/or
employment classifications.

The Position

Cineworks Independent Filmmakers Society seeks a Facilities + Equipment Manager
(FEM) to join our small but dynamic team of arts professionals.

The FEM will serve as the first point of contact between the organization and our
membership, providing assistance for facilities & equipment rental, member services, and
support for production
& post-production needs. The FEM is an integral part of the
Cineworks team, providing both technical support and guidance to our members, as well as
conducting outreach to advance the community that our membership serves.

The position supports Cineworks core mandate to provide facilities for the production of
independent, non-sponsored [KM1] film, and to foster experimentation with motion-picture
arts. The FEM will direct the public toward Cineworks capacity for supporting their creative
needs, relying on an in-depth knowledge of our equipment holdings, facilities, and
programs. The role will involve hands-on maintenance and upkeep of our digital and
analogue film equipment inventory, and administrative upkeep of our rental & membership
database.

Working closely with our Community & Education Liaison and Executive Director, the FEM
will help deliver our year-round activities, programs, and services to our members and the
general public.

Responsibilities

- Maintain and administer
film production & post-production equipment, facilities &
resources

- Prepare rental packages including camera packages, lighting and grip, sound, and
other resources available for rental

- Assist with the coordination of equipment for workshops and programming

- Deliver bi-weekly orientation sessions for prospective Cineworks members

- Schedule rentals to maintain effective inventory control and facilities bookings

post-secondary education institutions, partner film organizations, and local film festivals

Required Skills

- A post-secondary degree or
equivalent experience in film, preferably with a hands-on
film production focus; or equivalent combination of education and experience

- Holistic understanding of film production requirements with regards to the departments

- Understanding of film post-production requirements with regards to the departments of

- Adept in digital post-production software such as Adobe Creative Cloud, DaVinci

- Excellent organizational and multitasking skills

- Excellent written and oral communication skills, with the ability to listen and connect

- Self-sufficient and comfortable working independently

EXHIBITION | Hotam Press Gallery | Guesthood and Alienhood | MAR 20
Hotam Press Gallery presents Guesthood and Alienhood, an exhibition curated by Godfre
Leung.

Guesthood and Alienhood is a group exhibition of bookwork by Sun Yung Shin
(Minneapolis), Jinny Yu (Seoul/Ottawa), and Republic of the Other. It engages the
circulating apparatus of the book as a medium for creating bonds, and through these bonds
pursues identity forms beyond guest and alien.

At the centre of the exhibition is Gaze Observatory Threshold, a new collaboration between
Shin and Yu that retells the Korean folktale of Baridegi through a generative process of
passing poems and drawings passed back and forth via email and a Drive folder.

Other works include H"TE (guest/host), a new artist's book that Yu made during
quarantine; a selection of redacted documents from granted to a foreign citizen, a book of
poetry by Shin made from keepsakes from
her naturalization as an American citizen as a
young Korean adoptee; and Little Gray Book, Republic of the Others artists book
manifesto. Together, these bookworks initiate a conversation addressing the politics of
asylum, borders, family separation, and settler colonialism.
