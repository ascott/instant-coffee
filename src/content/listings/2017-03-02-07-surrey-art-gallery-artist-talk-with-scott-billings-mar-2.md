Artist Talk with Scott Billings
Surrey Art Gallery
March 2 | 7:309pm

For the past decade, artist Scott Billings has investigated the connection 
between cinema and sleep through his unique repurposing of the machinery of 
spectacle: film projectors, television monitors, programmable concert lighting, 8-
tracks, and IMAX film. Billings often opens up these devices to reveal the way 
their internal mechanisms move like animals. For this artist talk, titled Copying 
Machine, Billings will address many of these connections as they relates to his 
current exhibition.

Billings's current exhibit A Risky Jump, on display in the Surrey Art Gallery's 
TechLab, creates a unique cinematic experience with a seven-minute video that 
starts with the artist sleeping on his studio floor. We watch him literally "fall awake" 
as a trapdoor underneath him opens up. Billings's body freefalls through the air 
in slow-motion, suspended between sleeping and waking, levitation and falling. 
Billings reflects, "We are certainly present during our daily transition into sleep 
and back out again, yet it is difficult to assert that we truly experience it." By 
using a cutting-edge camera, Billings opens up this invisible and ordinary 
transition and turns it into something visible and extraordinary.

About the Artist
Scott Billings is a visual artist and designer based in Vancouver. His sculptures 
and video installations frequently centre on issues of animality, mobility, and 
spectatorship. Billings holds an MFA from the University of British Columbia, a 
BFA from Emily Carr University of Art and Design, and a BSc in mechanical 
Engineering from the University of Waterloo. He teaches at UBC and Emily Carr 
as a sessional instructor. More informaton at scottbillings.com.
