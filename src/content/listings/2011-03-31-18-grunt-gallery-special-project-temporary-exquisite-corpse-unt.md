temporary location at 1965 Main Street while engaging local artists

STATEMENT & BIOS FROM THE ARTISTS

Tania Willard

Title: Sneena ( Owl )

Sneena in our Secwepemc language ( Interior Salish language 
group ) was always something to be feared, if you were out too late 
or not listening Sneena may come and get you. Inspired by a sense 
of fascination and fear, the Owl has always transfixed me and fired 
my imagination. This Sneena is one imbued by a sense of spirit and 
fascination, three headed it speaks to ideas of the supernatural, the 
spirit embodied in the world around us. The three faces also 
represent the three artists collaborating in this exquisite corpse. An 
energy field and aura surround the owl emanating its unspoken 
secrets, the knowledge this spirit holds.

Tania Willard, Secwepemc Nation, has been working with Aboriginal 
youth, community, story and the arts for over 10 years. An honours 
graduate from the University of Victoria, her ground breaking work 
with Redwire Magazine, a national Aboriginal youth magazine, led 
the organization to be one of the first independent Aboriginal youth 
run arts and media organizations. Tania has since transferred her 
skills and passion to her work as an artist and graphic designer, 
again the focus of her work in this field is the Aboriginal community, 
the arts, health and social justice. Tania has worked as an artist in 
residence with Stanley Park Ecology Society, Gallery Gachet in 
Vancouver's Downtown East Side, as a writer in residence with 
Native Women in the Arts and the Banff Centre's fiction residency in 
visual arts as well as most recently as a curator in residence with 
grunt gallery. Collections of Willard's work include the Department of 
Foreign Affairs and International Trade, Kamloops Art Gallery and 
Thompson Rivers University Aboriginal Education Department. 
Tania has worked with grunt gallery to coordinate their community 
arts conference and publication, Live in Public: The Art of 
Engagement and recent online gallery projects including Beat 
Nation: Hip Hop as Indigenous Culture, Dana Claxton's 
retrospective and the First Visions site.

Diyan Achjadi

title: The Tale of the Self-Replicating Pigtails

pencil and pastel on paper

Diyan Achjadi works in print media, drawing and animation. Her 
works often figure Girl, a young golden-skinned girl clad in a red or 
pink dress, as an avatar to explore the potential for pictorial 
language to offer alternative narratives. Girl is simultaneously 
heroine, victim, and hapless bystander, in a candy-colored 
dystopian world populated only by herself and her clones. Diyan is 
based in Vancouver, BC, and teaches in the faculty of Visual Arts 
and Material Practice at Emily Carr University. Please visit 
[http://www.diyanachjadi.com](http://www.diyanachjadi.com) for more information.

Rebecca Chaperon

Title: Desire to Speak

A tapestry-like fire half-engulfs a female figure. In Desire to Speak, 
Rebecca Chaperon points at the compulsive desire to speak while

hinting at the uncomfortable but protective blanket that is won by 
silence.

Inspired by the misadventures of various heroines of classic 
literature, Rebecca Chaperon's paintings often portray a female

protagonist within a surreal landscape. Her work aspires to 
entertain the mind with possible narratives, while haunting with a 
deeper emotive undercurrent.

Born in England in 1978, Rebecca settled in Toronto, Ontario at age 
8. A graduate of Emily Carr University, she has exhibited her work 
across Canada and has been featured in several Canadian 
publications. She currently resides in Vancouver, BC and will be 
displaying her work in a solo exhibition, Like A Great Black Fire, at 
the grunt gallery in September 2011.
