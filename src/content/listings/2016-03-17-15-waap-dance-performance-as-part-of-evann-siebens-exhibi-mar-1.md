Homemade Again
created by Justine A. Chambers with the assistance of Elissa Hanson
performed by Justine A. Chambers
media by Evann Siebens

Homemade Again is inspired by Trisha Brown's 1966 performance of Homemade
where she strapped a projector to her back with a baby harness. Mediating
the space between image and image, Chambers combines Brown's original score
of working with "lived" not performed memory, and a set of instructions for
Duet (a section of Yvonne Rainer's Terrain) performed by Trisha Brown and
Yvonne Rainer in 1963. With the instructions of Duet acting as a physical
framework, the viewer is invited to witness the performer work through the
lived memory of the creation of Homemade Again.

Justine A. Chambers' choreographic interests lie in collaborative creation
and re-imagining dance performance. She is associate artist to The Dance
Centre and a founding member of project bk. Her work has been presented at
Dancing on the Edge Festival, Dance in Vancouver, Vancouver Art Gallery -
FUSE, The Western Front and New Dance Horizons. Recent collaborations
include projects with Josh Hite, Ben Brown, Claudia Fancello, Marilou
Lemmens & Richard Ibghy, Jen Weih, and Brendan Fernandes. Chambers works
actively performing in the work of other dance artists and leads classes
and workshops throughout Canada. justineachambers.com

art is either a complaint or do something else
performed by Natalie LeFebvre Gnam and James Gnam
media by Evann Siebens
music by John Cage

art is either a complaint or do something else was created by John Cage in
response to an interview the painter, Jasper Johns, had done with Mark
Rosenthal. The interview dealt with the creation of a painting.
Specifically, it questioned the painter's relationship to representation
and how he addresses space and time within his compositions. Cage then took
this text, re-arranged it with chance operation and then organized the
material into a series of mesostics. As a dance artist, I am concerned with
representation, embodiment and composition in space and time and so I was
attracted to this piece, this process as it brought with it new perspective.

In 2004, I organized a draft of art is either for two dancers. Over the
years, plastic orchid has performed varied versions of this work. With
questions sitting at the centre of the score, every incarnation of art is
either a complaint or do something else is different. It asks the
performers to re-focus their relationship to composition. To look at
representation and how it relates to their interests in building meaning in
a space over a period of time.
- James Gnam

JAMES GNAM's current practice lives in a world that considers a dialogue
between representation and embodiment. He strives to cultivate
relationships with other artists and move the exchange past transaction.
Where mutual investment, curiosity, communication and a sharing of
authorship and responsibilities are the currencies driving the
conversations. With plastic orchid factory, James collaboratively devises
work that considers the space and time living within the mind/body of the
performer. While simultaneously organizing representation within the space
to create context.

James Gnam trained at the National Ballet School of Canada and from 1998 to
the present, has worked with Les Grands Ballets Canadiens de Montreal,
Ballet BC, EDAM dance, Grand Poney and Mayday Danse. He has had the
pleasure of creating, collaborating and performing in the works of Crystal
Pite, Twyla Tharp, Mark Morris, George Balanchine, Peter Bingham, Thomas
Lehman, Lee Su-Feh, Jacques Poulin-Denis, Barak Marshall and Melanie
Demers. James' work and research have been supported internationally by
Opera Estate in Bassano Italy; nationally by Circuit-Est in Montreal,
Centre Q and the National Arts Centre in Ottawa; and in Vancouver by
Electric Company Theatre, The Dance Centre, The New Forms Festival, The
Vancouver Art gallery and SFU Woodwards. In 2008, the late Lola Maclaughlin
nominated James and his wife Natalie for the City of Vancouver's Mayor's
Arts Award for Dance.

The Indexical Dance-a-Thon! Performance
live camera shooting by Evann Siebens
improvisational dance performed by James Gnam, Natalie LeFebvre Gnam and
Justine A. Chambers with a 'viewer' guest appearance by Jane Ellison

A performed example of Siebens' technique for shooting dance. Using her
background as a former dancer and documentary filmmaker, Siebens will
create duets with her camera and the dancers while the audience views both
the projected image and the mediated duet that occurs in the moment.

EVANN SIEBENS makes media with movement. She has exhibited her projects at
galleries such as Eyebeam and Centre Pompidou, while her documentaries have
been screened at MOMA and on PBS. A former dancer with the National Ballet
of Canada and Bonn Ballet, Evann graduated from New York University. Now
based in Vancouver, Evann co-curated containR during the 2010 Olympics. She
has participated in residencies at the Banff Centre and ACME/UK with Keith
Doyle. Recent exhibitions include MediaArtLab/Urban Screen, Russia; dc3 Art
Projects, Edmonton; Burrard Arts Foundation, Wil Aballe Art Projects, Thru
the Trap Door and Gallery 295, Vancouver. Evann recently won the
ID/Identities Istanbul Best Video Prize and presented work at ISEA
Vancouver 2015. Evann has also been working on MIMMiC: a commission from On
Main Gallery and Paul Wong Projects, and will be in residence at Unit/Pitt
Projects for An Exact Vertigo in April. http://evannsiebens.com/
