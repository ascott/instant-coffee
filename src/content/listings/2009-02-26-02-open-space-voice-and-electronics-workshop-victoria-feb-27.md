Luigi Nono (1924 -1990) was an Italian composer who studied music with Gian
Francesco Malipiero, Bruno Maderna and Hermann Scherchen. He came to public
attention in 1950 with his work Variazioni Canoniche, orchestral variations
on a 12-
tone theme of Arnold Schoenberg, whose daughter Nuria he married in 1955. An
avowed communist, Nono often produced works of political substance, many of
which sparked controversy and reaction. He employed aleatory (chance)
techniques
and serialism, sometimes fragmenting language and using electronically
manipulated
sounds. His best-known work is the opera Intolleranza (1961).


Continuity: Zbigniew Karkowski and Atsuko Nojiri
Concert: Open Space. 510 Fort St.

A master sound manipulator, renowned Polish born, Tokyo-based sound
artist Zbigniew Karkowski employs a variety of tools to process and rework
original
acoustic instrument recordings. Continuity demonstrates Karkowski's relentless
attention to detail in a re-interpretation of his rich body of sonic
explorations built on
superbly executed and processed field recordings. Zbigniew Karkowski and Atsuko
Nojiri will perform at Open Space on Saturday February 28 at 8pm. Zbigniew
Karkowski will conduct a workshop on Sunday March 1 at 11am at Open Space.
Zbigniew Karkowski will discuss his earlier work and ideas about contemporary
composition, and will demonstrate and describe the sonic materials and
processes he
uses in his current noise-based work.

Continuity includes visual interpretations of Karkowski's compositions by
Japanese video artist Atsuko Nojiri. Rather than simply functioning as graphic
replications of the original musical works, Nojiri's visual pieces
seamlessly wrap
around Karkowski's sounds as though they were integral parts of their own
larger
multimedia compositions. In the process, Nojiri's videos become independent
of the
original musical works that they purport to document.

Zbigniew Karkowski studied composition with Iannis Xenakis, Olivier Messiaen,
Pierre Boulez, Georges Aperghis, and others. His commissioned orchestra,
opera and
chamber pieces have been performed in Sweden, Poland, and Germany. Zbigniew
has lived and worked in Tokyo for the past eight years where he is active
in the
underground noise scene, and is a founding member of the electroacoustic trio
Sensorband.
