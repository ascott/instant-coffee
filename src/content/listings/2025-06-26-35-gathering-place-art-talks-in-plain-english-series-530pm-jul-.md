Talk: Art Talks in Plain English Series "July 9 th 5:30 PM to 7 PM

Place: Connection Salon Gallery at the Gathering Place Community Center, 609
Helmcken

Graduate students from Emily Carr University of Art and Design will give a 20-minute
presentation about their work followed by informal discussion. Snacks and
refreshments will be provided.
Plain English is talk that a general audience can understand the first time they hear
it. Plain English is clear, straightforward expression.
In July the presentations will be:
Cimarron Knight
Title: navigating grief-and-loss as a sensory being
This artist talk explores the concept of deep listening as a method for processing
grief-and-loss. Themes include embodied grief, sensory connection to non-human
worlds, and the use of drawing and rendering to access the unconscious. These
become sites of deep listening, meditative acts that invite slowness and awareness
of impermanence. The talk concludes with how these methodologies became acts of
healing for me as an artist connecting to my own grief literacy.

Julya Hajnoczky
Title:All My Favourite People Are Plants - Making Art with the More-Than-Human
In 2017 my art practice changed forever. With a small arts grant, I built my tiny
teardrop trailer,
a combination camper and workspace, the Mobile Natural History Collection
Laboratory
(affectionately nicknamed the Alfresco Science Machine). Since then, Ive been
travelling
around Canada, studying and documenting the varied ecosystems from places like
Haida Gwaii
to Pacific Rim, Wood Buffalo and Grasslands National Parks and everywhere in
between.
My work attempts to understand our complex relationships with plants and other
living beings.
Guided in part by scientific research in biology and botany, my projects take shape
around
intriguing bits of information that I pick up in my reading.
I work primarily with photo-based processes, often with unconventional cameras
such as high-
resolution scanners or multi-aperture pinhole cameras that are slow and sometimes
unwieldy. My
cameras act as a way of focusing my attention on the beings Im studying,
reinforcing the
process of slow looking that is so essential to my work. By carefully studying the
forms and
ways of life of these beings, Im hoping to hear what they have to say. But with only
my
limited human senses to rely on, my translations are speculative at best, my
representations
inevitably biased. But I keep looking, undeterred.
