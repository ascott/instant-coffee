Opening on April 30th, Blanket Gallery is pleased to present a show of 
recent works by Berlin-based painter Lutz Braun. This is the artist’s first 
solo exhibition with the gallery, as well as his first exhibition in Canada. 

A small, neat, white house with a tiled roof stand in the middle of a 
forest, possibly some time in late winter. The trees emerge from the ground 
in broken, spiked columns or else feather out, leafless, into a yellow sky, 
dirtied with ochre and rust. A strange kind of crimson-hued explosion seems 
to have taken place behind a cluster of dead trees at the right of the 
picture ground, and, from that direction, thunders the silhouette of a 
horse. It is a simple picture, but one which inspires in the viewer a sense 
of quickening panic. Here are all the elements of an ancient dystopian fairy 
tale, the house, the dark forest, the introduction of a menacing spirit 
force into an environment of bucolic calm. This is the world of young German 
painter, Lutz Braun.

Braun’s works, though variant in subject matter, revert again and again to a 
number of images suggestive of myth, black magic and the Freudian 
subconscious. In “Norraby”, a spectral androgynous figure in profile and a 
crouching sage/troll regard a decaying female body in the foreground. The 
scene is shrouded in mist and ice; death is everywhere, and we are provided 
no comfort in the evidence of civilization that exists in the painting (a 
church, another small house.) Everything is rendered in milky, fuzzy hues of 
white, and grey, with the sparsest touches of red and orange suggestive of 
dying life. Another larger painting, “Loss”, though richer in terms of its 
palette, holds no promise of deliverance from a sense of dread. Here a 
copper-brown dog bounds across a country road. The foliage to the left of 
the picture ground is richer in hue and the sky is a multi-coloured swirling 
mass of pink, blue, white and yellow. But the dog, emaciated and snarling, 
has all the freakish suppleness of a beast from a Bosch triptych. The animal 
as harbinger of doom reappears in “Pferdeaugen” (“Horse’s Eyes”.) In this 
picture, the isolated earless head of the title animal surges forward. The 
right eye appears raw and recessed, while the left eye protrudes crazily 
from out of the picture ground. Rendered in smooth, looping strokes, the 
horse head hovers like a nightmare, suggesting a plethora of universal 
anxieties: fear of the animal world, fear of the spirit world, fear of 
sexual power, fear of familial dynamics, fear of the self.

From the queasy narratives in Braun’s “folk tale” paintings to his portraits 
(such as the tender, but still creepy “Geliebte”—“Lover”—a smiling woman’s 
head, surrounded by wafting tendrils of smoke and light), one senses in this 
young painter’s works a sensibility reminiscent of that of Edvard Munch, a 
world where agony and mysticism permeate and transform the ordinary.

Lutz Braun was born in Schleswig, Germany in 1976. He studied at the 
Städelschule, Frankfurt/Main and was the 2002-2005 recipient of the 
Scholarship of Monte Christo. His works have appeared, in both a solo and 
group context, at Galerie Christian Nagel, Galerie Antik, Galerie Sandra 
Bürgel and Instituto Divorcado in Berlin; The Gang Bang in Frankfurt; Court 
Yard Gallery in Beijing; Jack Tilton Gallery and Danese Gallery in New York, 
and ACME in Los Angeles.
