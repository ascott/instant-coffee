As part of (dis)location (dis)connect (dis)appearance, June 20-August 10, curated by 
Diane Hau Yu Wong. Centre A is hosting weekly Stitch n Bitch workshops. 

As a method to explore and rediscover their roots, a number of artists in the exhibition 
have employed textile, an often time-consuming medium, to explore familial 
relationships and their diasporic identity. 

Join us in reflecting upon craft as a cultural medium while learning hands-on textile art 
techniques from artists in the exhibition and other local textiles artists and organizations. 
Bring your own project or start something new with other textile-minded souls.

This week we will be doing boro with local artist Nancy Nguyen (Instagram: 
@inside.looking). Boro is a patching technique that sewn with repurposed fabrics that 
originated in Japan common during the mid-1900s. This style is being appropriated 
through knee patches using vintage Indigo textiles called Kasuri. Kasuri fabrics are 
usually 100% cotton, hand-loomed and come in various resist dye patterns. The knee 
patch Kasuri squares are layered on top of each other to create stronger mend with 
stylistic intentions. 

Stitch n' Bitch sessions will take place every Saturday until the end of the exhibition. 

Accessibility: The gallery is located on the second floor of the Sun Wah Centre, and 
accessible by elevator. Accessible washrooms are available. If you have specific 
accessibility needs, please contact us at (604) 683-8326.

Centre A acknowledges that this exhibition takes place on the traditional, ancestral, and 
unceded Coast Salish territories of the Musqueam, Squamish, and Tsleil-Waututh 
peoples. We respect, honour and give thanks to our hosts.
