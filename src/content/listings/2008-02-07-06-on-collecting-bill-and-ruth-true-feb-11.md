International Lecture Series: Conversations on Collecting

 

Howard Rachofsky in Conversation with Bill and Ruth True

with Ian Wallace, moderator

 

Monday, February 11 at 7pm

at the Vancouver Art Gallery

 

Explore the dynamic synergy between individual collectors and public collections in this exclusive conversation with Howard Rachofsky and Bill and Ruth True, moderated by internationally acclaimed artist Ian Wallace. These collectors, philanthropists and cultural leaders exhibit their private collections in innovative ways, working with public institutions to create thriving cultural communities.

 

Howard Rachofsky’s collecting is distinguished by its concentration on arte povera and minimalist art. His acclaimed international collection of modern and contemporary art includes works by Italian artists Piero Manzoni, Alighiero Boetti and Michelangelo Pistoletto, as well as significant holdings of works by Janine Antoni, Felix Gonzalez-Torres, Doris Salcedo and Robert Gober, among others. The Rachofsky House in Dallas, Texas, completed in 1996, was designed by Richard Meier to showcase this extensive art collection. The multilevel,  glass-and-steel building extends 11,000 square feet and also includes an expansive garden for sculpture and site-specific works. In 2005, Howard Rachofsky donated the Rachofsky Collection and The Rachofsky House to the Dallas Museum of Art, accompanied by an operating endowment that continues to provide for public access to The Rachofsky House.

 

Bill and Ruth True opened Western Bridge, a warehouse in South Seattle’s industrial district renovated by architect Roy McMakin in 2004. This non-profit public space for contemporary art has become renowned for its exhibitions showcasing the current state of art making around the world. The True Collection contains works in video, photography, and other media by an international roster of established and emerging artists, including Doug Aitken, Tracey Moffatt, Tony Oursler, Christian Marclay and Thomas Schütte. Western Bridge is a connector between private collecting and public exhibition. It also serves as a venue to exhibit, commission and donate art. Long time supporters of the Seattle Art Museum and the Henry Art Gallery, the Trues pledge one piece from every show to the University of Washington’s Henry Art Gallery and other museums.

07. OPEN TEXT | CLAIRE HUOT & ROBERT MAJZELS | FEB 12
============================
OPEN TEXT READING SERIES: CLAIRE HUOT & ROBERT MAJZELS
Sponsored by Writer's Union of Canada
& the Creative Writing Degree Program at Capilano College
=============================
The Spring 2008 OPEN TEXT series at Capilano College continues on February 12th, 2008 with a presentation by Calgary & Montreal poets, playwrights, novelists, and translators Claire Huot and Robert Majzels.
Majzels and Huot will present a collaborative, multi-media talk that addresses the reception of classical Chinese poetry into English.

Tuesday, February 12th
Library 321 @ 3:30
Capilano College
2055 Purcell Way
North Vancouver
 
CLAIRE HUOT has lived in and out of China for the past 20 years. Fully trilingual (French, English and Mandarin), she has written two books on contemporary Chinese culture— La Petite révolution culturelle, (Arles, France, 1994) and China's New Cultural Scene (Duke University Press, USA, 2000). The Prison Tangram is her first book of fiction. Huot is currently teaching Chinese film at the University of Calgary and has a feature column, in Chinese, in New World, a Chinese cultural magazine.
 
ROBERT MAJZELS is a novelist, playwright, poet and translator, born in Montréal, Québec. The Humbugs Diet is his fourth novel. In 2007, he won the Alcuin Society Prize for Excellence in Book Design for the limited edition of his book, Apikoros Sleuth. This Night the Kapo, his award winning full-length play, was produced at the Berkley Street Theatre in Toronto, in March 2004. He was attributed the Governor General's Award of Canada for his translation of France Daigle's Just Fine in 2000. With Erin Moure, Robert has also translated several books of poetry by Nicole Brossard. He is presently an Associate Professor in the English Department of the University of Calgary.

08. Surrey Art Gallery | Janet Cardiff + Open Sound 
Surrey Art Gallery invites you to join us on Saturday, January 12 (2-4pm) for the exciting opening reception for Forty-Part Motet and Open Sound, two groundbreaking sound exhibitions as well as the launch of the Glocal project studio. 
 
Janet Cardiff's Forty-Part Motet has been installed at the Museum of Modern Art (MoMA) in New York,  the Tate in Liverpool, the Musée d'Art Contemporain in Montreal, and now joins us at the Surrey Art Gallery for its first exhibition in British Columbia. 
 
Forty-Part Motet is a sublimely beautiful sound installation created by one of Canada's most significant contemporary artists. Janet Cardiff recorded members of the Salisbury Cathedral Choir performing Spem in Alium, by 16th century English composer Thomas Tallis. This is one of the most complex pieces of polyphonic choral music ever written. Separate voices emanate from forty speakers. As visitors stroll past they can single out individual singers. Standing at the centre, the combined choral harmonies wash over listeners. The This exhibition was organized by the National Gallery of Canada and is co presented as part of the 2008 Cultural Olympiad.
 
Open Sound is a new program developed by the Surrey Art Gallery that intends to support the production and presentation of audio artforms as part of contemporary art practice. The inaugural Open Sound exhibition will consist of a series of commissioned audio art projects by Jean Routier, Brady Marks, David Grove, and Eric Powell, installed in and around the Surrey Arts Centre facility. Visitors will be able to walk about our space and pick up the broadcast of these transmitted artworks using headphones, both inside and outside of the building as well as look at the machines that create, or listen to sound. This project was made possible with support of the Spirit of BC Arts Fund.
 
Also opening on January 12 will be the Gallery's TechLab, as the production studio for the Glocal project. Led by a team of artists in residence, Sylvia Grace Borda, M. Simon Levin and Jer Thorp, the Glocal project will use open source software, online social networking websites and other strategies to create an experience that joins the local with the global. This project is one of Surrey's Cultural Capitals of Canada initiatives, and will grow to involve thousands of contributors .
