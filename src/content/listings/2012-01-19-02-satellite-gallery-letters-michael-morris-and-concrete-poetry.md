In his roles as a curator and primarily as an artist, Michael Morris has 
been a key figure of the west coast art scene since the 1960s and his 
contribution to the development of Vancouver as a contemporary art 
city has been immense. Morris was engaged with Concrete Poetry in 
the 1960s. The movement was perhaps the first global art movement, 
springing up in south and North America, Japan, and Europe in the mid 
to late 1950s.

Recognizing the potential of Concrete Poetry as an area that included 
design, poetry, architecture, art, and communications, Morris co-
curated an important exhibition at the University of British Columbia 
Fine Arts Gallery in 1969. During this time, he was also working on his 
most ambitious series of paintings, "Letters" (1967-1969) that are 
named after the "Letter" column in "Art International" magazine. (Morris' 
work had been discussed in "Los Angeles Letter" in the December 
1967 issue.) Paris, London, New York, Peking, Rome, Los Angeles, 
Madrid; for the first time, all 7 are presented, plus a study for the 
largest, "New York Letter," which until now, has never been exhibited in 
Vancouver.

The titles give a sense of Morris' growing interest in the mail art network 
and in the possibilities of networks of peripheries and centres. The 
paintings include mirrors in order to make the works interactive with the 
viewer and positioned the genre against the metaphor of 
communications, which at the time were agitated by Marshall 
McLuhan's declarations of the coming paradigm shift of the digital era.

Approximately 100 items from the Belkin Art Gallery's collection of 
Concrete Poetry material created by Morris and by 
Vancouver/Canadian artists such as bill bissett, bpNichol, and Carol 
Itter and by international artists such as Ugo Carrega, Henri Chopin, 
Lily Greenham, Jiri Kolar, Ferdinand Kriwet, and Steve McCaffrey are 
presented. Some of these artists were part of the original 1969 
Concrete Poetry exhibition and their works situate Morris and 
Vancouver in the international movement.

"Letters: Michael Morris and Concrete Poetry" is co-curated by Scott 
Watson and Michael Turner.
