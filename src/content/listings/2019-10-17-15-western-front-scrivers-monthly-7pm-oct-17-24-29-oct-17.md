Scrivener's Monthly
Music Research Strategies Performing Political Education (Towards a Solidarity Economics
Conduction System for Improvisers)

Western Front
303 East 8th Ave
Vancouver, BC

Music Research Strategies (percussionist Marshall Trammell) instigates social practice
collaborations at sites of political opposition. Music Research Strategies began as a critical
ethnographic framework bridging Trammells ongoing interests with improvising strategies,
organizational improvisation and psychology, and street-level social justice action For this
residency and series of events at Western Front, Trammell will undertake a collaborate with
Vancouver musicians who use
percussion"including Eileen Kage and John Brennan"and
present his research around music as a tool for collective organizing.

All three evenings are free and open to the general public. For the sessions on October 17
and 24, we are also seeking participants from the Vancouver music community to take part
in Trammell's project. Interested musicians are invited to fill out a registration form at the
following link.

Event Details:
October 17, 7pm
Insurgent Learning Workshop: Solidarity (Cooperative) Economics Conduction System

Artist
Biography
Marshall Trammell (lives in Oakland, CA, USA and Albuquerque, NM, USA) is a self-styled
Music Research Strategist. He is an experimental percussionist and designer of
interculturally-situated, arts-driven social engagement interventions. He conducts Insurgent
Learning Workshops (ILWs) instigating renderings of alternative economics and anti-
violence, and community accountability frameworks as Creative Improvised Music.

He performs on drum set, bull roarers, and face jugs. Using an array of three face jugs,
created by Jim McDowell in the manner of pottery used to mark graves of early enslaved
Africans in North Carolina and ward off demons, Trammell performs narratives of fugitivity in
contemporary, experimental performance.

He was a fellow at Rensselaer Polytechnic Institute, Troy, NY (2004-06), and recently an
Intercultural Leadership Institute fellow (2018-19). In 2016, he published Music Research
Strategies for Sound American and is currently documenting ILWs for
publication. He is a
member of In Defense of Memory, featuring Laura Ortman and Carlos Santistevan. He was
co-director of bands Black Spirituals (Sige Records) and Mutual Aid Project.
