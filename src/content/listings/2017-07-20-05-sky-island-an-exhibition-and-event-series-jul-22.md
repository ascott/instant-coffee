Tarmac is a familiar backdrop that models a modern city. Paved grids navigate 
passengers to destinations, and in turn, places of storage. Seven stories atop sleeping 
vehicles is Sky Island, an art exhibition and event series occurring on the rooftop 
parkade at 211 East Georgia. Throughout the month of July emerging artists, dancers, 
musicians and writers are responding to the site and the urban context that surrounds it.

Besides storage a parkade can also provide space for congregating, solace for personal 
acts, a degree of shade for opportunistic weeds, and refuge for scavenging birds. 
Though these subtle acts of intervention are a part of everyday life, their occurrence 
influences the codes of public and private space, an awareness of place, and the ways 
space is repurposed. 

The site of Sky Island is many spaces at once. Being a thoroughfare its grounds can go 
unnoticed. Situated within Chinatown, cars pile on layers of asphalt inside a 
neighborhood community in constant odds with developments and subsequent 
displacement. Snowy summits and the lush environment frame the citys glossy 
condominiums, an endless reflection foregrounding lifestyle over affordable living.

In the hours when the sun wanes and drivers exit, the contributors of Sky Island make 
use of the parkade through social sculpture, installation, sound, movement, and 
recitations.
