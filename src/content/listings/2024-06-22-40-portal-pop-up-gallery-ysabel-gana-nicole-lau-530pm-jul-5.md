Reception on July 5th, 5:30 - 7:30pm

Artwork on display in window gallery:

June 21 - July 14, 2024

Portal opens its final exhibition before a summer break, presenting the work of two
emerging Vancouver-Based artists Ysabel Gana and Nicole Lau.

A recent graduate of UBCs Fine Art Program, Ysabel Gana presents two sculptural
works. Existing in the diaspora, Gana is interested in how online communication
parallels in their own experiences of migration, enabling interactions to occur
between different spaces, but existing neither here nor there. SKL is a minimal
soapstone sculpture modeled after an analog keycap. The title refers to a Taglish (an
English-Tagalog hybrid) acronym, meaning share ko lang - a term often used when
oversharing or joking with friends to not take a message seriously or read too deeply
into it. SKL also refers to a Simple Key Loader, a device used by American soldiers
to encrypt military data. Working with these
references, Gana is interested in how
the term is appropriated through its parallel use in Filipino culture, considering
Americas lasting impacts in the Philippines, and the potential of community and
language in online spaces to disseminate anti-colonial ideas. The other work, Tome,
considers the body as a site of projection.

Nicole Laus series of graphite on newsprint drawings intricately weave narratives of
myth and survival through a distinct visual language. Each piece highlights powerful
stories of feminine resilience and strength throughout history. Serving as a
compelling counterpart to the traditional heros quest, Laus work challenges us to
reconsider and redefine our cultural narratives on the heroines journey. It prompts
us to look inward and reimagine the profound strength and resilience embedded in
womens experiences. Through their minimalist composition and abstract forms,
Laus drawings evoke a sense of universality and timelessness.

Bios

Ysabel Gana is a visual
artist currently learning and practicing in Vancouver, on the
unceded territories of the Musqueam, Squamish, and Tsleil-Waututh peoples. Their
practice is interested in exploring memory and community - with a current focus on
Philippine material culture.

Nicole Lau, a Chinese Canadian with Hakka heritage, is a multidisciplinary artist
based in Vancouver, British Columbia. For three consecutive years between 2022
and 2024, Lau received the Research and Creation award and Professional
Development award through The Canada Council for the Arts. Lau held her first solo
exhibition "Home Kong" in Vancouver, BC and participated in group exhibitions
including The People's Forum in New York City, The National Housing Conference,
Richmond Art Gallery and Seymour Art Gallery in BC. She is a member of The
Historic Joy Kogawa House in Vancouver, BC. Lau hosts the Creative Diagnosis
podcast that explores the human first, and the artist second.
