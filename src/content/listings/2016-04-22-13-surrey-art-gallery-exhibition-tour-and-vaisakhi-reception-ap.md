Exhibition Tour and Vaisakhi Reception
Surrey Art Gallery
April 27, 7-9pm

British Columbia's Lower Mainlanders are fortunate to have one of the 
largest Vaisakhi celebrations outside of India in our very own backyard. 
Celebrations will be ongoing through mid to late April, marking the 
Punjabi New Year and the anniversary of foundation of Khalsa. This 
month has been filled with many forms of cultural expression including 
bhangra music and dance, kabaddi wrestling, gatka martial arts, sports 
competitions from grass hockey to soccer, and two of the largest Nagar 
Kirtan (parades) in North America.

If Vaisakhi celebrates Punjabi and Sikh culture, an important part of 
that culture includes the visual art produced by artists of the South 
Asian diaspora. Toronto-based artist Nep Sidhu is one such artist. He is 
rapidly gaining attention both inside and outside of Canada for his 
highly original fusion of sculpture, fashion, painting, and metalwork. 
Shadows in the Major Seventh, his current exhibit at the Surrey Art 
Gallery, expresses the fundamental importance of equality, social 
justice, and communityvalues of the Sikh faith and Punjabi culture. 
The exhibition Nep Sidhu: Shadows in the Major Seventh runs until 
June 12th.

On April 27th join us for this recognition of Vaisakhi and learn more 
about Nep Sidhu's artwork in this tour and talk on Wednesday, April 27 
from 7 to 9pm. The evening will begin with remarks by cultural 
organizer and commentator Kamal Sharma, followed by scholar 
Satwinder Bains, Director at the Centre for Indo-Canadian Studies at 
the University of the Fraser Valley. Jordan Strom, Curator of Exhibitions 
and Collections at the Surrey Art Gallery, will lead the exhibition tour, 
joined in conversation by Satwinder Bains. The event is free. 
Beverages provided by KVP Entertainers and food courtesy of Nanak 
Foods.

Surrey Art Gallery would like to acknowledge our community partners 
for this event: Centre for Indo-Canadian Studies, KVP Entertainers, 
and Nanak Foods.

Please RSVP at artgallery@surrey.ca or by calling 604-501-5186.

Guest Biography
Satwinder Bains works as an instructor, researcher, community 
educator, and consultant in the areas of Indo-Canadian Studies. She is 
the director at the Centre for Indo-Canadian studies at the University of 
the Fraser Valley. Her current research interests include: migration and 
integration; cross-cultural education curriculum implementation; race 
and ethnicity; women's rights and cultural politics; diaspora studies, 
Sikhism and the politics of identity and cultural historiographies. Bains 
has served the community as a diversity educator, community 
developer, and community activist in the field of race and anti-racism 
and immigrant settlement integration.
