Among the Plazas and the Courts: Writers Reading the City
with writers Sadhu Binning, Jeff Derksen, Cecily Nicholson and Matthew 
Stadler
Surrey Art Gallery
March 8, 6:30-8:30pm
How is the changing nature of urbanization shaping how writers portray 
the built environment? How is new art about the city influencing how we 
think – and write – about the city? This event features readings of 
poetry and innovative prose that address the complexities of urban 
spaces, and the relationships between the centres and peripheries of 
the new multi-centred city. The conversation will explore many different 
subjects, including how writers and artists depict the daily realities of 
‘ethno-burbs’ (culturally diverse suburbs), and how the changing nature 
of work and gender is shaping the spaces of the city.

Readers include local writers Sadhu Binning, Jeff Derksen and Cecily 
Nicholson, and Portland-based writer, Matthew Stadler. Following the 
readings, each writer will present a short talk on writing the city in the 
context of Metro Vancouver, Surrey and related cities. There will be 
time for open discussion, and for the audience to meet the writers over 
refreshments. This event takes place at the Surrey Art Gallery on 
March 8 from 6:30pm to 8:30pm. The event will be moderated by 
Surrey Art Gallery curator Jordan Strom. Admission to the event is free 
(donations are gratefully received). This event is presented with 
Kwantlen Polytechnic University’s English, Creative Writing, and Fine 
Arts Departments.

Among the Plazas and the Courts: Writers Reading the City is 
organized in conjunction with the group exhibition Beyond Vague 
Terrain: The City and the Serial Image, presented at the Surrey Art 
Gallery until March 18. The artworks – photography, painting, drawing 
and video – have serial qualities that relate to a sense of time and 
space in the city, and echo the repetition and reproductive quality of its 
structures. The artists are Sylvia Grace Borda, Michael de Courcy, 
Chris Gergley, Jeremy Herndl, Bill Jeffries, Roy Kiyooka, Owen Kydd, 
Khan Lee, Helma Sawatzky, Susan Schuppli, Jeff Wall, Ian Wallace, and 
Neil Wedman.

Writer biographies:

Sadhu Binning is an award-winning bilingual author, editor, educator 
and cultural leader who has lived in the Metro Vancouver area since 
migrating to Canada in 1967. He has published over fifteen books of 
poetry, fiction, plays, translation, and research, and is included in over 
thirty-five anthologies in both English and Punjabi. He is a founding 
member of Vancouver Sath, a theatre collective, Ankur, and various 
other literary and cultural organizations.

Jeff Derksen is an Associate Professor in the English Department at 
Simon Fraser University. He has published several books of poetry. 
Two books of essays, one titled Annihilated Time: Poetry and Other 
Politics, and the other on art and urbanism in the long neoliberal 
moment, After Euphoria, are forthcoming. Recent writings on cities 
include the article How High Is the City, How Deep Is Our Love? (Fillip), 
and a series of columns on the cultural politics of the Olympics 
(Camera Austria). Later this year, Derksen becomes the editor of West 
Coast Line, a Vancouver-based literary journal.

Cecily Nicholson is a poet and organizer who has worked with women of 
the Downtown Eastside community of Vancouver since 2000, the last 
four years as a coordinator of the Downtown Eastside Women's Centre. 
She facilitates community arts initiatives, collaborating most recently 
with the Audain Gallery of Simon Fraser University, VIVO Media Arts 
and the No One is Illegal Vancouver creative resistance committee. 
Triage is her first book of published writing.

Matthew Stadler is an award-winning writer and editor who lives in 
Portland, Oregon. He has written five novels, and has compiled four 
anthologies about literature, city life and public life. His essays have 
been published in magazines and museum catalogues around the 
world, and focus on architecture, urbanism, community and the problem 
of sprawl. Stadler's numerous essays and larger projects, such as 
suddenly.org (including the anthology Where We Live Now) explore 
these diverse subjects. Stadler is co-founder of the original Publication 
Studio in Portland and of Clear Cut Press, an independent literary 
publishing company and distributor.
