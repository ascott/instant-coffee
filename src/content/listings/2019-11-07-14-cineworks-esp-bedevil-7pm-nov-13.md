Blurb:
Free event

Cineworks and Fillip are pleased to present the fourth screening in E.S.P, an ongoing
film series looking at the relationship between film and speculative modes of visuality.

This screening celebrates the 26th anniversary of Tracey Moffats film BeDevil - the
artists first and only feature film, and the recipient of the Cannes Film Festival Official
Selection (1993). BeDevil is a three part horror film that delves into racial inequalities by
way of ghost stories told as a supernatural trilogy. Each story tells of peculiar characters,
haunted by a past memory of tragedy. Moffat skillfully plays with literal and figurative
horror tropes, layering BeDevil with different temporalities of hauntings. These hauntings
create meaningful disruptions to linear colonial histories, and Moffats cinematic form
shows the deep influence of the Australian landscape and oral storytelling. The
release
of BeDevil marked the first feature film directed by an Aboriginal Australian woman.

E.S.P.: BeDevil continues our investigation into the interdisciplinary space between
documentary, narrative, and formalist film, presenting work that complicates notions of
science and fiction and the binary systems of knowledge they have come to represent.

About Tracey Moffatt

Tracey Moffatt is an acclaimed Australian artist who has produced experimental and
risk-taking video and photography works for the past 30 years. In 2017, she was
appointed as the Australian representative at the 57th Venice Biennale with her solo
exhibition My Horizon. Her work has been exhibited throughout Europe, Canada, the
United States and Asia Pacific. Aside from BeDevil, some of her other works include
several short films such as Nice Coloured Girls (1987) and Night Cries: A Rural Tragedy
(1989), which was screened at the Cannes Film Festival in 1990. Moffatts works are
currently held in collections at the
Tate (London), Museum of Contemporary Art (Los
Angeles), National Gallery of Australia (Canberra), Art Gallery of South Australia
(Adelaide) and Art Gallery of New South Wales (Sydney).
