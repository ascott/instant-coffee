Exhibition Description

Improbable Dances
This body of work explores the way humans move through the landscape.

The nature of connection raises the question of how humans interact with nature: the
nature of our connection to it and our imposition upon it. We seek ways to heal
ourselves and reconnect to nature yet in our zealousness we dont always notice the
harmful effects we have on the rest of the creatures that live in the places we choose
to visit and inhabit.

Are we trying to get too close, encroaching on animals' space for the thrill, disrupting
nature by creating trails and recreating with vehicles? What are the ripple effects for
the animals that are living their lives in those environments?

A Series of 14 Improbable Dances includes drawings extracted from my
sketchbooks and superimposed onto imagined landscapes. "My practice of
sketchbook drawing in the dark during live dance performances started 30 years ago
and continues to this day." The drawings capture the
essence and spontaneity of
movement in loose gestures that can be, surprisingly, quite accurate.

Walking and paddling through a landscape is like a dance for me and a way to
connect to myself and nature. I created imaginary landscapes with textures and
landscapes that I had experienced in past travels and placed the dancer drawings
within them. Imagine travelling through the world with a lighter footprint.

Artist Biography

Joyce Majiskis work delves into the profound connection to place and the natural
world. Her background as a biologist and wilderness adventure guide in the Yukon
Territory enriches her artistic practice, leading her to explore remote wild spaces in
the North. With a deep fascination for the intricate interdependence between all
living beings, she meticulously observes and collects objects, infusing her artwork
with a profound sense of curiosity.

Majiski's artistic repertoire spans printmaking, drawing, painting, handmade paper,
sculpture, and multimedia
installation work. She collaborates with musicians and
fellow artists to create immersive environments, reflecting her intuitive approach to
art-making. Joyce's diverse interests and experiences have led her to artist
residencies in 11 countries, and she has showcased her solo exhibitions across
Canada, Mexico, the US, and Europe. She is the visionary behind initiatives such as
the Artist in the Park program in Ivvavik National Park and the Art Under Pressure
Giant Steamroller print event, demonstrating her commitment to fostering artistic
endeavors both locally and internationally. Joyce has called the Yukon home since
1984.
