VIVO Media Art Centre is proud to present Vancouver-based filmmaker
Barry Doupé's first feature-length animation, Ponytail.

"Computer animations that look like technology on the verge of
collapse describe a miasmic exploration of theory, philosophy and
personal expression." Whitechapel Gallery, London

"There is no ponytail in Ponytail. At the beginning there is a blonde
girl, looking at us out of a picture frame. Black ink is flowing from
her mouth and eyes. Surrounded by unnatural noises, the scene
distorts. A female voice on the radio repeats the words “far away”,
like a mantra. In Ponytail, Barry Doupé creates a bewildering collage
of rudimentary scenarios for his Sims-look-alike characters. Some of
the oppressed and rotten protagonists only appear once during this
course whereas others return and become familiar to viewers. Jennifer
is the glue that creates a connection between the characters- they
know her, they all strive to find her. While comparing this girl with
flowers, one Dutch speaking avatar explains that Jennifer symbolizes
better times. “We’ll come out okay too. Just like those flowers”. “The
thing about Jennifer is, the closer you get to her, the more time
slows down, so you’ll never actually be able to come near her”. Set in
supernatural light, crystalline, glowing, the shapes and colours in
the film seem to compensate for the characters' lack of determination.
All of them are calm, stigmatized with anxiety and a severe inability
to live out their personal desires. This makes the quiescent flow of
Ponytail both inspiring and alarming." Constanze Bauer

ABOUT BARRY DOUPÉ
Barry Doupe (b. Victoria, BC) is a filmmaker living in Vancouver. The
recipient of a 2005 and 2006 Media Arts Canada Council Production
Grant for emerging artists, he is also a founding member of The Lions
collaborative drawing group. Previous works, A Boy on a Dock Blowing
His Nose (2004), Distraught Mother Reunites With Her Children (2005),
At he Heart of A Sparrow (2006), have screened throughout Canada and
internationally including the Ann Arbor Film Festival, Anthology Film
Archives, Lyon Contemporary Art Museum, and the Tate Modern.

Named by the Canada Council as one of six high achieving young
contemporary artists, Emily Carr graduate Doupé took two years to
complete Ponytail. With previous screenings at Whitechapel Gallery in
London (Radical Animation Now) and Pleasure Dome in Toronto (The Lower
World), VIVO Media Arts Centre is proud to present the
Vancouver premiere of Ponytail on May 22nd 2009.
