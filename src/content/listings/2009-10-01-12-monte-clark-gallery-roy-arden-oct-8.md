ROY ARDEN
People of British Columbia

October 8 - October 31, 2009
Opening reception: Thursday October 8th, 6-8pm

In 2009, two new murals by Roy Arden were unveiled at the Vancouver
Convention Centre Expansion Project in Coal Harbour, Vancouver. The
Monte Clark Gallery is pleased to announce Arden's exhibition People
of British Columbia, featuring archival pigment print versions of
these murals alongside new companion works.

Using historic photo images from the Vancouver Public Library, Arden’s
giant collage Big House (2008) depicts events in pre-WWII British
Columbia. The focus of Big House is the social and economic struggles,
conflicts and realities of B.C.'s early years. The epic scope of the
work takes in the culture of the native peoples, the conflicts between
the various ethnic and social groups, the resource-based economy and
the building of cities and societies.

The second mural, People of British Columbia (2009), is a triptych
that focuses on the experience of the natural sublime and the
communion of peoples through leisure. Arden has collaged and
manipulated early photos of bathers at local beaches into a poem
reminding us that the object of our work and struggles is freedom and
joy.

Roy Arden was born in Vancouver and received his Masters of Fine Arts
from the University of British Columbia in 1990. His work has recently
been show at the Museum of Modern Art (New York), the LABoral Centro
de Arte in Gijon (Spain), and the Espai d´art Contemporani de Castelló
(also in Spain) as part of the International Contemporary Art Prize.
His solo survey exhibition was shown at the Vancouver Art Gallery in
2007. Arden’s work is held in numerous public collections including
the Art Gallery of Ontario, the Canadian Centre for Contemporary
Photography (National Gallery of Canada), the Los Angeles County
Museum of Art, Centre Pompidou in Paris, Museu d’Art Contemporani de
Barcelona, The Museum of Modern Art, and the Vancouver Art Gallery.
