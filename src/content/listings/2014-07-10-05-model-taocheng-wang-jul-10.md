REFLECTIONS

TAOCHENG WANG

JULY 10-AUG 9 2014

OPENING THURSDAY, JULY 10TH, 8PM-11PM

The exhibition Reflections combines five video pieces made by
Netherlands-based artist Taocheng Wang between 2012 and 2013, collectively
called Reflection Paper. These videos are based on text by the female
Chinese writer Eileen Chang (b. 1920; d. 1995). Chang's work was written
and published during the period of Japanese occupation prior to her move to
the United States in 1952. Her writing style reminds readers not only of
traditional Chinese literature and poetry, but also of New Wave Film,
fragments of Modern Art, and the taste and privilege of the well-educated
bourgeoisie -- a section of Chinese society that was effectively destroyed
during ten years of the Culture Revolution (though the the high-speed
economy of New China would seem to like to have it back again).

Every scene follows the text strictly, while a rapid voiceover explains the
scene over again. Wang's 'amateur' Chinese-English and long-winded,
frenetic voiceover can't really match and explain every scene
synchronously, leading the viewer to doubt the relationship between the
appropriated text and Wang's images. Is this a comment on Chang's text? Is
this a film seriously made? However, Wang points out, reality is often
inappropriate.
