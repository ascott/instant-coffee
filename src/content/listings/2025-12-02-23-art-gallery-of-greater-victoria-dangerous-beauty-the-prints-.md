The Art Gallery of Greater Victoria Presents:

Dangerous Beauty:

The Prints of Albrecht Drer

Victoria, BC - Opening on December 13, the Art Gallery of Greater Victoria presents,
Dangerous Beauty: The Prints of Albrecht Drer. Sharing artwork from the AGGV
collection, this exhibition presents twelve prints by Albrecht Drer (1471-1528). The
selected prints showcase the artists extraordinary skill as a printmaker and his fascination
with dramatic subjects centred on danger. The exhibition is curated by AGGV Chief
Curator & Director of Exhibitions, Steven McNeil.

Albrecht Drer was an exceptionally gifted painter and printmaker. Initially trained as a
goldsmith, Drer turned to printmaking as a young adult, producing woodcut prints for
wide distribution, as well as engravings for a more specialized audience. He brought a
level of technical skill to his profession that was ground-breaking and had a significant
impact on artistic practice.

Dangerous Beauty brings together a selection of prints " woodcuts and engravings " by
Albrecht Drer, an artist that was hugely important to Northern European Renaissance.
Drers work stands in terms of the painstaking level of detail: when his prints began to
circulate viewers were stunned by his skill, said McNeil. He was also revolutionary for
the way in which he distributed his work " at fairs across Europe - and carefully created a
public image for himself and a market for his own works. The prints in this exhibition show
a range of his subjects: wild mixtures of serene beauty alongside monstrous figures and
intense scenes of impending danger.

This exhibition was developed through the J. Paul Getty Foundation initiative, The Paper
Project: Prints and Drawings Curatorship in the 21st Century, a program that supports
training and professional development for curators of prints and drawings. Through this
program, Steven McNeil participated in The Curating Prints Seminar co-organized by The
Paper Project and the academic journal Print Quarterly in London, UK, in 2024. By
supporting curators in navigating the demands of the 21st-century museum, both by
preserving traditional skills that have been passed down through generations of specialists
and by supporting efforts to make graphic arts collections accessible and relevant to
todays audiences. The Paper Project inspired the realization of Dangerous Beauty: The
Prints of Albrecht Drer.
