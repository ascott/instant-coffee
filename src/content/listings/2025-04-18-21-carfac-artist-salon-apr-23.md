Looking for services to support and develop your art practice? Wondering how artists
get paid to show their work? What are the legal and economic rights of visual artists
in Canada? Where can an artist find a supportive creative community?

CARFAC, or Canadian Artists' Representation/Le Front des Artistes Canadiens is a
national non-profit organization that advocates for Canada's visual artists. Current
https://carfacbc.ca/ President, Trevor Van den Eijinden, will provide an overview of
the many resources they offer for artists including current guidelines in contractual
issues, artist fees and legal rights.

Bring along your questions and join in the discussion on strategies for artists at all
stages in their careers to further their practice and discover creative opportunities in
BC.

About the Presenter:
Van den Eijnden (they/them, he/him) is a visual artist, and educator born in Nova
Scotia and currently living in Vancouver, British Columbia. He received two
simultaneous undergraduate degrees in 2005-a BA from Dalhousie University and a
BFA from NSCAD University-that have both had an impact on his practice. In 2015
he completed a Master of Visual Art from Emily Carr University of Art + Design.
Having a lifelong interest in education, he has worked as faculty, department head,
and academic director at several private and public institutions in the Lower
Mainland of British Columbia. His practice as an artist usually coalesces around
time, grief, and nature, while as an educator he focuses on research, history, and
cross-media concept-focused production.

About CARFAC BC:
CARFAC BC the collective voice for professional artists in British Columbia
advocating for artists' legal rights, and offering practical advice and skill development
to support the professional practices of cultural producers.
Since 1990, CARFAC BC functions as a volunteer organization on behalf of its
membership. CARFAC BC receives no operational funding. It functions on the
resources of its members alone, and is primarily run by volunteers and an active
Board of Directors. Together, we have the strength to make fair contracts and
payments of proper fees the norm, to tackle the illegal reproduction of artists' work,
and lobby for tax reform, among other critical issues. With over twenty-five thousand
artists in British Columbia, we hold tremendous untapped power to change the
conditions in which we live and work.
