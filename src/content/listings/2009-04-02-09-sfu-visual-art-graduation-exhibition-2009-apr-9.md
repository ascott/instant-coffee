Amidst the train tracks, warehouses and factory outlets of east Vancouver,
Simon Fraser Universityâ&#65533;&#65533;s Visual Art program sits three stories up and
spans fifteen thousand square feet.

The space is unique in many ways; its open-concept design allows no corner
of complete solitude so that student artists quickly learn how to work
within a small art community. Space is shared, tools are borrowed, a
friendly dog runs through. Dishes are never done. Out of the program come
eighteen student artists, who for the most part, have spent their
university education as a singular unit- a cohort during four years of
studio classes. For better or for worse we have learned to function as a
group, how to share space and how to be constructively critical of each other.

Within this university context, students have the opportunity to study
across disciplines. In fact, most of this yearâ&#65533;&#65533;s graduating students have
another minor or even second degree ranging from molecular biology to
English, from psychology to publishing. Within the School for Contemporary
Arts itself, individuals can decide to explore dance, film, music, and
theatre in conjunction with their visual art practice.

As a result, SFUâ&#65533;&#65533;s art students make work that melds together these various
schools of thought within the world of contemporary visual art. While some
students devote themselves to a single medium, most are not specialists but
rather experimentalists, using the media most appropriate to articulate
their ideas. The result is that our grad show includes everything from a
monumental plaster death mask to self-modeled silicone dolls to a chapter
from economist Milton Friedman painstaking carved into re-purposed
cardboard While these things seem wildly different, inevitably living and
working in such close proximity to each other one work will naturally
influence another.

The exhibition is accompanied by a colour catalog that features essays by
Penelope Hetherington and Sarah Kathryn Young who are from respectively
SFU's English and Arts and Culture Studies programs.
