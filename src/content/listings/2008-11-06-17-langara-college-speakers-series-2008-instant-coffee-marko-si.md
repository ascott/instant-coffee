Instant Coffee Project for the Main Street Urban Transportation Showcase
Public Art Program Instant Coffee is an internationally recognized artist
collective based in Toronto and Vancouver. The collective developed, in
part, as a response to the division and exaggerated difference between
studio and exhibition practice. Through formal installations and
event-based activities, it builds a public place to practice, where ideas,
materials and actions can be explored outside of the isolated studio and in
a manner that renegotiates traditional exhibition structures, but is
still supported by them. For Instant Coffee, the triad of art practice
production, presentation and reception becomes jumbled, incorporating the
social as a priority.
Instant Coffee will discuss their newest project, due to launch in November
and running through to April, 2009. The first of a series of projects to
activate both the transit and civic infrastructure of Main Street, it
involves temporary and permanent artworks including exterior bus wraps and
panels, interior ad cards, and street signage. The project is supported by
TransLink, Transport Canad's Urban Transportation Showcase Program and the
City of Vancouver. Instant Coffee's most consistent members are Cecilia
Berkovic, Jinhan Ko, Kelly Lycan, Jenifer Papararo, and Kate Monro, with
the recent addition of Khan Lee. Instant Coffee has presented numerous
projects for public spaces in Canada, Europe, and the U.S. Learn more about
Instant Coffee at www.instantcoffee.org.

Marko Simcic
Park, a project for the Ontario Street Greenway Marko Simcic's Park will
activate the entire length of Ontario Street with a program of moveable
artworks beginning in November, 2008. Commissioned by the City of Vancouver
Public Art Program, Park involved extensive community consultations and
design reviews over a number of years, which informed the final forms and
method of delivery of the work. Park will consist of sculptural objects,
each approximately the size and weight of a small automobile. They will
temporarily rest in curb lane parking spots, and will be relocated from
time to time to various addresses along the route. Whether adjacent to
public sites, such as schools or parks, commercial properties to the north,
or permitted to occupy residential parking through their adoptionby
Ontario Street residents, Park tracks the many forms by which the street,
our most symbolic public space, is regulated. Simcic will discuss the
process of developing the project and its upcoming launch at the Pendulum
Gallery. Marko Simcic is an independent architect and artist with a history
of sited artwork commissions with the cities of Richmond and Vancouver.
Extensively researched, his practice engages with the multiple boundaries
found in social, spatial, political, temporal, bureaucratic and material
conditions. His architectural work has included several community projects
such as the Grace Church in Vancouver, the Cates Hill Church on Bowen
Island and several Gulf Island retreats. He has been a mentor for the
Architecture Institute of BC, and a thesis advisor and instructor at the
University of British Columbia. Awards include the Abe Roganick award
(1990) and a Canadian Architect award (2003) and a Lieutenant Governor's
Award in Architecture (2008). Recent public art projects include Light
Ring, a collaborative, sited work in Richmond. Future Speakers Our 2009
