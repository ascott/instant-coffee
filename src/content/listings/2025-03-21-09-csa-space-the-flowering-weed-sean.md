Alward | 6 PM | MAR 21
The Flowering Weed
Sean Alward
Friday, March 21
6:00-8:00 pm

An exhibition of new painting/sculpture
Curated by Jeffrey Boone

The paintings in this show are a combination of monoprint and painting, with carbon
black paint squashed and pressed flat onto the surface of raw linen. The subject is
both the flattened paint and the thing delivering the paint: common weeds such as
Himalayan Blackberry, Docks, and Japanese Knotweed, selected from the environs
of the studio where the paintings were made. These weeds represent a kind of
'marginal' nature ever present in our city, usually ignored, reviled, or at best,
tolerated. In their flattened form on the fabric they are a type of landscape painting
presented to scale, a life-size view of Vancouver. They are also a portrait of the
actual nature we live with in our city, rather than the idealized nature we imagine
existing outside it. Inevitably,
questions arise about this uncontrolled and unwanted
plant-life. How do weeds differ from other plants, why are they here, and what role
do they play in our conception of place?

The sculpture presented is a segmented bronze cast of a common Himalayan
Blackberry cane, also visible in the paintings. The cast in made though the 'lost
organics' casting process, in which the cane pieces are encased in plaster, burned
out, and the void filled with molten bronze. The result is a singular cast of the original
organic material, a kind of metallic 'snapshot' meticulously preserving the
appearance of the plant at that moment. The bronze sculpture appears to be a single
coherent plant, but is in fact composed of many plants harvested at different times of
the year and stages in their cycle of sprouting, flowering, and bearing fruit. Natural
time collapses and is figuratively flattened.
