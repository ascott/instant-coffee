10. CSA space | Danna Vajda | until JAN 12
CSA space presents :

upagainstacurtainwall
Danna Vajda


Opening reception Thursday 13 December 2013, 6-9pm
12 December 2013 through 12 January 2013

Co-curated by Steven Tong and Steffanie Ling

A curtain printed with real estate is the artist's new dress

A hand that gives once tilted is the hand that asks

The paper trail of ATM receipts lead to a silk road, or a pit of tears

This project assembles recent textile works, small sculptures, paintings, and 
a script that hovers around issues of private property and the extent that the 
body flows through, with, in and against it. A curtained wall as architectural 
concept is invoked throughout the exhibition, as a side step from the notion of 
the "glass ceiling" within labour hierarchies, to address the limits of personal, 
political, and creative acts. On one surface these acts appear to be governed 
by a smooth representational or linguistic order, the mirrored glass, but on the 
reverse side, a hard complex material structure. Accordingly the works in the 
exhibition are a veil or frame for the central text: a collage of legal, official and 
personal testaments to the internalization of a city and its property relations.

Danna Vajda, is an artist and writer based in Brooklyn, New York. Her work 
has shown at in New York (Art in General, Sculpture Center, e-flux, CCS 
Hessel Museum), Chigago (The Suburban), L.A. (Night Gallery) and London 
(FormContent).

See Pulpfiction Books (2422 Main St.) for admission during regular business 
hours:
Mon-Wed 10am-8pm, Thurs-Sat 10am-9pm, Sundays and holidays 11am-7pm

12. Alternator Artist Co-op | Call | JAN 31
Call for Submissions : Alternator Artist Co-op

The Alternator is currently accepting applications to participate in our next 
Artist Co-op program, starting in March 2014.

If you require a studio space to work in and are interested in developing new 
ways to engage the community, we want to hear from you! 

The Artist Co-op program is designed to allow access to a dedicated studio 
space for emerging artists, designers and makers interested in a 
concentrated period of community-focused arts practice.

Each Co-op partnership will include atleast 3 months of project development 
in Studio 111 and conclude with a paid professional exhibition in the 
Alternator Window Gallery. The current CARFAC rate for the window gallery 
is $414.00.

Successful applicants will share the expense of hosting the co-op and will be 
responsible for contributing $400/month towards the program costs. Your 
proposals should include details on how the use of Alternator Artist Co-op 
studio space will impact your professional practice and a summary of ways 
the you would like to engage the public as part of the program. This may be 
as part of a community art project, public art project, the inclusion of open 
studio days or artists talks which will contribute to the Alternator 
programming.

To apply for the Alternator Artist Co-op, please submit the following:

· A brief description (maximum 500 words) clearly outlining how the use 
of Alternator Artist Co-op studio space will impact your professional practice 
and a summary of ways you would like to engage the public as part of the 
program. Please include an outline of the creative work you will undertake 
during the co-op and a preliminary description of the work you intend to show 
in the Window Gallery.

· Artist statement (maximum 200 words)

· Curriculum vitae

· 5-7 images with an image list detailing medium and size

Please submit images in jpeg format. Label jpeg images with number and title, 
for example: 01_title.jpg, 02_title.jpg etc. Jpeg images must be 72 dpi, 
maximum 1024 x 768 pixels. 500 KB (0.5MB).

Deadline for submissions for the March 2014 co-op is January 31, 2014.

Submissions for the Alternator Artist Co-op are accepted online only. Please 
email your submission to info@alternatorgallery.com with 'Artist Co-op 
Proposal' in the subject line.

13. AMS Art Gallery, UBC | Paradigm Shift | CALL FOR SUBMISSIONS | JAN 08
Paradigm Shift | CALL FOR SUBMISSIONS
Deadline: January 8th 2014
Show running from January 20-31st 2014 at the AMS Art Gallery, UBC

Submissions are wanted for an exhibition inspired by the concept of the 
paradigm shift, a term popularized by the physicist and philosopher of 
science Thomas Kuhn. Although this term has become part of everyday 
vernacular, Kuhn originally uses it to describe momentous shifts in scientific 
thinking that give birth to new concepts and practices that challenge and 
change the way we think.

This exhibition will address the effect of paradigm shifts within science as 
well as their power to change the way we think about and understand the 
world in a larger context—politically, socially, artistically, and spiritually.

Submissions in any medium are welcome and should be emailed to 
ubcsaganeditor@gmail.com. Please include: 

-An artist statement (title, name of artist, date made, medium, dimensions)
-A high-resolution (300dpi+) image of the work(s)
-Technical needs (if submitting a video, sound, or performance piece)

The show will be put on by the Carl Sagan Association for the Communication 
of Science. For more information, please visit: http://ubcsagan.org 


14. BC Arts Council | Launch new grant | Early Career Development | JAN 20
The BC Arts Council is pleased to announce the launch of a new pilot 
program to support the development of artists and arts administrators across 
the province.

The BC Arts Council’s Early Career Development Program will help bridge the 
gap for emerging artists and practitioners, supporting their ability to work as 
artists and cultural workers, while building their body of work, portfolio, 
professional exposure and/or career experience through residency, 
internship and mentorship opportunities. This program is accessible to both 
individual artists and organizations. Individuals can apply for the residencies 
or mentorship components.

The program will support the career development of B.C.-based early career 
and emerging practitioners through three components:
Component I: Internships
Component II: Residencies
Component III: Mentorships

For the purposes of this program, early career and emerging artists and arts 
practitioners are defined as artists and practitioners who either:

o Are under the age of 30; or

o Have less than five years professional experience since the completion of 
basic training in their discipline.

This pilot program is being launched through the BC Creative Futures 
strategy. Announced last January, BC Creative Futures supports 
opportunities for youth in communities throughout British Columbia to engage 
in the arts and creative thinking and encourages young people to pursue 
creative careers. 

The application deadline is January 20, 2014. For more information, including 
program guidelines, application forms and FAQs, please visit the BC Arts 
Council website at:

ent.html

If you have any clarifying questions please feel free to contact either of
