Thursday, January 26, 4:30-6:30 pm
Emily Carr University of Art + Design, South Building 301

Hafthor Yngvason has been the Director of the Western Gallery and Sculpture 
Collection at Western Washington University, Bellingham since September 2015. He 
was the Director of the Reykjavik Art Museum, in Iceland from 2005 to 2015 and the 
Director of Public Art at the Cambridge Arts Council in Cambridge, Massachusetts from 
1995 to 2005. After earning graduate degrees in Philosophy and Art History in the 
United States, Yngvason started his career as the managing director of Harcus Gallery 
in Boston. He has served as speaker, panelist and art juror for various universities and 
public art organizations in the United States and Europe.

Yngvason will speak to his research and preparations for an exhibition that expands on 
a theme found in Western Washington University's outdoor sculpture collection, which is 
that many of the artistBruce Nauman, Isamu Noguchi, Nancy Holt, Lloyd Hamrol, and 
George Trakainvite viewers to look from their sculptures at something else. These 
expanded sculptures will be associated with works from the Washington Art 
Consortium's exceptional collection of works on paper from the same period, 
predominantly the 1970s, with the aid of ideas from Benjamin Buchloh' and Margaret 
Iversen's writings on "diagrammatic" abstractions in contemporary paintings and 
drawings as "fields for performance." This forthcoming project is the second in a series 
of such exhibitions at Western Gallery planned over the next few years, where the works 
on paper collection is used to expand on the campus' public art.

This event is the first in a series of Curator's talks to be held at Emily Carr University of 
Art and Design, produced in partnership with the Vancouver Art Gallery and 221A. The 
series is associated with the exhibition Vancouver Special: Ambivalent Pleasures and 
supported by the Province of British Columbia's Creative Economy Strategy. Further 
Details: http://221a.ca/vancouver-special-ambivalent-pleasures-library-and-public-
program
