You can't turn on the radio, scroll through your newsfeed, or go to 
coffee with a friend without mention of Vancouver's affordability crisis. 
As if to add insult to injury, the onslaught of heritage home demolitions 
in recent memory has severed new generations from a history of 
craftsmanship, wasting precious first growth wood and destroying 
tender narratives in the process. In the height of Vancouver's real 
estate frenzy, more often than not these well-built homes are given no 
future beyond becoming a gaping hole in our suburban 
neighbourhoods.

Whether Early Vernacular homes of the 1870's, or Vancouver Specials 
of the 1960's, our historical structures are, what Caroline Adderson 
calls, "repositories for narrative." They represent our colonial pasts--
expressing all things difficult and all things beautiful about our City's 
collective heritage. In partnership with the Historic Joy Kogawa 
House's Writing Residency Program, the publication "(un)realty" aims 
to honour the histories, alternative futures and temperaments of 
Vancouver's heritage houses.

Each contributing artist or writer will create a text (poetry, creative non-
fiction, short story or play) or image based response to a heritage 
house facing demolition, considering (but not limited to) the following 
themes:
- An alternative future for the house, whether fictitious or non-fictitious.
- A narrative based on a researched or proposed character that at one 
point lived in the house.
- A letter to the house, anthropomorphizing the building.
- An article discussing the house's history as situated within the 
neighborhood.
- Photograph(s), drawing(s), painting(s), digital work(s), or documented 
performance(s) relating to the house's history, politics, or the broader 
subject of heritage homes in Vancouver.
- A text/image exploring what the concepts of "heritage" or "home" 
mean within a colonial context.

In order to submit, please include the following information in a single 
PDF and send to unrealtyvan@outlook.com:
- 250 word (max) abstract that relates the basic concept of your 
submission and what form the work will take (what kind of text/artwork 
it can be described as). Please note an approximate word count, and 
whether this work is in progress/ completed/ can be completed by May 
21st.
- 100 word (max) biography/artist statement.
- 1 page CV.

Proposals for the physical publication are due on May 5th. Ongoing 
submissions for online content will be accepted until May 21st.
For more information, visit: http://www.unrealtyvan.wordpress.com, or 
contact: unrealtyvan@outlook.com.

Historic Joy Kogawa House is the childhood home of Canadian author 
Joy Kogawa. Joy and her family lived in the house until 1942, when 
they were sent to an internment camp along with thousands of other 
Canadians of Japanese descent. Now used as a site for author 
residencies and literary events, the house stands as a historical 
reminder of the internment experience of Japanese Canadians during 
the Second World War, and by extension, to the experiences of diverse 
cultural and ethnic groups within Canadian society.
