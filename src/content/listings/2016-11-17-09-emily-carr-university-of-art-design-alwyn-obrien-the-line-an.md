North-West Ceramics Foundation presents:
Alwyn O'Brien: The Line and the Fold

Thursday, November 17, 2016 - 7:30 pm
Rm 245, North Building
Emily Carr University of Art + Design
1399 Johnston Street, Granville Island

All Welcome, free

The North-West Ceramics Foundation is pleased to present a lecture by Alwyn 
O'Brien. For O'Brien, the vessel is the "foundational metaphor" of her practice, 
but her vessels in no way resemble conventional containers. Consisting of 
assymetrical helps of lacy clay filaments elaborated to the point of near collapse, 
her ambitious yet fragile structures explore aspects of desire, entropy and loss. 
As a maker, she is interested in "the threshold where the immaterial and the 
material...fall into each other..." Incorporating historical references--in particular, 
to the Baroque--her vessels undermine notions of stability, embody fluidity, and 
celebrate the irregular and the possible.

Born and raised on Salt Spring Island, Alwyn O'Brien attended Sheridan College 
of Craft and Design, Oakville (diploma 2002), Emily Carr University of Art + 
Design, Vancouver (BFA 2004), and the University of Washington, Seattle (MFA 
2010). She has attended residencies at Pilchuck Glass in Washington State, 
Skowhegan School of Painting and Sculpture in Maine and the international 
wood-fired ceramics symposium in Kohila Parish, Estonia.

In 2012, she was nominated for the RBC Emerging Artist competition held at the 
Gardiner Museum, Toronto, and was awarded the North-West Ceramics 
Foundation Award for excellence in the field of ceramics. In 2015 O'Brien won 
the prestigious Winifred Shantz Award for Ceramics after which she studied 
Talavera Pottery at the Arquetopia Foundation in Puebla, Mexico. She has 
participated in numerous solo and group exhibitions in Canada and the United 
States, and her work held in public and private collections in both those 
countries. She is represented by the James Harris Gallery, Seattle, WA and is 
currently an instructor in ceramics at Langara College, Vancouver.
