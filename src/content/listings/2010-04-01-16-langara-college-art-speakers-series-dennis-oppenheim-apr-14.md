Langara Public Art Speakers Series presents: Dennis Oppenheim

Wednesday, April 14, 7pm
Langara College, 100 W 49th Ave
Free Admission

Langara College Centre for Art in Public Spaces and the Vancouver Biennale
invite you to a discussion with internationally renowned artist Dennis
Oppenheim. Join us for an engaging panel discussion about art, philosophy,
and more.

About the Artist
Dennis Oppenheim is one of the most influential, innovative and respected
figures in contemporary art today. Vancouverites first experienced his work
during the 2005 Vancouver Biennale, which included the infamous Device to
Root Out Evil (aka Upside Down Church) and Engagement. After much public
debate, Device was shipped off to Calgary's Glenbow Museum. Engagement,
which coincided with Canada's same sex marriage debates, got a better
reception. The work has since been acquired by the Vancouver Biennale
Legacy Foundation and is located on Sunset Beach.
Oppenheim returns to the Vancouver Biennale with Arriving Home. The
freedom and exhilaration of travel and mobility are represented in the
rhythmic movement of this circular sculpture. Positioned at the Vancouver
International Airport arrivals terminal, the sculpture stands as a greeting
to travelers as they arrive to the city.
Dennis Oppenheim has exhibited works in the most prestigious galleries,
museums and exhibitions throughout the world including the Tate Gallery,
Whitney Museum of American Art, the Museum of Modern Art (New York), the
Venice Biennale and Johannesburg Biennale. He has received numerous
international commissions including the 2008 Beijing Olympic Games and
George Bush Intercontinental Airport in Houston. He received a Lifetime
Achievement Award at the 2007 Vancouver Biennale.

About the Centre for Art in Public Spaces
The Langara College Centre for Art in Public Spaces engages communities in
the research, production, and presentation of art in the public realm. The
Centre encourages dialogue, and fosters opportunities to collaborate on,
experience, and learn about art in public spaces.

About the Vancouver Biennale
The Vancouver Biennale is a non-profit organization that mounts bi-annual
exhibitions of international contemporary art in public spaces including
parks, beaches and urban plazas, turning the city into an open-air museum.
Each exhibition includes a diverse mix of world renowned artists and rising
super stars in sculpture, new media and performance works. The Vancouver
Biennale is committed to leaving a legacy of public art in the city, as
well as creating educational materials, professional symposiums, public
lectures and publications.
