Michael Morris in conversation with Scott Watson
Tuesday, March 20, 7pm
Vancouver Art Gallery
750 Hornby St.
4th floor, 4 EAST

The tumultuous era of the 60s was punctuated by a rich development 
of modernist painting across the country. Michael Morris was a central 
figure in the Vancouver scene, and his work helped define the strong 
regional flavour of West Coast painters and their contemporaries. Join 
Morris in conversation with curator Scott Watson, as he speaks on his 
experience during this formative time in Canadian art history and the 
expansion of his painting and experimental practices then and now.

Presented on the occasion of the exhibition Lights Out!: Canadian 
Painting from the 60s (February 18 - April 29, 2012), organized by the 
Vancouver Art Gallery and curated by Ian Thom, senior curator, 
historical.

Michael Morris is a painter, photographer, video and performance artist 
and curator. His work is often media based and collaborative, involved 
with developing networks in the production and presentation of new art 
activity. In 1969 he founded Image Bank with Vincent Trasov, a method 
for personal exchange between artists; in 1973 he was co-founder and 
co-director of Western Front Society, Vancouver; in 1981 he was 
invited with Trasov to Berlin as guests of the Berliner 
Künstlerprogramm DAAD; in 1990 he and Trasov founded the 
Morris/Trasov Archive, currently housed at Morris and Helen Belkin Art 
Gallery, Vancouver. Morris has curated numerous exhibitions. His work 
is represented in private and public collections nationally and 
internationally.

Scott Watson is director/curator of the Morris and Helen Belkin Art 
Gallery and professor in the Department of Art History, Visual Art, and 
Theory at the University of British Columbia, Vancouver. He is director 
and graduate advisor for the Critical Curatorial Studies program, which 
he helped initiate in 2002. Recent distinctions include the Alvin Balkind 
Award for Creative Curatorship in British Columbia Arts (2008), the 
UBC Dorothy Sommerset Award for Performance Development in the 
Visual and Performing Arts (2005), and invitation to the UBC 
Chancellor's Circle (2005). Watson has published extensively in the 
areas of contemporary Canadian and international art.
