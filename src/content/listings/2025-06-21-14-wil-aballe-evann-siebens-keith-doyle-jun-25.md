Part artist book, part project documentation and now an exhibition, Pedestrian
Protest chronicles the forty-three dancers, activists, performance and visual artists
who collaborated with Evann Siebens and Keith Doyle on their public art installation
at the Vancouver Art Gallerys Offsite in 2021. Texts in the newly-launched
publication include a blessing by Coast Salish Elder Kat Norris, a historical essay by
VAG curator Diana Freundl; an interview between Siebens, Doyle and Alejandro A.
Barbosa; and essays, text and excerpts from interviews with each of the participants.
The book features photographs and media collages made by Siebens in
collaboration with each of the participants.

From the lead artists:

"We acknowledge that we live and create on the unceded territories of the
xwmkwy" m (Musqueam), Swx w7mesh (Squamish) and sli" lwta (Tsleil-
Waututh) nations. As white artists, with settler origins of European ancestry, we hope
to humbly honour these lands with our creative endeavours. While working on
Pedestrian Protest, we were privileged to collaborate with extraordinarily talented
artists from many communities. We do not aim to claim their practices as our own,
rather to stand alongside, as allies, to give space and voice to the artists that make
up the layered project Pedestrian Protest. We asked our local community of dancers,
activists, musicians, and visual artists to respond to the concept of protest. Each was
asked to choose a site along Georgia Street, or beyond, and to create an action,
choreography or performance that would be documented though media and
photography. Created during COVID-19 through socially distant exterior film shoots,
the creativity and intimacy of the experience is palpable. Many spoke through
movement and interviews of how their existence embodies protest, that moving
through the world in their body is a protest. Still others spoke of their quiet activism
and how protest can be seen as healing and as medicine, particularly in the urban
environment.

Part of our own activism has been to bring attention to performers and performers
rights. Each artist or collective was compensated beyond CARFAC (Canadian
Artists Representation) rates, fully credited, and also holds shared rights to anything
involving their creative work and contribution to Pedestrian Protest. Collaborating
artists also receive master copies of media, to edit and to create their own ongoing
projects."

- Evann Siebens + Keith Doyle

Evann Siebens and Keith Doyle collaborated with forty-three artists for the making of
Pedestrian Protest, both for the original public artwork at Vancouver Art Gallery
Offsite in 2021 and now in book form. Siebens makes media, performance and
photos with movement. Her lens-based practice negotiates the human body as an
archival site and the politics of the female gaze. Recent exhibitions include Trieste
Contemporanea, Italy; Cloud Seven and Wil Aballe, Brussels; MACBA, Barcelona;
and Kunstinstituut Melly in Rotterdam. Evanns documentary work previously
screened at the Museum of Modern Art in NYC and was broadcast on PBS. Her
work from 'A Performance Affair' in Brussels, Belgium was featured on the front page
of the International New York Times in 2019. Keith Doyle is Associate Dean,
Associate Professor of Design + Dynamic Media at Emily Carr University of Art +
Design. He is a Co-Director of Material Matters, which was featured in the
Vancouver Art Gallery exhibition Fashion Fictions in 2023. Doyle will be releasing the
book Material Matters Lab: Here//Too//For in 2026, published by the Vancouver Art
Gallery.
