In conjunction with the Gallery’s newest exhibition The Tree: From the Sublime to the Social, join exhibiting artist Jochen Gerz as he presents his current research on the role and function of public art in a cultural society.
 
Jochen Gerz is an artist whose public commissions deal with issues of memory, visibility and collaboration with the public. Recent works include The Place of the European Promise (Bochum) and Salviamo La Luna (Milan). Since the 1980s, he has created a number of anti-monuments in Germany, France, England and Ireland, subverting the idea of commemoration, involving the public and media in the process of creating and defining artwork: Monument against Fascism, Harburg, (with Esther Shalev-Gerz) 1986; Bremen Questionnaire, Bremen, 1990; 2,146 stones - Monument Against Racism (The Invisible Monument), Sarrebrück, 1990-93; The Living Monument, Biron, 1996; The Witnesses of Cahors, 1998; The Public Bench and The Future Monument, Coventry, 2004; amaptocare, Dublin, 2006.

09. Western Front Media Arts | Judy Radul: Artist-in-Residence
Western Front Media Arts 

Judy Radul
Artist-in-Residence: February 4 - February 29, 2008
In-Progress Presentation:  February 29, 2008, 8 PM

Celebrated Vancouver-based media artist Judy Radul will be developing the technical aspects of a major new video production project World Rehearsal Court (working title). The project is based on ongoing research into the performative and media aspects of the contemporary court of law. Radul's time at the Western Front will be spent developing new servo-controlled, programmable, repeatable camera movements for the series of live cameras that will be used in the installation. Specific trajectories will be designed for the cameras with the objective of providing viewers with an alternate perspective on the built environment in the gallery. This environment will refer to the theatre of the court, law, judgment, evidence, reiteration, and testimony. The completed work will be first shown at the Morris and Helen Belkin Gallery in the fall of 2009.

Seen from without, the Gasoline Romance is an imagined story; City
life permeates us. Its constancy, its information shrinks our scale of
meaning. Places we enter, objects we know suffer as well this
collective shift existing more as expressions of relativity then
expectations of being, as such. While some who pass through this
shifted landscape note its momentum as 'of the city', others imagine a
counter-melody.

Spaces want to be contemplated, symbols need their 'power' turned up,
objects have participatory expectations… through an innocent
engagement with the defiant re-imagining of a spectator, the duplicity
of acculturation is acknowledged. Its sudden absence, as an expression
of prior organization may well be the insinuation that a local
resonance holds the key to any larger understanding.

Seen from within, this referential idea, itself the vestige of an
earlier conceit allows an experience of the city-life that is not
beholden to its everyday reality. The Gasoline Romance, as such.

12. Shudder Galley | Paul Christian | FEB 8

 Periphery, February 9th - March 1st

Opening reception, February 8th 7pm - 11pm

A collection of photographic work by Paul Christian.

Paul Christian is a photo based artist who resides in Vancouver, B.C. His work is derived from an academic perspective that is both art historic and urban geographic. These influences contribute to the aesthetics and content of his photographs. A recurrent theme of predominately man made constructs concerning city and it's inevitable decay are evident in Paul's work. There is a sense of unveiling the reality behind the glory of our "Olympic" cityscape. The work examines that which is overlooked, discarded, and valueless in a city that is rapidly transforming.

In this time of great change, rebirth, and reconstruction, it is important to have documentation of the diversity of Vancouver, before it is white washed into a sleek, steal, suburbia. Paul's work documents aspects of the city soon to be lost and will have you looking at your surroundings with a new perspective.

Out On Screen, producer of the Vancouver Queer Film Festival, invites you to submit your film or video for consideration for our 20th annual event, to be held from August 14th – 24th, 2008. The VQFF is the largest queer media event in Western Canada, and each year we host 11 days and nights of outstanding cinema, performances, networking events and parties, all in the backdrop of beautiful mountain and ocean views. 
The Vancouver Queer Film Festival privileges outstanding innovation, regularly showcasing work that confronts stereotypes, pushes boundaries, provokes and titillates, and, above all, highlights the tremendous diversity of queer artists working in Canada and internationally. We present over 120 films each year, including short- and feature-length documentaries, narrative and experimental works. 
Situated in one of the most spectacular cities in the world, we welcome visiting artists and industry professionals, and provide great opportunities for both work and play.
