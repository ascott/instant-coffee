Book Launch
Contemporary Sculpture and the Critique of Display Cultures

by Dan Adler
Thursday, September 26 at 6 pm 

READ Books, 520 E 1st Ave

READ Books is pleased to invite you to the Vancouver launch of Dan Adler's book 
Contemporary Sculpture and the Critique of Display Cultures.

In this book, Adler addresses recent tendencies in contemporary art toward assemblage 
sculpture and how these works incorporate tainted materials " often things left on the 
side of the road, according to the logic and progress of the capitalist machine " and 
combine them in ways that allow each element to retain a degree of empirical specificity.

Adler develops a range of aesthetic models through which these practices can be 
understood to function critically. Each chapter focuses on a single exhibition: Isa 
Genzkens OIL (German Pavilion, Venice Biennale, 2007), Geoffrey Farmers mid-
career survey (Muse dart contemporain, Montral, 2008), Rachel Harrisons Consider 
the Lobster (CCS Bard Hessel Museum of Art, 2009), and Liz Magors The Mouth and 
Other Storage Facilities (Henry Art Gallery, Seattle, 2008).

Dan Adler is Associate Professor in the Department of Visual Arts & Art History at York 
University in Toronto. His most recent publication is Tainted Goods: Contemporary 
Sculpture and the Critique of Display Cultures (Routledge, 2019). His other books 
include the monograph Hanne Darboven: Cutural History 1880-1983 (Afterall Books/MIT 
Press, 2009). With Mitchell Frank, he co-edited German Art History and Scientific 
Thought: Beyond Formalism (Ashgate Press, 2012) and with Janine Marchessault and 
Sanja Obradovic co-edited Parallax: Stereoscopic 3D in Moving Images and Visual Art 
(Intellect Books/University of Chicago Press, 2013). He is a former senior editor of the 
Bibliography of the History of Art at the Getty Research Institute in Los Angeles and 
regularly contributes reviews to Artforum. Adler is an alumnus of the Whitney Museums 
Independent Study Program and, with Lesley Johnstone, he co-curated a Liz Magor 
retrospective exhibition at the Muse dart contemporain de Montral, which, in 2017, 
traveled to the Migros Museum fr Gegenwartskunst, Zrich; the Kunstverein in 
Hamburg; and the Muse dArt Moderne et contemporain in Nice, France. The 
accompanying catalogue, Liz Magor: Habitude, was published by JRP Ringier. His other 
curatorial credits include the exhibitions Francis Bacon and Henry Moore: Terror and 
Beauty (2014) held at the Art Gallery of Ontario and When Hangover Becomes Form: 
Rachel Harrison and Scott Lyall (2006), held at the Contemporary Art Gallery in 
Vancouver and Los Angeles Contemporary Exhibitions (LACE).

Contemporary Sculpture and the Critique of Display Cultures is published by Routledge.
