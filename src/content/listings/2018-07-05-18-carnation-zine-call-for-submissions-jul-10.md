Carnation is a new submission-based zine! We publish art and writing created 
through a lens of diaspora and displacement, prioritizing contributions from black, 
Indigenous, and people of colour.

[https://www.facebook.com/events/333761187150707/?ti=as](https://www.facebook.com/events/333761187150707/?ti=as)

Submissions:
Topics may include but are not limited to: space/place, language, home, memory, 
intergenerational inheritance, assimilation, hyphenated identity, resistance, healing, 
solidarity, power structures, and social justice.

We encourage various forms of writing that may include but are not limited to:
-Contemporary art writing
-Critical responses to popular culture, art, books, films, politics, etc.
-Personal essays
-Stories 
-Poetry

Art should be submitted as hi-res images (300 ppi). If you are submitting multiple 
works, please indicate if they are to be published as stand-alone pieces or as part of 
a grouping (ie. diptychs, writing with an accompanying image, a series, etc). Include 
any credits as necessary.

To submit, email carnationzine@gmail.com by July 10 at 11:59pm with the following:
-Your name to be published
-A bio (max 50 words)
-Title(s) (even if that means "Untitled")

Other notes:
-At this time this is an unfunded grassroots project and we are not able to pay artist 
or writer fees.
-Each contributor will get a free copy of the publication.
-Your work belongs to you. We will accept previously published material; please 
include credits as necessary.
-Your submission will become a part of Carnations collection, meaning it will be part 
of the first print and any future reprints.
-Your work may be featured on social media. Please let us know if you do not want 
to appear online.

About the editors:
Carnation is edited by Christina Hajjar, Luther Konadu, and Mariana Muoz Gomez. 
We are based out of Winnipeg, Manitoba on Treaty 1 Territory and the Homeland of 
the Mtis Nation. Treaty 1 Territory is the traditional territory of the Anishinaabeg, 
Cree, Oji-Cree, Dakota, and Dene Peoples.

Christina Hajjar is a first-generation Lebanese-Canadian queer femme emerging 
artist, writer, and organizer. Through poetry, performance, and installation, her 
practice grapples with diaspora, intergenerational inheritance, food culture, and 
feminism. She is a co-creator and co-editor of Whiny Femmes zine and the Flux 
Writing Program. christinahajjar.com

Luther Konadu is an emerging writer and an image-maker. He's also a content 
contributor for the online publication Public Parking, a collaborative project for 
highlighting the working practices of emerging creatives. His studio activities are 
project-based and realized through photographic print media and painting 
processes.

Mariana Muoz Gomez is a Mexican-Canadian artist and writer. Her current practice 
addresses topics of identity and language through postcolonial and feminist lens. 
She works in various media including text, printmaking, and photography. She has 
worked with many arts organizations in Winnipeg and was a co-founding member of 
Sappho Zine Collective. marianamunoz.ca
