Liz Magor: The Mouth and other storage facilities
January 10 - February 21, 2009
Simon Fraser University Gallery, SFU Burnaby Campus
Saturday January 10: Artist talk at 2pm
Opening & Book Launch from 3 to 5PM

A new installation by Vancouver-based artist Liz Magor opens the 2009
schedule at the SFU Gallery in Burnaby. The Mouth and Other Storage
Facilities includes work from the past three years and continues the
artist's decades-long interest in exploiting the consequences of
reproduction and repetition in the material world.

The show combines work seen at the Equinox Gallery last spring with work
never shown in Vancouver. The highly detailed sculptures present images of
domestic debris and suggest a confusion of values as the acts of acquiring
and discarding become inextricably linked. Cigarettes, ashtrays, candy and
small mammals are arranged and cast as one object, seamlessly merging the
endurance of the discarded with the allure of the new. These works pivot on
their ability to trick the eye, as does Magor's public sculpture LightShed,
installed at Coal Harbour in 2004. LightShed, cast in aluminum and appearing
to be an example of ordinary of wood-work, is in fact metal foundry work
that can simultaneously claim both a low and an exalted status. The Mouth 
furthers this analysis of contradiction by presenting an idea of the luxury
of exhaustion on a personal scale.

This exhibition & catalogue are a co-production with the Henry Art Gallery
in Seattle; the exhibition is co-curated by Sara Krajewski and Bill
Jeffries. It contains loans from several private collections and is
supported in part by the Equinox Gallery in Vancouver and the Susan Hobbs
Gallery in Toronto. Liz Magor's work is in many public collections,
including the National Gallery of Canada, the Art Gallery of Ontario and the
Vancouver Art Gallery. Funding for the exhibition and book was provided by
The Canada Council for the Arts and DFAIT through the PromArt program.

Liz Magor studied at the Vancouver School of Art and at Parsons School of
Design in New York City. In the 1980s she was an instructor at the Ontario
College of Art and has been an instructor at Emily Carr University since
1999.
