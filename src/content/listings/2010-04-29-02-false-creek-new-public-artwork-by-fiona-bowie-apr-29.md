New public artwork explores marine life of False Creek

If you've ever wondered about the health of False Creek's marine life,
Vancouver artist Fiona Bowie's new installation Surface may provide some
answers. Commissioned as part of the City's Olympic and Paralympic
Public Art Program and launched today, the project will track the
underwater activity of the urban waterway for up to five years.

By bringing to the surface what is usually hidden, Bowie hopes to
reflect the impact of our collective activity on the environmentally
fragile system. Once teeming with aquatic life, False Creek has been
hard hit by decades of industrialization along the waterfront and is now
slowly starting coming back to life. The artwork will enable the public
to track its recovery and also discover what lies beneath the water's
surface.

Best described as an ongoing live documentary, Surface will transmit the
submarine activity of False Creek via a camera mounted under an aquabus
(a False Creek commuter ferry). The live stream functions as an abstract
mix of colours and impressions, until crustaceans or fish swim by. After
dusk, Surface takes on a more intimate world, when tiny crustaceans,
larval animals and eels can be seen.

The submerged camera will feed streaming video 24 hours/day directly to
www.surfacer.ca, where it can be viewed anywhere, and to a TV monitor
mounted inside the boat, which people can watch while riding the ferry.
Viewers can also watch the live footage at two sites on Granville
Island: a large outdoor screen beside the Ocean Construction Supplies
site at 1415 Johnston Street and a TV monitor at the False Creek
Community Centre at 1318 Cartwright Street.

Fiona Bowie is well known for her work with 360-degree immersive
narratives and the creation of unique image systems. Flow, her new work
with Sidney Fels at Kingsway 1, is Vancouver's first photo/media-based
permanent public art work and was commissioned by the City of Vancouver
for the new Mount Pleasant Community Centre. Her art has also been
exhibited in Europe, America and across Canada. A graduate of UBC, Bowie
is an Assistant Professor of Integrated Media at Emily Carr University
of Art + Design.

The Surface aquabus departs seven days a week from the dock by the Arts
Club on Granville Island. For schedules and more information, visit
www.surfacer.ca.

The City's Olympic and Paralympic Public Art Program is an ambitious
collection of more than 20 new permanent and temporary public artworks
commissioned especially for the 2010 Games. For more information, visit
the Host City website at olympichostcity.vancouver.ca/cityhighlights/.
High resolution photos of Surface and other artworks can be downloaded
from the City's media gallery at vancouver.ca <http://www.vancouver.ca>
