DAN GRAHAM
ARTIST TALK

31 OCTOBER 2008, 1PM
ECU THEATRE, SOUTH BUILDING, ROOM 301

Dan Graham is a New York based artist who continues to be one of the most
influential Conceptual artists since the 1960s. Much of his work is
situated outside the gallery and addresses recurrent themes that are rooted
in everyday urban life. Graham's early projects took the form of
instructional conceptual texts inserted into popular magazines. In the
1970s Graham worked primarily with performance, film, and video, using
loops and mirrors to examine relationships between the performer and the
audience. His pavilions and architectural installations deal with ideas of
public and private, and issues of social space. He has collaborated with
numerous artists including Vancouver's Rodney Graham and Jeff Wall. Dan
Graham is also an influential critic writing texts that span music, popular
culture, architecture, and art.

Dan Graham's talk is presented in collaboration with the Charles H. Scott
Gallery, the ECU Speaker Series, the UBC Department of Art History, Visual
Art, and Theory, the Morris and Helen Belkin Art Gallery, and the Rennie
Collection.
