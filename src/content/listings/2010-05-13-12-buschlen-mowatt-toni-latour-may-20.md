The Family Project
Toni Latour

Buschlen Mowatt Galleries
May 17 - June 4, 2010
Reception: May 20, 6-8pm

The Family Project: Latour Family, 55.5x37", 2007-10

The Family Project consists of 24 large scale photographs of Latour’s family,
her former partner Rina Larsson's family and merged images of both families
and the former couple. These photographs are accompanied by a vitrine that
holds a taxidermied mouse and a plaque describing Kaguya.

The taxidermy is a stand-in for the living mouse, Kaguya, who was created by
Japanese scientists in 2004. Led by Tomohiro Kono at the Tokyo University of
Agriculture, a team of researchers used cells from two different female mice to
make a single unique animal. Kaguya is the first mammal ever born from two

eggs and no sperm. She is a first in human history.

Through photography and installation, Toni Latour's newest project looks at

burgeoning reproductive technologies in relation to expanding definitions
of family.

Examining genetics, family heredity and lesbian reproductive possibilities,
it puts

forward a utopian dream many queer couples have of creating their own genetic

children, and offers a glimpse of hope, albeit controversial, into our future.
