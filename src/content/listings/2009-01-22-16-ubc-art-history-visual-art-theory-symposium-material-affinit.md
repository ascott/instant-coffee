"Material Affinities: Intersections that Matter"

28th Annual UBC Art History, Visual Art and Theory Graduate Student Symposium

Saturday February 7th, and Sunday February 8th, 11-5pm

Opening Reception February 7th, 5:30pm

Lasserre Building (6333 Memorial Dr) room 102, UBC Campus

(between the Belkin Art Gallery and Koerner Library)

The theme of this year's conference investigates the notion of materiality
as a methodological approach to artistic and cultural production.
Materiality is a theoretically and philosophically embedded concept which
can describe the substance or quality of a 'thing' and demands an attention
to the relationships that it solicits from the human agents around it.
Ultimately, it has offered art historians a means of retaining an interest
in 'context' whilst reinvesting in the 'formal' qualities of art (without
the 'formalism'). At the same time, it is a way of opening up a space for
discussion between disciplines and modes of production of knowledge, both
scholarly and creative.

Keynote Speakers:

Saturday Feb 7, 11am: Dr Jennifer Marshall (U of Minnesota)

"Materiality's Material Conditions: Toward a Social Art History of "The Thing"

Marshall is an art historian whose research investigates how aesthetic
discourse works to negotiate cultural and economic meanings for
three-dimensional things things, which, by dint of their inherent
materiality, appear to vouchsafe those very semiotic claims. Her keynote
address discusses the complex and integral relationship between the
social and the material.

Sunday Feb 8, 11am: Dr. Jeff Derksen (Simon Fraser University)

"Yesterday's Euphorias: The Temporality of Cultural Critique."

Derksen is a well-known local poet, art critic and professor in the English
Department at SFU with many publications to his credit. His address will be
based on an upcoming publication and will consider the dematerialization of
the 'material' in the contemporary moment.

The student speakers represent a diverse range of interdisciplinary and
international emerging scholars and artists. Their papers and presentations
have been organized into four sessions. For a full detailed schedule please
visit http://www.ahva.ubc.ca, or to reach us directly please email the
coordinators at gradsymp@interchange.ubc.ca
