TALKS at the Vancouver Art Gallery

Tuesday August 23, 7-9pm

The Culture Series

David Wisdom

As part of the Culture Series at the Vancouver Art Gallery, musician, 
photographer and former CBC radio host David Wisdom presents 
two special summer nights of his legendary slide shows. Featuring 
Bill Jeffries, Karin Bubas, Rodney Graham, Stuart McCall, Robert 
Kleyn, Connie Kuhns, the evening will unfold with Wisdom and his 
line up of speakers presenting their slide shows set to music.

David Wisdom is best known for his 30 year career on CBC Radio 
as host and producer of many music programs, notably Nightlines, 
Radio On and Pearls Of Wisdom. He was a member of the bands 
French Hand Laundry, UJ3RK5 and The Young Winstons, and has 
written about popular music for a number of publications. His other 
principal interest is photography. He has had an exhibition of his 
pictures of Vancouver in the 1970s at the Teck Gallery (2009), and 
has organized and presented a number of multi-artist slide shows, 
reviving the group presentations he organized throughout the 1970s, 
The Point Grey Slide Festival.

The Culture Series at the Vancouver Art Gallery presents artists, 
writers, critics, historians, performers and other cultural practitioners 
from all disciplines who are changing the way we think about art, 
creativity, culture, contemporary theory and more around the world.

Marking the launch of the series were talks by musician and artist 
Brian Eno and experimental American filmmaker Barbara Hammer 
in January 2011. Previous talks include Vancouver-based artists 
Althea Thauberger, Isabelle Pauwels, Glenn Lewis as well as Peter 
Samis, associate curator of interpretation at the San Francisco 
Museum of Modern Art (SFMOMA), and Mark Allen, founder of 
Machine Project in Los Angeles.

Capacity is limited to 100.

14. Higgledy Piggledy Parade | Ghost Poo by Arvo Leo, Roberts Creek | SEP 20
Ghost Poo
by Arvo Leo

September 20th, 2011
Higgledy Piggledy Parade
Roberts Creek.

The Ghost, as an undecidable, does not conform to either polarity of 
a dichotomy. For example - present/absent cure/poison and 
inside/outside.

The figure of a ghost seems to be neither present or absent, or 
alternatively, 

it is both present and absent at the same time.

*

It is customary during a festive parade for participants to usually 
provide the spectators with some kind of sugary sweet. If, rather 
than a lollipop, a token on arguably desirable excrement is offered 
by a participant, in guise and anonymity, how then does this ghostly 
offering trouble notions of obligation and reciprocation within a gift 
giving transaction?

16. Irving K. Barber Learning Centre, UBC | Jordy Hamilton | until | OCT 14
Irving K. Barber Learning Centre, UBC

THE POSE STANDS FOR POTENTIALITY

Ridington Room, Irving K. Barber Learning Centre, UBC

Aug 11 to Oct 14, 2011

Featuring works by Jordy Hamilton

Curated by Toby Lawrence

Working with the permanent art installation in the Irving K. Barber 
Learning Centreâ€™s Ridington Room, The Pose Stands for Potentiality 
juxtaposes the University of British Columbia s formal presidential 
portraits with a series of small works from Vancouver-based artist 
Jordy Hamilton. This collection, 96 Studies for a Painting, developed 
out of an interest in intuitive processes and in the extension of 
leftovers from studio practices and failed attempts. Beginning with a 
practical desire to create an abstract painting, the works 
unintentionally return to the figural image. Hamilton s initial desire 
has resulted in a continuing serial practice of fragmenting and 
extending this figural abstraction, wherein each small painting, built 
on prior failures, retains a prevailing sense of mutability. Moreover, 
Hamilton s abstracted representations instigate conversation 
between contemporary and traditional forms of painting.

By contrasting the hierarchical position of the presidential 
portraiture, the juxtaposition becomes emphatic of the process of 
both individual and artistic achievement. The Pose Stands for 
Potentiality considers the multiplicity of figural constructions and 
further highlights a practice that embodies a state of flux through a 
plurality of positions and continuous development.
