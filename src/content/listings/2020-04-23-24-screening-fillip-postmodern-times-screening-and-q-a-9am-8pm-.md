In Modern Times (1936), Charlie Chaplins Little Tramp struggles to survive in the modern,
industrialised world. As the story follows the picaresque adventure of the Tramps trials and
tribulations,
the film reveals the desperate employment and financial conditions many
people faced during the Great Depression"conditions created, in Chaplins view, by the
efficiencies of modern industrialisation.

Eighty years after Modern Times, artist Michael Mandiberg initiated Postmodern Times, a
remake of Charlie Chaplins film outsourced to freelancers on the popular gig workers
platform Fiverr.com. A result of the labor of 182 digital freelancers living in over 25 countries,
Postmodern Times is a largely fragmented and chaotic work that reflects the conditions of
digital labour itself.

If Modern Times is a portrait of the Fordist organisation of work (the assembly line),
Postmodern Times portrays the digital factory and the post-Fordist organisation of work, in
which workers have no wage, and work (usually from home) on small, underpaid tasks for
often unknown patrons. Mandibergs film only takes on more significance in the context of
our current global health crisis, as one-third of the
human population faces some form of
lockdown and we collectively brace for the crush of a looming global recession.

About the Artist
Michael Mandiberg is an interdisciplinary artist whose work crosses multiple forms and
disciplines in order to trace the lines of political and symbolic power as it takes shape online.
Mandibergs projects have been presented at the Museum of Modern Art (New York), Los
Angeles County Museum of Art, the New Museum (New York), Muse d'Art Moderne de la
Ville de Paris, Arizona State University Museum & Library
(Tempe), and Transmediale
(Berlin), amongst others. Mandibergs work has been written about widely, including in
Artforum, Art in America, ARTnews, the New York Times, the New Yorker, and the Wall
Street Journal.
