There is a dynamic collision taking place in Sonny Assu's work, where
traditional motifs come face to face with contemporary technologies. In
these works, the curvilinear and liquid lines of the iPhone assume the
place of the Northwest Coast ovoid and u-form, while in others, traditional
narratives are derailed by a marked self-awareness that questions the
restrictions of conventions without dispensing with them entirely. While
Assu's formline design is unorthodox, the balance and harmony between form
and colour remain strong aspects of his work. Through his invention of new
hybrid forms, he brings to light a complex conversation between established
practices and unconventional approaches.

Since graduating from Emily Carr University of Art + Design, Sonny Assu's
work has been exhibited across North America, including solo shows at the
Belkin Satellite (Vancouver); the Art Gallery of South Western Manitoba
(Brandon); and Equinox Gallery (Vancouver). He has also been included in
many group exhibitions: Changing Hands: Art Without Reservations 2 (Museum
of Arts & Design, New York; Anchorage Museum of History & Art; Weisman Art
Museum, Minneapolis; Naples Museum of Art; Institute of American Indian
Arts Museum, Santa Fe); How Soon is Now? (Vancouver Art Gallery);
Challenging Traditions (McMichael Canadian Art Collection, Kleinburg); SAM
at 75 (Seattle Art Museum); and Futuristic Regalia (Grunt Gallery,
Vancouver; Campbell River Art Gallery; Alternator Gallery, Kelowna). His
work is in the collection of the National Gallery of Canada, the Museum of
Anthropology at the University of British Columbia, and the Seattle Art
Museum. Sonny Assu currently lives and works in Vancouver.
