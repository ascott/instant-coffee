Elizabeth Zvonar: There Art No Rules
Western Front
Opening April 10, 8pm
April 11 - May 16, 2009

THERE ARE NO RULES, a solo exhibition of new works by Elizabeth Zvonar,
pays tribute to and embraces an unconventional approach to art making and
offers possibilities for rethinking our understandings of art history. The
1972 text, History of Art by H.W. Janson, is the starting point for
Zvonarâ&#65533;&#65533;s collages and porcelain sculptures. The handcut collages, intimate
in scale, take pages of Jansonâ&#65533;&#65533;s text of iconographic paintings and
sculptures and combine them based on their similar compositions. The
juxtaposition of familiar images from a variety of historical contexts
complicates the readings of originals, and enables them to be seen anew,
radically divorced from their original contexts.

Also borrowing from the images from the same text are porcelain sculptures
made of slip cast replicas of an index finger. The clusters of fingers use
as a guideline, compositions found from History of Art. Oscillating between
figurative and abstraction, the sculptures resemble both, resulting in
organic, baroque aesthetic objets dâ&#65533;&#65533;art. Fingers feature predominantly in
early painting particularly those done during the Baroque period when the
narratives presented, particularly in painting, were simplified and
dramatized as a means of appealing on an emotive level to the masses.
Pointing, particularly skyward (implying God), called attention to the
invisible within the topography of the visible. The fingers in Zvonarâ&#65533;&#65533;s
sculptures are ornamentalizedâ&#65533;&#65533;glazed with accents of gold, silver and
opalescenceâ&#65533;&#65533;they reference polychromy and the â&#65533;&#65533;feminineâ&#65533;&#65533; lean towards the
decorative.

Elizabeth Zvonarâ&#65533;&#65533;s works have spanned a variety of mediums, from
performances and interventions to installations, and most recently,
sculpture, digital collages, and text-based works. Zvonar (b. 1972) is a
graduate of Emily Carr University. Her recent exhibitions include Parallel
Dimension at Artspeak Gallery (Vancouver), Fade Away and Radiate at the
Cohan and Leslie Gallery (New York), Concrete Language at the Contemporary
Art Gallery (Vancouver), and The Thing, All That Is Solid Melts Into Air,
organized by the Museum Van Hedendaagse Kunst (Mechelen, Belgium).
Upcoming, she has a solo exhibition at the Contemporary Art Gallery in
November 2009. Zvonar was the winner of the Vancouver Art Development Award
in 2007 from the Vancouver Foundation and the Contemporary Art Gallery. In
2008 Zvonar was the inaugural artist at the Malaspina Print Research
Residency and was an artist in residence at the Banff Centre during the
Janice Kerbel led thematic residency, Cosmic Ray Research. She lives and
works in Vancouver, BC.
