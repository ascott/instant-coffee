Event: Cineworks' Film&Media Showcase
Date & Time: Wednesday October 23, 7-9pm 
Location: 1131 Howe Street (back lane entrance) 
Description: During the evening, three artists will showcase their films 
and engage in meaningful dialogue with the audience. Drop by and 
mingle with artists in our community while watching great work. Get 
ready to be inspired. Open to the general public Free event. 

https://www.facebook.com/events/587424844632791/
http://www.cineworks.ca/events/day/2013/10/23/

Zachary D. KerrHolden will showcase: “Power Man”
Zachary is a filmmaker from Northern Ontario. Zachary has spent most 
of his life studying the language of cinema, closely examining film 
through the act of editing. Films he has made and cut have seen the 
likes of Cannes Film Festival, The Toronto International Film Festival, 
Vancouver International Film Festival, and have been on display in the 
Museum of Modern Art.
A spotlight into Joey LeBlanc, a trucker with 40 years of experience on 
the road. Picking up strangers and leaving people behind. He speaks 
of his wife, his children, and one particular night when he came across 
an injured woman in a car accident by the side of the road.
Javier Badillo will showcase “Afternoon In Gudrun”
Javier Badillo plays in a Latin punk rock band called Caracas. He loves 
pulling espresso shots, and your leg too if you give him the chance. He 
has a dark past drawing cartoons for TV, and has a bunch of other little 
skills with almost no practical purpose.
A shy wine bar waitress is challenged to step out of her shell when a 
bold customer asks her to teach him to dance the waltz.
Jason Vanderhill will showcase: “Vancouver, a family film in 16mm”
Jason likes to call himself a digital archivist and amateur historian. In 
his free time, he has been compiling a collection of visual art relating to 
the city of Vancouver on a little blog called Illustrated Vancouver. He 
also happens to enjoy collecting old home movies, and has been 
reviving some long lost 8mm Kodachrome film recently.
In honor of Home Movie Day (October 19), I'll be showing a short reel 
of vintage home movie footage from Vancouver, dating back to the 
early 1930s! I have remixed this footage along with audio from an 
interview that was made in the 1990s to provide a snapshot of life in 
the big city in the pioneering days of film. I'd also like to make an 
appeal for everyone to check the state of their family film archives and 
do the same!
