Easy to see, hard to know

Between 1932 and 1933, the second Bauhaus location in Dessau was
shut down and its final Berlin location founded. These are also the
years between which the Landhaus Lemke was constructed, the last
architectural structure designed by then Bauhaus director Ludwig Mies
van der Rohe before his exile to the US in 1938. Though modest in
scale, design and materials, the garden house is an early example of
open-plan, transparent structures that are now synonymous with Mies
van der Rohe’s international style.

The artists visited the Landhaus Lemke, which is now a modern
art museum, for the first time late last summer. On the parquet floors
in front of the floor-to-ceiling plate glass windows of one of the
rooms (the Lemkes’ study) they discovered a dead cricket. This fatal
accident could be interpreted as a literal manifestation of the early
twentieth century claims of emotional impact (or domestic warmth) made
possible by otherwise ‘cold’ industrial materials and design. The
exhibition for the Ministry of Casual Living features the relocation
of the cricket and a partial reconstruction of the single herringbone
parquet floor, raised to meet the bottom of the gallery window.

Nicole Raufeisen and Ryan Witt graduated from Simon Fraser University
in 2004. The artists have participated in a number of residency
programs in Canada, the U.S., and Europe.
