Colleen Heslin
Slow Peripheral Movements
May 10 to June 9, 2018
Opening reception: Thursday May 10, 6pm to 8pm

Monte Clark Gallery is pleased to present Colleen Heslins most recent
series of fibre-based paintings, Slow Peripheral Movements. This body
of work amplifies the push and pull of macro and micro shifts through
poetics of broken and joined lines, and simulations of chromatic
overlaps. The production of these works is akin to quilt-making
practices, where fabric scraps are cycled from the periphery and into
a central focus. Tension of connection is an ongoing actor in Heslins
work through the pressure of seams stitched through fabric and
stretched over a large frame.

Considering histories of abstraction and textiles from a feminist
perspective, issues of labour, value and power are re-occurring in the
works. The process of dying fabrics and sewing the paintings lends
attention towards different scales of textile manufacturing; on a
larger scope, we are all participants in the labor and environmental
crisis of fast-fashion, including global disparities of wage based on
race, place and gender. As a group, the paintings are ruminations on
late capitalism and alternatives to this crisis, looking towards
emerging movements of decentralization and de-growth. Slow Peripheral
Movements looks back to trailblazing early Modernist female artists,
and forward to slow and small-scale modes of working with deep
material connections to everyday contemporary life.

Colleen Heslin graduated with an MFA from Concordia University in
2014. Her most recent solo exhibitions were held at the Esker
Foundation (Calgary), the McMichael Canadian Art Collection
(Kleinburg), and the Charles H Scott Gallery (Vancouver). She has also
exhibited at the Vancouver Art Gallery, Leonard & Bina Ellen Art
Gallery (Montreal), SFU Gallery, Burnaby Art Gallery, Unit/Pitt
Projects, Kinderhook & Caracas (Germany), RATS Collective
(Switzerland), and numerous other venues. Her work has been written on
and published in Artforum, Blouin Artinfo, Canadian Art, Pyramid Power
and other Canadian and international periodicals. Heslin won the RBC
Painting Competition in 2013.

Also on display:

Scott McFarland
May 10 to June 9, 2018
Opening reception: Thursday May 10, 6pm to 8pm

Monte Clark Gallery is pleased to present a selection of work from two
of Scott McFarlands most recent bodies of work, Sky Leaks and Lens
Cleaning. Both series continue the artists well-established history
of examining and depicting the science, mechanics, and process of
photography.

The Sky Leaks series consists of chromogenic prints installed in
lightboxes, depicting two types of spontaneous imagery: cloud
formations, and anomalies created by film that has been damaged by
exposure to light and water. These works continue the artists
interest in exploring light sources and their relationship to
photography, as well they are a nod to McFarlands history of
depicting cloud formations as markers of duration.

In their subject matter, works in the Sky Leaks series also reference
Alfred Stieglitzs Equivalent, photographs of cloud formations taken
in the 1920s and 30s. Steiglitzs photographs were meant to express
how to hold a moment, how to record something so completely, that all
who see [the picture of it] will relive an equivalent of what has been
expressed.* McFarland is known for exploring this concept in detail,
and looking back at the last two decades, many of his works challenge
and explore the assumption that a photograph represents one single
moment.

In each of McFarlands Lens Cleaning works, the artist is shown wiping
dust and debris from his camera lens using his own t-shirt. Multiple
overlapping exposures show the movement of his hands, creating a
dynamic impression of the moment before a photograph would be taken.
Housed behind the glass and within the physical frame that holds the
print is dust and debris from the artists camera bag, a visceral
layer to the work that highlights realities of analog photography.
This acknowledgement of detritus"which has classically been
undesirable and bothersome to photographers"is another example of
McFarland illuminating the photographic process by working within
inherent atmospheric phenomena.

Scott McFarland lives and works in Toronto, Canada. His works are
included in public collections such as the MoMA, the J. Paul Getty
Museum, the National Gallery of Canada, the SFMoMA, the Walker Art
Center, and the Vancouver Art Gallery. In 2014, the Art Gallery of
Ontario exhibited McFarlands solo exhibition Snow, Shacks, Streets,
Shrubs which surveyed his recent work. Other major surveys of
McFarlands work have been exhibited at the National Gallery of Canada
(2010) and the Vancouver Art Gallery (2009). McFarlands works have
recently been shown at Fort Gansevoort (New York) and Choi&Lager
Gallery (Cologne).
