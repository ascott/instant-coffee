17. MONTE CLARK GALLERY | KARIN BUBAS | NOV 20
KARIN BUBAS - Valley of the Heart's Delight
November 20th - Year end

Opening Reception: Thursday, November 20th, 6-8pm
MONTE CLARK GALLERY
2339 Granville Street

The Monte Clark Gallery is pleased to announce the solo exhibition
"Valley of the Heart's Delight" from Vancouver-based artist Karin
Bubas.

Partially inspired by early Technicolor films and hand-tinted
photographs, Bubas' large-scale digital photographs are presented as
subtly orchestrated scenes of colour and light. Each of the five
images presents for the viewer a carefully costumed figure in a
reminiscent landscape. The result is a mysterious and story-like
dynamic between the setting and the individual. Taken on a panoramic
camera, two of the images explore this format in a horizontal
configuration emblematic of the landscape genre, and for three Bubas
has aligned her images in a vertical format to dramatic effect.

The inherently cinematic narratives are derived from archetypal myths
and stories, creating for the viewer imagery both ominous and
enchanting, the figures appearing as apparitions in their mystical
settings. As Bubas carefully stages these five tableaux, allusions of
myths and new romanticism come together with cinematic sensitivity.
The end result is a series of supernatural costume scenes reminiscent
of Waterhouse's Pre-Raphaelite paintings as well as the films of
Alfred Hitchcock.

Karin Bubas was born in North Vancouver and currently lives and works
in Vancouver, BC Canada. Since studying at the Emily Carr Institute of
Art and Design, her work has been exhibited nationally and
internationally.

18. CSA SPACE | Kelly Lycan | NOV 21
CSA SPACE
Presents: SAVE NOW
By Kelly Lycan
November 22 ­ January 10, 2008
Opening Reception: 7pm
Friday, November 21, 2008

The act of collecting, and the recycling of existing collections 
lies at the
conceptual and material core of Kelly Lycan's sculptural 
practice. Save Now,
Lycan's solo exhibition for CSA continues her examination of 
the visuals of
consumer culture, but more specifically addresses her material 
pursuit and
research of runaway consumption. This includes an 
investigation of spaces
that catch the overflow of abundant objects that are in a state of
suspension from appreciation to depreciation.

If the acquisition of things is both a palliative act and a 
stimulant, what
happens to these collections after they serve their therapeutic 
and
consumptive purposes? Whether it is the stuff of junk drawers, 
ordered
shelves or deep storage (self-storage is now a 17 billion dollar 
annual
industry in the North America), Lycan is interested in looking at 
the
configuration and negotiation of what has selectively been 
gathered and
saved, and not yet abandoned.

Collecting is a common social practice that reflects behavioral 
patterns as
it mirrors aspects of public and private life. It constructs a way in 
which
we relate to each other and instructs how we order the material 
world. One
main focus of Lycan's work has been the way that objects are 
valued and
devalued depending on their place of display. Save Now 
materially stores,
orders and reorders consumer culture and its relationship to 
industrial
design and contemporary art practices, creating a link between 
market
culture and visual culture in order to address how aesthetic 
values are
created; how materials are used in the formation of identities 
and
ideologies; and how these forms take up space in her life.
