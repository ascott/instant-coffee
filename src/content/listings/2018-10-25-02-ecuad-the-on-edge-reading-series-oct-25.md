The On Edge Reading Series invites you to hear:

Saleema Nawaz
Thursday Oct 25 - 6 pm
at the library, Emily Carr University of Art & Design,
520 East 1st Avenue
Vancouver, unceded Coast Salish lands

Everyone is welcome!

Bio:

Saleema Nawaz is the author of the short story collection Mother Superior and the 
winner of the 2008 Writers Trust of Canada/McClelland & Stewart Journey Prize for her 
story "My Three Girls." Her first novel, Bone and Bread, won the Quebec Writers 
Federation Paragraphe Hugh McClennan Prize for Fiction and was a 2016 Canada 
Reads selection.

We gratefully acknowledge that these readings take place on unceded Coast Salish 
lands and thank the Canada Council for the Arts for its support.

Thurs Nov 8 " Christy + Margaret Fenton
Thurs Nov 22 Garth Martens 

Bios:

Christy Jordan-Fenton is the co-author of four award-winning childrens books about 
Inuvialuk elder Margaret Pokiak-Fentons time at Indian Residential School. Fatty Legs 
and A Stranger At Home have been added to numerous Canadian school curriculums, 
staged as musical productions, featured in a music video, and translated into French 
and Korean. 

Margaret Pokiak-Fenton, or Olemaun, is the best known as the indomitable subject and 
co-author of four award winning childrens books about her time at residential school in 
the 1940s, the best known being FATTY LEGS (2010). Being Inuvialuit, she was born on 
Baille Island in the Arctic Ocean, en route with her nomadic family to their winter hunting 
grounds on Banks Island. 

Garth Martens won the 2011 Bronwen Wallace Award for Emerging Writers. His first 
book Prologue for the Age of Consequence (House of Anansi Press) was a Shortlisted 
Finalist for the 2014 Governor General's Literary Award in Poetry.
