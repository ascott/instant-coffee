PORTAL POP-UP GALLERY

407 W. Cordova St., Vancouver BC

presents

Jay Cabalu

Reception on Saturday, May 25th. 2-4pm

Artwork on display in window gallery:

May 17 - June 9, 2024,

Jay Cabalu is a Filipino-Canadian pop artist based in Vancouver, BC, whose works

are made of 100% hand-cut collage. In subject matter ranging from pop icons, self-
portrait and still
life, Jay reappropriates the visual language of advertisements and
popular culture, inverting depictions of pleasure and luxury to portray a collaged
world overflowing with contradictions. At Portal, Cabalu presents portraits of two
queer pop icons RuPaul and Britney Spears.

RuPaul has been a pioneer of drag and identity politics since the 1980s. The collage
elements in this work plays with gender-coded imagery culled from magazines and
features Lady Bunny,

Ellen Degeneres, Laverne Cox, Shea Coulee, Troye Sivan, Naomi Campbell and
Oprah. While RuPauls cultural contribution is undeniable, he is a controversial pop
figure. Despite his revolutionary presence early in his career, many feel he has
become a staunch, money-hungry capitalist, even leasing his Wyoming ranch to oil
fracking companies. In creating this portrait, Cabalu wanted to animate RuPauls
infectious laughwhile also evoking a maniacal, larger than life super-villain.

Evoking Andy Warhols iconic Marilyn portraits, Cabalus
Britney Spears portrait
captures Spears during her 2007 MTV Video Music Awards performance, a pivotal
moment amidst her public struggles, marked by the invasive and misogynistic tabloid
coverage. Created in 2021, amidst the fervor of the #FreeBritney movement, this
artwork reflects the cultural reckoning surrounding Spears; unjust conservatorship.
Utilizing collage, the artist references Britneys dense narrative and musical
catalogue to create a world as exploitative as the culture in which she exists. This
portrayal of Spears resonates with the socio-political landscape of the 2020s, where
online activism empowers movements addressing issues ranging from politics to
climate change and popular media.

Bio

Jay Cabalu has a BFA from Kwantlen University and has exhibited his work since
graduating in 2013. A series of self-portraits Jay created from 2017-2019 garnered
the attention of international exhibitions centred on queer-Asian identity. In 2018, he
exhibited with the Foundation for
Asian-American Independent Media in Chicago. In
the UK, Cabalus work was part of exhibitions with Queer Asia in 2018 and 2019, for
which he gave a virtual artist talk at the British Museum. Jay has exhibited work in
Vancouver with SUM Gallery and On Main Gallery in 2019 and 2020 in two pan-
Asian focused showcases. His self-portrait De Los Reyes, appears on the cover of
the McGill-Queens University Press publication Canadian Culinary Imaginations,
which explores the role of food as multimodal media. Cabalu has had two recent
solo exhibitions in Vancouver: Extra, in 2022, curated by Paul Wong, and POP
ODY$$EY at Deer Lake Gallery in 2023. His third solo exhibition is scheduled for the
summer of 2024 at the Kube in Gibsons, British Columbia, Canada.
