Second Site II at VanDusen Botanical Garden (37th and Oak)

Artist Talks:

by David Floren, Sunday July 12th at 1:30 pm
by Robin Ripley, Sunday July 19th at 1:30 pm

As part of its exhibition at VanDusen Botanical Garden from July
through September, Second Site collective will present a series of
talks by the artists.

(1) David Floren (July 12th at 1:30 pm) will talk about "Diaspora".
This piece hangs from a large tree branch. It is composed of six giant
seed pods that respond to the wind and sun. Solar power drives motors
that open and close the pods. The wind blows through and scatters
seeds. Over time, the audience can see distinctive patterns that the
seeds make as they fall from the tree.

David Floren is best known for complex mechanical, electronic
sculptures that mix philosophical and scientific ideas. His current
works are experiments in translocalism, and explore the development of
technology and cultural systems, incorporating embedded webservers and
miniature cameras. Theoretical interests include cultural diaspora,
and the human diurnal/temporal narrative. He has also produced various
art books and two collections of prose.

(2) Robin Ripley (July 19th at 1:30 pm) will talk about "Alate". This
is a series of works that explore the temporal phases of the garden's
maple grove. The pieces are wind and solar powered amalgams of organic
and man-made materials, with a focus on winged maple seeds. They
reference biomimicry and "nature-inspired" technology.

Robin Ripley is a Vancouver artist who focuses on the re-examination
of mundane objects as a way to explore sense, memory, and knowledge.
Her working processes are often laborious, questioning current
economic models of speed and "efficiency" while suggesting that
transformation is still possible through small gestures if we make
time to notice the details of the world. Her works have been widely
shown throughout British Columbia.

To attend the artist talks, meet just outside the entrance to the
garden near the bicycle racks and totem poles. Admission is charged to
enter the garden.
