VIVO MEDIA ARTS CENTRE
CALL FOR CURATORIAL PROPOSALS FOR VIVOs CRISTA DAHL MEDIA LIBRARY AND ARCHIVES

VIVO Media Arts Centre is a not-for-profit media production, exhibition and
distribution centre. We are an evolving organization operated by and for
artists and media/community activists. Founded as the Satellite Video
Exchange Society in 1973, VIVO has grown into a vibrant artist-run centre
with state of the art facilities and educational workshops.

VIVO houses an archive of over 4000 educational, documentary and
experimental videos, both Canadian and international work, dating from the
late 1960s to the present. The tapes are non-commercial in nature, and
their contents vary from performing, visual and video arts to social and
political issues: human rights, the media, gay and lesbian issues,
environmental concerns, feminist politics, etc. The archive also includes
an extensive collection of artist documentation, catalogues, photographs,
and critical writing.

Over the past two years, we have completely re-organized our collection.
The tape archive and the paper/document archive once housed in separate
spaces have merged. A team of library and archive students and volunteers
has assisted us in cross-referencing and cataloguing previously unsorted
materials by artist and date.

The archive recently celebrated its 30th year in existence, and were
approaching the 40th anniversary of the centre as a whole. Reflecting back
on what has been a very diverse history of production and exhibition, were
putting out an open-themed call to curators to assemble a video program on
a topic of their choosing from our archive.

Interested curators are asked to search our online catalogue, located at
www.videoout.ca, to select at least three tapes and briefly describe a
program that could be developed around them. Successful applicants will
then be asked to spend time viewing tapes in our archive in March and April
2011 to develop a more in depth proposal that will be submitted in a Canada
Council Initiatives grant on May 15th. Successful notification of this
application in September 2011 will secure curators with funds for research,
critical writing, artist fees and technical support, as well as the
publishing of collected texts and production of a DVD box set of the video
programs. An exhibition of the programs at VIVO will follow in Winter 2012,
with plans of touring the program nationally and internationally in 2012-2013.

Submission Guidelines
