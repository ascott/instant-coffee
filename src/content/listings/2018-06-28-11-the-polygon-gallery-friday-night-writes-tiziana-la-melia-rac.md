Friday Night Writes Workshop
Tiziana La Melia and Rachelle Sawatsky
Friday June 29, 6:30-9PM at The Polygon Gallery

Artists and writers Tiziana La Melia and Rachelle Sawatsky will host a hands-on 
creative workshop, in response to Susan Hillers interest in stream-of-consciousness 
writing and automatic drawing. They will lead participants through experiential writing 
exercises, and discuss subjects that Hiller explores in her artworks such as 
subconscious and unconscious states of mind, psychic experiences, and intuitive 
associations between image and text. These ideas are also active in La Melia and 
Sawatsky's visual and literary practices, and the artists will draw upon methods and 
strategies they use in their own studios. This workshop is suitable for people ages 
16 and older. Space is limited. All materials will be provided in the workshop.

Tiziana La Melia is an artist and writer based in Vancouver. Spanning painting, 
installation, and text, La Melias work centres on embodied experiences and how 
these experiences are mediated through technology and culture. She has 
participated in numerous recent exhibitions, and forthcoming projects include her 
book of poetry The Eyelash and the Monochrome published by Talonbooks, 
participation in Manifesta 12's collateral projects, Palermo (2018) and a solo 
exhibition at Franz Kaka, Toronto (2018). Her writing has been published in The 
Organism for Poetic Research, Agony Klub, C Magazine, The Capilano Review, and 
The Interjection Calendar.

Rachelle Sawatsky is an artist and a writer based in Los Angeles. Her work 
encompasses painting, writing, ceramics and drawing, and has been included in 
exhibitions at the Tate St. Ives, Galerie Mezzanin in Vienna, Presentation House 
Gallery in North Vancouver and most recently as part of Ambivalent Pleasures at the 
Vancouver Art Gallery. Sawatsky has been written about in Artforum, Frieze, C 
Magazine, Art Review and the LA Times. She holds an MFA from University of 
Southern California in Los Angeles (2013) and a BFA from Emily Carr Institute of Art 
and Design in Vancouver (2005).
