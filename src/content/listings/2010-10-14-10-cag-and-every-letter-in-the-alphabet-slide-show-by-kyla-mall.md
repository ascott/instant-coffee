Co-presented by the Contemporary Art Gallery and EVERY LETTER IN THE ALPHABET 

Kyla Mallett
A slide presentation: How to See and Read the Aura: Finding the Invisible 

In conjunction with Following A Line, the exhibition current on view at the Contemporary Art Gallery, Kyla Mallett will give a slide presentation. This talk will examine several areas in which there is a quest to see, read and understand that which is invisible, elusive, or simply non-existent. The artist will explore several examples in relation to her own practice, specifically to her latest work, How to See and Read the Aura, 2010, and her current research. 

Kyla Mallett (b. 1976, Vancouver, BC) has drawn critical attention over the past decade for her photographic work. Mallet documents rather quotidian objects and issues in an evidentiary manner that emphasizes their relevance. The artist uses a similar method of photo documentation for her solo exhibition Marginalia (2006-07) in which she represents the research process through itinerant notes written by anonymous readers in book margins. For How to Read and See the Aura (2010), Mallett is learning to read the aura while also figuring out how to convey and interpret her understanding which, to a large extent, is an aesthetic reading.  This work is the beginning of Mallett’s research into the genre of self help and new age books. She has had solo exhibitions at Artspeak, Vancouver (2006) and Mt. Saint Vincent University Gallery, Halifax (2008) as well as Gossip, which was made during a residency at ThreeWalls, Chicago (2005). Her formative series Legendary Teens (2000) exhibited at
Facing History: Portraits from Vancouver, Presentation House Gallery, North Vancouver (2001); and the Canadian Cultural Centre, Paris (2004). She was also featured in a survey of contemporary artists in British Columbia in How Soon Is Now, Vancouver Art Gallery (2009) and Generation (2008), Edmonton Art Gallery. Her writing on “The Library” can be found in Prefix Photo magazine (2009) and she has written for Canadian Art.

Following A Line also includes work by Pablo Bronstein, Peter Gazendam, Susanne Kriemann, Alex Morrison, Frances Stark and Paul Sietsema, and runs until November 7, 2010. In conjunction with the exhibition is an upcoming schedule of events:
