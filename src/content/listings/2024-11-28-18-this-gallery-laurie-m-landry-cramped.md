NOV 30
THIS Gallery presentsCramped, a solo exhibition by artist Laurie M. Landry, running
from November 22 to November 30, 2024. This thought-provoking collection features
three monumental oil paintings that probe societal constraints, specifically exploring
the proverbial box imposed on larger women.

In Cramped, Landry employs a representational style to portray Rubenesque figures
in bold, tightly framed compositions that reveal only the torso and hands. This
deliberate omission of the head invites viewers to look past individual identity and
connect with the broader themes of anonymity and universality, capturing the
emotional weight of societal expectations. The figures hands act as visual barriers,
underscoring the boundaries imposed by external ideals and the discomfort of being
confined within a narrow definition of beauty.

Landrys work in Cramped speaks to the tension between self-expression and
conformity. The rich colour palette and intentionally constrained compositions evoke
both resilience and vulnerability, encouraging viewers to confront the complexities of
identity, self-image, and societal expectation. The exhibition challenges perceptions
and celebrates the strength inherent in resisting the cramped spaces imposed by
external ideals.

Artist Bio

Laurie M. Landry is a Deaf Canadian contemporary artist. Her work focuses on
contemporary portraiture of marginalized subjects such as those who are Deaf or
living with a disability. In her figurative art, she is currently exploring the corporeality
of heterogeneous bodies. She is particularly intrigued by the ways in which the
hands and bodies can communicate an idea without speaking.

Landry has participated in guided artist residencies across Canada and her work has
been shown in art galleries across Canada, and in international magazines. She has
also curated a group show featuring figurative works by contemporary
female artists
exploring themes of youth and aging bodies. She holds a Certificate in Fine Arts
Techniques from Emily Carr University of Art + Design, and has been awarded
Canada Council for the Arts and British Columbia Arts Council grants to support her
art practice.
