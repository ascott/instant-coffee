Cineworks presents:

Doc Talk: Spar Tree / Nails –– A Screening with Editor and Educator Ray
Hall, in conversation with Zoë Druick (SFU School of Communication)

Cineworks Studio, 1131 Howe Street (lane entrance)
Monday September 21
7.00pm – 9.30pm, FREE

Join Cineworks and DOC BC in Doc Talk, for a screening and discussion of
two celebrated Canadian works in the Theatrical Short documentary form.
Exemplifying West Coast documentary innovation of the late 1970s, Spar Tree
and the Oscar-nominated Nails each feature the Director/Editor team of
Phillip Borsos and Ray Hall, combining 35mm cinematography, archival
footage, music and sound design to construct non-commentary expositions of
environmental and industrial subject matter.

Filmmaker and educator, Ray Hall––UBC Emeritus Professor in Film Production
and editor on both works––will be in attendance, in conversation with Zoë
Druick, Associate Professor in the School of Communication, SFU.

Nails (1979; 35mm; Colour; 13 min)
Director: Philip Borsos
Editor: Ray Hall

This Oscar-nominated documentary short tracks the shift in the relationship
of an individual to work between the 19th century and today. Focusing on
how nails are made, we first see a blacksmith laboring at a forge, shaping
nails from single strands of steel rods. The scene then shifts from this
peaceful setting to the roar of a 20th century nail mill, where banks of
machines draw, cut, and pound the steel rods faster than the eye can follow.

Spar Tree (1978; 35mm; Colour; 15 min)
Director: Philip Borsos
Editor: Ray Hall

Mixing archival footage with spectacular photography, Spartree shows one
man's work in nature. Eschewing images of modern-day heavy machinery used
in the logging industry, the film shows us one of the most interesting, and
dangerous, aspects of tree-felling: the spartree. Hap Johnson, spartree
champion of the world, choses the tree, climbs it, makes his cut, and ends
the process in the traditional and heart-stopping way. Phillip Borsos’
visual style had reached its full maturity with this, his second ‘process’
work, and the long tracking shots through the Vancouver Island rainforest
are some of the most striking images of British Columbia ever shot on
motion picture film.

Ray Hall is Emeritus Professor in Film Production at UBC. Beginning his
career as a Professional Officer with the UN Relief and Welfare Agency (set
up in 1949 as a temporary agency to look after the Palestinian refugees
until they could go back to their homes), he worked as head of the film
unit in Beirut, Lebanon, for three years. The small unit made documentaries
for UNESCO, WHO and other UN agencies in several Middle Eastern countries:
Syria, Jordan, Palestine, Iraq, Qatar and Egypt. Back in Vancouver in 1973,
he and a partner started the post production company Petra, which helped to
launch the careers of many friends and students. In 1979 the documentary he
was working on with Phil Borsos – Nails – was nominated for an Oscar, and
it was while working on Borsos’ drama The Grey Fox that Ray was approached
by Joan Rynartson, then head of the film department at UBC, and asked to
teach film production. Ray became a permanent faculty member in 1980 and
spent 17 years with UBC film students until his retirement in 1997.

Zoë Druick conducts research on the history of educational, documentary and
reality-based media in Canada and beyond. Her books include Projecting
Canada, Alan King's A Married Couple, and the edited collections
Programming Reality, Cinephemera, and The Grierson Effect. She teaches film
and media studies in the School of Communication at SFU.

Doc Talk is a monthly screening and discussion of feature documentary,
presented by DOC BC in collaboration with Cineworks, exploring approaches
and challenges to the documentary form through diverse filmmaking practices.
The Documentary Organization of Canada (DOC) is the largest professional
non-profit association of independent filmmakers in Canada, with more than
850 members across the country and over 200 members in the BC Chapter.
Since 1983, DOC (formerly called the CIFC/Canadian Independent Film Caucus)
– has been the champion of independent documentary film and television in
Canada.

http://docbc.org/

Cineworks Independent Filmmakers Society (est.1980) is an artist-run
production and exhibition centre that supports independent filmmakers and
media artists. We engage our membership and the broader community through
initiatives and services that foster dialogue and experimentation with the
moving image.
