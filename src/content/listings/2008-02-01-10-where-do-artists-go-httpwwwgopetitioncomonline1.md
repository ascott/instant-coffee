Seen from without, the Gasoline Romance is an imagined story; City
life permeates us. Its constancy, its information shrinks our scale of
meaning. Places we enter, objects we know suffer as well this
collective shift existing more as expressions of relativity then
expectations of being, as such. While some who pass through this
shifted landscape note its momentum as 'of the city', others imagine a
counter-melody.

Spaces want to be contemplated, symbols need their 'power' turned up,
objects have participatory expectations… through an innocent
engagement with the defiant re-imagining of a spectator, the duplicity
of acculturation is acknowledged. Its sudden absence, as an expression
of prior organization may well be the insinuation that a local
resonance holds the key to any larger understanding.

Seen from within, this referential idea, itself the vestige of an
earlier conceit allows an experience of the city-life that is not
beholden to its everyday reality. The Gasoline Romance, as such.

    So where do artists go? What happens to a city when there is less and less AFFORDABLE studio space for painters and sculptors?  We need to provide space within the city for working artists. The city government should act now to preserve 901 Main Street and other studio  spaces, and to form a coherent, workable policy to protect a  community which contributes to the richness of our society and to renovate former industrial,	retail or hotel buildings for artist's studios, not more "live-work"  condominiums.
  We, the artists in this building, believe that centres of grass roots creativity have a value beyond that of inflationary real estate profits, and deserve to be protected and recognized for the cultural wealth that they bequeath to the city. As such, we will be presenting our case to Vancouver city council at city hall on Cambie and 12th ave on the 12th of February at 7:30, to hold a moratorium on the permit process in view of the cultural legacy that would be lost with the redevelopment of this site. We encourage everybody to attend this  public council meeting. Numbers count, and this is an opportunity to be heard and be proactive in a city where it is becoming increasingly difficult to access affordable space. A creative city is nothing without the presence of creators within its boundaries. Stand up with us at city hall on the 12th of February.
