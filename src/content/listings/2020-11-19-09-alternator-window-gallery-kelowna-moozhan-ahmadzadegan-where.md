Exhibition:
Where Are You Really From?
Artist: Moozhan Ahmadzadegan
Date: November 20, 2020 - January 9, 2021
Location: The Alternator Centre for Contemporary Art [Window Gallery]

Where Are You Really From? A new exhibition at the Alternator Centre for
Contemporary Art prompts visitors to contemplate the ways in which we communicate.

Where Are You Really From? is an exploration by artist Moozhan Ahmadzadegan into
the complex intersections of ethnicity, cultural hybridity, and nationality.

Ahmadzadegan, a Kelowna artist who recently completed his Bachelor of Fine Arts at
UBC Okanagan, is interested in the ritual conversation he finds himself having quite
often as an Okanagan resident. He is often asked the question where are you from?.
He responds, as a second-generation Canadian, that he is from Canada. The follow-up
question is almost always the same, where are you really from?.

Artistic and Administrative Director, Lorna McParland expands This exhibition offers a
unique perspective on
conversations we often overhear in our community, or
experience first hand. Ahmadzadegans investigation into the ways we navigate our
own experiences, identities and our perception of the other prompts viewers to
consider their own biases and challenge what we understand to be local.

Where Are You Really From? is on view at the Alternator Centre for Contemporary Art
in the Window Gallery from November 20th 2020 until January 9th of 2021.

The Alternator Centre for Contemporary Art is an artist-run centre located in Kelowna
at the Rotary Centre for the Arts, 421 Cawston Avenue. The Alternator Centre for
Contemporary Art is a registered non-profit charitable organization dedicated to the
development of the creative community. Since 1989, the Alternator has shown the work
of emerging Canadian artists, focused on innovative and non-traditional mediums
engaged in social and cultural issues.

The Alternator Centre for Contemporary Art respectfully acknowledges our presence
on the unceded
territory of the Syilx (Okanagan) people.
