good, better and DEFT SENF
Opening Reception: Thursday, October 16, 2104. 7:00 PM
108 E Broadway
Vancouver

Burrard Arts Foundation is pleased to announce the dual opening of two
exhibitions at BAF Studios.

good, better: Aaron Koehn
Aaron Koehn (b. 1983, Cleveland, OH) is an artist working between
Cleveland, Ohio and Queens, New York. The span of his practice resists
simple categorization; good, better represents a collection of recent
multi-media conceptual experimentations in sculpture. A declared painter in
the traditional sense, Koehn's interest in image-making has recently
brought him to the realm of objects, language and ideas.

The exhibition good, better features a brand-new body of work. Using a
direct-to-substrate printer, Koehn gives three-dimensionality to images
normally limited to a computer’s screen. He packages the surfaces of Ikea
LACK tables (a standard, ubiquitous and inexpensive piece of furniture) in
his own veneer of images of highly reflective car hoods found on the
internet. The fetishized, perfectly waxed and polished car surfaces reflect
their surrounding environments, removing the images from the anonymity of
the web and giving them a physical existence in the gallery. While
undeniably posited in a history of minimalist sculpture, Koehn’s sleek,
rectangular LACK tables don't deny their objecthood or history in product
in their neat, tight packaging.

Koehn's practice often engages the legacy of the readymade, a history
concerned with utilizing a found object and instilling within it a
redefined aura. Path Illumination is a work comprised of a full box of
solar powered garden LED stakes that charge during the day in a bright part
of the gallery and are moved to a darker spot by an attendant to illuminate
their literal new-found charge.

Similarly, Koehn changes the context of a highly buffed and polished, now
unusable early generation Macbook Pro. Interested in the fact that an open
laptop shows its glowing logo to onlookers rather than the user themselves,
Koehn mechanically sanded the object until it became a shining relic, an
object even holier than it was in life, a caricature of if its own
sleekness. Koehn's highly buffed childhood baseball bat also becomes a
monument to memory and objecthood.

These and accompanying works in the exhibition are exemplifications of
ideas, explorations of semiotics and the unstable use of language, symbols
and objects to represent the world. This is Koehn's first first solo
exhibition in Canada.

DEFT SENF: Gabriel Dubois
Gabriel Dubois (b. 1982, Collingwood, ON) is an artist living and working
in Vancouver, BC. Dubois' artistic practice ranges from collage,
installation, abstract paintings on panels, architectural interventions and
public murals. Raised in B.C., Dubois attended Emily Carr Institute of Art
and Design which provided him with an important foundation in printmaking
and painting. While in school, Gabriel explored a fascination with the
fringe cultures of Vancouver and the Gulf Islands, both developing into
important influences in his work. In the early 2000’s he embarked on a
journey around the world, living abroad in Germany, India and Britain for
the past decade.

Drawing inspiration from a nomadic lifestyle and the diverse cultures he’s
been immersed in, Dubois has executed colourful, intricate murals in spaces
as diverse as doorways in France, abandoned temples in India and shopfronts
in Vancouver. His paintings and public artworks employ a penchant for
formal positioning, line, curve and balance which result in highly
orchestrated compositions. Dubois is known for painting colourful,
energetic works referencing circuits and labyrinths while interacting with
and complementing the architecture of his chosen site. DEFT SENF will
feature a new body of paintings on panel and a public mural commissioned by
the gallery at 295 Powell Street.

Aside from his public art projects, Gabriel has participated in 13 solo
shows, over 50 group exhibitions, and various art fairs and residencies
over North America and Europe.
