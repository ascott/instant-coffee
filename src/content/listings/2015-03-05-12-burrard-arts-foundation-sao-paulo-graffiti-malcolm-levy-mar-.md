Sao Paulo Graffiti
Malcolm Levy
March 7th to April 4th, 2015
Opening Reception: March 7th, 2pm to 5pm

Burrard Arts Foundation is pleased to present a solo exhibition by Malcolm
Levy.

Sao Paulo Graffiti is a body of work centered around video footage captured
of graffiti in a single alleyway in Sao Paulo, Brazil.
Non-representational, the work is an intermediary between painting,
photography and film. Levy's post-production manipulation of the footage
exposes the inherently flawed nature of digital-to-analog conversion, a
process in which signals having a few (usually two) defined levels or
states (digital) are converted into signals having a theoretically infinite
number of states (analog). The conversion process results in "imagined"
pixelated images to fill the gaps. Levy isolates the made-up frames in this
transference and treats them as abstracted landscapes, effectively turning
the accidents into the main feature. The result is cinematic and painterly,
almost a dreamscape accidentally wandered upon.

Graffiti culture in Sao Paulo is of particular significance: in 2006, the
municipal government banned all outdoor advertisements including
billboards, transit, and in front of stores. Within a year, 15,000
billboards were taken down and store signs shrunk so as to not meet heavy
fines. This "reduction of visual pollution" resulted in a dramatic break in
the dominant visual language of the city and for months, the urban
landscape appeared alien and war-torn. Graffiti artists responded by
covering outdoor spaces in paint, a practice both politically and
physically dangerous. Levy uses found audio to construct a narrative that
tells the story of just how perilous this defiant act of transgression can be.

Malcolm Levy is an artist and curator based in Vancouver, Canada. He was
the Director of the New Forms Festival from 2001-2014, and the curator of
CODE Live, the Digital Festival during the 2010 Olympics Games in
Vancouver. He is the co-Artistic Director for the International Symposium
of Electronic Art in 2015.

Recent exhibitions include the Maraya Arts Park (Sharjah), CSA (Vancouver),
Gallery 295 (Vancouver), Supermarkt (Berlin), Audain Gallery (Vancouver),
Grim Museum (Berlin), Nuit Blanche (Toronto), the INIAF (Yichang), the FCMM
(Montreal) and Next Wave (Melbourne). Upcoming exhibitions include Transfer
Gallery (New York) and WAAP (Vancouver). He is currently finishing an MA in
Media Studies at the New School, and teaches "Contemporary Practices in
Installation Art" at the Centre for Digital Media, a combined program from
Emily Carr and SFU.

Silver Rapids
Nicolas Sassoon
March 9th to April 4th, 2015
Closing Reception: Thursday, April 2nd at 7:30pm

Burrard Arts Foundation is pleased to present an installation by Nicolas
Sassoon.

Silver Rapids is an installation taking form as a video projection across
the window facade of Burrard Arts Foundation at night. The projection
structure is based on a waterfall, with variations occurring throughout a
looping cycle. It begins by mimicking the natural motion of a waterfall,
displaying series of waves passing through the screen from top to bottom.
After the speed crests, it changes directions, and then comes back to its
starting point. The animation is created using digital moire patterns;
composed of pixelated textures and 6 tones of grey, forming a greyscale
gradient from black to white.

Silver Rapids is articulated around the experience of nature through
technology and the experience of motion through screen-based and
projection-based animation. The work comes from a personal sense of
disconnection and reconnection with the natural through the practice of
screen-based animation, and the desire to objectify digital technology as
an environment within a public space. Formally, the piece explores an
elliptic cycle inspired by tidal and wave movements.

Sassoon's projection will be on view from outside the gallery at night from
March 9th to April 4th, with a closing celebration held at the gallery on
April 2nd.

Nicolas Sassoon (b.1981 in Marseille, France) lives and works between
Biarritz, France and Vancouver. His work makes use of various
computer-based processes to generate fantasized visions of architectures,
landscapes and domestic environments. While most of his work is published
online as animated GIFs, Sassoon also materializes his web-based practice
into sculptures, prints, textiles, and site-specific installations, as well
as collaborations with other artists, architects, music producers and
fashion designers. Sassoon's work often explores the contemplative and
projective dimensions of the digital, as well as the manner in which
virtual space can (or cannot) be inscribed within the physical realm.

Nicolas Sassoon has shown in international venues and events such as the
New Museum (US), 319 Scholes (US), Eyebeam (US), May Gallery & Residency
(US), Contemporary Art Gallery (CA), Charles H.Scott Gallery (CA), Western
Front (CA), PRETEEN Gallery (MX), the Centre d'Art Bastille (FR), Arti et
Amicitiae (NL), MU Eindhoven (NL) , Victoria & Albert Museum (UK), Today
Art Museum (CN), the Berlin Fashion Week (DE)) and the New-York Fashion
Week (US). Nicolas is a member of the online collective Computers Club and
a founder of the collective W-A-L-L-P-A-P-E-R-S.
