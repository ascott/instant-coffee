Noon Hour Lecture
Lorraine Weir: "What's in a Face? Anime and the Criminal Code of Canada"
February 25, noon - 1 pm

Join us for a lecture by Lorraine Weir (Professor, English, UBC). She is
currently working on the Court's concept of representation in various
Canadian land rights and expressive freedoms cases.

Weir has interdisciplinary and cross-cultural interests in contemporary
theory, Indigenous Epistemologies, Law and Society, and contemporary
writing (indigenous and settler). She has served as an expert witness in
expressive freedoms cases including Little Sister's (1996), Surrey School
Board (1998), R. v. Sharpe (2002).

In addition to working on censorship and the project of civility in
Canadian cultural production, Weir has worked on literary Modernism in
Europe (Writing Joyce: A Semiotics of the Joyce System) and in Canada (Jay
Macpherson, co-edited, Margaret Atwood: Language, Text and System).
Lorraine Weir is a Professor in the English Department at UBC.

This lecture is in conjunction with, "Faces: Works From The Permanent
Collection"
to April 10, 2011

"Faces" explores the diverse ways "faces" are represented, looking
specifically at how ideas of gender, race, class, and historical contexts
affect our understanding of them.

"Faces" includes over 90 paintings, photographs, sculpture and video from
the collections and archives of the Morris and Helen Belkin Art Gallery. In
addition, research and travel photographs that date from the 1890s to the
mid 1970s have been loaned by the Museum of Anthropology (UBC), the
Canadian Museum of Civilization (Gatineau), and the American Museum of
Natural History Library (New York).
