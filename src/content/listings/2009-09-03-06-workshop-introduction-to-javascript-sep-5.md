WORKSHOP – Introduction to Javascript
1PM – 4PM, September 05, 2009
HUB:LAB

Morris and Helen Belkin Art Gallery
1825 Main Mall
Vancouver, British Columbia,
Canada V6T 1Z2

JavaScript, best known for its ability to add functionality and enrich the experience of otherwise 
static web pages, is one of the most popular scripting languages that exist today. In this 
workshop you will learn the basics of working with JavaScript. Topics include a discussion of some 
artworks that use JavaScript, language structure and syntax, and some common uses on the 
Internet. Through a series of in-class projects, participants will learn what they need to start 
working with JavaScript as well as the concepts that will become the basis of any future 
programming projects.

Workshop capacity is limited. Please register online by visiting

at the bottom of the page.

WORKSHOP – Introduction to Processing
1PM – 4PM, September 19, 2009
HUB:LAB

Morris and Helen Belkin Art Gallery
1825 Main Mall
Vancouver, British Columbia,
Canada V6T 1Z2

Processing is an open source programming language and environment for students, artists, 
designers, researchers, and hobbyists who want to program images, animation, and interactions. 
In this workshop you will learn the basics of working with Processing. Topics include a discussion 
of artworks made with Processing, language structure and syntax, creating simple interactivity 
and image creating and manipulation. Through a series of in-class projects, participants will learn 
what they need to start working with Processing as well as the concepts that will become the 
foundation of any future programming projects.

07. VanDusen Botanical Garden | Second Site II Closing Party | SEP 6
Second Site II at VanDusen Botanical Garden (37th and Oak)

We are having a "Closing" on Sunday September 6th, noon to 4 pm.

It's your last chance to see the collection of outdoor electronic
works at the garden.

Presenting "Audio Pools" by Diana Burgoyne, "Poison Mentor" by Peter
Courtemanche, "Diaspora" by David Floren, "Alate" by Robin Ripley,
"Pondering" by Matt Smith, and "Weidenhammer's Sonic Therapy Spa" by
Lori Weidenhammer.

From July through September 2009, Second Site collective is presenting
its second series of outdoor interventions and installations at the
VanDusen Botanical Gardens in Vancouver. The works illustrate simple
mechanisms of nature as well as form a hybrid between bio-diversity
(as opposed to bio-industry) and electronic art (as opposed to
industrial technology). The works include hybrid sculpture that merges
plants with electronics, robotics, a fungus that monitors levels of
man-made radiation, hand-crank-powered audio pools, and a sonic
therapy spa based on the biorhythms of plants. All of the pieces are
self-powered either by the sun, or through interaction with the
viewer. They are designed to be autonomous, with no need for
electrical outlets or other materials from the site. In this way the
objects become independent organisms that inhabit the gardens in the
same way as biological creatures.

Second Site collective is a group of artists who create electronic art
(sound, kinetics, robotics, primal circuitry, sculpture) in green
spaces – parks, gardens, areas of urban agriculture, beaches, and
"urban wilderness". The art created by Second Site causes the audience
to do a "double take", transforming a familiar space into a
"meta-site" – a place where art has created a heightened sensory
experience. It causes a sense of curiosity and enchantment in the
viewer that is designed to make people think about the relationship
between human intervention, technology, and bio-diversity.

08. Jeffrey Boone Gallery | Anna Plesset | SEP 9
Please join Anna Plesset at the opening reception of her first solo show with the gallery on 
Wednesday September 9th from 6-9PM.

Headlines: Sunday, August 19, 2007 - Friday, April 25, 2008 is a collection of paintings and 
drawings that explores time, control, and the everyday - three recurring themes with which she 
has worked over the past few years. Some of the concepts addressed in this body of work are 
ephemerality, illusion, how narratives evolve and change over time, and how our perception of 
and relationships to objects, signifiers, events, and artwork itself changes over time.

In these works, what was once text written to entice a reader’s interest in a story becomes an 
object Plesset uses to entice a viewer to experience a moment of prolonged looking. The 
illusionistic techniques she employs create a potential moment of uncertainty (is it real?) for the 
viewer. During this period of looking, the viewer becomes aware of the physical attributes of the
headlines depicted. Acting as a bridge, the now objectified newspaper headline encourages the 
viewer to think about the daily barrage of headlines and our inability to control the events of our 
time.
