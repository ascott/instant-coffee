Learn to make a traditional Coast Salish weaving featuring Kwantlen Elder Hazel Gludo 
on July 6 @ 11:00 am - 1:00 pm

Kwantlen Elder, Hazel (Fillardeau) Gludo has been weaving cedar for a number of 
years. From an early age, Hazels mother had taught her the importance that cedar has 
and how to embrace its values. Today, Hazel teaches and talks about how important it 
is in our lives and how years ago the people used it, and how we have great respect for 
our cedar tree and we too need to learn respect our trees that are around us, for the air, 
and how beautiful it is that we have trees.

Hazel is a prominent employee within the National Historic Fort Site in Fort Langley, BC. 
Visitors often come in just to visit with Hazel. Hazel uses the art of Cedar Weaving to 
share her stories.

Hazel will harvest the cedar she uses to weave hats, bracelets, medicine pouches, 
roses. One of Hazels Cedar Hats is on display with the We Are Kwantlen exhibit to 
show the importance that cedar has always had with the Kwantlen First Nation. Cedar 
has long been a valuable resource to the Northwest Coast First Nations people, but it 
also plays an important role in spiritual belief and ceremonial life.

Please join us at the Deer Lake Gallery for the workshop on July 6 @ 11:00 am - 1:00 
pm. Kits cost $5 each. Come on out and learn how to make a traditional coast salish 
weaving . Fun & interactive class. You must register early as space is limited!

$5 includes all supplies for the workshop required to participate. However, the 
demonstration is Free and open to all public to observe.
