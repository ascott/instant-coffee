the heart that has no love/pain/generosity is not a heart is a multifaceted 
installation comprising of photographs, documents, miniature paintings, 
drawings, videotapes and objects which examine the aftermath of the 
destruction of the colossal 5th century Buddhas of the Bamiyan Valley in 
Central Afghanistan (destroyed by the Taliban in March 2001). Recording the 
destitution of central Afghanistan today, the exhibition focuses on the 
situation of the Hazara people, a persecuted Shiâ€™a Muslim minority, who 
believe themselves to be descended from the sculptors who produced the 
colossal figures of the Buddha, UNESCOâ€™s efforts to conserve the Buddhist 
statues and caves, and rebuilding efforts following the stationing of NATO 
forces in the Bamiyan Valley.

In April 2008, Vancouver-based artist Jayce Salloum travelled with Afghan-
Hazara artist Khadim Ali from Karachi, Pakistan to Kabul, Afghanistan and 
then overland into the Bamiyan Valley. The artists' traveled independently and 
to some extent clandestinely. The land is scarred by decades of conflict, 
ravaged by drought and desperate poverty, troubled by tribal rivalries and a 
persistent Taliban presence. Of specific interest to the artists were the cave 
sites of the Buddhas which provided a productive site to examine the stories 
behind the issues, and the scenes behind the stories creating a contemporary 
archive from which to work from. Salloum's videotapes include interviews with 
a village Shahnameh reciter, pans of the majestic valleys, and school girls 
singing poetry at their early morning assemblies in Kabul. Their reflections 
suggest the complexity of the situation in Afghanistan, which is essentially 
divided along tribal lines and loyalties, fuelled by generations who have grown 
up only knowing a culture of war but striving to build their lives despite the 
difficulties. 

Through the process of a cross-cultural collaboration the exhibition records 
the destitution of current conditions in Bamiyan, reflecting on the sites of 
signification and rupture shaping an incipient modernity in Afghanistan. The 
project documents a significant moment in the history of the Hazara people, 
many of whom are seeking asylum in the West. The exhibition engages a 
sense of the complexity of the current situation in Afghanistan, taking up 
themes of the possibility of resistance, hope, beauty in the context of ongoing 
conflict. the heart that has no love/pain/generosity is not a heart was curated 
by Haema Sivanesan and co-commissioned by SAVAC (South Asian Visual 
Arts Centre), Toronto and the Alternator Centre for Contemporary Art 
(Kelowna).
