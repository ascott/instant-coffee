This is a post-exhibition discussion of Tegan Moore's exhibition titled "Is Evaporation 
Emotion?" at Spare Room.

Jennifer Fisher's Tactile Affects has been an ongoing point of reference for Moore's 
exhibition as a method to "destabilize habits of one-way consumptive viewing" " and 
through this consideration of physical presence (haptics, embodiment) explores 
alternative modes of aesthetic experience.

"Invisible architecture" in the context of "Evaporation" addresses modes of oppression 
that exists in western, male-centric notions of pseudo-ideal office working conditions set 
forth during the 60s to standardize thermal comfort levels, also known as The Comfort 
Index. Fisher's reading is contextualized within this exhibition narrative to look at the 
possibilities in materializing immaterial conditions in everyday routine.

Please join us for an informal discussion of the reading at Spare Room.
