SESSIONAL LECTURESHIPS, SUMMER 2009 (Visual art and Art History)

The Department of Art History, Visual Art, and Theory will offer sessional
lectureships in Visual Art for the summer session 2009 as follows. For
more details on courses see www.ahva.ubc.ca. The positions are tentative
and subject to enrollment and final budgetary approval.

VISA 110 Foundation Computing for the Visual Arts

Term 1 (June 15-July 24): Tues, and Thur from 2- 3pm **

**plus oversight and or teaching in associated lab sections see
www.students.ubc.ca under courses for more information.

Preference will be given to applicants with a degree in their discipline
(MFA for Visual Art) and with teaching experience at the college or
university level.

The current salary range for sessional lecturers is $5,778 to $6,270 per 3
credit course (exclusive of salary increase effective 1 July 2009).
Effective July 1, 2009 the salary range for Sessional lecturers is $5,970
to $6,477. Salaries are dependent on the period of appointment, percentage
of full-time load, and years of appropriate full-time post-secondary
teaching experience.

With the letter of application, candidates should include a statement on
pedagogy, teaching and research methods, sample course outlines, teaching
evaluations, a CV, and a transcript of academic record and publications and
CD portfolio. The CV should include a detailed list of all post-secondary
courses taught including name, length, credit value, and teaching
responsibilities. If applying from outside the department, three
confidential letters of reference, in sealed envelopes from appropriate
persons, should also be forwarded.

All positions are governed by UBC's Agreement on Conditions of Appointment
for Sessional and Part-Time Faculty members.

Please send letters of application to: Whitney Friesen, Administrator

Department of Art History,
Visual Art, and Theory

University of British Columbia

403-6333 Memorial Rd.

Vancouver, BC V6T 1Z2

Deadline for applications is April 10, 2009.
