Exhibition: One of Them Will Be Unlucky
Artist:
Sora Park
Date: November 20, 2020 - January 9, 2021
Location: The Alternator Centre for Contemporary Art [Main Gallery]

From Korean to German, Sora Park explores language and family history in One of
Them Will Be Unlucky at the Alternator Centre for Contemporary Art

Sora Parks creative practice explores the impact globalization and migration have on
the creation of subcultures around the world, and how the movement of people and their
culture affect the sustainability of these subcultures. For her upcoming exhibition at the
Alternator Centre for Contemporary Art, One of Them Will Be Unlucky, Parks practice is
investigated through the lens of her great-grandfather, one of the first Korean residents
of Berlin, Germany.

Through an exploration of her great-grandfathers belongings, Park reveals a web of
facts, memory and superstition, complicated by translation across three languages. One
of Them Will Be Unlucky explores the role language plays in understanding the
complexities of
migration and diaspora.

We are excited to share Sora Parks One of Them Will Be Unlucky in our space and
hope the exhibition initiates conversations amongst our viewers about the complexities
of immigration on culture and identity. (Artistic and Administrative Director, Lorna
McParland).

One of Them Will Be Unlucky is on view in the Alternators Main Gallery from November
20, 2020 through to January 9th, 2021.

Sora Park is a Korean-Canadian interdisciplinary artist. She received her BFA in
Photography from Emily Carr University of Art and Design in Vancouver, Canada and an
MA in Fine Arts from Bergen Academy of Art and Design in Bergen, Norway. She is a
recipient of grants from Arts Council Norway, Canada Council for the Arts and BC Arts
Council.

The Alternator Centre for Contemporary Art is an artist-run centre located in Kelowna at
the Rotary Centre for the Arts, 421 Cawston Avenue. The Alternator Centre for
Contemporary Art is a registered non-profit charitable organization
dedicated to the
development of the creative community. Since 1989, the Alternator has shown the work
of emerging Canadian artists, focused on innovative and non-traditional mediums
engaged in social and cultural issues.
