The Back Gallery Project is pleased to announce an upcoming painting group exhibition with 
works by Noah Becker, Adam Dood and Silvana Iriondo:
Extreme Pacific (where North meets South)
Please join us for the artists reception on Friday, September 25th, 2009 at 6 pm

306 Abbott Street buzz # 10
Third Floor

This group exhibition has the common denominator that the artists use painting as their medium, 
they are under 40 years of age and they are connected through the Pacific Ocean, the work 
presented is a comment among other things in how they view the landscape in their own unique 
way. Artist Noah Becker is based in Victoria, BC and his work has recently been shown at the 
Vancouver Art Gallery as part of the exhibit How Soon Is Now. Adam Dodd is based in 
Vancouver, BC and as young and upcoming artist his work has been included in several group 
exhibits among them Grace Gallery, Back Gallery Project and several one night shows. Silvana 
Iriondo is an artist from Santiago, Chile who has recently relocated to Vancouver. 

Noah Becker Drawing upon a diverse group of references—Bruegel, Bosch, Varley and Victorian 
era cartoons, just to name a few—Becker’s works challenge the viewer to consume vast amounts 
of visual information in images saturated with a complex iconography that treat pressing issues 
like war, cultural, and social decay in a carnivalesque manner. According to the artist, « When 
ideas drawn from medieval folklore are used in a contemporary context, interesting things can 
occur. »

In all of his works, Becker has abdicated the background so that the landscape and the figures 
that inhabit it are left floating in an ambiguous and empty space. Like Bruegel who privileged the 
depiction of the quotidian over historical themes that had traditionally defined the genre of 
landscape painting, Becker utilizes seemingly banal scenarios to show how history invades the 
everyday and is present at every level of society. 
The notion of globalization is evident in his work. Individuals choose to partake as consumers in
the shared concepts and notions of cultures outside of their own, even if they are not aware of 
their involvement. Together people are building a universal point of recognition; however no one 
is quite sure how to encompass identity into a single definition. In other words, the lack of 
certainty of the meaning and repercussions of globalization is represented. 

Noah Becker is also well known as a jazz musician and the publisher of Whitehot Magazine. He is 
also semi finalist of this year RBC Painting Award Competition, which final results will be made 
public in October 2009

Silvana Iriondo The works that this Chilean painter has been creating for the past three years 
present the viewer with a style of painting that is clearly contemporary, yet at the same time 
profoundly Latin American. 
When observing how the artist takes on topics such as landscapes and portraits, certain 
characteristics of Latin American geography, culture, history and identity appear in her work 
represented through colour, shape and objects that compose the total of the image. At the same 
time, these elements are approached from a present- day point of view, that takes distance from 
could be called " Stereotype of Latin America" and are, on the contrary, an intimate 
representation charged with reflection, which shows us a rooted Latin American identity but that 
also absorbs external elements, making them her own.

Mountains, volcanoes, wild exotic plants that go beyond the typical landscape a person from 
south america would be used to, are represented in the work. There is a mix of what is known 
and unknown , a feeling of isolation an perplexity before a landscape that always surpasses its 
inhabitants. This is why the amount of elements or objects that Iriondo manipulates are a few, 
the artist wants to emphasize the feeling of isolation, charging each of the elements in the image 
with a symbolism that, in the simplest manner, allude to the identity, but which also emphasize 
the contemporary and austere condition of the image, by the way they are solved pictorially. 
text by Vicente Jose Cocina 

Adam Dood received his BFA from Emily Carr University in 2006 his work has futuristically evolve 
within a short period of time. From the urban paintings with round shapes and flat colours to the
likeness of modernism as we understand it from the 1950's "to becoming post modern refugees" 
(quoting the artist) in his work there is no doubt that through extensive work and dedication 
Adam Dood is finding pleasure in dealing with more geometrical forms that have multiple 
readings. 
His most recent work represents a mountain depicted twice in the same format, as if he was trying
to make sure we get the hidden message by repeating it twice in the canvas. Like when a 
personal dialogue takes place and people repeat themselves to make sure the message is clear. 
Here the message evolves into something else, we are force to look twice in again the same 
canvas, and by doing so the image becomes more complex, mysterious and certainly interesting.
