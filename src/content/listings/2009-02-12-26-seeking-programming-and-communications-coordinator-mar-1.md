27. Charles H. Scott Gallery | Germaine Koh | til | MAR 8
Germaine Koh
Fallow

Exhibition continues until March 8, 2009

The Charles H. Scott Gallery is pleased to present Fallow, a solo
exhibition by Vancouver-based artist Germaine Koh. In this installation,
plants and groundcover from a fallow urban lot have been transplanted into
the gallery, completely filling the exhibition space. The sense of disuse
and non-productivity associated with vacant lots is transformed through
this process of relocation. What was once identified as a void within the
landscape of urban development is ascribed with new use value. As Koh says,
Although withdrawn from constructive use, the space is far from empty,
but rather full of richly non-productive time and process.

29. Richmond Art Gallery | Kristi Malakoff & Heidi Nagtegaal | til | MAR 22
Guise

Curated by Carie Helm

An exhibition by Kristi Malakoff & Heidi Nagtegaal

Exhibition Dates: February 5 - March 22, 2009

Guise by Kristi Malakoff and Heidi Nagtegaal is new works that delineate
between what is visible and what is hidden and considers the possibilities
of potential or imagined lives and experiences. Through craft-based
interpretations of the guise of culture, the artists provide opportunities
for reflection and reinterpretation of craft traditions.

Malakoff's impressive site-specific installation incorporates intricate
works of paper cuts constructing a static scene of children dancing around
the Maypole. The 20 free-standing dancing figures (10 boys & 10 girls) are
frozen in time and place, and preserved in a petrified state as though
turned to stone in an old Germanic fairytale. This thoughtful installation
engenders a fantastical interpretation of a cultural event, wandering
between beauty and intimidation, wonder and apprehension.

Heidi Nagtegaal crochets/knits an impressive series of facial hairpieces in
Facial Hair Study, an examination of costume and identity. Heidi Nagtegaal
uses crafts in contemporary art as a way of re-identifying one practice in
another. Both artists elevate these objects beyond reductive associations
with marginalized craft histories.

Kristi Malakoff is a 2005 graduate of ECI in Vancouver where she was the
recipient of many awards, including the Governor General's Award. Malakoff
has exhibited extensively in both group and solo shows throughout Canada
and in England, the US and Mexico. Her work is held in private collections
across Canada and the US.

Heidi Nagtegaal received a BFA from ECI in 2005. She has had recent
exhibitions at CSA, Zulu Records, Western Front, Centre A, and the Helen
Pitt Gallery ARC.

The exhibit is on display until March 22, 2009. The Artist will give a
guided Talk & Tour of their work on Saturday, February 7th at 2:00pm in the
Gallery.

Carie Helm is a practicing artist, emerging curator and former RAG
Curatorial Assistant. She earned a BFA from Okanagan University College
(now UBC-O) in 2000 and has organized exhibitions for the Kelowna Art
Gallery, the Alternator Gallery and the Richmond Art Gallery and exhibits
her own work in solo and group exhibitions throughout British Columbia.
