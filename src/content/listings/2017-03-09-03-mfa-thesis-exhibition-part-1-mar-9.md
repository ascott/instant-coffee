04. Republic Gallery | Marian Penner Bancroft | radial systems | MAR 9

Marian Penner Bancroft
radial systems

March 9 - April 15, 2017
Opening reception: Thursday, March 9, 6 - 8 PM

radial systems is a series of colour photographs and videos depicting organic objects found on 
or near the ground and in the water, both cultivated and not. The use of a macro lens makes 
possible both a change in scale and a scrutiny of forms not so readily accessible to the naked 
eye. Each item photographed is in a state of material flux, apparent in some more than others. 
The video work attempts an apprehension of activity almost too slow for perception, focussing 
on ordinary movements; logs drifting, birds flying, garbage burning, and tugboats plying the 
Fraser River.

Marian Penner Bancroft's work has been exhibited nationally and internationally and can be 
found in a number of collections including those of the Vancouver Art Gallery, the Morris and 
Helen Belkin Art Gallery, the National Gallery of Canada in Ottawa and Canada House in 
London, UK. In 2012, the Vancouver Art Gallery mounted a 1975-2000 survey of Bancroft's 
work entitled SPIRITLAND:t/HERE. In the same year she received the Audain Prize for Lifetime 
Achievement in the Visual Arts. Bancroft is a Professor Emeritus at Emily Carr University of Art 
+ Design in Vancouver and her work is represented by Republic Gallery, Vancouver.

TASTEMAKERS Artist Talk: Phranc

Big Rock Candy Mountain presents Tastemakers, a series of artist talks taking
place at Queen Alexandra Elementary School this spring. These talks bring a
diverse array of artists and cultural producers into conversation with the Grade
4/5 students. The short talks are followed by workshops where the artists' ideas
are enacted, tested, and unraveled by the student population.

This week, Tastemakers presents Phranc who introduces herself as "the All-American Jewish 
Lesbian Folksinger." As a visual artist, she has adopted the moniker "The Cardboard Cobbler." 
As a teenager she attended The Feminist Studio Workshop at The Woman's Building in Los 
Angeles, California, where she focused on songwriting and silk-screening. In the late 1970's 
she was a member of the Nervous Gender and Catholic Discipline in the L.A. punk rock scene. 
She has recorded for Rhino Records, Island Records, and Kill Rock Stars and toured 
internationally with many acclaimed and notorious artists. Both her music and visual work 
employ humor to raise consciousness, trigger response, and provoke discussion. She is 
exhibits her visual work at the Craig Krull Gallery in Santa Monica California and at Friesen 
Gallery in Ketchum, Idaho.

Forthcoming Tastemakers:

Friday, March 31, 1:10 pm - Ron Tran

More about Big Rock Candy Mountain:

Begun in 2015, Big Rock Candy Mountain (BRCM) is a flavor incubator and
taste-making think-tank between artists Hannah Jickling and Helen Reed and
students, teachers and support staff at Queen Alexandra Elementary School in
East Vancouver. The project takes its name from a folk song that has been
revised and rewritten countless times to reflect a comic utopia, where we hear a
"...buzzin' of the bees in the peppermint trees, 'round the soda water fountains."
BRCM is a post-proportionate world where adults and rationality no longer define
the rules and limits of what is possible.

BRCM is comprised of meandering workshops, edible editions, artist invocations,
strange conversations and installations on school grounds. Rather than investing
in a single result, BRCM privileges the school as a kind of (candy) factory,
engaged in a wide range of productive capacities and processes. Here, creative
flavour-making, pop art riffs and explorations in kid-defined 'persuasive' language
intersect with the larger context of economy, labour, taste-as-power and culturally
defined objects of desire.

Big Rock Candy is curated by Vanessa Kwan and produced by Other Sights For
Artists'Projects. We gratefully acknowledge the support of the BC Arts Council,
the Canada Council for the Arts, the City of Vancouver and the Vancouver
School Board. See bigrockcandymountain.ca and othersights.ca. Follow
@bigrockcandy.mountain on Instagram.

Bita Joudaki
Technimate
March 14 - April 11, 2017

Bita Joudaki's Technimate is a non-linear series of scenes presenting private moments that are 
mediated by technological devices. In every scene a woman is found alone, usually in a 
domestic setting, with their gaze directed at a screen. Joudaki considers the contemporary 
experience of intimacy and communication that is simultaneously isolated from human 
interaction.

Bita Joudaki is a filmmaker based in Vancouver, BC. She graduated with a BFA in Film, Video + 
Integrated Media from Emily Carr University. She has worked as a campaign writer for social

with the crucial journal Tessera and is a founding editor of the French-language cultural journal, 
Spirale, and co-edited the key anthology, Biting the Error: Writers Explore Narrative 
(Coachhouse, 2004).

Artist Talk: April 8 2-4pm

Carolina de la Cajiga's series 'City in Flux (Work in Progress)' focuses on the hasty changes our 
cities are going through. The expected growth requires carefully orchestrated programs as the 
available land is limited by nature -water and mountains are our fixed boundaries.

While the issue has serious repercussions affecting everybody now living in the city and those 
who will eventually move in or wish they could, De la Cajiga's approach is whimsical. She 
states, "I am free to do what architects and developers can only dream about. I bend 
engineering protocols and planners' minds, to construct new urban environments -reality 
transformed to produce different or implausible spaces. Photography and imagination allow me 
to move buildings to new settings, to reshape, reduce or enlarge them. In these times when real 
estate prices are hitting the sky, I'm multiplying the land available... Through illusion, I bring the 
matter to the fore."

In the same way, as houses seem to evaporate, workers appear and disappear from our view 
and mind, even in their neon uniforms. They are so easy to forget... We live in a City in Flux...

De la Cajiga's work is based on photography plus digital painting and illusion. As with all her 
artwork, what you see is not what is but what her imagination sees.

Each artwork is composed of various photographs stitched together. The largest one consists of 
58 shots plus 43 additions. The final size is 33" x 165". It is a panorama of the North Shore 
from Lions Gate to the Second Narrows bridges.

14. Terra Vivente Art Studio | Artist Residency | MAR 15

The Terra Vivente Art Studio is located in southern Italy, not far from Naples, in the village of 
San Potito Sannitico. The residency program runs in July and August. Artists come for one 
month to work in the studio, explore the village and the region, and participate in the festivals 
and exhibitions. A maximum of four artists participate in the main residency, with 
accommodation in two two-bedroom apartments with an indoor and outdoor covered studio.

In addition to the public art and film festivals that take place in San Potito, there are many other 
festivals in nearby villages, notably in Guardia Sanframondi every August, there is a 6-day wine 
festival, Vinalia, a wonderful opportunity to participate in cultural events and engage with the 
community. In addition, the Riti Settennali, an important religious ceremony that takes place 
only once every seven years will be happening in August in Guardia Sanframondi. This is a 
region that is off the tourist route; participating artists have the opportunity to immerse 
themselves in the local culture. The residency program aims to foster interaction with the 
community, dialogue and collaboration, with an eye on environmental sustainability within 
artistic practices.

For details on how to apply, the residency fee, and to download the application form, see 
website, www.terraviventeartstudio.com.

The application deadline is March 15, 2017. Contact email: hwadsley@telus.net
