Thomas Iksiraq Philippa Iksiraq

A Survey

January 13 - February 8, 2009

Opening reception, January 15, 2009, from 7pm - 9pm

Thomas and Philippa Iksiraq are among the founding members of the Baker
Lake Printmaker's Co-Operative in Nunavut. Their images speak to the
contemporary challenges faced by the Inuit people as they stand at the
brink between a nomadic way of life that was tied closely to the land and a
lifestyle heavily influenced by Western values and ways of living. Through
their practice, Thomas and Philippa seek to maintain their history and
culture for the next generation of Inuit people and to tell their stories
to the world.

Initiated in 1970 as a means to disseminate the artwork of the Inuit people
of Baker Lake, then a part of the North West Territories, the Baker Lake
Printmakers Co-operative provided printmaking facilities and connections
with the south to produce and exhibit portfolios of their outstanding
artwork. The various print co-operatives of the North produce a wide range
of styles and subject matter that highlight the cultural diversity among
the Inuit people and the unique artistic expression of individual artists.
The print studios provide not only a way of making a living but a form of
cultural affirmation.

The Iksiraqs' work combines traditional subject matter with images of
contemporary life in Baker Lake. They have witnessed the impact of drastic
cultural change to the Inuit people as mining, industry and Western
traditions took over a way of life that had been tied harmoniously to the
land for thousands of years. This changing way of life is evident in their
images. They want to tell their story to the younger generations of Inuit
people and to the people living in southern Canada so they to can glimpse a
way of living in harmony with the land even in one of the harshest climates
on earth. Through print media Thomas and Philippa are enabled to
communicate their important message to others in Canada and throughout the
world.

Call for Submissions

All proposals and applications for residencies and scholarships should
consist of a current curriculum vita, a statement of intent for the
proposed exhibition, a general statement about your practice and a compact
disk with no more than 20 images or 5 minutes of video, if appropriate.
Please contact Malaspina Printmakers if you have any questions
regarding the submissions process.

Expressions of Interest for 2010 Programming Curators, Artists and
Researchers are invited to submit project proposals for Malaspina
Printmakers' 2010 programming season, which will be based on the theme of
Print Culture in Vancouver. Encompassing all forms of printed text and
printed visual communication, Print Culture extends to and includes all
cultural effects on society created by printing technology and printed
communication. Print is intrinsically tied to culture; politics, economy,
and meaning-making are all intertwined with printing in its various forms.
The current shift between a materially-print-based and electronic-based
information society ’ and the many claims that print culture is in its
demise ’ posits print media in a unique position to comment upon
contemporary cultural, economic and political issues. Malaspina Printmakers
welcomes diverse interpretations of how we may envision the theme of Print
Culture within Vancouver's rich history and contemporary life. Project
proposals will be accepted until August 1, 2009. All submissions of
interest that are outside the range of the 2010 programming are also
welcome on an ongoing basis. Until August 1, 2009

Visiting Artist Residency Print-based artists living outside of the
greater Vancouver area are invited to apply for Malaspina's Visiting Artist
Residency. Visiting Artists have 24-hour access to the studio during the
one-month residency and the opportunity to exhibit their recent work.
Individuals participating in the Visiting Artist Residency will also
conduct demonstrations, workshops and artists talks. Deadline: April 30, 2009

Print Research Residency The Print Research Residency pairs a local artist
with an expert printmaker to work on an identified project for the artist.
This unique program allows interdisciplinary artists to consult with
technically literate and highly-skilled print-artists to experiment in the
print medium and create a body of work at the Malaspina Studio. Deadline:
July 30, 2009

Student Scholarships Student Scholarships are awarded to two continuing
print students for four months and four recent graduates (within 12 months)
for six months of free studio use. The scholarship program addresses the
need of recent graduates and current print students to access studio space
to continue their practice between semesters or immediately after
graduation. Deadline: February 30, 2009
