21. Teck Gallery, SFU | Nick Danziger: Blair at War | NOV 24
Nick Danziger: Blair at War
November 24, 2008 - February 14, 2009
Teck Gallery, SFU Vancouver Campus
Artist talk on Afghanistan: Thursday, November 27, 7pm, SFU Vancouver
Artist walking tour of Blair at War: Saturday, November 29, 3pm, Teck
Gallery

Ever wondered what decision-making looks like when the participants know
that human lives will hang in the balance? This exhibition of photographs by
Nick Danziger is the result of a seemingly uncontroversial assignment to
document then-prime minister Tony Blair's 50th birthday for The Times
Magazine. Global events forced a metamorphosis of the assignment ­ it became
a month-long study of a British prime minister deliberating the pros and
cons of participating in the war in Iraq, during which Danziger, and his
collaborator on the project, Times Literary Supplement editor Peter
Stothard, had round-the-clock access to Blair's inner circle, often
wondering why they were not asked to leave.

The text panels in the show document who said what and are a record of the
process of the UK government assimilating information that often proved to
be unreliable. There is a film noir quality to the pictures even though the
scenes of dialogue look more like everyday considerations of whether to
order this or that type of wine for dinner.

Five years after the invasion of Iraq, these striking photographs do what
photography often does best ­ provide privileged access to inaccessible
people, events, and processes.

Artist talk: Thursday, November 27, 7pm
The artist will present a talk on his recent assignment photographing Hamid
Karzai, President of Afghanistan. Room 7000, SFU Vancouver Campus. This talk
is co-presented with and co-sponsored by the SFU Centre for the Comparative
Study of Muslim Societies and Cultures.

Artist walking tour: Saturday, November 29, 3pm
Please join us for a tour of the Blair at War exhibition with Nick Danziger
- at the Teck Gallery, SFU Vancouver Campus.

This exhibition is presented in collaboration with the National Portrait
Gallery, London, UK, with support from the British Council.

22. helen pitt gallery | Vanessa Kwan & Eric Metcalfe | til | DEC 19
Vanessa Kwan & Eric Metcalfe

The Set-Up

November 7 - December 19, 2008&#8232;

Closing reception, Friday December 12th, 2008 at 8:00 pm&#8232;Conversation
between artists and curators, Saturday December 13th at 3:00 pm

Guest curated by Alissa Firth-Eagland and Johan Lundh

The Helen Pitt Gallery is pleased to present a new and unprecedented work
created between artists Vanessa Kwan and Eric Metcalfe. Over the period of
five weeks, Kwan and Metcalfe will conceptualize and produce a mural work
in the gallery, culminating in a reception held December 12th, 2008. The
Set-Up will develop through and reflect upon dialogues involved in creative
exchange. Consequently, it will explore and examine the generosities and
risks involved in collaboration. The exhibition will be accompanied by a
publication available at the closing reception. 

Vanessa Kwan is a Vancouver-based artist and writer. Her solo work has been
exhibited at the Or Gallery, Access Gallery, The Richmond Art Gallery and
the Art Gallery of the South Okanagan, among others. She has published
reviews of art and culture in C Magazine, Fuse Magazine, Canadian Art
magazine, the Georgia Straight, and Pyramid Power. In addition to her solo
practice, she is a founding member of the artist group Norma whose
performance and installation works have been exhibited locally and
internationally since 2002.

Eric Metcalfe is a Vancouver-based artist. Toronto curator Peggy Gale
recently described Metcalfe’s practice as the epitomization of the
avant-garde in Canadian art. In 1973, he co-founded Western Front, a
pioneering artist-run centre in Vancouver. He has exhibited at the
Artspeak, Charles H. Scott Gallery, University of British Columbia, Art
Gallery of Greater Victoria, Vancouver Art Gallery, Whitney Museum of
American Art, among many others. In 2008, his practice was recognized with
a Governor General’s Award.

Alissa Firth-Eagland is a curator, writer and artist based in Vancouver.
She is currently the Director/Curator of Media Arts at the Western Front.

Johan Lundh is an independent curator and writer, dividing his time between
Vancouver and Stockholm, Sweden.

Jenipher Hur & Avery Nabata

FLAB

November 7 - December 19, 2008&#8232;

Closing reception, Friday December 12th, 2008 at 8:00 pm

Curated by Joshua Bartholomew and Robin Selk

Jenipher Hur and Avery Nabata's site-specific installation for the Helen
Pitt Galler Artist Run Centre comments on the production and utility that
the space of the gallery serves. They do this by extending the gallery into
storage space and storage space into the gallery. The space of the gallery
diminishes while the role of the utility closet becomes exaggerated. Their
project is an attempt at substantially questioning the composition of an
exhibition as well as the part-to-part construction of its content.

Avery Nabata: is a fourth-year GFA student at Emily Carr University.

Jenipher Hur: is a fourth-year GFA student at Emily Carr University.

The Comox Valley Art Gallery's public gallery provides an

engaging art and related cultural practices. CVAG’s primary 
concern is to
support the professional development of emerging artists 
through critical
activities that include exhibitions, lectures, publications, events 
and
special projects. Local emerging artists are encouraged to 
submit proposals
and are often invited to exhibit alongside established artists.
ARTS & CRAFT GALLERY MANDATE:
This gallery is an innovative approach to exhibit two-
dimensional artworks
complemented by three-dimensional artworks to achieve a 
combination that
reflects craft as fine art. The manipulation of paint, clay or any 
medium
with the intention to create fine art, is a craft. To 
accommodate this aim
some rules of aesthetics have to be set. An emphasis must be 
on good
presentation so that the gallery appears pleasing as a set piece.
WINDOW GALLERY MANDATE:
The Window Gallery is in place to promote contemporary 
artwork and
community projects that reflect excellence in the arts. This 
exhibiting
area is open to view 24 hours a day and is most suited to three 
dimensional

mainly for the

24. Broken Pencil | Indie Writers Deathmatch | DEC 31
Indie Writers Deathmatch
ENTER NOW TO WIN...
the world's most dangerous short story contest! 

Welcome to the Second Annual Indie Writers Deathmatch!!!

Last year's Deathmatch drew hundreds of thousands of hits to our Online
Arena. Fans from all across the planet voted for their favourite story in
the culture's bloodiest fiction contest. This year's battle should prove to
be just as violent -- and you don't want to be left out! Sharpen those
quills and enter now to secure your spot.

What is an indie writer death match?

Well, it’s the most innovative and interactive fiction contest in North
America. Eight stories are selected from all entries to compete, and then
they go head-to-head, one-on-one, in our Online Arena where the Broken
Pencil readers get to vote on which story they like the best, adding their
own comments and communicating with the writers themselves on their
exclusive blogs.

What's in it for me?

Your $20 entry fee gets your story in the front door of the competition,
and snags you a one-year subscription to Broken Pencil. The winner of the
Deathmatch receives $300 and has their story published in the magazine.
Second, third and fourth prize winners also have their stories published
and receive $100 each.

What happened in last year's contest?
Controversy! A couple of the writers got touchy when it became obvious that
their stories weren't going to get enough votes to win in their rounds, and
publicly released screeds of damnation against the contest itself, their
fellow competing writers, and Broken Pencil. Which, it should be noted,
didn't win them any additional votes. Then there was some site-hacking at
the tail-end of the contest, which we had to step in and correct with a
SUDDEN DEATH BONUS ROUND. It was all a very entertaining spectacle to
witness. Check it out here.

I'm convinced -- how do I enter?

Just head over to brokenpencil.com/deathmatch for full contest details, and
to submit your kick-ass story. Deadline for entries is December 31, 2008..
Indie Writers Deathmatch is an annual event organized by Broken Pencil, the
Magazine of Zine Culture and the Independent Arts. Broken Pencil, PO Box
203, Station P, Toronto, ON M5S 2S7 Canada
