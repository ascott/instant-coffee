Sameer Farooq and Mirjam Linschooten 
White, Steel, Slice, Mask 
Window spaces
September 10, 2016 - January 8, 2017
Bear Claws Salad Hands
Off-site: Yaletown Roundhouse Station, Canada Line 
September 10, 2016 - January 8, 2017

Public reception: Saturday, September 10, 3-6pm
Artists present

Contemporary Art Gallery presents an ambitious new dual venue 
project by collaborators Canadian artist Sameer Farooq and Dutch 
artist Mirjam Linschooten. Interrogating ways in which cultural 
diversity is narrated, two works comprise the commission: White, 
Steel, Slice, Mask in CAG window spaces and Bear Claws Salad 
Hands at Yaletown-Roundhouse Station, Canada Line.

Working together for over a decade, the duo's interdisciplinary 
practice typically instigates community-based models of 
participation in order to reimagine a material record of the present. 
They investigate tactics of cultural representation while utilizing the 
methods of anthropology to examine various forms of collecting, 
interpretation and display.
Over the past year Farooq and Linschooten have undertaken a 
series of cumulative research trips to Vancouver via our Burrard 
Marina Field House Studio Residency Program. Integral to this and 
core to informing the new commission were workshops at the 
Museum of Anthropology with the Native Youth Program (NYP) 
involving a group of indigenous youth from Greater Vancouver. 
Farooq and Linschooten invited participants to consider their 
personal narratives in relation to displays at the museum.

Bear Claws Salad Hands at Yaletown-Roundhouse Station 
repurposes found language from local souvenir shops that sell a 
plethora of Canadian "knick-knacks", cultural reproductions which 
capitalize on perceptions of Vancouver's identity. Through the 
simple act of selecting and reproducing the language of these 
stores' inventory lists, Farooq and Linschooten highlight the active 
commodification of culture which reinforces hierarchy, difference 
and discrimination.

Across the window spaces at CAG, White, Steel, Slice, Mask are 
collections of mass-produced cultural objects purchased from 
shops across the lower mainland, referencing and perhaps 
representative of, Vancouver's largest immigrant communities. 
Replicating yet also subverting the supposed objective aesthetic of 
museum vitrines, display mechanisms such as shelves, hooks and 
bars are used to fracture the objects, disrupting their form and 
meticulous arrangement while suggesting the often uneasy 
relations between the conserved and custodian, artifact and 
everyday object, and revealing the unintended violence of display.

Generously supported by the BC Arts Council Innovations Program, 
the Mondriaan Fund, Ontario Arts Council and the Hamber 
Foundation. Bear Claws Salad Hands is presented by the 
Contemporary Art Gallery in partnership with the Canada Line 
Public Art Program - IntransitBC.

Farooq and Linschooten have exhibited in Belgium, Canada, China, 
Egypt, France, Montenegro, Morocco, Netherlands, Serbia, Spain, 
Switzerland and Turkey. Recent projects include The Figure in the 
Carpet, Blackwood Gallery, Toronto (2015); Faux Guide, Trankat, 
Morocco and Something old, something new, something borrowed 
and
something blue, Artellewa, Cairo (2014); The Museum of Found 
Objects, Art Gallery of Ontario, Toronto and Sanat Limani, Istanbul 
(2011-10).
