Vancouver Art Gallery to Survey What's Happening
NOW in British Columbia Art

Sprawling exhibition showcases the work of more than 30 of the province's
most compelling artists

February 5 @ 9AM - Media are invited to preview the exhibition and to meet
the artists

VANCOUVER, BC - After more than 120 studio visits, the Vancouver Art
Gallery has assembled an exhibition of some of the most compelling new work
being produced in the province. How Soon Is Now: Contemporary Art from
Here, on view from February 7 to May 3, 2009, brings together 34 British
Columbia artists working in sculpture, painting, video, audio,
architectural interventions, site-specific projects, performances and
events. The exhibition puts a finger on the pulse of the province's
contemporary art world to provide a fresh snapshot of its most recent
developments.

"It is exciting for the Gallery to look out into British Columbia's
thriving artistic communities and bring together this massive celebration
of the here and now," said Vancouver Art Gallery director, Kathleen
Bartels. "There is a tremendous level of excitement and anticipation in the
Gallery for this exhibition. Almost all of the work being presented has
never been exhibited in Vancouver and much of it is being created
specifically for this show. We are pleased to be the catalyst for what
promises to be another important moment for the visual arts in our province."

Taking a cue from a song by English rock band The Smiths, the exhibition
title evokes a central characteristic of the work being exhibited-a sense
of immediacy that speaks to the present moment. Bringing together divergent
artistic practices, the exhibition focuses on artworks that privilege
events over objects, process over product and interaction over
contemplation. Organized according to connections between works rather than
a single theme, How Soon Is Now identifies a number of recurring motifs and
subjects, including the recording studio, spaces of social interaction,
architecture, the unconscious, literature, and a sense of experimentation
and risk.

The exhibition offers an opportunity to experience British Columbia's
impressive range of artistic approaches. Father and son team Jim, Cedric
and Nathan Bomford will build an elaborate two-story architectural
installation using wood salvaged from their relatives' yards on Vancouver
and Bowen Islands. Jackson 2bears will present a hip-hop-based video
performance challenging racism and Native stereotypes. Samuel Roy-Bois will
construct a soundproof recording booth where visitors can jam on
instruments, with their music piped into the Gallery's lobby. Holly Ward
will create a mobile sculpture consisting of a pile of soil that is moved
regularly by volunteers. Mark Soo will create an audio-based work that
approximates the sound of a neighbour's music being heard through the wall
of an apartment. Kristi Malakoff will create a massive star on the floor of
the Gallery's lobby with tape used by stage actors to block scenes. Abbas
Akhavan will install speakers in the trees at the Gallery's entrance that
will greet visitors with the sound of European Starlings. Antonia Hirsch
will create a wall installation using convex surveillance mirrors to spell
out the words "the invisible hand" in Braille. Aaron Carpenter will create
a flag for the Gallery's Georgia Plaza flagpole that has not flown a flag
in years. Christian Kliegel will create a number of elevator doors that
lead nowhere. Kevin Schmidt will present a screening/performance on a boat
floating down the Fraser River. These are just a sampling of the
innovative, often ironic and interactive works that will be on display.

"I am absolutely thrilled to be showing my work as a part of How Soon Is
Now. It's a real honour to be recognized as part of the stellar group
selected for the show," said Nelson based artist Kristi Malakoff. "British
Columbia's art scene is rock solid and has an amazing international
reputation for its energy and diversity. This exhibition will provide a
wonderful opportunity to experience the exciting contemporary art being
produced in the region right now."

Spilling beyond the spatial constrains of a traditional exhibition, How
Soon is Now places artworks inside and outside of gallery spaces, on
ceilings and floors, in alcoves, on the Gallery grounds, at the entrance,
in the windows, in the lobby and behind walls. Gallery spaces will be
platforms for a range of transformative, social, sensory, introspective and
political experiences. The exhibition invites viewers to rethink their idea
of the Gallery through a number of site specific works, as well as
performances and events that emphasize viewer interaction.

Artists in the exhibition include: Jackson 2bears, Abbas Akhavan, Sonny
