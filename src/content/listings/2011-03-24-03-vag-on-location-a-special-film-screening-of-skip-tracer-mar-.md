Thursday March 24, 7pm

ON LOCATION: A SPECIAL FILM SCREENING OF SKIP TRACER

Presented in conjunction with the Pacific Cinematheque / located at 
1131
Howe St

10 dollars 50 cents, regular / 9 dollars, seniors and students

In conjunction with the Vancouver Art Gallery's exhibition WE: 
Vancouver -
12 Manifestos for the City, writer and critic Michael Turner presents a
special screening of SKIP TRACER, Zale Dalen’s legendary 
1977 feature about
a repo man, shot on location in Vancouver and Richmond. Also on 
the program
are two shorts by Turner, followed by a Q+A period and discussion.

Between 1949 and 2011, 167 feature films were shot in the 
Vancouver area.
Some of these films were set in the city (The Sweet and the Bitter, 
1967),
while some were set elsewhere (Rumble in the Bronx, 1995). Over 
the years,
film production has had a strong impact on Vancouver, conflating our
understanding of the reality of the city with its filmic representation.
Indeed, there is a perceived anxiety about a city that spends more 
time
standing in for other cities than it does in playing itself. Turner’s
extensive research into film in Vancouver comprises one aspect of 
the
city’s past that continues to resonate today: its filmic image that 
is
projected beyond the city boundaries to elsewhere.

Turner is a Vancouver-based writer of fiction, criticism and song, and
founder of Arsenal Pulp Press. Recent publications include 8x10 
and The
Pornographers Poem. Turner has also worked in collaboration with 
artist
Stan Douglas on two experimental-video screenplays, titled Journey 
Into
Fear (Istanbul Biennial, 2001) and Suspiria (Documenta XI, 2002), 
as well
as on a screenplay with filmmaker Bruce LaBruce, titled Untitled Von
Gloeden Project, based on the life and work of photographer 
Wilhelm Von
Gloeden. In 1996 his book Hard Core Logo was adapted into a film 
direct by
Brice McDonald
