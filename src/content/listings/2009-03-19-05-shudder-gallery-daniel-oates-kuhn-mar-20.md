True Stories of Falseworks
Daniel Oates-Kuhn

Shudder Gallery
433 Columbia St.
Vancouver, British Columbia, Canada 
www.shuddergallery.com
604 488 5477
Friday, March 20th, 2009 - Saturday, April 11th, 2009
Opening Reception: Friday, March 20th, 2009 at 7:00 PM - 11PM

Falsework is identified as any temporary structure or article used to
support a permanent structure while it is not self-supporting. This also
includes structures or articles which provide for a safe working
environment within the permanent structure's immediate vicinity. However,
in the exhibition "True Stories of Falseworks" Daniel Oates-Kuhn aims to
extend this definition to incorporate any temporary form employed in the
construction of something permanent, from the cautiously engineered and
designed latticework of scaffolding, to the most arbitrary and precarious
instances of transitional architecture, like a piece of DayGlo orange
ribbon haphazardly lassoed around several pylons.

Although these configurations are often arbitrarily assembled, ignored,
avoided, eventually dismantled, and forgotten, they are formally complex
and aesthetically beguiling arrangements, inanimate elucidations of the
relationship between existing and potential construction. They are tales of
the body coping with instability, doubt, gravity and by-laws. This
falsework is performed with a level of intuition and capriciousness which
rarely occurs within structured and systematic environments. Oates-Kuhn
chooses to not only venerate these arrangements through indexical
investigations, documentation and meticulous reconstruction, but to also
repurpose them. What are normally understood as obstructions or impediments
of a path become diversions from it; areas of refuge, congregation and
random occurrences. These sculptural forms are attempts to instigate
discovery and promote inquiry into the phenomenological incidents within
the prevailing tautological nature of built environments.

This falsework, which enacts such ambient and overlooked functions, has the
ability to inform how we perceive and experience permanent space. 
Oates-Kuhn's manipulations are attempts to illustrate the unintelligible
correlation between stasis and transition, to reevaluate preconceived
notions about the ergonomics and aesthetics of the architecture that
contains us, and the infrastructure that transports us.
