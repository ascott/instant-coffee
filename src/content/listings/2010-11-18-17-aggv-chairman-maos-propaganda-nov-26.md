Chairman Maos Propaganda on Display at the AGGV

Victoria, BC-The Peoples Republic of China and the legacy of Chairman Mao
Zedong will be examined in an innovative new exhibition at the Art Gallery
of Greater Victoria. Charged with depicting the ideal of a better life
artists created thousands of propaganda posters from the 1920s through to
the 1980s.

Communist Paradise: Chinese Propaganda Posters will feature more than 70
posters from the personal collections of AGGV Curator of Asian Arts Barry
Till and UVIC Professor of Pacific and Asian Studies Richard King. The
exhibition opens on November 26 and runs through February 6, 2011.

Till and King began their collections of propaganda posters while living in
China during the late 1970s as exchange students. Both Richard and I
collected them because they offered a good snapshot of what was happening
in China while we were studying there, said Till. The posters were
extremely cheap at the time, you could buy them for about five to ten
cents. The dorm I stayed in China had plain whitewashed walls, so I
covered my room in posters to brighten things up.

With the formation of the Peoples Republic of China in 1949 Mao Zedong and
the Communist Party used posters as the primary source of communication to
a largely illiterate audience in pre-television China. Many of the
countries most talented artists and designers were hired by the various
government and party organizations that were responsible for propaganda
posters.

The posters were used to send messages of economic prosperity to the
peasants in the countryside and the workers in the industrial sector. The
men and women depicted are shown hardy and healthy with big smiles on their
faces, happily pursuing their work for a better life, Till added.

Depictions of an unselfish life in the service of the party, the community
and the country are another theme of the posters. Peoples faces in the
posters either show happiness and friendliness towards each other or
determination and hatefulness towards U.S. and British imperialism, as well
as towards the "Four Old Evils", old ideas, old culture, old customs and
old habits, commented Till.
