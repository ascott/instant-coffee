Gordon Winiemko
They Came Together to Perform Heroic Gestures (in the manner that was meaningful
to them)
Exhibition Jan 30th, - March 06, 2021

"Ive never been so disappointed to find that my work is timely or relevant"
Gordon Winiemko January, 6, 2020

Through the juxtaposition of two milieus (which already have some points of overlap),
Winiemko frames political protest as a performative spectacle and the assumption of
the guise of a costumed hero as political. In this way, Winiemkos work presages and
illuminates the storming of the Washington Capitol by a costumed hoard of would-be
cospatriots.

Winiemkos practice is an exploration of the relationship of the personal to the
public, of
the construction of subjectivity in relation to the cultural framework " those things we
do whether they be institutionalized to the idiosyncratic, which can be so
commonplace that we often forget how they shape our lives, for better or worse.
Winiemko fluidly moves between mediums, often taking on the role of performer both
to use the figure the artist as a signifier of subjectivity and to perform those things we
do, often in ways that subvert those practices or re-present them in the form of
interruption or intervention.

Collaboration is an important part of Winiemkos practice as a means to foreground
and model social interaction, inviting the public at large to participate in the re-
examination of otherwise transparent social structures. The moving image and the
screen and the outsized role both play in the construction and dissemination of cultural
frameworks is a central focus of Winiemkos practice. By framing performative actions
and social interactions within the
screen and by appropriating and remixing tropes from
a variety of screen-based media, Winiemko sets the stage for a discussion and
examination of the social sphere.
