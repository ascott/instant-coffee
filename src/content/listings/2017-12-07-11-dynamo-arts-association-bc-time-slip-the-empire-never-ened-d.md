2 - 4 pm Sunday Dec 10th

John Cussans is organizing an informal gathering to discuss a project he has been 
working on called BC time-Slip: The Empire never Ended. He would like to re-situate it 
back in the space where much of it was filmed to emphasize the time-slip thematic. Here 
is the link to the project material.
http://bctimeslip.skullcrackersuite.org/

He will be showing some of the edits of the material that he shot last summer in order to 
start a conversation of where, if anywhere, the Skullcracker project might go next. The 
main question is what, if any, practical "decolonizing" value could art about historical 
settler-indigenous relations in British Columbia have when it's made by foreign artists, 
and exhibited in largely metropolitan art centres? 

JOHN: "Im also very practically confused about the contemporary politics of cultural 
appropriation relative to a (largely) un-invited desire to make the situation, struggles and 
resurgence of Native populations and culture in BC known to a wider international 
audience. I understood that this was part of Beau Dick's wider vision. And that's why we 
started the project in the first place (i.e. on the basis of Beau telling Steve Calvert that 
he wanted more people to know about the political significance and resurgence of the 
Kwakwakwa'wakw potlatch culture, and it's significance for global indigenous and 
ecological activism). But what now, without the blessing and concrete alliance with 
members of local indigenous communities can we do to continue this project? "
