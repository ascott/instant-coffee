EXHIBITION: THE ART OF MILDRED VALLEY THORNTON
FROM THE COLLECTION NUYTTEN NORTHWEST COAST COLLECTION

Saturday, April 13 through Saturday, April 27, 2024
Location: Uno Langmann Limited, 2117 Granville Street, Vancouver

Uno Langmann Limited is pleased to present a collection of 32 works by Canadian
Artist Mildred Valley Thornton, hung
alongside the Nuytten Northwest Coast Collection of entrepreneur and author Phil
Nuytten (1941 -2023). Collected by
Nuytten over many years, often as gifts to his wife, the works represent a rare cross-
section of Thorntons life and
includes
both watercolours and oils. Subjects range from indigenous portraits to landscapes
and historical works documenting both
indigenous communities and the Canadian landscape. Phil Nuytten, author of The
Totem Carvers describing the life of Ellen
Neel and her family, had an interested in Thorntons work due to the connection
between the two artists who had much in common,
both passionate about advocating for women artists and preserving the traditional
arts of the aboriginal people. Many works in the
exhibition are illustrated in The Life and Art of Mildred Valley Thornton, the
comprehensive biography published in 2011 by Sheryl
Salloum as part of The Unheralded Artists of BC series. In an excerpt from
Thorntons biography Uno Langmann describes his
appreciation of Thorntons technique:
There are very few people who can paint in watercolours and oils. There are very
few people who can span landscape and go into
portraiture... Her work is full of life. She has a wonderful palette,
which you do not
see with many other painters. There is
cheerfulness to her work and immediacy in her paintings.
Mildred Valley Thornton (Canadian 1890-1967), born Mildred Valley Stinson in 1890,
gained renown for her artistic portrayal of
First Nations communities across Saskatchewan, Alberta, and British Columbia. Her
work encompassed portraits, landscapes,
mythological, and historical subjects, capturing the essence of Indigenous culture
and traditions. Thornton's dedication to preserving
vanishing Indigenous cultures led her to travel extensively, often to remote regions,
where she meticulously documented community
life through her vibrant paintings. She was not only an artist but also a fervent
advocate for Indigenous and women's rights,
contributing to various art and culture groups throughout her life. After marrying John
Henry Thornton, Mildred settled in Regina in
1913 before relocating to Vancouver during the Depression. There, she continued
her active involvement in the art
and culture
communities of both Saskatchewan and British Columbia, teaching painting and
exhibiting widely. Additionally, she served as an art
critic for the Vancouver Sun and contributed to The Native Voice, the first Indigenous
newspaper. Mildred's passion for preserving
Indigenous arts and advocating for women artists was evident in her friendship with
Ellen Neel, another prominent artist. Together,
they championed the cause of women artists and worked towards preserving
traditional Indigenous arts. Mildred's legacy extends
beyond her art, encompassing her tireless efforts to promote cultural understanding
and equality.
Alongside the collection of Mildred Valley Thornton paintings is exhibited The
Northwest Coast Collection of Phil Nuytten.
Nuytten was a legend and a pioneer in many fields " underwater explorer and sub-
sea engineer, inventor, tech manufacturer,
businessman, songwriter, carver, author, native advocate, and avid collector. The
exhibition is split between a selling
exhibition
and works destined for two British Columbia institutions, and for sale are exceptional
examples including early works, as
well as modern artists including Robert Davidson, Beau Dick, Ellen Neel, Bill Holm,
John Livingston, Bill Reid, Doug
Cranmer, Don Smith (Lelooska), Joe Seaweed, Dorothy Grant, Henry Hunt, and
more. The works intended for institutions
include an impressive and rare collection of works by Charlie James, Mungo Martin,
and Ellen Neel that Phil Nuytten
assembled throughout his career. The exhibition and sale are available to see at
2117 Granville Street, Vancouver, through
Saturday, April 27, 2024. The collection is posted for viewing online at
www.langmann.com.
