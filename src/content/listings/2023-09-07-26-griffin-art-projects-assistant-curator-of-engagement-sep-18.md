Assistant Curator of Engagement

PTE: 3 days per week + 7.5 hrs, 9:00-5:00PM + Flex hours (One Year Contract -
renewable)

Start date: September 30, 2023

Deadline for Applications: September 18, 2023

Salary: $26.50/hour

Griffin Art Projects is seeking an innovative and dynamic thinker who can help to build

audiences and excitement through the development and delivery of public programs
and a

residency program that capture the publics imagination and expands the experience of
art and art galleries.

Griffin is a non-profit art gallery and residency located in North Vancouver. GRIFFIN is
a non-

collecting
institution that has quickly become a vibrant contributor to the North
Vancouver

cultural landscape. Griffin is offering a one-year renewable contract for the role of
Assistant Curator of Engagement.

The primary tasks of the position will be to assist in the coordination and delivery of
Griffin

residency and public programs. The successful applicant will assist in the design of
programs

that offer unique opportunities for visitors of all ages and levels of interest to engage
with art and culture at GRIFFIN. This position will be responsible for the coordination,
development and delivery of public programming for all ages. Griffins residency and
public programs support artists in their work and encourage new audiences to
participate, generating knowledge and innovative thinking on contemporary art and
culture. The ambition of the program is the creation of dynamic engagement and
learning opportunities for a wide range of visitors and attendees whose familiarity with
contemporary
art may differ.

This role will engage with best practices in pedagogy and public engagement and offer
a unique opportunity to create a meaningful program that integrates deeper learning
and participation, involving high schools, higher education institutions, the local,
national and international arts

community, and the general public, and liaising with artists, thinkers, and innovators
from

various disciplines to support Griffin in producing innovative and impactful learning and
public

engagement.

Reporting to the Director, the Assistant Curator of Engagement will also work with
Griffins

Adjunct Curator, contract curators and other stakeholders to produce a schedule of
public

programs, events and community outreach activities that animate, complement, and
enhance

our exhibitions and residency program, and GRIFFINs exploration of art collecting and

collections practices and methodologies.

Duties include: assisting with the design and conceptual development, administration
and

implementation of adult and youth focused public programs including but not limited to:
artists

talks and tours, lectures, workshops, film series, conferences and performances. The
Assistant Curator of Engagement will also manage and prepare grants, assist with the
development and

Griffin Art Projects coordination of special community-based partnerships and
education initiatives and related special projects. This is a renewable one year, part-
time three day per week position with 7.5 flex hours occurring throughout the week set
at the discretion of the Coordinator in consultation with the Director, with regularly
scheduled work taking place from Thursday to Saturday, 9:00 am to 5:00 pm, and flex
hours on selected Sundays for program delivery. This position includes some evening
hours, as required to fulfill responsibilities and be on hand for gallery programs.

Qualifications

The successful candidate will possess a Bachelors or Masters degree in art or art
history,

museum or
curatorial studies, thorough knowledge of Canadian and international art
history and issues in contemporary art.

It is essential that the candidate have a minimum of 2 years work experience designing
and

administering public programs in a museum, public art gallery and/or artist run centre
context,

as well as demonstrated experience in successful program development and delivery
and

working directly with artists. Grant writing experience is also an asset.

Exceptional written, presentation and interpersonal communication skills are required.

Experience working successfully with internal and external partners including artists,
educators, curators, producers, interns and academics is essential. This position
requires the ability to problem solve and work on multiple time-sensitive deadlines and
tasks independently and accurately. The role requires the candidate to manage the
program on budget, while also keeping statistics, documentation, and monitoring. Must
be able to work with
youth as well as adults, with the relevant clearances.

The successful candidate will be an effective communicator, both orally and in writing
and bring networks and understanding of the local and regional arts community. Strong
organizational and administrative skills and the ability to work under general direction
within a small team environment are key, as is the ability to take initiative and be a self-
starter. Professional experience developing programs for youth and post-secondary
district school systems is highly recommended. Knowledge of marketing and audience
development in the arts, and fluency in an additional language(s) are considered
assets.

Please apply with a cover letter and resume by September 18, 2023, by email to
info@griffinartprojects.ca with the subject line "Assistant Curator of Engagement".

OPENING | Griffin Art Projects | SHIFT: Ecologies of Fashion, Form + Textile | SEPT 15
| 6-8PM

SHIFT: Ecologies of Fashion, Form + Textile

Dates: September 16 "
December 10, 2023

Location: Griffin Art Projects, 1174 Welch St, North Vancouver, BC V7P 1B2

Opening Reception: September 15, 6:00-8:00PM

www.griffinartprojects.ca

SHIFT: Ecologies of Fashion, Form + Textile addresses the intersection of visual art,
fashion and textiles, to consider these practices as worldmaking forms of knowledge
and a human encounter that comprises every aspect of daily life. Including a residency,
conference, and works by China Adams, Jason Dodge, Olaf Holzapfel, Brian Jungen,
Annette Kelm, Christiane Lohr, Medrie MacPhee, Manuel Mathieu, Meret Oppenheim,
Wolfgang Tillmans, and Janet Werner, among others, SHIFT considers the ways in
which power, colonialism and consumerism are expressed within fashion and proposes
forms for an emancipating futurity.

This exhibition is generously supported by the Michael and Inna O'Brian Family
Foundation.
