The Burnaby Art Gallery is thrilled to host an Exhibition Storytelling at the BAG series
in response to the exhibition of Roy Henry Vickers. Storytelling within the main
gallery will occur on the first Sunday of every month.

To begin this series, Roy Henry Vickers and Lucky Budd will join us on February 4
from 1:00 pm to 1:45 pm and 2:30 -3:15 pm. These two sessions are open to all
ages.

Roy Henry Vickers is a renowned
Indigenous artist of Tsimshian, Haida, Heiltsuk,
and English ancestry, with over six decades of printmaking expertise. Born in the
northern village of Laxgaltsap (Greenville), Vickers has spent a lifetime drawing
inspiration from the Gitxsan, Wetsuweten, Gitxaala, Tla-o-qui-aht, and Lkwungen
traditional territories he has called home. Vickers studied traditional First Nations art
and design at the Gitanmaax School of Northwest Coast Indian Art in Hazelton, and
in 1986 opened his own gallery in Tofino, set within a traditional northwest coast
longhouse.

Lucky Budd holds an MA in history, focusing on Oral History, from the University of
Victoria and has digitized many high-profile oral history collections including that of
the Nisgaa First Nation. In addition to co-authoring the award-winning First West
Coast Book series and Northwest Coast Legends series, he is also the author of
Voices of British Columbia (Douglas & McIntyre, 2010)"a bestseller which was
shortlisted for the 2011 Bill
Duthie Booksellers Choice Award"and its sequel, Echoes
of British Columbia (2014). He currently lives in Victoria, BC.

This storytelling event is a free drop-in program with no pre-registration required.
Follow the Burnaby Art Gallery on Instagram to stay tuned about upcoming
storytelling events.
