Black Lives Matter Memorial Art Exhibition Debuts in Vancouver

The Black Lives Matter Memorial Project, dedicated to honoring Black lives lost to
racial injustice and police violence, will be held at Alternatives Gallery in Vancouver
from July 26 to August 2, 2023.

The exhibition features powerful artworks by renowned artists Dr. Syrus Marcus
Ware (Toronto), Maestro Jason Ikeem Rodgers of Orchestra Noir (Atlanta), and Toni
Latour (Vancouver). The exhibit aims to shed light on the systemic racism that has
led to the tragic deaths of Black individuals in both Canada and the United States.

Black lives are inherently valuable. We need to all fight to ensure that every Black
person gets to live long enough to become an elder. These incredible human beings
depicted here should be alive and with us today. We fight for abolition and Black
liberation everyday to help usher in a freer future " one where
Black safety and
Black life is assured, says Dr. Ware.

The project has been three years in the making, and is funded by the Canada
Council for the Arts, the B.C. Arts Council, and the Vancouver Biennale.

The exhibition comprises three components:

The first component showcases ten portraits drawn by Dr. Syrus Marcus Ware,
depicting Black individuals who lost their lives at the hands of law enforcement.
Accompanying each portrait is a text panel describing the person's full and rich life
and the circumstances surrounding their untimely death. The panels are
complemented by analog clocks moving forward and reverse, and screens
displaying digital life-long clocks counting forward from birth to death and backward
from death to birth. This poignant display emphasizes the injustice of lives cut short
by racist police forces and the oppressive systems that perpetuate them.

The second component features black stone slabs engraved with the names, birth
and death dates, and ages of over 400
Black people killed by police since 2012.

The third component of the exhibition is the video installation titled "black elegy."
Maestro Jason Ikeem Rodgers, founder of Atlanta-based Orchestra Noir, composed
an original piece of music specifically for this project. The video recording of the
performance by his all-Black orchestra, capturing the emotions of the composition,
will be on display during the exhibition.

OPENING RECEPTION
The Black Lives Matter Memorial Project offers free admission to the public, and an
opening reception will take place on July 29 from 3-5 p.m. with refreshments and
food from Riddim & Spice on Commercial Drive. The reception will feature guest
speaker Krystal Paraboo, lead curator for the Black Strathcona Resurgence Project
with Vancouver Mural Festival. Renee Austin will contribute insights on racial justice
and commemoration and Phabion Sutherland will offer an Indigenous welcome and
blessing.

FEATURED ARTISTS:
Dr. Syrus Marcus Ware (he/him)is a Vanier
Scholar, visual artist, activist, curator, and
educator living and working in Toronto, ON. Using drawing, installation, and
performance, Syrus explores social justice frameworks and Black activist culture. His
work has been shown widely, including solo shows at Grunt Gallery in 2018 and Wil
Aballe Art Projects in 2021 (Irresistible Revolutions). His work has been featured as
part of the inaugural Toronto Biennial of Art in 2019 in conjunction with the Ryerson
Image Centre (Antarctica and Ancestors, Do You Read Us? (Dispatches from the
Future)), as well as for the Toronto Bentways Safety in Public Spaces Initiative in
2020 (Radical Love). Syrus has participated in group shows at the Never Apart in
Montreal, the Art Gallery of Ontario, the University of Lethbridge Art Gallery, the Art
Gallery of York University, the Art Gallery of Windsor, and as part of the curated
content at Nuit Blanche 2017 (The Stolen People; Won't Back Down). His
performance works have been part of festivals
across Canada, including Cripping
The Stage (Harbourfront Centre, 2016 & 2019), Complex Social Change (University
of Lethbridge Art Gallery, 2015) and Decolonizing and Decriminalizing Trans Genres
(University of Winnipeg, 2015).

Jason Ikeem Rodgers (he/him) is a renowned Atlanta-based conductor, composer,
and arranger. He is the Founder and Music Director of Orchestra Noir, the Atlanta
African-American Orchestra. He began his conducting career as an apprentice to
John Mauceri, the founding director of the Hollywood Bowl Orchestra and protg of
Leonard Bernstein. Since then, Maestro Rodgers has achieved international
recognition, winning prestigious awards such as first prize in the London Classical
Soloists Competition and the International Conducting Competition in the United
States. He has conducted orchestras in various locations, including Europe and
North America, and made his debut with the Orchestra Di Toscana Classica in Italy.
His word has been exhibited at the Atlanta High
Museum of Art and he has gained
notoriety for his arrangements, including one for hip-hop artist 2Chainz's song
Forgiven featured on The Tonight Show Starring Jimmy Fallon. He has also
contributed to Oprah's OWN Network and the Amazon's series Harlem.

Maestro Rodgers holds degrees in Orchestral Conducting from the Cleveland
Institute of Music and the University of North Carolina School of the Arts, where he
studied under notable mentors such as Carl Topilow, Louis Lane, and Serge
Zehnacker. Additionally, he has a Bachelor's Degree in Piano Performance from the
University of North Carolina School of the Arts, where he was mentored by Clifton
Matthews.

Toni Latour (she/her) is a queer femme white settler and second generation Italian
Canadian artist. Toni is both a visual artist and professor. She works in
photography, installation, text-based practices, drawing, video, sound, public and
performance art.
Toni received her BFA from the University of Windsor in 1998 and her MFA from the
University of Western Ontario in 2000. Her work has included social commentary,
public art work, and large-scale installation work that concerns itself with
representation within the queer/trans/non-binary communities in Coast Salish
Territory (Vancouver). Toni has exhibited her work nationally and internationally in
both solo and group shows since 1994. Her work is held in private and public
collections, including the National Portrait Gallery of Canada, the Surrey Art Gallery,
and the Vancouver Biennale. She is the Non-Regular Union Executive
Representative at the University of the Fraser Valley where she advocates for labour
rights and equity. Toni is also an active member of UFVs RAN (Racism and Anti-
Racism Network) and the SOCA: EDI Committee (School of Creative Arts: Equity,
Diversity and Inclusion). Toni is also a single mother to an Autistic neurodivergent
daughter and they live together on the unceded territory of the Musqueam,
Squamish, and Tsleil-Waututh Nations,
colonially known as Vancouver.

The Black LIves Matter Memorial Project is grateful to its exhibition sponsors: the
