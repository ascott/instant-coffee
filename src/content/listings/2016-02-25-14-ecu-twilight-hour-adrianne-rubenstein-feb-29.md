Twilight Hour Artist Talk

Monday, February 29 at 4:30 PM

Room 410B, South Building, Emily Carr University

Adrianne Rubenstein makes paintings whose starting points are oblique
memories of adolescent self-righteousness and fluttering identity. Images
start out with a craving and are rendered ambiguously, hopelessly. Layers
of paint and scuffling brushwork recall the swirling pattern from an old
friend's duvet cover, an earring she coveted from a downtown kiosk in the
90s located on eBay in 2015, or simply a window at night, with a branch.
The images aim to replicate the aches of tweenhood that go unarticulated
until decades later when the smell of an old closet makes it all flood back
into the chest. Together the works excavate the home she holds in her
mind's eye - transformed by dreams and envisioned anew.

Adrianne Rubenstein is a New York-based artist from Montreal, Canada. She
earned her BFA at the Nova Scotia College of Art and Design in 2006 and her
MFA from the San Francisco Art Institute in 2011. Adrianne has exhibited
her work at Galerie Bernard Ceysson, Luxembourg; CANADA, New York; Derek
Eller, New York; David Petersen, Minneapolis; et al., San Francisco; and
Field Contemporary, Vancouver. Upcoming exhibitions include White Columns,
New York, and Harper's Books, New York. Curatorial projects include
Maraschino at Fourteen30 Contemporary, Portland; If you throw a spider out
the window, does it break? at Brennan & Griffin, New York; Snail Salon at
Regina Rex, New York; and Forget About the Sweetbreads co-curated with
Joanne Greenbaum at James Fuentes, New York. Adrianne is the director of
CANADA, New York.

Adrianne is curating and showing in the exhibition A Short Story with Al
Freeman, Chris Hood and Monique Mouton at Field Contemporary (February 27 -
March 26, 2016).
