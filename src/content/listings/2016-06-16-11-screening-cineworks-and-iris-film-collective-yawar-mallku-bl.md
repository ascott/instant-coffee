Yawar Mallku / Blood of The Condor
Tuesday Jun 21; 9:30pm - 11:00pm
Falaise Park Fieldhouse, 3434 Falaise Avenue, Vancouver
FREE

A rare 16mm feature presentation for National Aboriginal Day in 
Canada,Blood of the Condor (Quechua: Yawar Mallku, Spanish: 
Sangre de cndor) is a 1969 Bolivian film by director Jorge Sanjins. 
The film tells the story of an indigenous Bolivian community 
receiving medical care from the Peace Corps-like American agency 
Cuerpo del Progreso (Progress Corps) which is secretly sterilising 
local indigenous women.

Sanjins' Yawar Mallku is thought to have led to the expulsion of the 
Peace Corps from Bolivia in an act of anti-imperialist cultural 
nationalism by the indigenous people. After showings of Yawar 
Mallku, Sanjins learned that many Indigenous people had criticism 
about the difficulty of his films due to the use of flashback narration 
to address Indigenous issues -a form influenced by European art 
cinema, difficult to read for those uninitiated in its conventions. 
Sanjins took this on board in later films, leaving Yawar Mallku to 
stand as an important, if culturally miscalculated attempt
to raise the profile of indigenous issues as a matter of global social
justice under ongoing colonialism.

Iris Film Collective is a Vancouver-based group of independent 
artists creating, exhibiting and touring film-based works single 
channel, expanded, sculptural, installation with the goal of 
increasing the visibility and accessibility of experimental media art. 
Above all, their interest in cin filmactual celluloidat a time when this
