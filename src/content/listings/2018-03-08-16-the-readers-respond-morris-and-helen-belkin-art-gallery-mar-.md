The Readers Respond
Morris and Helen Belkin Art Gallery
Wednesday, March 14, 2018
2-3pm

Presentations by Julia Aoki, Brenna Bezanson, and Cicely Blain, moderated by 
Alexandra Bischoff

Celebrating the excessive abundance of the archive, "Beginning with the Seventies: 
GLUT" is concerned with language, depictions of the woman reader as an artistic genre 
and the potential of reading as performed resistance. Central to the exhibition, 
"Rereading Room" is a reconstruction of the Vancouver Womens Bookstore (1973-
1996) in the second iteration of a project by Alexandra Bischoff.

Rereading Room (2016-18) is activated by "The Readers" (2018); a performance 
orchestrated by Bischoff wherein thirteen trans- and cis-women, and non-binary artists, 
authors, and activists have been invited to occupy the installation for the duration of the 
exhibition. They work with and against the inventory by reading, annotating and 
supplementing the collection to form a dossier of responses.

The Readers are given an opportunity to respond to their time in the Gallery by 
participating in the monthly series The Readers Respond. They may share excerpts 
from the installations available books, read a text that theyve authored, screen a 
related video, or moderate a group discussion surrounding topics that have become 
important to them during their time in Rereading Room.

Events occur at the Morris and Helen Belkin Art Gallery monthly throughout the 
exhibition, from January - April 2018.

--

Julia Aoki is the General Manager of VIVO Media Arts Centre and maintains a practice 
as a writer, researcher and organizer. Her writing on spatial narratives and marginal 
histories can be found in TOPIA, Space and Culture and most recently Communication, 
Culture, and Making Meaning in the City, a collection published by Lexington Books. 
She aspires in her work to find the policed boundaries of collective imagination, then 
carefully posit or guess wildly at what might be beyond them.

Brenna Bezanson identifies as an intersectional feminist, an activist, and an imperfect 
ally with a lot to learn and even more to unlearn. She is a settler on stolen land, a 
woman, a trans person, a retired sex worker, a seamstress, a survivor, and a wannabe 
creative type. Growing up in rural Nova Scotia, her first introduction to nonprofit work 
began at the age of 15, serving on the board of directors for The Red Door Sexual 
Health Clinic, leveraging that position to form the first LGBT youth support group in her 
home county. Since that time her work has included frontline support, governance, 
management and capacity building. Most recently she has been working as a private 
contractor and consultant for multiple projects with Vancouver Coastal Health on topics 
of peer engagement, inclusion, equity, and striving to create safer spaces. When she's 
not working she can usually be found somewhere, saying something sarcastic.

Cicely Blain is a writer, facilitator, activist and artist from London, UK. Cicelys passion 
rests in social justice through anti-oppressive education. Through their company, Cicely 
Blain Consulting, they offer a range of services that aim to inspire, empower and 
encourage participants to make change within their communities for greater inclusivity, 
accessibility and justice. Cicelys experience stems from their work as a founder of Black 
Lives Matter, Vancouver; a political organization instrumental in changing conversations 
about racial justice in British Columbia and part of a larger movement that is shaping the 
future of Black liberation. With years of experience working within LGBTQ and 
marginalized communities, Cicely wants to support people and organizations to create 
meaningful change for their students/clients/employees. Among other achievements, 
Cicely is the 2017 winner of the CCPA Power of Youth Leadership Award in Social 
Movement Building.

Alexandra Bischoff is an Edmonton-born performance artist, researcher and writer, who 
is obsessed with obscure intimacies. She holds a BFA in Visual Art from Emily Carr 
University and was named the valedictorian of her 2015 undergraduate class. Recently, 
she has performed and exhibited with the Morris and Helen Belkin Art Gallery 
(Vancouver), Unit 17 (Vancouver), IKG Live (Calgary), 221A (Vancouver), Vancouver Art 
Book Fair, Nuit Blanche (Edmonton), and The Works International Art & Design Festival 
(Edmonton). Previous residencies have included N.O.P.E. at 221A and Naked State 
produced by Arts Unfold (Toronto), as well as writing residencies with the Burrard Arts 
Foundation (Vancouver) and Historic Joy Kogawa House (Vancouver).e
