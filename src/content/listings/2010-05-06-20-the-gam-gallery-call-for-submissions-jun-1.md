The Gam Gallery - Call for Submissions - June 1

The Gam would like to pose a series of questions related to the genre of
science-fiction; in the role that imagination has in building future selves
and societies. We are asking artists to consider how our post-modernist
condition will affect the construction of the future, and how these ideas
reflect upon our present and future identities.

Is, as Donna Haraway suggests in The Cyborg Manifesto, the construction of
a new consciousness based on the model of a cyborg a way to liberate
ourselves? Or are we doomed to end up living in the post-apocalyptic
landscape of a cyberpunk novel? In what ways does our vision of the future
manifest itself? How are the boundaries of our imaginations connected to
the architecture of the future, whether structural, social or economic?
What will our relationship with technology evolve toward? The coupling of
technology with organic materials summons up images that science fiction
writers have explored in depth of detail. The utopian vision of the future
evidenced in the literature of the 40's and 50's gave way to a darker,
post-industrial landscape that was high-tech and low-life. What does our
current post-modern condition reflect upon the future? Conversely, what
might our vision of the future reflect upon us? How does this teach us
more about our present selves? With the rise of technology, urbanization
and mechanization there is unequivocal marks left on modern/post-modern
culture and cultural bodies. What will it mean to inhabit a body in the
future, and how does that affect identity? The idea of the mechanical
process compels a more or less unremitting attention to phenomenon of an
impersonal character. To move beyond the constructs of "natural" vs.
"unnatural," "machine" vs. "organism," "God-Given" vs. "man-made," surely
calls for a self-reflexive fracturing that only post-modernism can offer.
How can science-fiction teach us? And how will outer-space connect with
inner space?

Please send your submissions to thegam@live.com by June 1st, including
image, title, dimensions, and media. Please also include a short artist
statement.
