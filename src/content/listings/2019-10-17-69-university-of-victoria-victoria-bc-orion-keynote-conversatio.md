3.30-5.30pm
Hickman Theatre, University of Victoria
3800 Finnerty Rd, Victoria, BC

ORION KEYNOTE CONVERSATION
Beautiful Trouble: A Conversation on Activism, Art and Buddhism
with Suzanne Lacy (artist) and Jodie Evans (activist)

Los Angeles-based artist Suzanne Lacy is internationally renowned as a pioneer of
socially engaged art. She was introduced to Buddhism in the mid-1980s when the artist
Allan Kaprow introduced her to her teacher, Joko Beck. She later studied with the Zen
teacher, Yvonne Rand. Lacy's essay, Having it Good: Reflections on Engaged Art and
Engaged Buddhism discussed the shared genealogy between
engaged art and
engaged Buddhism in the United States in the 1960s.

Jodie Evans has been a peace,
environmental, women's rights and social justice activist
for fifty years. She is the co-founder of CODEPINK, a women's anti-war organization
dedicated to promoting peace. Evans has worked with the Zen master, Thich Nhat
Hanh, the founder of the concept of engaged Buddhism, to bring mindfulness to
Oakland, California; and has also worked with His Holiness the 14th Dalai Lama as co-
producer of The World Festival of Sacred Music.

Evans states, "Can you be a Buddhist and not be an activist? Art is activism in the same
way...(it) invites us to see differently which breaks us out of the shackles of the status
quo, isn't that activism?"

Join us in a conversation between two longtime friends, exploring the relationship
between art and activism, activism and Buddhism, and the spaces in between.

This keynote conversation is organized by the Art Gallery of Greater Victoria in
partnership with the University of Victoria, Faculty of Fine Arts, Orion Series in Fine Arts.
It is part of the
major project In the Present Moment: Buddhism, Contemporary Art and
Social Practice. A Research Convening curated by Haema Sivanesan. Learn more
about this project here: https://aggv.ca/in-the-present-moment-buddhism-contemporary-
art-and-social-practice
