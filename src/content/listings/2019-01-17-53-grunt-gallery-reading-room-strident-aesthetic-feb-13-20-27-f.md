Reading Room: Strident aesthetic. Towards a new liberation
February 13, February 20, and February 27th 
grunt gallery 
350 E 2nd Ave, Vancouver, BC

The Reading Room is an opportunity to discuss themes in Carlos Colns exhibition, 
Strident aesthetic. Towards a new liberation, through short theoretical, historical, or 
literary texts selected by Carlos Coln and curator, Glenn Alteen. 
grunt will host three Reading Room sessions during the run of Strident aesthetic. 
Towards a new liberation. Each session will focus on a different reading. 
Please email Nellie, nellie(at)grunt.ca, to register or for more information. Readings 
will be emailed to you ahead of the Reading Room sessions.

Strident aesthetic. Towards a new liberation
Carlos Coln 
January 11 " March 2 2019
This new series of work by Mexican Canadian artist Carlos Coln merges symbols of 
Latin American conceptualist art, and Latin American colonialist history, past and 
present, and its diaspora. Working with archives, books, footage, and audio material 
related to Latin American history, the artist creates a work based on photographs, 
text and/or audio with parallels between, arts, politics, religion, and society.

Carlos Coln was born in Guadalajara, Mexico in 1980 and grew up in Mexico City. 
His practice explores how art can create links between Latin American societies and 
its diasporas by looking at how artists use local knowledge, realities, and histories in 
social resistance as new expressions of social and cultural progress. Coln holds a 
Masters of Fine Arts degree from the National School of Fine Art in Mexico City, and 
a second MFA from the University of British Columbia. He is currently a PhD 
candidate in the Interdisciplinary Studies Graduate Program at UBC where his 
research explores the manifestations of baroque as a colonial legacy in Latin 
America and its diasporas. Coln was awarded the 2016 Emerging Artist Mayors 
Arts Awards for the City of Vancouver in Visual Arts; the 2017 Artist Studio Award 
Program; and the Canada Council grant to the Research and Create component in 
2018. His work has been shown at the Art Gallery of Greater Victoria; Gallery 1515, 
Vancouver; and La Boligoma Art Gallery, Mexico City.
