Griffin Art Projects
Marian Penner Bancroft and Michael Morris
Friday, November 18, 7:30 PM

Please join us for an evening of conversation with distinguished artists Marian 
Penner Bancroft and Michael Morris. 

Marian Penner Bancroft has been a practicing artist for over thirty years. In 
addition to her photography, Penner Bancroft's practice has included text, 
sound, drawing, sculpture, and video work. Her photographic work often serves 
as a vehicle for investigating and exhuming the past, and re-configuring the 
authoritative structures and systems that shape our understanding of history. 
Penner Bancroft's collecting practice is expansive and varied, ranging from found 
rocks to 19th century collectibles. Penner Bancroft will discuss the personalities 
and memories of the objects loaned to the exhibit, addressing ideas of collecting, 
memory, narrative, and history. 

Michael Morris has been an integral figure in the development of Vancouver's 
artistic character. During his studies in London, the influence of Fluxus left a 
lasting impact on the artist. Morris' early experiments with hard-edge painting 
changed the landscape of Vancouver's art scene, creating a dialogue with both 
the European avant-garde as well his contemporaries in the United States. 
These dialogues led to the establishment of the Image Bank in 1969 while his 
interest in experimental forms and media led to his contributions as a co-founder 
of the Western Front in 1973. In 1990, Morris and Vincent Trasov founded the 
Morris/Trasov Archive to document the Image Bank's legacy. In this conversation 
with Griffin Art Project's Director Lee Plested, Morris charts a winding path 
between his artistic practice over the years, the plurality of Vancouver's art 
scene, as well as the impact of ceramic art and the history of ceramics on the 
West Coast.
