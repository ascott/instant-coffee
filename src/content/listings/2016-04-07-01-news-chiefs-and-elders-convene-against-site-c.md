Stop Site C: Treaty 8 Chiefs and Elders, David Suzuki, and Grand 
Chief Phillip Acknowledge Supporters' Dedication to Protection of 
Treaty Rights and Environment 

(Coast Salish Territory/Vancouver, B.C. - April 5, 2016) A delegation of 
Chiefs and elders from Treaty 8 traveled down to Vancouver from the 
Peace Valley to visit supporters protesting the proposed Site C Dam, 
who are camped outside of the BC Hydro building. They were 
accompanied by David Suzuki and Grand Chief Stewart Phillip, who 
have both committed to stand with the Treaty 8 First Nations in 
opposition to Site C.

Chief Roland Willson, West Moberly First Nations, stated "We are 
inspired and deeply honoured to have the support of so many 
individuals in our fight to stop the proposed Site C Dam. In particular 
we would like to publicly thank and acknowledge Kristin Henry for the 
great personal sacrifice she made in undertaking a lengthy hunger 
strike to bring broad attention to the issue. Our Elders continue to 
remind us that we must protect the land, and exercise our Indigenous 
Rights and Treaty Rights. Every week we learn of a new reason why 
Site-C should not proceed, from increases that ratepayers will face, to 
the severely detrimental impacts the project will have on life sustaining 
trout in the Peace River, and as the public voice of opposition grows 
louder, we know the Christy Clark Government will be forced to listen."

"As the most affected by the proposed Site C project, our Elders 
continue to believe our needs and interests are not being addressed in 
the fast-track approvals process for provincial permits. As a recent 
example, Treaty 8 asked to be directly consulted and meaningfully 
involved in the development of the Fish and Wildlife Compensation 
Program but we were ignored," said Prophet River Chief Lynette 
Tsakoza. "It is why West Moberly First Nations and Prophet River First 
Nation felt compelled to file an appeal of the main water licenses 
'granted' for the Site-C Project with the Environmental Appeal Board."

"Last year, the B.C. Auditor General's office stated Site-C was among 
the projects to be reviewed in the 2016/2017 fiscal year. Today the 
UBCIC is asking the BC NDP to formally request that the Auditor 
General's office prioritize a truly credible and expedient review before 
this project arrives at, what Premier Clark has proclaimed to take it to, 
the 'point of no return,' remarked Grand Chief Stewart Phillip, President 
of the Union of BC Indian Chiefs. "The UBCIC maintains that there is 
no urgency to issue or implement provincial approvals, given that the 
power from Site C is not needed at all and consequently has no willing 
buyer. Alarmingly, BC Hydro customers will be forced to pay for this $9 
Billion boondoggle through their monthly Hydro bills."

David Suzuki, world renowned environmentalist and human rights 
advocate, added "Government promises to uphold and respect treaty 
rights ring hollow when construction of the Site C Dam is green-lighted 
before ongoing First Nations' court cases against the proposed dam 
are finished. BC Hydro must stop work immediately until the court 
cases are decided."

The delegation then moved to the BC Supreme Court in solidarity with 
the PVLA court action against Site-C. 

Site C is a proposed 60-metre high, 1,050-metre-long hydroelectric 
dam on the Peace River on Treaty 8 territory in northeastern British 
Columbia. It would create an 83-kilometre-long reservoir submerging 
78 First Nations heritage sites, burial grounds and places of cultural 
and spiritual significance in flagrant violation of the Constitution Act, 
while also flooding approximately 5,550 hectares of ALR land.

Speakers called out the Trudeau and Clark Governments for violating 
Federal Law. The Province is only able to remove land from the ALR if 
it is deemed in "provincial interest", Site-C is clearly not in public or the 
provinces' best interest. Site-C violates International and Federal law 
through breaking treaty rights. Site-C and all construction therefore are 
in direct contravention of section 35 of the Constitution Act; Section 35 
falls outside of the Charter of Rights and Freedoms, and it begins Part 
II of the Constitution. This allows Section 35 to be exempt from the 
"Notwithstanding Clause" that applies to the Charter. Essentially, this 
means the Provincial and Federal Governments cannot make 
decisions that override indigenous rights.. It is therefore evident that 
the Provincial Government is acting in extreme negligence of duty, as 
they are actively violating Federal Law. Of further interest is the fact 
that, once impounded behind the Site-C dam, the previously free-
flowing water of B.C.'s Peace River would become a NAFTA 
commodity and that Site-C Dam is central to the Kuiper, NAWAPA and 
Grand Prairie schemes for continental water sharing, which is 
something that people need to be questioning.
