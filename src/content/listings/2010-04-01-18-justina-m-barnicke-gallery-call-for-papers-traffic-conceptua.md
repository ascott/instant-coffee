Traffic: Conceptualism in Canada

The Justina M. Barnicke Gallery at the University of Toronto invites
proposals for contributions to a conference concerned with the
manifestations of conceptualism in the history of contemporary art in
Canada. Taking as its starting point the notion that conceptualism was (and
continues to be) a global phenomenon, the conference seeks to develop a
greater understanding of the specificity of local manifestations in
artistic centres in Canada (Halifax, Montreal, Winnipeg, Calgary, Edmonton,
London, Vancouver and Toronto), and of the exchanges amongst these and
their international counterparts.

Some of the topics the conference seeks to explore include the role that
new forms of exchange and communications played in the formations of
conceptualism (and in the institutional cultures) in Canada, from the mid
1960s to early 1980s. Equally importantly, the conference will be concerned
with the politics of appropriation, influence, mimicry, hybridization and
adaptation, and how these figure in the discourse of centre and periphery
in art history when it comes to the unprecedented transnational fluency and
exchanges amongst artists from the 1960s on.

What constitutes the specific receptivity for conceptualism in Canada? What
are its particular manifestations and effects? At a broader level, the
conference will examine conceptualism's continued influence and pervasive
presence in contemporary art; its relevance and relation to burgeoning mass
media strategies, as well as its continued popularity decades after its
pronounced end or failure. Topics may include, but are not limited to
broader issues such as the theory and problematics of developing local
histories in a global context, the role of media (from printing and
publishing, to telex, video, and television), and diverse artistic
disciplines as contributing factors (from theatre to architecture and
poetry, among others), as well as the general turn from the traditional
formats of art history and gallery exhibition to practices centered on the
archive and networks.

The conference will coincide with the exhibition Traffic: Conceptual Art
in Canada c. 1965 to 1980, curated by Grant Arnold, Catherine Crowston,
Michèle Thériault with Vincent Bonin, Jayne Wark, and Barbara Fischer. The
exhibition is produced by the Vancouver Art Gallery, Art Gallery of
Alberta, Leonard and Bina Ellen Art Gallery, Halifax, INK, and the Justina
M. Barnicke Gallery. The exhibition will premiere at the four major
galleries of the University of Toronto (University of Toronto Art Centre,
Blackwood Gallery, Doris McCarthy Gallery, and Justina M. Barnicke Gallery)
in September 2010 and tour across Canada in 2011 and 2012. Centered on the
exchanges between artists, the exhibition will include a broad spectrum of
practices marked by the conceptual turn (including the turn to language,
performance, photo, video) in contemporary art in Canada and internationally.

We accept presentations in French and encourage submissions from diverse
disciplines, as well as artistic responses. Submissions from young
scholars, including Graduate and Ph.D. students are most welcome. Proposals
(250 to 300 words) should be sent to conceptual.art@utoronto.ca by April
19, 2010. Please include name, address, telephone number, email address,
and a short biographical note (up to 100 words).

Proceedings from the conference may be bound into a compendium of papers to
be published in conjunction with a forth-coming exhibition catalogue.
Support for travel may be available pending on funding.
