Grand Opening of The Pavilion

Date: January 14, 2010

Time: 4-8pm

Location: Langara College, 100 West 49th Ave. Vancouver BC

Description:

The Langara College Centre for Art in Public Spaces invites you to the
grand opening of The Pavilion, a project by current Artist in Residence
Holly Ward. On January 14, 2010, from 4-8pm, students, artists and the
general public are invited to join us for launch of its first exhibition,
Ideas and Integrities: A Utopian Library. The exhibition will take place
inside The Pavilion dome located on the front lawn of Langara College.

Ideas and Integrities: A Utopian Library features the utopian literature
collection of Denman Island anarchist poet and scholar Ron Sakolsky. The
exhibition serves to highlight aspects of The Pavilion project as a model
of utopian thinking by providing visitors with a selection of utopian
literature, theory and case-studies. These will be available to the public
to read on-site in the dome's lounge area from 1-5pm on Thursdays, Fridays
and Saturdays until February 6, 2010. Sakolsky will also be present on
January 28 at 6pm to read from his new book of poetry, Swift Winds.

From January through April 2010, The Pavilion will host a variety of
projects, including readings, exhibits, and performances. Please check
www.langara.bc.ca for a list of upcoming events.

Artist in Residence Holly Ward

Vancouver-based artist Holly Ward received her Masters of Fine Arts from
the University of Guelph in 2006 after completing her undergraduate degrees
in both Fine Arts and English at the Nova Scotia College of Art and Design
and the University of New Brunswick. Her work has been in a number of
exhibitions including How Soon is Now, Vancouver Art Gallery; Radical
Rupture, Morris and Helen Belkin Gallery, UBC; and Voice Overhead, Insa Art
Space, Seoul, South Korea.

Langara College Art in Public Spaces

The Langara College Centre for Art in Public Spaces engages communities in
the research, production and presentation of art in the public realm. The
Centre encourages dialogue, opportunities for collaboration, and enhances
experience of art in public spaces through Artist in Residence programs,
guest speakers, and continually evolving curricula.
