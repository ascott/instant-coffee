CONTEMPORARY ART GALLERY

ROBERT ORCHARDSON & CORIN SWORN

November 18, 2011 to January 15, 2012 
OPENING RECEPTION: Thursday, November 17th, 7pm to 10pm

Starting November 18th, the CAG is pleased to present the first exhibition in 
Canada of work by British artist Robert Orchardson coinciding with the first 
North American presentation of new work by Canadian/British artist Corin Sworn.

Both artists’ practises consider performance, memory, time and space and 
each exhibition will present an interesting and ambitious installation which will 
challenge audiences in how they interact and experience contemporary art.

Robert Orchardson
Endless Façade
November 18, 2011 to January 15, 2012 
B. C. Binning Gallery
British artist Robert Orchardson makes sculptures inspired by science fiction, 
stage sets and futuristic design. By drawing inspiration from stage sets and 
using un-futuristic materials such as wood and resin, Orchardson creates large 
colourful geometric shapes to create unexpected environments and experiences 
creating a narrative between viewers, performers and the space itself. 

For Endless Façade, Orchardson revisits stage sets designed by Isamu 
Noguchi created in 1955 for the Royal Shakespeare Company’s production of 
King Lear. Noguchi aimed to create an atmospheric world, where abstract, 
mobile forms created a shifting landscape against which the play unfolded. 
However Noguchi’s designs were met with damning criticism. Orchardson was 
fascinated by this aspiration offset by failure, and revisits Noguchi’s designs and 
intern creates huge monochrome wall constructions that act as a backdrop for a 
series of highly-coloured prop-like forms. The wall construction will pervade the 
whole gallery, reinforcing the deliberate sense of entering another world. Against 
this, a series of coloured objects resemble the nebulous shapes similar to those 
seen in works of surrealist artist like Yves Tanguy. 

This elaborate installation will transform the gallery space into an immersive 
environment that visitors will enter through a triangular opening unveiling an 
environment that speaks of competing implications, abstraction versus figuration 
and the present versus the future and beyond. 

Corin Sworn
Endless Renovation 
November 18, 2011 to January 15, 2012
Alvin Balkind Gallery 

Born in London, UK, Corin Sworn grew up in Canada, studied at Emily Carr 
University of Art and Design and now lives and works in Glasgow. Sworn’s 
research-base practice investigates time and history, combines found objects 
and texts, light and shadows, storytelling and speculation and often include an 
abstract or incomplete dimension that encourages viewers to develop their own 
narrative, experience and interpretation. 

Endless Renovation is an installation inspired by a chance discovery of a 
discarded collection of nearly 600 35mm slides in a dumpster in a Glasgow 
alleyway near the artist’s home. The CAG is thrilled to present the first North 
American presentation of Sworn’s new work after its recent presentation at Tate 
Britain. 

Endless Renovation was first performed in front of a live audience, where the 
artist read from a transcript corresponding to a selection of found images. By 
employing a typical art school lecture format, Sworn used two projectors as if 
comparing one image to another and merged the images with poetic quotes and 
her own thoughts. The act of conveying her impressions consequently shapes 
the narrative and thus viewers understanding. 

The performative element of this initial presentation remains central to the 
exhibition at the CAG and the narration is now recorded and synced to two 
automated slide projectors. Also integral to the install are glass and mirror 
shelving, a flower arrangement, various vases and tailored curtains assembled 
into the room’s composition. The scent of flowers, the diffused and changing 
light, the question of the origins of the slides, the narrative combining 
meandering thoughts with excerpts from poetry, all combine to transform the 
gallery into an animated set animated and experience.

A public opening, with the artists in attendance, to celebrate Robert Orchardson 
Endless Façade and Corin Sworn Endless Renovation will take place at the 
CAG on Thursday November 17th from 7-10pm. 

An in conversation event between the artists and architect Richard Henriquez 
and Leslie Van Duzer, Director and Professor, School of Architecture and 
Landscape Architecture, UBC, will be held on Friday November, 18th at 7pm, 
free admission. 

ABOUT THE CAG
The CAG is the only free public art gallery in Vancouver dedicated exclusively to 
presenting contemporary art. Established in 1971 as the Greater Vancouver 
Artist's Gallery, the CAG was incorporated in 1976 as a non-profit charitable 
society. Vancouver artists were hired for six-month periods to produce art for 
exhibitions at the gallery, and for inclusion in the City of Vancouver Art Collection. 
In 1984 the CAG became an artist-run centre and by the early 1990s the 
exhibition program expanding to include national and international artists as well 
as presenting some of the first institutional exhibitions for many important 
Vancouver artists, including…? . In 1996 the CAG became the only independent 
public art gallery in downtown Vancouver. In May 2001, the CAG moved to its 
current location, a new purpose-built facility under the aegis of the City of 
Vancouver's Amenity Bonus program in cooperation with Bosa Ventures Inc. 

The Contemporary Art Gallery is a publicly funded institution, generously 
supported by the Canada Council for the Arts, the City of Vancouver and the 
Province of BC through the BC Arts Council and the BC Gaming Policy and 
Enforcement Branch. We are very grateful for this support and the support we 
receive from the Vancouver Foundation and our members, donors and 
volunteers.
