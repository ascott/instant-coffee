Public Talk
Marianne Nicolson
Artist Talk

Tuesday, October 14 at 7pm

at the Vancouver Art Gallery

Marianne Nicolson is a member of the Dzawada'enuxw Tribe of the
Kwakwaka'wakw Nations of Kingcome Inlet, British Columbia. She first came
to prominence in 1998 when she scaled a vertical rock face in Kingcome
Inlet to paint a 28 x 38-foot pictograph the first in the inlet for over
sixty years to mark the continued vitality of her ancestral village of
Gwa'yi. Nicolson has studied at the Emily Carr Institute of Art and Design,
at the University of Victoria and also with Kwakwaka'wakw carver Wayne
Alfred. Nicolson incorporates the study of Kwakwala language with her art
training and is currently engaged in PhD research on the importance of
indigenous language to an indigenous world view. Nicolson has shown her
work both nationally and internationally at venues including Art Gallery of
Greater Victoria, Artspeak, the National Gallery of Canada and Taipei Fine
Arts Museum, and she has created a number of site-specific artworks for
public spaces.

Free with Gallery admission.
