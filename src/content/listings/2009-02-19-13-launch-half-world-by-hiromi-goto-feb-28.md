14. Surrey Art Gallery | Sound Thinking 2009 | FEB 28
Sound Thinking 2009: Conversations on Audio Art

Organized by the Surrey Art Gallery

February 28

Bringing together specialists in audio art, communications, science and
technology, Sound Thinking 2009 is a symposium that sets out to address
new developments in radiophonic space, the nature of hearing, and the
making and transmitting of sounds. This event is a forum designed to
further discussion and debate within the sound art community and related
disciplines along with a broader public interested in the world of sound
and radio communications. Sound Thinking 2009 takes place Saturday,
February 28 from Noon to 5pm at the Surrey Art Gallery. Admission to
this event is free.

Taking such landmark audio transmission art exhibitions/events as Radio
Rethink (Walter Phillips Gallery, 1994) as a reference point, Sound
Thinking 2009 addresses the key issues facing sound artists who use the
radio or whose art is influenced by notions of radio communication
today. The symposium will re-examine the history of audio art in light
of recent events and changes to technologies of distribution and
broadcast, and debates within the fields of art and communication.

Participants in the conference include Keynote speaker Heidi Grundmann,
the former head of Kunstradio on the Austrian Broadcasting Network (ORF)
and author of the anthology Reimagining Radio; Toronto-based sound
artist Anna Friz, who has presented work in festivals, museums and
produced numerous radio works; Canadian audio artist and Resonance FM
steering committee member Dr. John Wynne; University of British Columbia
Audiologist Alison Miller; and David Jaeger, one of CBC's
longest-serving broadcasters of Canadian contemporary music.

Anna Friz will present Respire, a multi-channel collaborative sound
work.

Convened by Canadian sound artist Jean Routhier, Sound Thinking 2009 is
presented in conjunction with John Wynne: Wireframe and Open Sound 2009,
an exhibition featuring the sound art of Frederick C. Brummer, Ian
Gregory James, Lee Hutzulak and Giorgio Magnanensi.

Surrey Art Gallery is located at 13750 - 88 Avenue, Surrey, BC.
604-501-5566 | www.arts.surrey.ca <http://www.arts.surrey.ca>

15. containR | a film festival in shipping containers |til | MAR 1
containR: a film festival in shipping containers

Think of it as green cinema meets performance films. Recycled shipping
containers are reconfigured to house free screenings of Canadian and
international dance, sport and performance films that celebrate
physicality in art and sport.

Presented by Springboard in partnership with the Vancouver 2009
Cultural Olympiad, containR is a street installation at the nexus of
video, public art and urban design. Sited on the northeast corner of
Vancouver's Library Square, containR sits at the cross roads of
mountain and urban culture; art and sport cinema, embracing public art
and sustainable design.

Free and accessible to audiences of all ages.

Open February 18 - March 1

ARTIST TRADING CARD - CALL FOR SUBMISSIONS

"Regina ATC Celebrates 6 years: make 6 cards, get a 6 back!"

Submission deadline March 31, 2009 to: ATC Celebrates 6, MacKenzie Art
Gallery, 3475 Albert St.,Regina, SK S4S 6X6.
Please include: 1. Self-addressed & stamped, return-envelope 2. Brief
statement: tell us a little about yourself & discuss your techniques
and tools, and your image content
3. How to reach you: contact information and e-mail (so we can send
you an invites & e-vites to the display)
All cards submitted will be displayed in the Learning Centre at the
MacKenzie Art Gallery;
Please tell us if you DON'T want your work to be part of the web
display. Opening reception: Sat April 25, 2pm. Display Closing in
June,
when you will receive a surprise 6-pack mailed back!

Artist Trading Cards are miniature works of art no larger than 2.5 by
3.5 inches (64 by 89 mm).There are no restrictions as to medium:
Cards can be painted, collaged, found images, mini-comics, sewn, 2D or
3D, stained glass, metal, wax, computer generated imaging,
photography,originals or in a series-- as long as they fit into the
plastic sheets designed for hockey/baseball cards. Cards are signed
and dated and include an edition number, if necessary. ATC's are
tradedone for one, never bought nor sold.

Artist Trading Cards were invented in 1997 by Swiss artist M. Vanci
Stirnemann while visiting The New Gallery in Calgary. Developed out
of a performance concerned with non-hierarchical, non-monetary,
non-judgemental exchange, Artist Trading Card sessions have spread all
over theworld. The Regina Chapter is facilitated by Sylvia Ziemann,
Nicolle Nugent, Sharon Eisbrenner and Margaret Bessai.

CALL FOR SUBMISSIONS for the 2009 Joseph Plaskett Award.

This award honors excellence in painting by providing $25,000 for travel,
studio work or study in Europe for one year. Criteria for eligibility and

The Royal Canadian Academy (RCA) has partnered with the Joseph Plaskett
Foundation to administer the award which is aimed at MFA students in the
field of painting. The prize was created six years ago by Canadian artist
Joseph Plaskett who worked in Paris for many years. He continues to paint
in the UK.

Applications are due according to the website guidelines by March 31st at
the RCA National office.

Artists/Students who have recently graduated with their MFA in painting are
also invited to apply.

18. Malaspina Printmakers | Call for Submissions 2009 | APR 30
Call for Submissions 2009
Malaspina Printmakers accepts submissions of proposals on an ongoing basis.
All proposals and applications for residencies and scholarships should
consist of a current curriculum vita, a statement of intent for the
proposed exhibition, a general statement about your practice and a compact
disk with no more than 20 images or 5 minutes of video, if appropriate.
Please contact Malaspina Printmakers if you have any questions regarding
the submissions process.

Expressions of Interest for 2010 Programming
Curators, Artists and Researchers are invited to submit project proposals
for Malaspina Printmakers' 2010 programming season, which will be based on
the theme of Print Culture in Vancouver. Encompassing all forms of printed
text and printed visual communication, Print Culture extends to and
includes all cultural effects on society created by printing technology and
printed communication. Print is intrinsically tied to culture; politics,
economy, and meaning-making are all intertwined with printing in its
various forms. The current shift between a materially-print-based and
electronic-based information society -- and the many claims that print
culture is in its demise -- posits print media in a unique position to
comment upon contemporary cultural, economic and political issues.
Malaspina Printmakers welcomes diverse interpretations of how we may
envision the theme of Print Culture within Vancouver's rich history and
contemporary life.

Visiting Artist Residency
Print-based artists living outside of the greater Vancouver area are
invited to apply for Malaspina's Visiting Artist Residency. Visiting
Artists have 24-hour access to the studio during the one-month residency
and the opportunity to exhibit their recent work. Individuals
participating in the Visiting Artist Residency will also conduct
demonstrations, workshops and artists talks.
Deadline for applications: April 30, 2009

Print Research Residency
The Print Research Residency pairs a local artist with an expert printmaker
to work on an identified project for the artist. This unique program
allows interdisciplinary artists to consult with technically literate and
highly-skilled print-artists to experiment in the print medium and create a
body of work at the Malaspina Studio.
Deadline for applications: July 30, 2009

Student Scholarships
Student Scholarships are awarded to two continuing print students for four
months and four recent graduates for six months of free studio use. The
scholarship program addresses the need of recent graduates and current
printmaking students to access studio space to continue their practice
between semesters or immediately after graduation.
Deadline for applications: March 1, 2009
