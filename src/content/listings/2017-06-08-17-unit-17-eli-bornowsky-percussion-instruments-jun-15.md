ELI BORNOWSKY
'Percussion Instruments'

16 June - 31 July 2017
opening reception: Thursday 15 June 6 - 8pm

'Abstract art is designed to clear a safe space to rigorously interrogate the concrete 
particulars of art and thereby rigorously interrogate the concrete particulars of 
realitywe enter an art gallery in the face of the most ridiculous and absurd and 
terrifying human existence, and we take pause with the concrete reality of 
abstraction(a)nd here abstract art really begs the question for us to ask ourselves: 
What is real anyway? Is your identity real? Is your social position real? Is private 
property real?' i 

Unit 17 is pleased to announce the exhibition 'Percussion Instruments,' a solo 
presentation of recent and archival work by Eli Bornowsky in the gallerys downtown 
space. 

This exhibition showcases several bodies of works from the last seven years. The 
earliest works consist of repeated coloured circles of varying sizes in oil on canvas 
and goauche on paper. In the two paintings on display, a square grid of circles frame 
another grid of smaller circles around an empty centre. The small circles correspond 
or deviate in colour from their larger counterparts, creating an optically charged and 
rhythmically active image. Here the circle and the grid serve to structure 
Bornowskys engaged research on the experience of colour. 

Bornowsky states: I like to meditate, to clear the mind and yet keep it moving at the 
same time. It is this idea that brought the artist to a new series of polymorphous 
paintings on shaped wooden panels. With varying sizes of wooden spheres attached 
to their engineered Arp-like bases, their surfaces recall the same variances as in the 
earlier paintings, exploring Prussian blue in high gloss, impastoed yellows and 
muted earth-tones. Using a pure random generator, the works in this new series 
begin as random numbers that determine colour, shape, composition and the 
selection of spheres. Gradually these arbitrary beginnings are reworked through the 
artists intuitive processes. Here the value of artistic subjectivity is thrown into 
tension with the arbitrary nature of chance. But randomness, the artist says, is 
simply a structure that speaks to our current age, much more than the Greek and 
Italian mathematical discoveries that determined great pictures of the past. The 
works are idiosyncratic yet structured, quiet yet rhythmic, and they sit comfortably 
between a multitude of interpretations. 

In a group show curated by Bornowsky at Or Gallery last summer, titled 'Pure 
Numerical Formula Describes Wetness and Light,' six Indian tantra paintings found 
their way to the gallery walls through rather chance circumstances of discovery. 
These discrete, anonymous and potent works sit at the crux of the artists recent 
thinking. Their small but nuanced surfaces show masterly restraint. Simple, 
colourful, geometric shapes painted in gouache float near the centre of their 
browning paper supports. Each work is both Tantric symbol and universal abstract 
form at once, silent and also reverberating. In thinking about these ancient forms, 
Bornowsky has arrived at his own abstract pictures that explore the relation between 
somatics, mathematics, spirituality and philosophy.

Additional works in the exhibition include a site-specific wall painting and re-
contextualized gallery furniture. 

Eli Bornowsky (b. 1980) received a Master of Fine Arts from the Milton Avery 
Graduate School of the Arts, Bard College, New York in 2014 and a Bachelor of Fine 
Arts from Emily Carr University in 2005. His work has been shown at the Vancouver 
Art Gallery, Burnaby Art Gallery, G Gallery (Toronto), Diaz Contemporary (Toronto), 
Western Front (Vancouver), Contemporary Art Gallery (Vancouver) and In Limbo 
(Brooklyn), among others.
