Vancouver Art Gallery
Public Talk

Kim Nielsen

Investigate, Ask, Tell, Draw, Build

 Thursday, February 28 at 7pm at the Vancouver Art Gallery in Room 4East

Join us for a lecture by leading Danish architect Kim Herforth Nielsen, as he presents an analogy of the exhibit The Tree: From the Sublime to the Social, relaying it to architecture.  Nielsen will discuss how architecture should not be the building as object, but the building as home to a user community of one sort or another.


The Tree: From the Sublime to the Social considers the tree as a subject in art from the late nineteenth century to the present. The diverse representations of the tree in this exhibition are indicative of its enduring power as an evocative symbol of our complex and changing relationship with the natural environment.


Kim Herforth Nielsen is principal architect at 3XN where he has been the main driving force behind projects such as the Royal Danish Embassy in Berlin, the Architects’ Building in Copenhagen, the Music Building in Amsterdam and the upcoming Museum of Liverpool. Since 1986 3XN has emerged as one of Scandinavia’s leading architectural firms. 3XN, a Denmark based firm, is known for their working method which ‘investigates’ place, ‘asks’ people about the project, ‘tells’ the story of people and place in the conceptual design, ‘draws’ the detail of the project, and finally ‘builds’ – all with the cultural, historic, environmental and human context in mind.

 

This lecture is presented in partnership with the Royal Danish Consulate General.

LES Gallery is pleased to present an exhibition of works on paper by Vancouver artists, The Lions. A forthcoming catalogue featuring an essay by Vancouver writer Lance Blomgren and short story by Lee Henderson will be published for the exhibition.

Committed to a process based approach to drawing, one that puts stock in drawing’s ability to allow for emergences of meaning over its pictorial conclusion, The Lion's amicable practice has resulted in a slightly disconcerting body of work, a series of odd mish-mashes of genre, style, narrative experiment, fantasy, and spectacle.  While The Lion's work through familiar archetypes, colloquial pictorial forms, jokes and gags, these are never left as they are but are inevitably subverted and mutated into something unlikely.	Detournement to detournement, forms, characters, and pictorial conventions are fragmented, disfigured, and eventually rendered absurdly incongruent.  This juxtaposition of incongruent elements allows a critical distance to be formed, through which the Lion's project extends to a formal investigation of image making.

The Lions (Tasha Brotherton, Matthew Brown, Barry Doupe, Collin Johanson, James Whitman) are based in Vancouver, BC.   The Lions have exhibited throughout Canada, the USA and Europe.	 Recent exhibitions include, Dripped, Dropped, Split, Upended, Exploded, Tracey Lawrence Gallery, Vancouver; Tinyvices, (curated by Tim Barber, NY)  NY, Paris, London, Tokyo, Oshkosh (Wisconsin) and Mexico City; and Extensions of Our Hands, Khyber Arts Centre, Halifax, NS.  Since 2003 The Lions have maintained www.lionspile.ca, an online gallery that extends to 450 works.
06. Cinematheque | The Here To Gathered:Sharon Kahanoff | MAR 4
The Here To Gathered | Sharon Kahanoff
16mm, 22.5 min
2008

March 4, 2008
Pacific Cinematheque
1131 Howe St. Vancouver, BC
Opening Reception and Screening 7:30 - 10:30

A reception hall, a park, an art gallery, a living room, a street, a beach and a gymnasium– The Here To Gathered plays inside these theatres of the social, performing along with the actors who move through them.  Gestural, visceral, corny and critical, meditative, artificial and often realistic, the film threatens to erupt at the seams from its own difference.  But The Here To Gathered is held in check by a rigid structuralism that, like any law excessively applied, vents itself in the absurd.  Lurking on the borders between documentary, narrative and experimental film, The Here To Gathered is a portrait, in both major and minor keys, of nothing more than passage.


Sharon Kahanoff is a filmmaker, artist, and writer. Her artwork has been exhibited in galleries and cinemas locally and in the US.  Interested in critical writing, Sharon publishes in exhibition catalogues and art journals and gives occasional lectures.  This exhibition is in partial fulfillment for the degree of Master of Fine Arts at Simon Fraser University.  http://www.sfu.ca/sca

OPEN CALL FOR APPLICATIONS

They arrive to UNIDEE in the middle of June.
They begin to question, to look around, to inquire. 
They observe you with eyes in stereovision; one eye keeps close watch, the river rush by the foot of Cittadellarte, the textile factories, the city of Biella extending up into the mountains and its social fabric and cultural institutions. The other eye looks into the distance, watching the world, their own city woven by its inhabitants, living organisms and institutions, and the planet itself immersed in one grand illusion.
They want to understand what this Cittadellarte is. They have their own Cittadellarte in mind, maybe a rough sketch, an idea, or a dream. They will talk about it and discuss at length, filling these spaces where textiles were once produced with the energy of the water, enterprise, and the labor of an entire generation.
They are artists, curators, managers of socio-cultural projects; perhaps all these roles together. They question, try to understand and then act; producing ideas, elaborating on them and discussing their thoughts. They develop these thoughts into working plans, projects, and then transform them into a concrete practice. They are activators of creative processes for social responsible change.
They live in Cittadellarte, day and night. They have their private rooms, but the work space is open and common. The experience is shared in group of 20 young people from various cultures, together they form a micro-society.
Then in the middle of October the residence concludes. But the University of Ideas continues and changes course. They return to their epicenter where they input their accumulated potential, no longer alone but rather participating in a global movement that advances as one tide; gaining energy from the everyday practice and work of tens, hundreds, and thousands of individuals and organizations.
It is in their epicenter where they can get engaged rather as activators than as individual artists or curators. Where they can step in the game interacting with existing structures, or creating new initiatives, while giving rise to their own Cittadellarte.
Deadline for submissions: 29th February 2008 (postmark date)

11. 1:1 projects | THE UNFAIR FAIR | Rome
THE UNFAIR FAIR

29 February– 3 March, 2008
Loto Arte Via Civinini, 41 - 00197 Rome

Preview: Friday, 29 February, 18:00 to 24:00
Opening hours: Saturday 9:30-14:00 and 15:00-24:00
Sunday and Monday 9:30–14:00 and 15:00-19:30

THE UNFAIR FAIR adopts an economy of form and means where disintegration, experimentation and the poetics of play are emphasised over commodified products and the market economy. THE UNFAIR FAIR is a space for distanciation, for experiments, for collaborations. The aim of the event is to show the process of artistic productions and transitional artworks that index broader practices by each of the invited artists. THE UNFAIR FAIR will find its final shape during the course of its development, allowing for flexibility and contradictions, adapting itself to the projects proposed by the artists and participants. THE UNFAIR FAIR brings together over 70 international artists. A special exhibition edition and display will be created by German artist Wolfgang Berkowski.
