FREE ADMISSION! In Michael Snows tenth film, made in the twilight of the American 
lunar missions, the camera, attached to a robotic arm, casts its roving 360-degree eye 
across a remote, seemingly otherworldly mountaintop in northern Quebec. To a 
soundtrack based on the waves and pulses of the camera-activating machine, La 
Rgion centrale transports its audience to a rugged Canadian landscape that is 
discovered at noon and then explored in seventeen episodes of dizzying motion as the 
machines shadow lengthens, night falls, and light returns (Martha Langford, Art 
Canada Institute). Snows film is preceded by Dachi Satos kaleidoscopic exploration of 
the patterns and contrasts in the landscape of a Montreal park.

Michael Snow catapults us into the heart of a world before speech, before arbitrarily 
composed meaning, even subject. He forces us to rethink not only cinema but our 
universe. " Louis Marcorelles, Le Monde, on La Rgion centrale

The overall effect is of when you tightly shut your eyes after staring at a forest on a 
sunny day, leaving the intense rhythmic, bodily pulsing bleed of positive and negative, 
light and dark, bare, soft shadows and overwhelming color printed on the backs of your 
eyelids. " Daniel Kasman, MUBI Notebook, on Trees of Syntax, Leaves of Axis
