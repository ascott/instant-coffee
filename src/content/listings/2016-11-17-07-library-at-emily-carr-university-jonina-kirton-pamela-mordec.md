The On Edge Readings Series presents:

Jonina Kirton & Pamela Mordecai 
7 pm on Thursday Nov 17
in the Library at Emily Carr University of Art & Design
1399 Johnston St.
Granville Island, Vancouver
unceded Coast Salish territories

The public is welcome 

In conjunction with Poets for the Peace, this night will be dedicated to send 
some love up to the Peace River in northern BC, in the spirit of solidarity with the 
Treaty 8 First Nations who are protecting the river valley from being destroyed by 
the Site C dam. The $9 billion dollar dam would flood over 100 km of river valley, 
destroying important fish habitat, hunting grounds, wildlife migration corridors, 
precious farmland and much more. The dam violates treaty rights, and has not 
been assessed with due diligence by the BC Utilities Commission. There is still 
time to stop the dam, which will take many years to build. 
http://www.poetsforthepeace.ca/

Author bios

Jonina Kirton Kirton, a Mtis/Icelandic poet, currently lives in the unceded territory 
of the Coast Salish people. She brings her experience as a graduate of the SFU 
Writer's Studio, a sacred circle facilitator, and a student of Continuum, to her 
writing and teaching. She remains curious about memory and the many ways 
one can access it. Her first collection of poetry, page as bone ~ ink as blood, 
released in April 2015 by Talonbooks, has been described as "restorative, 
intimate poetry, drawing down ancestral ideas into the current moment's breath." 
www.joninakirton.wix.com/poet.

Pamela Mordecai writes poetry and long and short fiction. Her first novel, Red 
Jacket, published by TAP Books, was shortlisted for the 2015 Rogers Writers' 
Trust Fiction Prize. Mawenzi House also published her sixth book of poetry, de 
book of Mary, in 2015. A former language arts teacher , she writes poetry, plays 
and stories for children and is committed to the use of heart languages in poetry 
and fiction. Born in Jamaica and educated there and in the USA, she and her 
family moved to Canada in 1993. She lives in Kitchener, Ontario.
