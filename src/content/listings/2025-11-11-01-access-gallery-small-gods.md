Small Gods
Forensic Architecture, Steven Cottingham, Serena Lee, Weaver, and Kevin Sudeith
Curated by Kitt Peacock
16 October " 14 December 2025
Opening reception 16 October 2025, 6 PM
Small Gods considers the drone"as violent machine, mythic being, eye of god,
ancestral technology, and resident of our speculative futures.

I. Veiled by the thickness of the air, the drone appears from the ground to be a
smudge, a void, or nothing at all. This is a story about seeing as violence.

II. Before the drone was a drone, it was a sound. From medieval mourning music to
the quiet slide of the guqin, the meditative hum of the drone asks the listener to
separate mind and body, surrendering their senses to the direction of a divine
operator.

III. In the counter-gaze, the drone leaves its mark on the land. Vernacular textiles
capture their passage overhead; simulations fracture and multiply their sightlines.
The aerial gaze is frozen, torn apart, reconstituted as a tool of resistance.

Small Gods features works by Forensic Architecture, Steven Cottingham, Serena
Lee, Weaver, and Kevin Sudeith.
Bios:

Forensic Architecture (FA) is a research agency based at Goldsmiths, University of
London,
investigating human rights violations committed by states, police, militaries, and
corporations.
FA works in partnership with institutions across civil society"grassroots activists,
legal teams,
international NGOs, and media organisations"to carry out investigations with and
on behalf of
communities and individuals affected by conflict, police brutality, border regimes and
environmental violence. FAs investigations employ pioneering techniques in spatial
and
architectural analysis, open source investigation, digital modelling, and situated
interviewing.
Findings have been presented in international courtrooms, parliamentary inquiries,
citizens
tribunals, exhibitions at leading cultural institutions, and across international media.

Steven Cottinghams work engages with virtual realism and the politics of
visualization. His practice critically examines the influence of emerging image
technologies through case studies including bodycams, surveillance advertising,
military simulation software, and AI in prisons. Cottinghams work has recently been
presented at Dazibao (Montreal, 2025), Center for Performance Research (New
York, 2024), Wil Aballe Art Projects (Vancouver, 2023), The 8th Floor (New York,
2023), Catriona Jeffries (Vancouver, 2023), ENTRE (Vienna, 2023), Natalia Hug
Galerie (Cologne, 2022), and Artists Space (New York, 2022). He has undertaken
residencies with the Canadian Forces Artist Program (2024"2025) and the Whitney
Independent Study Program (New York, 2021"2022), and is currently based in
Vancouver.

Serena Lee traces the circulation of energy through transdisciplinary practices.
Playing with expanded cinema, martial arts, place-making, sound, performance,
writing, and collective study, her projects unfold through situated and collaborative
processes. Most recently, her research on taijiquan as a method of aesthetic inquiry
was shared as an exhibition and study program at Casco Art Institute (Utrecht) in
Spring 2025. Serena is based between Toronto and Vienna, where she completed a
PhD at the Akademie der bildenden Knste Wien.

The Weaver is an Afghan textile artist. They were a refugee for many years in
Pakistan, and returned to Afghanistan shortly before the withdrawal of United States
military forces in 2021. Their rugs depict the military technology that has shaped the
social landscape of life in Afghanistan under the American occupation. They
understand their work as having far-reaching political repercussions, and have
asked to remain anonymous in this presentation of their work. Brown Predator
Drones on Rust Red Field was commissioned in 2015 by Kevin Sudeith.

Kitt Peacock is an artist, curator, and settler from Oodham Jewe, currently working
on the unceded territories of the xmky"m, Swxw7mesh, and slilwta
nations. Their practice is centred around the creation and maintenance of trans
worlds, and considers how violent conditions can be redeployed as the very material
of trans life. Peacocks ongoing work with the Centre for Near Life Research forges
alliances with non-living relations"contaminated waterways, lethal algae blooms,
and sites of harm"in order to discover new strategies of queer/trans resistance.

Kitt holds a BFA from Emily Carr University of Art +Design (2019) and an MFA from
the University of British Columbia (2023). Their social practice work has appeared in
recent exhibitions at the MoMA PS1 (New York), Haus der Kulturen der Welt (Berlin),
and the Museum of Contemporary Art Tokyo.
