Emerge 2015 Presents: Post-Graduate Pathways in the Arts

Raymond Boisjoly, Allison Collins, and Kimberly Phillips in 
Conversation

Wednesday, October 7th 2015 | 5:45 to 7:15pm

Audain Art Centre, room 1002 | 6398 University Blvd

University of British Columbia alumni Raymond Boisjoly, Kimberly 
Phillips, and Allison Collins will discuss their post-graduate paths, 
providing insight into the early stages of making a career in the arts. 
These distinguished individuals will discuss the varying roles of the 
artist, the curator, and the writer.

This event is intended for both graduate and undergraduate students 
interested in furthering their practical knowledge of the arts to support 
future career steps in the fields of art making, curating, and more.

Raymond Boisjoly is an Indigenous artist of Haida and Quebecois 
descent based in Vancouver, Canada. His practice concerns the 
deployment of images, objects and materials in and as Indigenous art. 
A reflexive approach is used to foreground the discourses which frame 
and delimit the work produced by Indigenous artists. Boisjoly has been 
included in exhibitions and projects at SITE Santa Fe, Triangle France 
(Marseille), La biennale de Montréal, Musée d'art contemporain de 
Montréal, Vancouver Art Gallery, The Power Plant (Toronto) and 
Presentation House Gallery (North Vancouver). Boisjoly holds an MFA 
from the University of British Columbia. He is an Assistant Professor at 
Emily Carr University of Art and Design and is represented by Catriona 
Jeffries Gallery. 

Allison Collins is a Vancouver-based curator, writer and researcher. 
Currently Curator of Media Arts at Western Front where she runs the 
media arts residency program, her previous independent projects 
include (with co-curator Michael Turner) Mainstreeters: Taking 
Advantage 1972-1982(2015), Moveable Facture (2012), Suspicious 
Futures: Selected works of Susan Britton, (2012) and Hold Still Wild 
Youth: The Gina Show Archive, (2010). From 2011-2012 she was 
Event Manager for Institutions by Artists, a three-day international 
conference on artist-run culture, during which time she completed 
ARCLines, a series of historical profiles documenting the origins of 
Vancouver's artist-run centres on Arcpost.ca. Her writing has been 
published across Canada, including Stride Gallery, Calgary; 
PLATFORM, Winnipeg; Or Gallery, VIVO Media Arts Centre, Republic 
Gallery, C Magazine,Fuse, ARTSlant, and with Publication Studio, 
Vancouver. Collins holds a BFA in Visual Art from the University of 
Ottawa and an MA in Critical and Curatorial Studies from the University 
of British Columbia.

A writer, curator, and educator, Kimberly Phillips is Director/Curator of 
Access Gallery, a Vancouver artist run centre committed to supporting 
the work of emergent and experimental artists. She holds a PhD in art 
history from the University of British Columbia. She was Head of 
Interpretation at the Vancouver Art Gallery from 2009—2013 and a 
Curatorial Resident at 221A artist run centre for 2012—2013. At 
Access Gallery she has launched a multi-year residency and exhibition 
project which offer selected artists passage aboard cargo ships bound 
for Shanghai, who are considered “in residence” for duration of the 
voyage. Alongside her position at Access, Phillips maintains an active 
teaching practice. She is Course Leader of the Masters of Applied Arts 
Low Residence Program at Emily Carr University of Art and Design. 
She has authored, edited and contributed to numerous bookworks, 
exhibition catalogues, and critical anthologies, and her writings have 
appeared in Artforum, C Magazine and Fillip.

Emerge 2015-16: A series of artist talks, professional workshops, and 
behind-the-scenes tours is organized by the Visual Arts Student 
Association and the Morris and Helen Belkin Art Gallery with support 
from the Department of Art History, Visual Art & Theory at the 
University of British Columbia. The series complements classroom 
learning by presenting creative and relevant learning opportunities for 
students who are interested in professional art practices.
