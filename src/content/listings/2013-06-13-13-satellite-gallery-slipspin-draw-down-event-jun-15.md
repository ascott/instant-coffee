Slip/Spin: From Studio to Satellite Gallery

Draw Down Event on Saturday, June 15th

Dunbar Pottery Studio Open House with potter Martin Peters

10am to 12pm

4056 W. 27th Ave (Between Camosun St and Crown St)

Vancouver

Satellite Gallery with potter Ron Vallis

2pm to 4pm

560 Seymour Street, 2nd Floor

Immerse yourself in the worlds of mark making and pottery from the artists
studio to the gallery. In conjunction with Satellite Gallerys current
exhibition of West Coast studio pottery, High Fire Culture, view sketches
and studies made by potters at a special open house at the Dunbar Pottery
Studio for Vancouver Draw Down. Later in the day, visit Satellite Gallery
to observe and draw potter Ron Vallis throwing pots on a Leach style
kick-wheel.

The creative work of the potters in High Fire Culture is linked to the
approach of English master potter Bernard Leach, who was well known for his
sensitive line drawings done on paper and on clay. Leachs skill as an
etcher and print maker brought him to Japan where he was introduced to the
potters art.

High Fire Culture features over one hundred retrospective pots, as well as
a collection of educational and archival material including historical
photographs, videos, reference books, drawings, and a Leach kick-wheel.

Satellite's new teen group "The Graey" will also be presenting a drawing
activity at the front doors of the Gallery and throughout the Downtown
core. Follow their easels to the drawing event in the Gallery lobby before
coming upstairs for the Satellite's event.

ABOUT THE ARTISTS

MARTIN PETERS

Martin Peters studied ceramics with Walter Dexter at the Kootenay School of
Art in Nelson, BC, in the early 1970s, followed by an apprenticeship with
Michael Henry at the Slug Pottery in Roberts Creek, BC, and six months
apprenticing at the Leach Pottery in St. Ives, Cornwall. Peters continued
to make pots while practicing law in Toronto and Vancouver, first at his
Crawford Street Pottery (Toronto), followed by the Rosedale Pottery
(Toronto) and since 2003 the Dunbar Pottery (Vancouver), where he works
with Ron Vallis.

RON VALLIS

After graduating from the Vancouver School of Art in 1975 and studying at
the Banff School of Fine Arts, Ron Vallis ran a pottery business with Tam
Irving making standardware; he also worked with Sam Kwan and John Reeve at
the Old Bridge Street Pottery on Granville Island. For many years he has
supported the provinces ceramic community through his leadership roles in
the North-West Ceramics foundation and the Potters Guild of British Columbia.
