A Walking Tour: Saturday, September 25th, 4pm

Starting at the CAG

The impetus for Peter Gazendam's casual walking tour of four of Vancouver's
public sculptures is A Saloon Keeper, A Newspaper, Two Wars, A Doorman.
This new work, which is a founding part of the group exhibition Following A
Line, uses as its base a series of collages made from snapshots of
innocuous and modest, yet somewhat odious, public sculptures that the
artist encountered in his daily routes through the city. Like the
collages, which Gazendam has cut out of their environment and recomposed,
he will further fragment the sculptures by discussing their partial
histories and formal properties through a highly subjective lens.

Peter Gazendam's (b. 1977, Kingston, Ontario) multidimensional art practice
includes sculpture, drawing, photography, video, sound works, readymades,
and installation. Gazendam assembles his work through archival research
into a range of obscure scientific histories and cultural phenomena.
Gazendam has participated in group exhibitions such as Thoughts about
Sound, Music, Silence & Confusion, Diaz Contemporary, Toronto (2006); The
Horse They Rode In On, Wynick/Tuck Gallery, Toronto (2005); Path to Shore,
Mercer Union, Toronto (2005); and 10 Days in July, Gallery TPW, Toronto
(2005). He graduated with his MFA from the University of Victoria in 2008.

Following A Line also includes work by Pablo Bronstein, Susanne Kriemann,
Kyla Mallett, Alex Morrison, Frances Stark and Paul Sietsema, and runs
until November 7, 2010. In conjunction with the exhibition is an upcoming

10. Centre A | CONTEMPORARY ARCHIVING | SEP 25
Centre A presents

CONTEMPORARY ARCHIVING
Issues around the archiving of contemporary art practice & histories

Saturday, September 25, 2-4pm
Centre A Lounge / free
This event is presented as part of Culture Days culturedays.ca
Live on Ustream: www.ustream.tv/user/centrea

Lead Patron: Anndraya T. Luui

At the closing of the exhibition, "The DIG", a 10-year survey of our "
informal collection", Centre A presents a panel discussion on "Contemporary
Archiving".

How to establish archiving strategies that meet the needs of contemporary
art practice? As the visual arts have diversified, the documentation and
archiving of complex practices, including performance, conceptual art,
site-specific interventions and media art, have become serious subjects of
discussion.

"Who speaks to whom?" "How do we record plural histories and preserve
multiple perspectives?" "What platforms and media are best suited to
archiving?" " How can we establish a new network to share the archive"
These are the key topics.

Centre A has invited professional archivists and artists to address these
questions.

[Lorna Brown]

Lorna Brown works between studio practice, curation and writing to explore
interests in social phenomena such as boredom, administrative structures
and systems, and the dynamics of public spaces. Recent exhibitions include
The Chatter of Culture, Artspeak, Vancouver; Threshold (cont.) at the
Koerner Library at UBC, and AdmIndex, commissioned by the Audain Gallery at
SFU Woodwards. Recent independent curatorial and editorial projects include
Group Search: art in the library and Ruins in Process: Vancouver Art in the
Sixties, an online digital archive. Brown was the Director/Curator of
Artspeak Gallery from 1999 to 2004.

[Fern Bayer]

Fern Bayer is an independent researcher/curator/archivist. Formerly Chief
Curator of the Government of Ontario Art Collection, she retrieved,
researched, documented, and re-assembled the remains of a public art
collection, which had been amassed by the Government of Ontario from 1855.

1984) reveals the saga of Ontario's "lost art" and is one of the most

Calling all grunters,

Please join us on *Saturday, September 25th, between 5 and 10pm* for an end
of summer Media Lab fundraising BBQ!! This is an opportunity to
get-together with our nearest and dearest to raise funds for our exciting
Media Lab project.

We will be barbecuing burgers and hot dogs (veggie and otherwise), as well
as serving beer and wine for refreshment (cash bar). Please stop in and
catch up with old friends, while enjoying live music by Kempton Dexter and
Joe Haag, our current exhibition featuring Patrick Bergeron and a silent
auction full of prizes to bid on.

Affectionately dubbed, "The Kitchen", our Media Lab project involves a
renovation that will transform our kitchen area into a high-tech lab for
the development and presentation of new media artworks and educational
programming. It will also facilitate the streaming of live content through
our website, allowing us to create more international impact for the
artists we support, and provide the public with a means of accessing our
extensive archives.

We have raised nearly $100,000 through grants and donations in support of
this project, but need your help to raise the last little bit. You can
support us by enjoying a meal and drink at our BBQ, bidding on a silent
auction prize or by making a financial donation. Tax receipts will be
issued for all donations and those donating $250 or more will be listed on
the donor wall in the new Media Lab.

Thank you so much for your support. We look forward to seeing you at the BBQ!
