After A.A...Looking for L(e/a)na: Aesthetics, Appropriation, Allyship and Agency

Masking Collective
(Madelyne Beckles + Delilah
Rosier)

August 28 - October 2, 2021

Exhibition Text by Cason Sharpe

AA Bronson describes Lena (Cries) as a totally politically incorrect story about a 14 year
old black girl, and each chapter puts her through a completely different kind of sexual
scenario. It's like her sexual education in a way. In a way it's kind of a hilarious book, but it
was almost immediately banned in Canada. Lena (Cries) was originally published in 1968,
just shortly before Bronson formed what would become the celebrated queer art collective
General Idea alongside collaborators Felix Partz and Jorge Zontal. Despite the states initial
response (the books seizure was part of a larger crackdown against the sale of illicit
pornography, and thus not a specific target), Lena (Cries) was reprinted twice, first in 1971
as Lena by A.C. McWhortle, and then again, surprisingly, in 2009 as Lana by AA Bronson,
with a new cover by Richard Prince. I dont want to read it, but I could if I wanted to,
because two of my
friends recently uncovered a copy.
