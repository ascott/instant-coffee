Gallery FUKAI is very pleased to announce it’s final exhibit, WHAT NOW? A 
solo show of the gallery’s own founder, owner and director, Sascha 
Yamashita. After taking almost a five year hiatus from art production to focus 
on his gallery and curatorial practice, Yamashita returns to art production with 
a new body of work that conceptually deals with an artists’ return to creating 
after an extended absenteeism and the relevance of the art object. 

Largely, his work questions the interwoven relationships of viewer/art object 
and process/result, while simultaneously implying antithetical views about 
these dynamic relationships. T.W. Adorno said, “A successful work of art is 
not one which resolves contradictions in a spurious harmony, but one which 
expresses the idea of harmony negatively embodying the contradictions, pure 
and uncompromised, in its innermost structure.” Sascha's work references 
literature, art history, religion, philosophy, and often appropriates artworks of 
critical significance. His critiques hinge on polar ideological relationships with 
his imagery inspired by both traditional and pop, Japanese and Canadian 
culture.

Please join us Friday June 07 at 8pm for the gallery's last opening reception. 
Gallery FUKAI is located at 602 Hastings Street at Princess. The exhibit will 
have walk-ins scheduled for June 15 & 29 from 2-4pm. For more information, 
or to schedule a private viewing please email: galleryfukai@gmail.com.

Following the exhibits’ final walk-ins on June 29, one final celebration will be 
held to commemorate the past five years as Gallery atsui/FUKAI. The 
celebration begins at 8pm.


Sascha Yamashita received his BFA from ECUAD and after a much 
respected art career, founded the personally funded Gallery FUKAI (and 
formally atsui) in Vancouver, BC. He has kept up a rigorous showing 
schedule, with more than 40 exhibits and special events, which featured over 
200 artists, spanning the last 5 years. Gallery FUKAI remains reputable 
amongst its peers yet managed to maintain it’s underground roots and 
forefront vision in contemporary art.

Currently, a recent move has made Squamish his new home where 
Yamashita intends to further his artistic and curatorial pursuits.
