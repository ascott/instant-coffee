The Apartment is honored to present a selection of video art made by John Baldessari and Lawrence Weiner between the years 1970 and 1976. Pulling at the formal constraints of what constitutes an artwork, these early projects utilize seemingly mundane objects and actions to question the structurial underpinnings of image making. Delivered with a common sense of wit and precision, thirty years later these works continue to provoke questions about arts role and relationship to daily life.

The five videos which make up the show will include some of John Baldessari’s most iconic works including I Will Not Make Any More Boring Art, 1971, and Teaching a Plant the Alphabet, 1972 along with Lawrence Weiner’s notoriously explicit A Bit of Matter and a Little Bit More, 1976. We will also show a text work by Lawrence Weiner, As Long As It Lasts. This public work will be installed in The Apartment and will remain on public exhibition until the end of the foreign occupation of Iraq. This gesture is to remind us of what in contemporary Canadian society we have not experienced, an occupation by a foreign force. This gesture is also aimed at combating the cultural amnesia that has emerged after an initial frenzy of discourse through artist works and exhibitions, which have abated even though the situation has not. The work shall be a constant reminder to us of the startling invasive conditions of occupation.



The Apartment is also honored to present the first Canadian solo exhibition of art works by Matthew Higgs. Extrapolated from his ongoing immersion in printed matter, Higgs’ art appropriates pages of typography and book design, largely from contemporary art publications, which he isolates as individual framed images. His choices make for simple but resonate visual poetry. In these small gestures Higgs establishes a profound economy of means, not merely in his project of recycling printed material but through a reductive isolation which frees the images to grow a life of their own, free of their initial context and yet an able critique of the language and presentation of art. Known as one of the most erudite critics, curators and artistic producers of our time, Matthew Higgs’ art insists on looking again, observing and considering the formation and contextualization of culture in our time.
