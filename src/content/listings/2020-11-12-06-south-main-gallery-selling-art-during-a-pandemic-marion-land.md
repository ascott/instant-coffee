MARION LANDRY

Fall Triangles, 2016. Oil on Canvas. 58 x 58 inches
Marion Landry (b.1974, Montreal: lives/works: Vancouver) is a painter who uses
geometrical abstraction as a system to manipulate the pictorial space and engage with
the unstable subjectivity of perception. She is interested in the physical properties of
painting and how through the manipulation of pattern, surface, color and illusion of light
a painting can perform a mapping of reality.

Landry holds an BFA from Emily Carr
University of Art + Design. In 2019, she attended
a month-long residence in the heart of Athens, Greece where she focused on a series of
painting using the square as the foundational shape. Following her residency, she was
invited by art historian and curator Elizabeth Plessa to participate in a group show in
Athens.

Landry has been part of numerous solo and group shows across Canada and was a
finalist of the Salt Spring National Prize for the year 2019/2020.

A Shift With Hope, 2020. Acrylic on wood. 96 x 144 inches

During the 2020 spring lockdown in Vancouver, Landry organized the installation of a 4
artists mural at Chambar Restaurant in partnership with Vancouver Mural Festival for
the #MakeArtWhileApart project. Her mural was based on a painting title A Shift
shortlisted for the 2016 IDEA Art Award. The mural intended to uplift the viewer with its
gradient of bright colours and offered a sense of harmony within a rigid grid structure. It
continued Landrys interest with
perception as a subjective reality by playing with
transparency, loose brush marks pushed against rigid line work, and misalignment of the
grid structure to create motion within the pictorial space.

Landry recognizes the importance of the creative process and the wealth of work that
stems from it as an integral part of her practice. Her recent installations regroup a
selection of studio work and painted objects created alongside a final painting in an
attempt to contextualize the work within its community. For her these installations
emphasize on the importance of belonging " or being part of " in which a painting can
be nurtured and grow revealing the hierarchy and relationship between studies,
explorations, ideas and the final piece.
