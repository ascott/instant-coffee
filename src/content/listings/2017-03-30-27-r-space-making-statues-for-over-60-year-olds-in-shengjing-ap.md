Making statues for over-60-year-olds in Shengjing Village
ARTISTWeishan Tan
CURATORSteven Dragonn
EXHIBITION DATESApr 5 - Jun 5, 2017
VENUER Space 123 East 8th VancouverB.C.Canada
OPENING RECEPTIONWed April 05, 2017 6:00 pm - 9:00 pm

In Shengjingcun, in Chinas Hubei province, more than half of its 639 residents 
work in other metropolises, while most of its elderly stay in the village. In 
traditional, rural China, a portrait is hung in the home after the death of an elderly 
person, as a remembrance. However, in Shengjingcun, most of the elderly 
population did not have any portrait of themselves.

Photographer Weishan Tan offered to take portraits of the Shengjingcuns 
residents over the age of sixty, free of charge, which the artist then printed out 
and framed for the villagers. When we look at the portrait of these people, we are 
touched by their faces and the multitude of stories they convey.

Tans series is mounted in R Spaces outdoor window display area.

Weishan Tan was born in Guangdong, China. He started his news photography 
career in 2002, and in 2011 became engaged in news documentary directing 
and production work. He currently works at the Southern Metropolis Daily 
(Guangzhou, China) as Editing Director.

Journey to the West and Edward Hopper
ARTISTXuefeng Li
CURATORSSteven Dragonn, Yi Liu
EXHIBITION DATESApr 5 - Jun 5, 2017
VENUER Space 123 East 8th VancouverB.C.Canada
OPENING RECEPTIONWed April 05, 2017 6:00 pm - 9:00 pm

In the sixteenth-century Chinese novel Journey to the West, the monk and his 
party go through a variety of tribulations, complete a great journey, and finally 
achieve the scriptures and fruition. Here in the farthest west, we are still 
learning, and the monk and his apprentices seem not to have any further news. 
What are they doing now?

The characters of the great American painter Edward Hopper are expressionless, 
pensive"as if they lead lonely and boring lives. Artist Xuefeng Li borrows 
Hoppers schema for his fictional vision of the monk and his apprentices after 
their journey to the west. The photos were taken in Maershan, an abandoned 
village thirty kilometres from Shenyang, China. Once a magnificent plan, it has 
become a broken dream, where abandoned houses look lonely.

Xuefeng Li was born in Shenyang, China, and graduated in 2010 from the Lu 
Xun Academy of Fine Arts with a BFA. Since 2014, he has been undertaking an 
MFA at Lu Xun Academy, studying contemporary Chinese painting and language 
research.

ABOUT R SPACE
R Space Art Development Foundation (R Space) is a non-profit art organization 
run by Canadian Chinese artists in Vancouver, B.C. Canada. Launched in 
November 2016, R Space aims to promote cultural exchange through art and 
focuses on the clashing and merging of Chinese and Canadian cultures with an 
emphasize on the contemporary cultural theme of immigration, and introducing 
Chinese culture to the mainstream. R Space brought up the concept of to dock 
with mainstream thoughts. 

R Space is named after and the Chinese character "Rong" which means to 
merge. Our mission is to encourage the integration of the East-West cultural and 
aesthetic ideas. The space serves as a professional art platform for Chinese 
immigrants and diverse local audiences to create art works, discuss issues and 
research various topics.
