Jump In, It’s Warm.

Opening: Thursday, Decemeber 5th at 7 p.m
Location: AVENUE, 165 E.Hastings at Main Street, Vancouver

Tommy Chain and Gabi Dao explore the serendipity of recurring motifs that are woven into the programming of amateur applications such as word processors, sandbox gameplay and IRL vignettes. The sculptures and video animations are reflections of 2D shape, form, and composition that have implicated themselves in virtual and physical 3D realms. 

Translation inevitably generates information while simultaneously abstracting it or even deleting segments of it, the limiting ideals of ‘flatness’ and ‘purity’ which pre-occupied modernist painting practices as well as other medium-specific concerns, could be framed as a point of critique of the forms in which we obtain knowledge. 

The two explore the ways in which the breadth of various information delivery systems, from books and journals to videogames and the internet, ostensibly shape our structure of discourse through historical narrative and the questioning of the authorship of said information.
      
The possibility of a common visual language that involves rudimentary shapes, forms, and narrative structures perhaps reflects an aestheticized learning process inherent in analog approaches and in amateur technologies. Is this language a means to implement some form of structure to cushion turbulent waves of information, or is the language a structure in and of itself?
