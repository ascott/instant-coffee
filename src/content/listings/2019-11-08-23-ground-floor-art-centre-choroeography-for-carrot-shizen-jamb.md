Choreography for Carrot takes various figures of speech as points of departure for
explorations of how clich and common phrases reflect broad societal mores around
value, labour, and creativity. The exhibition is comprised of two videos: one which is
viewed through a peephole and is set in a fictive artist's studio and one which features
an interview with the
fictive artist credited with the creation of the former video. The
videos satirically explore questions concerning the conceptualization of art on display
and the myth surrounding arts production.

Shizen Jambor is an artist of Chinese-Thai and European settler descent based in the
unceded territories of the Musqueam, Squamish, Tsleil-Waututh nations. Her work
incorporates video, sculpture, and installation to investigate and satirize mythologies
around the production of art and constructions of identity. Theatrics, comedy, and visual
metaphor are important elements in her work. She uses these elements to explore
questions around performativity and translations between language and ideology.
Jambor graduated from ECUAD with a BFA in Visual Arts in 2017. Recent exhibitions
include High Performance Object at Charles Clark Gallery and Gloves Off at the Hatch
Gallery.
