THE BLUE CABIN EXHIBITION OPENING RECEPTION
THURSDAY JUNE 14 7 PM - 10 PM
AT GRUNT GALLERY
JEREMY AND SUS BORSOS
CURATED BY GLENN ALTEEN

In this exhibition, artists Jeremy and Sus Borsos present a body of work
that documents their journey of the Blue Cabin remediation, while providing
us a history of the cabin before Al Neil and Carole Itters tenancy, and
offering us new insights into its earlier inhabitants.

ABOUT THE ARTISTS:

Jeremy Borsos studied at Emily Carr School of Art and later attended the
Art Students League in New York where he studied 19th-century classical
painting. He worked in many facets of the motion picture industry before
entering into a multidisciplinary practice that includes architecture,
writing, photography, installation, painting, and video. In his works,
ephemeral histories are redefined through a course of material discovery
and translated through the language and dialects of the archive. He
exhibits his works nationally and internationally.

Sus Borsos was born in Denmark. After studying statistics and computer
sciences at Copenhagen University, she worked managing Scandinavian Stage
Design, where she oversaw the creation of stages for major events in Europe
in the 1980s and early 90s. She moved to Canada in 1992, and worked with
Jeremy to build their home, which was created from salvaged architectural
fragments. Since then, she has worked in multiple creative capacities,
including digital film and sound editing, and the design and construction
of multiple residences.

In early 2018, Jeremy and Sus completed a full remediation of the Blue
Cabin, the studio component of a soon to be launched floating artist
residency in Vancouver, Canada. The Borsos current projects include the
redesign and rebuild of a studio and living space in Athens, Greece. They
live and work on Mayne Island, British Columbia and in Athens, Greece.

The Blue Cabin project is led by grunt gallery, along with Other Sights for
Artists Projects, and Creative Cultural Collaborations (C3).

grunt gallery is generously supported by the Canada Council for the Arts,
the British Columbia Arts Council, the City of Vancouver, the Province of
British Columbia, and the Audain Foundation for the Visual Arts.

Accessibility: grunt gallery is a wheelchair accessible venue. Street
