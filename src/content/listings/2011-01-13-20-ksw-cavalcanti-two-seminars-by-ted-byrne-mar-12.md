Fee: $40 - no one will be turned away.

Summary: 
This course aims to look at potential relationships between, and 
futures for, narrative in cinema and literature. As a starting point, the 
course asks: what is implicit about the way in which a work of film 
narrates as opposed to the way a work of literature narrates? The 
course posits that the answer lies in the inherent differences 
between the two mediums and sets out to explore those differences 
through the critical lens of film and text.

Cavalcanti: Translation, Politics & Ethics in "Donna me prega" runs 
three weeks, on Sundays, April 10-24. 
Location: KSW Meeting Space - Community Meeting Room, 
W2 Community Media Arts, 
Woodward's Heritage BuildingFee: $30 - no one will be turned away. 
Capacity: 15 - spaces limited. 
Summary: This seminar will investigate translations of Calvalcanti’s 
thirteenth century song, “Donna me prega” [A woman asks me] in 
three sessions. It will begin with the facilitator’s own 
literal/interlinear translation, move on to Ezra Pound’s translations, 
and conclude with Louis Zukofsky’s translations—both “A”9 and the 
translation into Brooklynese, “A foin lass bodders me.” The course 
will explore the material in relation to poetics, translation, 
psychology, politics, science, atheism, woman, masculinity, and 
orientalism. For more information and registration, please visit the 
KSW Courses and Workshop Site, Seminar: Cavalcanti: Translation, 
Politics & Ethics in "Donna me prega" -- or copy and paste this 
shortlink: [http://wp.me/p1281q-1y](http://wp.me/p1281q-1y) **Facilitator Bios: 

Ted Byrne is a poet who has always used translation to prime the 
pump. Current work includes translations of Louise Labé’s and 
Guido Cavalcanti’s sonnets.

Aaron Peck is the author of The Bewilderments of Bernard Willis 
(Pedlar Press) and, in collaboration with artists Adam Harrison and 
Dominic Osterried, Letters to the Pacific (Publication Studio). His 
fiction has appeared in Golden Handcuffs Review, Maxtrix Magazine 
and W, while his recent art criticism and journalism have appeared 
in artforum.com, 01 Magazine, Art Papers, Canadian Art, C 
Magazine, Pyramid Power, and Fillip. He also has contributed to 
numerous exhibition catalogs in Canada and has had an article 
appear in La Fábrica’s Spanish-language magazine Matador. He 
lives in Vancouver, BC, and currently teaches Emily Carr University.
