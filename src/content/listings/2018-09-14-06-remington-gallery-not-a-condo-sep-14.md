Not a Condo
September 14 8pm-12am
Remington Gallery Vancouver
108 East Hastings st.
www.remingtongallery.net

Not A Condo is about life in the city. Mainly Vancouver, but the thoughts
extend outward. The work exhibited is about the joys and stresses of city
life as shown through the structures, moments and expressions of the people
who live there.Each artist in turn strips the city down to an aspect; Adam
Flewelling makes improvised collages of house hold objects, Alex Waber
strips away the traces of most liveable city letting only separated
structures remain, Benjamin Poulton explores individual relationships to
life in the city and how it can pull us in different directions, and
Trenton Eleque James shares his own journey through city life in which he
pushes through his personal comfort zones to capture fleeting moments of
beauty.

Adam Flewelling
https://cargocollective.com/AdamFlewelling/

Adam relies on the practice of collage to experiment with ideas quickly and
effectively. He uses chance observation to create hastily produced
arrangements which are often glued according to the laws of
chance.Through this process, he abandons logic and embraces irrationality
and chaos.

Adam Flewelling (b. 1995) is a multidisciplinary artist living and working
in Vancouver, British Columbia. He received his Bachelor of Fine Arts with
a major in Photography from Emily Carr University of Art + Design. Adam
works primarily in collage and photography and has shown in multiple group
shows in Vancouver.

Trenton Eleque James
www.elequejames.com

Trenton Eleque James (b. 1987) is a passionate street photographer who has
quickly mastered the art of hiding in plain sight with his camera in tow.
Born and raised in CT, Trenton was a relatively shy child. His shyness
progressed, and ultimately led to a diagnosis of social anxiety and
alternative schools as a teen. A concerned teacher once wrote of Trenton,
He clearly avoids social situations with those with whom he is unfamiliar.
He is overly concerned about other people staring at him. Ironically, he
now spends much of his day staring at others who often go unseen, and
purposely places himself in situations where he is not only seen by
strangers, but must also engage. Street photography has allowed Trenton to
engage with random strangers in ways that often highlight humanity in an
exceptionally rare way. His passion for street photography also pushes
Trenton to face his anxiety head on each and every day.

Benjamin Poulton
https://www.benjaminpoulton.com/
The Divide is Uneven is a topography exploring personal feelings of
division. Through the use of the subjects vulnerability and the repetitive
abstract nature of the composition, I hope to show that there is a
familiarity to our struggles. That one can find themselves in these
photographs. That through the visceral experience, one can find meaning.

Benjamin Poulton (b. 1979) is a photo-based artist living in Vancouver.
Originally from Montreal, his interest in photography developed in his
early twenties while attending college. After graduation, he spent the next
decade pursuing a career as a professional outdoor adventure guide while
still exploring and maintaining his photographic interests. In the latter
years of his career, he became increasingly curious about photography as an
art form, which lead him to re-evaluate his direction in life.
Currently, he is a working artist and continues to study photography (since
2014) under the mentorship of local art photographers. He has also attended
Langara College in Vancouver, honing his studio skills and darkroom
practice. To date, his work has been shown at the Remington Gallery,
Interurban Gallery, Gallery 49 and the Queen Elizabeth Theatre. He is also
the recipient of three Vancouver Foundation Arts Grants as well as a
technical instructor for analogue photography.

Alex Waber
www.alexwaber.com
The Vancouver Studies are an on going project about life in the city.
Watching the city constantly change, Waber started to document the elements
that make up the city; small objects, roads, moments and structures. The
work exhibited here are the structures. Waber has intentionally and
obviously cut out the greenery and the people leaving you to ask what makes
a city, what do we want out of it and what do we have? Printed on an office
printer the objects reflect ideas of production, consumption and longevity
while being presented as a display in a realtors office. Feel free to take
one home.

Alex Waber (b. 1987) is a professional fashion and portrait photographer by
day and fine art photographer by night. He has had work published
internationally, including portraits in The Guardian and Rolling Stones,
and exhibited across the Lower Mainland and at the Las Angeles Centre for
Digital Art. He also teaches several photography courses for kids at Arts
Umbrella on Granville Island.
