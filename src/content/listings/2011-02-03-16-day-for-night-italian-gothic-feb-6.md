DAY FOR NIGHT and guest presenters Donato Mancini and 
Guinevere
Pencarrick present a Sunday afternoon double bill of Italian Gothic
Horror classics starring Barbara Steele (Black Sunday, Fellini's 8
1/2).

Nightmare Castle (1965) directed by Mario Caiano
104 minutes

As she does more often than most, Barbara Steele plays two roles in
Nightmare Castle: the raven-haired wife of an immortality-obsessed 
mad
scientist, Dr. Steven Arrowsmith, as well as her own blonde-haired,
half crazy sister and heir. Barbara Steele #1 is tortured to death
along with her lover when the doctor discovers them bonking in the
greenhouse. The doctor then develops a Lady Bathory-like 
technique
using virgins’ blood to restore youth to his own lover, while working
to both marry and drive mad Barbara Steele #2 in order to take 
control
of her fortune. Things go increasingly wrong. Nightmare Castle is
lugubriously paced, shot in an icy chiaroscuro.

The She Beast (1966) directed by Michael Reeves
79 minutes

Campy and hyperactive, visually clever at every turn, The She-Beast 
is
set in the countryside of Transylvania, where a young couple on their
honeymoon stumble into the middle of an ancient curse on a small
village. An 18th century witch is due for resurrection, to take her
revenge on the descendants of her murderers. Unfortunately for
everyone, the only person to take the curse seriously is a skinny,
alcoholic old count Von Helsing (John Karlsen, looking scarily like
George Bernard Shaw). Barbara Steele, as usual in her gothic roles, 
is
used more as an effective visual element than as a dramatic 
actress.

We will be screening these films as part of our DAY FOR NIGHT 
series
on Sunday February 6th.

About the Presenters:

The interdisciplinary practice of Donato Mancini focuses mainly on
bookworks, poetry, and text-based visual art. His first two books of
procedural and visual writing, Ligatures (2005) and Æthel (2007) 
were
both nominated for the ReLit Award. He also co-directed the world’s
first genuine in-world avatar documentary, AVATARA (Centre A,
Vancouver 2003), now part of the UbuWeb international archive of
experimental film and video. His third collection of poetry and visual
works, Buffet World, is due in April 2011.

Guinevere Pencarrick majored in drawing and printmaking at the 
Emily
Carr Institute of Art and Design in the early 2000s. Now focusing on
book art and collaborative drawing; she has also worked extensively
with community art programming and housing in the DTES and 
South
Granville area for the past six years. Check out Perro Verlag Books 
by
Artists to see recent work.

About Day For Night: Film in the Afternoon.

Every Sunday, come down to the Waldorf's lower-level clubhouse to
spend the afternoon in the dark with friends and a stiff cocktail to
watch some of the world's great films selected by the hotel's resident
cineaste, Anu Sahota. DAY FOR NIGHT presents an intimate 
environment
to share in a love of classics and eccentrics from Hollywood,
documentaries from all over, NFB shorts and Canadian television, 
and
rarely-screened art films. DAY FOR NIGHT will surprise you with 
guest
presenters, archival footage and broadcast ephemera - maintaining 
a
focus on Vancouver's culture and history. The perfect tonic to end a
rousing weekend, DAY FOR NIGHT is the Waldorf Hotel's 
celebration of
film.
