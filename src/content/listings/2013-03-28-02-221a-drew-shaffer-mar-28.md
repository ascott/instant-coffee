SAD 1: On Objects and Melancholy, Drew Shaffer
MAR 28

Melancholy, it seems, hangs with particular heaviness around objects.
We soothe ourselves with material things; objects surround us, define
our world, yet remain fundamentally silent, inert and indifferent.
Invested with memories, they are ultimately a reminder of our own
mutable selves-- and our fear of what we will lose.

The history of our attachment to objects reflects this human condition.
Since the 1700s, porcelain has been such a valuable commodity that, if
damaged, professional menders were employed, often at great
expense, to repair the damage with metal staples and a bizarre array
of adhesives (including one memorable recipe that involved cheese).
In some examples, the repairs are so extensive and obvious, the
objects appear as Frankenstein-esque shadows of their former selves.
Regardless, these repairs are a testament to someones deep regard
for a particular object, and the ingenuity of the craftspeople that
repaired them. These objects, now seen through the lens of history,
are imbued with a tragic and moving beauty.

Drew Shaffer is a Vancouver-based artist and antique dealer with
Baker's Dozen Antiques on Main Street. His work, both in the shop and
in the studio, is centred around people, objects and the relationships
between the two.

SAD is a series of talks accompanying the exhibition Stratiform | Kara
Uzelman. Curated by Vanessa Kwan and Kimberly Phillips, these
presentations investigate objects and practices in the context of
contemporary forms of melancholy. For more information, visit
