Vancouver Art Gallery | Notes From Elsewhere : Screening and Panel 
Discussion | SEP 7

1pm |John Smith's Hotel Diaries, Screening in the Gallery
3pm | Panel Discussion with Stephanie Rebick, Kevin James and Jenni Pace-
Presnell

This final event for the exhibition 'Grand Hotel: Redesigning Modern Life' 
considers the hotel as a place that frames our social order. Beginning with a 
screening of John Smith's internationally acclaimed 'Hotel Diaries,' the event 
is followed by a panel discussion featuring visual analyses of hotels as social 
spaces with scholars Jenni Pace Presnell and Kevin James. Introduced and 
moderated by Assistant Curator Stephanie Rebick.

Hotel Diaries consists of a series of video recordings made in the world's hotel 
rooms over a period of six years, all of which relate personal experiences to 
the current conflicts in the Middle East. In these works, which play upon 
chance and co-incidence, the hotel room is employed as a 'found' film set, 
where the architecture, furnishing and decoration become the means by which 
the filmmaker's small adventures are linked to major world events. Works in 
the series include Frozen War (Ireland, 2001), Museum Piece (Germany, 
2004), Throwing Stones (Switzerland, 2004), B & B (England, 2005), 
Pyramids/Skunk (The Netherlands 2006/7), Dirty Pictures (Palestine 2007) 
and Six Years Later (Ireland 2007).

Jenni Pace will present a travelogue of colonial resorts ranging from the late 
nineteenth century to the interwar period and concluding in the post-colonial 
era, highlighting the uses of architectural forms and public space and, later, 
preservation discourse, to mediate ever-changing metropolitan-colonial 
relations.
Kevin James will consider the relics of hotel and inn visitors' book where 
morsels of text yield insight into travellers' often comedic accounts of the 
culture of hotel leisure, foregrounding the voice of the guest and revealing 
complex identities of travel writers. The books' banalities and excruciating 
verse and prose inscriptions, despite the low literary esteem within which it 
was held, proves an indispensable feature of travel writing.

Jenni Pace is a historian of modern art and architecture and UBC doctoral 
candidate. Her research interests include the visualization of housing and 
public space within post-war urban expansion schemes, and relational 
practices that challenge end-solution planning. Her work on metropolitan-
colonial planning discourses extends to resort hotels and leisure activities 
that served to negotiate and rationalize the terms of global modernity.

Kevin James is Associate Professor of History at the University of Guelph in 
Ontario. His research interests lie in the social and cultural history of 
Victorian travel in Britain and Ireland. Kevin's forthcoming book 'Tourism, Land 
and Landscape in Ireland: The Commodification of Culture' explores the 
history of tourism, hospitality and rural Irish society. The study of these 
themes is extended in his current research on nineteenth-century inns and 
hotels. Both projects have been supported by the Social Sciences and 
Humanities Research Council of Canada.

Free for Members or with Gallery admission.

Vancouver Art Gallery | FOCUS ON THE COLLECTION | Artist Talk: Tom 
Sherman | SEP 10

The artist will give a long-distance talk related to his work 'Exclusive Memory' 
(1987). This multi-channel video and aluminum panel is part of the Vancouver 
Art Gallery's permanent collection, on display as part of 'Portraits in Time' an 
exhibition of video and time-based works of portraiture from the Gallery 
collection.

Canadian/American artist Tom Sherman has worked with video and related 
media since 1970, often examining the pervasive presence of technology in 
daily life. The videotapes that form the core of 'Exclusive Memory' are based 
upon excerpts from a six-hour monologue in which the artist narrates his 
attempts to provide a transfer of experience to a computer-based robotic 
device with human attributes. Sherman explores the relationship between 
people and machines, and his monologue anticipates a day when robots will 
manifest biological attributes. The installation also comprises a computer-
generated video scroll that presents random, two-colour variations on an 
image of a sleeping robot. Not a conventional portrait, Sherman's work 
contemplates the nature of human experience, how it might be captured in the 
memories of machines and what it means to be human.

Tom Sherman is an artist and writer. He works in video, radio and 
performance. His interdisciplinary art has been exhibited internationally, 
including shows at the National Gallery of Canada, the Museum of Modern Art 
in New York, and the Venice Biennale. The Banff Centre Press published 
"Before and After the I-Bomb: An Artist in the Information Environment," an 
extensive anthology of his texts in 2002. He was awarded the Bell Canada 
Award for excellence in video art in 2003 and received the Governor General's 
Award in Visual and Media Arts in 2010. Sherman is a professor in the 
Department of Transmedia at Syracuse University in New York.

Free for Members or with Gallery admission.
