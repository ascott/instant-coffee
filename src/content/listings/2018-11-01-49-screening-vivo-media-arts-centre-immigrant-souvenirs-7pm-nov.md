"Immigrant Souvenirs"

Screening of:
"(4-29) Sa-I-Gu: From Women's Perspectives" (1993) dir. Dai Sil Kim-Gibson - 
40min

And Yet We Survived (1995)" dir. Hwangbo Kangja - 41min

The name of the screening references a chapter in Svetlana Boym's book titled The 
Future of Nostalgia, which the lasting memories at the point of geographical 
departure represents the very last modernity living and breathing in the context of its 
origin. As a ritual rescue of the past, this narrative becomes substituted as a 
"souvenir" to meant to symbolize a previous life, moments before rapid change takes 
places in the form of immigration and/or exile.

The readings contextualize the Korean diasporic narratives from early to mid 1990s, 
featuring documentaries by Dai Sil Kim-Gibson "(4-29) Sa-I-Gu: From Women's 
Perspectives" (1993) - a look at the 1992 Los Angeles Riots through a series of 
interviews by Korean Women - and Hwangbo Kangja's " " And Yet 
We Survived (1995) from VIVO's Archives - interviews survivors of Korean "Comfort 
Women", speaking on their experiences after a 50+ year silence.

This screening event is a continuation of Text to Speech: Svetlana Boym reading 
group that takes place on Thursday November 22.

VIVO Media Arts Centre would like to acknowledge that we are on unceded territory 
of the Coast Salish Peoples, including the territories of the Musqueam, Squamish, 
and Tsleil-Waututh Nations.
