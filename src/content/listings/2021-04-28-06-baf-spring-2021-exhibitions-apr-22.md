BAF Gallery Spring 2021 Exhibitions

Rydel Cerezo: New Ending
Kriss Munsya: Monolithic Introspection
Pippe Lattey: String Together

258 East 1st Avenue
April 22 - June 19, 2021
Open to the public 12-5, Tuesday to Saturday

On Thursday, April 22nd, we will be unveiling new exhibitions from resident artists
Rydel Cerezo and Kriss Munsya, and a new Garage installation from
Pippa Lattey.

Due to current COVID-19 safety regulations, there will be no opening reception.
Instead, we invite you to visit us alone or with your household between Thursday, April
22nd and Saturday, June 19th.

For photographer Rydel Cerezo, on the other side of failure is a world full of possibility.
Taking aesthetic cues from the high-drama emotionality of Filipino soap operas and
religious rituals, Cerezos new photographic exhibition, "New Ending," points to the
beauty and awkwardness of failure and the experience of coming of age.

Deeply personal, the photographs are infused with Cerezo's perspective as a queer
Catholic Filipino man navigating desire, cultural conditioning and shame. Through the
work, he processes his own entanglement with colonial mentality, religion, and
racialized identity.

"Monolithic Introspection" is a new photography project by Kriss Munsya that centers
the experiences of communities disproportionately impacted by environmental racism.

The series
illustrates the tensions that exist between white exceptionalism, entitlement
to land (its resources and peoples,) and the finitude of such exploitation. It beckons
interrogations of our personal relationships with one another, our environments, and
how we distribute worthiness.

In a new installation titled "String Together," Pippa Lattey has draped a black line
across the interior of the Garage and strung it with colourful plastic detritus"chunky
jugs, broken toys, and pieces of lovingly collected junk. Like an oversized necklace, the
line moves in space, causing the strung pieces to shift and slide.

When Lattey works with movement, she is interested in simple rhythms and patterns
that can produce complex and fascinating movements. Through repetition, these
patterns unfold over time to create new forms.
