SKODEN INDIGENOUS FILM FESTIVAL 2022
The 4th annual Skoden Indigenous Film Festival is back in-person to screen films
exclusively by Indigenous filmmakers from across Turtle Island! The student led festival
runs over three days from Thursday March 31, 2022 until Saturday April 2, 2022 and
will be available online through Vimeo until April 11, 2022.

March 31 - 6:30pm - Skoden Sampler
*This event will be held in the Westminster Savings Theatre at SFU's Surrey Campus
(13450 102 Ave Unit 110, Surrey, BC) and provides a
sampling of films from across the
festival to set the tone for this amazing weekend!

April 1 & 2 - Skoden Indigenous Film Festival:
*The festival will take place in the Djavad Mowafaghian Cinema at SFU's Goldcorp
Centre for the Arts (149 W Hastings St, Vancouver, BC)

April 1 - 5pm - Opening Program: "Stoodis! (Let's Do this!) + Opening Ceremony and
remarks
April 1 - 7:30pm - Program 2: "Evolution Revolution"

April 2 - 1pm - Youth/Family Program: "Carrying The Fire"
April 2 - 3pm - Matine: "Where We Come From, Where We're Going"
April 2 - 5pm - Closing Program: "Skoden! (Let's Go Then!) + Witness Ceremony,
Award Ceremony, Q+A

Tickets are sliding-scale to allow accessibility for all!
Buy tickets through the Eventbrite portal on our website:
https://www.sfu.ca/skoden/Festival/skoden-indigenous-film-festival-2022.html
Check us out on Instagram to keep updated in the run-up to the festival and to find out
ways to contribute to the festival. Instagram:
https://www.instagram.com/skodenfest/

We hope you will join us for our in-person screenings and take the opportunity to
immerse yourself in the work of incredible Indigenous creatives!

Our class would like to acknowledge the ongoing governance of the xmky"m
(Musqueam), Skwxw7mesh (Squamish), Sl"lwta"/Selilwitulh (Tsleil-Waututh), and
kik"m (Kwikwetlem) Nations, on whose traditional ancestral territory since time
immemorial we are privileged to house our SFU campuses and to continue our
education upon.

Currently, there are over 200 nations in BC, and of the four nations our university is
situated upon, none have signed or agreed with the BC or Canadian government about
land rights. Our goal is to contribute to undoing colonialism through our film festival
platform, to promote
proactive reconciliation by listening, learning, and sharing with
Indigenous film and filmmakers.
