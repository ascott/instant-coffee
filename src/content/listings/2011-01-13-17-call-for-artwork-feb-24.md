POIESIS:A Journal of the Arts & Communication Volume 13, 2010: 
Artwork
submissions
Deadline: February 24, 2011

POIESIS is an international periodical which annually publishes 
research from
the fields of Expressive Arts and Media and Communication. We are 
currently
accepting submissions for artwork to include in the journal.

This year's theme for the 13th annual issue: THE HEAD OF 
ORPHEUS: Voice,
Song and the Poetics of Resistance and Transformation

Legend tells us that after the Thracian women tore the body of 
Orpheus into
pieces, they threw his head into the Hebrus river. As it floated 
downstream, he
kept on singing. In more recent times, Victor Jara 
sang “Venceremos” a cappella
when his fingers were broken by Pinochet’s soldiers and he could 
no longer play
the guitar.

Can voice and song be vehicles for resistance and transformation?
What is there in the human voice that is capable of transcending 
ordinary speech
and creating new worlds?

For this special issue of POIESIS, we invite submissions which 
explore the
potential of the voice for transformation and resistance. 
Contributions may also
address the question of whether new media change our 
relationship to the
voice. Does the therapeutic emphasis on bodily expression run 
aground on what
Derrida called the “metaphysics of presence”? Can voice and 
speech mediated
through cyberspace introduce new possibilities for communication 
or is there
necessarily something lost in transmission?
If circumstances permit, spoken word, sound and musical 
contributions will
be included in the issue in audio format. Please contact us for 
submission
guidelines.

20. KSW: Cavalcanti | two seminars by Ted BYRNE | MAR 12
the Kootenay School of Writing presents

two seminars: 

Cavalcanti: Translation, Politics & Ethics in "Donna me prega" [A 
Woman Asks Me] - facilitated by Ted BYRNE

&

Narrative, Cinema, Literature - facilitated by Aaron PECK

*****

Narrative, Cinema, Literature runs four weeks, on Saturday, March 
12 to April 2. 
Location: KSW Meeting Space - Community Meeting Room, 
W2 Community Media Arts, 
Woodward's Heritage Building
Fee: $40 - no one will be turned away. 
Capacity: 15 - spaces limited

Summary: 
This course aims to look at potential relationships between, and 
futures for, narrative in cinema and literature. As a starting point, the 
course asks: what is implicit about the way in which a work of film 
narrates as opposed to the way a work of literature narrates? The 
course posits that the answer lies in the inherent differences 
between the two mediums and sets out to explore those differences 
through the critical lens of film and text.

For more information and registration, please visit the KSW 
Courses and Workshop Site, Seminar: Narrative, Cinema, 
Literature -- or copy and paste this shortlink: http://wp.me/p1281q-15 

*
Cavalcanti: Translation, Politics & Ethics in "Donna me prega" runs 
three weeks, on Sundays, April 10-24. 
Location: KSW Meeting Space - Community Meeting Room, 
W2 Community Media Arts, 
Woodward's Heritage BuildingFee: $30 - no one will be turned away. 
Capacity: 15 - spaces limited. 
Summary: This seminar will investigate translations of Calvalcanti’s 
thirteenth century song, “Donna me prega” [A woman asks me] in 
three sessions. It will begin with the facilitator’s own 
literal/interlinear translation, move on to Ezra Pound’s translations, 
and conclude with Louis Zukofsky’s translations—both “A”9 and the 
translation into Brooklynese, “A foin lass bodders me.” The course 
will explore the material in relation to poetics, translation, 
psychology, politics, science, atheism, woman, masculinity, and 
orientalism. For more information and registration, please visit the 
KSW Courses and Workshop Site, Seminar: Cavalcanti: Translation, 
Politics & Ethics in "Donna me prega" -- or copy and paste this 
shortlink: http://wp.me/p1281q-1y **Facilitator Bios: 

Ted Byrne is a poet who has always used translation to prime the 
pump. Current work includes translations of Louise Labé’s and 
Guido Cavalcanti’s sonnets.

Aaron Peck is the author of The Bewilderments of Bernard Willis 
(Pedlar Press) and, in collaboration with artists Adam Harrison and 
Dominic Osterried, Letters to the Pacific (Publication Studio). His 
fiction has appeared in Golden Handcuffs Review, Maxtrix Magazine 
and W, while his recent art criticism and journalism have appeared 
in artforum.com, 01 Magazine, Art Papers, Canadian Art, C 
Magazine, Pyramid Power, and Fillip. He also has contributed to 
numerous exhibition catalogs in Canada and has had an article 
appear in La Fábrica’s Spanish-language magazine Matador. He 
lives in Vancouver, BC, and currently teaches Emily Carr University.

****
Both seminars are part of the Kootenay School of Writing Courses 
and Workshops Program. For more information, the schedule, 
updates, and registration, please visit: 
http://kswcourses.wordpress.com
