Artist Talk: Arni Haraldsson and Jayce Salloum
Thursday July 18
6:00pm
Audain Gallery

Please join us in the gallery for a free informal talk with the artists
Arni Haraldsson and Jayce Salloum, in conversation with Amy Kazymerchyk,
the curator of the Audain Gallery.

At first look, Haraldsson and Salloum's works in the exhibition couldn't
appear more different: Haraldsson's clear images of in-situ architecture
contrast with Salloum's exhaustive installation of ephemera. Formal
differences aside, both artists equally employ extensive research and
fieldwork to engage directly with the subjects of their work. In this case,
for Haraldsson a study of Chandigarh, India, a city designed by Le
Corbusier, and for Salloum a self-reflexive examination of the production
of knowledge about Lebanon and the Middle East. With a shared critical and
investigatory process in common, their works present comparable
concentrations of information at different registers of focus: Haraldsson's
from a relative distance and Salloum's with a personalized closeness. By
foregrounding the indexical signs of the social, political, and economic
forces that surround their subjects, both artists also share a
documentarian quality, revealing the stories that are materialized at the
level of everyday life.

Artists' Biographies

Arni Haraldsson has exhibited his photographic work nationally and
internationally in numerous solo and group exhibitions. Examining urban
spaces as landscapes, his work explores the faded utopian principals of
modernism, particularly as expressed in architecture and the design and
life of the city, which become emblematic of larger social structures. With
a MA from UBC, Haraldsson is an Associate Professor in the Faculty of
Visual Art and Material Practice at Emily Carr University of Art and Design.

Based in Vancouver, Jayce Salloum works at the intersection of
installation, photography, drawing, performance, text, and video. He is
also active as a curator, workshop facilitator, and cultural activist,
coordinating an array of cultural projects. Exploring identity, migration,
and the shifting borders and territories of the contemporary world, his
practice exists within and between the personal, local, and transnational.
Salloum's work has been exhibited widely nationally and internationally.

About the Exhibition

Running from May 9 to August 17, 2013, The Biography of Images: Parallel
Biographies is the third in a series of group exhibitions based on the
Austrian Federal Photography Collection, in this case bringing together
artists from Vienna and Vancouver. The exhibition includes work by the
Vienna-based artists Thomas Freiler, Krüger & Pardeller, Tatiana Lecomte,
Christian Mayer, Sissa Micheli, and Anita Witek, and the Vancouver-based
artists Arni Haraldsson, Tim Lee, Kyla Mallett, Jayce Salloum, Kevin
Schmidt, and Elizabeth Zvonar.

The Biography of Images: Parallel Biographies is curated by Sabine Bitter
and Ruth Horak and is presented with support from the Austrian Federal
Ministry for Education, Arts and Culture (BMUKK).
