KELLY HOLMES 
Finite Appearances

WAAP is pleased to present this exhibition, curated by graduating students from 
UBC's Art History and Visual Arts department, as a way to provide space for 
independent and emerging curatorial talent. 

*** 

Kelly Holmes exhibition Finite Appearances consists of ephemeral pieces created 
from imprinted bodily traces of intimate and mundane everyday rituals, now living in 
documentary media. The artworks also express a loss of the body and its re-
performance in documentations.

The body has been mobilized in response to a formalist notion where the artist could 
extract their presence and embed it an autonomous work of art. In the context of a 
heavily-male dominated practice, some of the most creative, subversive and potent 
use of the body/performance art came from female artists, such as Ana Mendieta, 
Carolee Schneemann and Marina Abramovic. Despite the effective blurring of 
subject/object roles of the artists, the ephemeral nature of performance/body works 
left many ideas to be explored regarding documentation, traces and the body 
presented as a loss. 

Aware of this discourse, Holmes art balances the tragedy evoked through the 
absence of the body as well as its undeniable presence. Through exaggerated 
repetition -- a theme that emerges in many of her works -- she negotiates the fragile 
balance between the ephemerality and the obsessive elements of love. Also found in 
Holmes work is the repeating action is the notion of practicing for the perfect 
moment, and never arriving to a certain end result. She utilizes common and readily 
available materials to perform actions that bring to the fore objects and moments 
that we have turned blind to our constant exposure to them. Holmes presence thus 
brings to the forth an eerie experience of being confronted with absent moments of 
our daily lives. 

Kelly Holmes is a visual artist based in Vancouver British Columbia, originally from 
Calgary Alberta. She realized her passion for the arts at a young age through artistic 
mentorships and portraiture classes. The human body has been a primary focus in 
Holmes work, her fascination for portraiture began an interest for human beings and 
their physical presence that has permeated into her present contemporary practice. 
Through recent years, her practice has shifted from painting to a contemporary focus 
not fixed to any defined medium. Holmes is currently in her last year of her BFA 
Honors in Visual Arts at the University of British Columbia.
