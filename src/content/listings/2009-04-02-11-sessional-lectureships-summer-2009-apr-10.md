Elizabeth Zvonar: There Art No Rules
Western Front
Opening April 10, 8pm
April 11 - May 16, 2009

THERE ARE NO RULES, a solo exhibition of new works by Elizabeth Zvonar,
pays tribute to and embraces an unconventional approach to art making and
offers possibilities for rethinking our understandings of art history. The
1972 text, History of Art by H.W. Janson, is the starting point for
Zvonarâ&#65533;&#65533;s collages and porcelain sculptures. The handcut collages, intimate
in scale, take pages of Jansonâ&#65533;&#65533;s text of iconographic paintings and
sculptures and combine them based on their similar compositions. The
juxtaposition of familiar images from a variety of historical contexts
complicates the readings of originals, and enables them to be seen anew,
radically divorced from their original contexts.

Also borrowing from the images from the same text are porcelain sculptures
made of slip cast replicas of an index finger. The clusters of fingers use
as a guideline, compositions found from History of Art. Oscillating between
figurative and abstraction, the sculptures resemble both, resulting in
organic, baroque aesthetic objets dâ&#65533;&#65533;art. Fingers feature predominantly in
early painting particularly those done during the Baroque period when the
narratives presented, particularly in painting, were simplified and
dramatized as a means of appealing on an emotive level to the masses.
Pointing, particularly skyward (implying God), called attention to the
invisible within the topography of the visible. The fingers in Zvonarâ&#65533;&#65533;s
sculptures are ornamentalizedâ&#65533;&#65533;glazed with accents of gold, silver and
opalescenceâ&#65533;&#65533;they reference polychromy and the â&#65533;&#65533;feminineâ&#65533;&#65533; lean towards the
decorative.

Elizabeth Zvonarâ&#65533;&#65533;s works have spanned a variety of mediums, from
performances and interventions to installations, and most recently,
sculpture, digital collages, and text-based works. Zvonar (b. 1972) is a
graduate of Emily Carr University. Her recent exhibitions include Parallel
Dimension at Artspeak Gallery (Vancouver), Fade Away and Radiate at the
Cohan and Leslie Gallery (New York), Concrete Language at the Contemporary
Art Gallery (Vancouver), and The Thing, All That Is Solid Melts Into Air,
organized by the Museum Van Hedendaagse Kunst (Mechelen, Belgium).
Upcoming, she has a solo exhibition at the Contemporary Art Gallery in
November 2009. Zvonar was the winner of the Vancouver Art Development Award
in 2007 from the Vancouver Foundation and the Contemporary Art Gallery. In
2008 Zvonar was the inaugural artist at the Malaspina Print Research
Residency and was an artist in residence at the Banff Centre during the
Janice Kerbel led thematic residency, Cosmic Ray Research. She lives and
works in Vancouver, BC.

13. Douglas Udell Gallery | Michael Batty til | APR 11
Michael Batty

New Works

Exhibition continues through April 11th, 2009.

The Douglas Udell Gallery is excited and proud to present a solo exhibition
of new works by Vancouver artist Michael Batty.

The exhibition NEW WORKS at Douglas Udell Gallery Vancouver marks a
continuing effort for me to compress my visual vocabulary into a single
formal element and explore its potential and longevity.

These pieces mark a movement away from the static image of previous works,
towards an image with movement. The works subject are balance, structure
and memory. The strokes of color could be regarded as a mark in time,
evidence of ones existence, a record of a moment with a beginning and an end .

Lastly, in order to provide some kind of orientation, the paintings are
titled after the day, month and year they were executed. I wish to plot a
specific location for each of the works within my artistic practice.

This exhibition is curated by Keith Wallace and explores an aspect of
performance art that is prominent in mainland China and in particular,
Beijing's -East Village- that has taken place over the last 20 years.
Performance art in China during the early 1990s represented a moment when
the avant-garde resurfaced in a powerful way in order to break with the
past and to forge new artistic directions within a rapidly evolving
society. Many of these performances were recorded by photography and video
to provide a document. These early photographs were not necessarily
intended to be presented as artwork, yet have emerged as such in various
exhibitions. The artists were quick to acknowledge the power of the
photograph and increasingly looked to photography as an integral component
of their live performance work. By the late 1990s, performance became a
more clearly defined practice, and for many artists the photograph
functioned not only as a document, but also as a work of art in itself. As
a further development, many performances were specifically created for the

Lorna Brown: Threshold, (cont.)
to May 10, 2009

Walter C. Koerner Library, Main floor, 1958 Main Mall, University of BC
Library hours: Mon - Fri 8am - 11pm, Sat - Sun 10am - 11pm
<http://www.library.ubc.ca/koerner>

"Threshold (cont.)" by Lorna Brown is part of on ongoing inquiry into the
topic of "boredom" that she began in 2000. Within various fields of
research, "boredom" has been written about in diverse ways: as a condition
that is dangerous to society, a precursor to positive radical change, and
the result of becoming overwhelmed by tedious, repetitive activity. This
video installation is made up of quoted texts from sources in literature,
criticism, sociology and other discourses. Placed in the context of a
university library where the published results of research are stored and
accessed through different media, the work raises questions about how
knowledge is circulated, the processes of engaged learning, and the
weariness or lack of interest that can result from information overload.

Lorna Brown is a Vancouver-based artist, writer, and curator. She has
exhibited her work internationally since 1984 and has taught at Emily Carr
University of Art and Design and at Simon Fraser University. Brown was the
curator for "Group Search: art in the library," a series of artistsÃ¢ï¿½ï¿½
