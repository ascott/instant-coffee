Exhibition Opening: Friday, March 8, 2013, 7-9 pm

PATRICK FAIGENBAUM
March 9 to June 2, 2013
Organized by the Vancouver Art Gallery and co-curated by director Kathleen 
S. Bartels and artist Jeff Wall

Since receiving international acclaim in the mid-1980s for his portraits of 
Italian aristocratic families, photographer Patrick Faigenbaum has situated his 
work within the heart of the pictorial tradition, emphasizing the expressive 
power of photography and its position within the lineage of art history. This will 
be the first major exhibition of Faigenbaum's work in North America in almost 
two decades, featuring over 75 photographs from various bodies of work from 
the 1970s to the present.

For this Paris-born artist, the photograph-whether presented alone or within a 
series-offers a time and space set apart from life and held in tense, 
contemplative stillness by its frame. Over the past 25 years, Faigenbaum has 
travelled throughout Europe creating portraits of people and places, inscribing 
the dense past and a textured present within his compositions. Initially trained 
as a painter, his large-scale photographic tableaux are infused with the artistic 
effects of light and shadow, textures and other visual cues that reference the 
histories of painting, photography and cinema. His portraits of individuals 
reveal both the fleeting intimacy and ultimate distance between artist and 
sitter, while his photographs of urban peripheries and rural landscapes grasp 
the sensations of the uneven socioeconomic changes taking place within 
Europe. By capturing the details of the situations he encounters, Patrick 
Faigenbaum creates a compelling ambience that isolates a moment outside 
the incessant flow of time, if only to have us acknowledge the impossibility of 
fully understanding the complex narratives that extend beyond the image.
