June 7 to August 3, 2014 
Opening reception and book launch: Saturday, June 7, 3 PM

"Bruce Stewart: Dollarton Pleasure Faire, 1972" 

"A Thousand Quarrels: Liz Magor" 

"Soviet Hippies: The Psychedelic Underground of 1970s Estonia"

Presentation House Gallery is pleased to announce our forthcoming 
exhibitions: three projects that explore the pleasures and pitfalls of the 
utopian spirit. Representing diverse geographies and social contexts, these 
exhibitions share a fascination with experiments in alternative living and 
countercultural resistance.

"Bruce Stewart: Dollarton Pleasure Faire, 1972" - curated by Bill Jeffries - 
showcases Stewartâ€™s photographs from the Dollarton Pleasure Faire: a two-
week celebration of alternative living at the embattled Maplewood Mudflats in 
North Vancouver. Stewart, who had been documenting renaissance faires 
and hippie-era festivals throughout the province, acted as the eventâ€™s official 
photographer. His images capture the sun-kissed pleasure, playful repose 
and rebellious exuberance of the Faire before the well-publicized destruction 
of the Mudflats in the early seventies. West of Eden, a publication situating 
Stewartâ€™s project within the social tensions of the time, will be launched at the 
exhibition.

As one of Vancouver's most celebrated artists, Liz Magor presents 
photographic work in "A Thousand Quarrels". For this exhibition she has 
drawn from her ongoing photographic archive of provisional dwellings found 
in the West Coast wilderness. These photographs reflect her longstanding 
preoccupation with shelter and refuge, alluding to the psychology of escape 
and the human need for protection.

"Soviet Hippies: The Psychedelic Underground of 1970s Estonia" - curated by 
KIWA and Terje Toomistu - provides insights into alternative cultural activities 
in Estonia under the Soviet regime. The curators conducted in-depth 
anthropological studies of the Estonian underground for this exhibition of 
documentary materials and film interviews with individuals who opposed the 
official socialist codes of behaviour. These manifestations of the hippie 
movement behind the Iron Curtain give a different perspective on global hippie 
culture during Soviet times.

Bruce Stewart and Liz Magor will be in attendance
