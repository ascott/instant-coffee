Thursday, March 2, 4:30 pm
Emily Carr University, South Building, Room 301

The Combative Phase 

This talk will focus on a forthcoming curatorial project at Yale Union, that 
addresses a particular history of film production centered on UCLA during a 
period from 1969 to 1978 to draw out two parallel narratives: of intersections 
between Third Cinema and the use of film within decolonial movements in the 
U.S.; and of the university as a site of struggle around accumulated advantages, 
sustained through inclusionary imperatives.

Richard Birkett is curator at Yale Union, Portland. Between 2010-2016 Birkett 
was curator of Artists Space, New York and from 2007-2010 at the Institute of 
Contemporary Arts in London. He has curated exhibitions including Nought to 
Sixty (ICA, 2008), and Materials and Money and Crisis (with Sam Lewitt, mumok, 
Vienna, 2013), Terry Atkinson (Yale Union, Portland, 2014), Bernadette 
Corporation: 2000 Wasted Years (Artists Space, 2012), Hito Steyerl (Artists 
Space, 2015) and Cameron Rowland: 91020000 (Artists Space, 2016). He has 
coedited publications including Cosey Complex (Walther Knig, 2012), Bernadette 
Corporation: 2000 Wasted Years (Walther Knig, 2013) and Tell It To My Heart - 
Collected by Julie Ault, Volume 2 (Hatje Cantz, 2015)
and his writing has featured in numerous publications, journals and magazines. 
He serves on the board of Working Artists and the Greater Economy (W.A.G.E.).

This talk is the fifth in a series of Curator's talks to be held at Emily Carr University
presented in partnership with the Vancouver Art Gallery and 221A. The series is
associated with the exhibition Vancouver Special: Ambivalent Pleasures and 
supported by the Province of British Columbia's Creative Economy Strategy.
