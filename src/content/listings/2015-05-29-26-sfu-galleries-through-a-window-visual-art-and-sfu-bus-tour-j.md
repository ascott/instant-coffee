Through a Window: Visual Art and SFU 1965-2015

Exhibition Bus Tour with Curators and Artists
SAT, JUN 6
2PM SFU Gallery / 3.30PM Audain Gallery / 4.30PM Teck Gallery
Curators Amy Kazymerchyk and Melanie O'Brian lead a tour of each part of
the exhibition in dialogue with artists Sabine Bitter and Helmut Weber,
Lorna Brown, Allyson Clay, Vishal Jugdeo and Reece Terris. A free bus
departs from Audain Gallery at 1PM and returns for the 3.30PM tour. Book
now by emailing denise_ryner@sfu.ca

Through a Window is a three-part exhibition that looks at visual art
production at SFU since 1965. Considering each of SFU's campuses as
windows, the exhibition takes up Henri Lefebvre's Rhythmanalysis as a
framework for reflecting on the rhythms of visual art at SFU over the last
fifty years. Artists in the exhibition are affiliated with SFU as former
students, faculty or collaborators.

At SFU Gallery, Through a Window looks specifically at the rhythms of
framing space formally and conceptually through the lens, with works that
interrogate the frame, seriality and representation. In addition to the
exhibition, past student projects have been remounted outside the gallery
at the Burnaby campus. At Audain Gallery, the exhibition explores the ways
in which contemporary art is socially and politically engaged with the
city, and includes works that draw on the literary, performativity,
activism and the aura of industrial film. The Teck Gallery installation
explores tensions between academic freedom, education and collective agency.

In addition to the exhibitions, artists and writers were invited to
undertake rhythmanalyses from various windows at SFU campuses and studios,
as well as a number of sites within the city that connect to SFU's visual
art history. The resulting texts are presented as posters that will be
dispersed throughout the city.

Through a Window is supported by a program of public tours, mobile talks,
sound walks, listening events and lectures that will unfold throughout the
summer. All exhibitions and programs are free and open to the public.
