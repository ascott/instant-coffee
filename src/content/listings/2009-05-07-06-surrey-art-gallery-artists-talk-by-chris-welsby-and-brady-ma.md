Artist’s Talk with Chris Welsby and Brady Marks
Thursday, May 7, 7:30pm
Free admission

Join Chris Welsby and Brady Marks for an illustrated presentation about their new media 
installation project Heaven’s Breath – a collaboration with Scheherazaad Cooper. They will 
discuss the manner in which Heaven’s Breath came together as a project, the work that went into 
its production, and situate Heaven’s Breath within their respective art practices. 

There will be time for questions and conversations after the talk. The event concludes by 9pm. 
Everyone is welcome and admission is free. Refreshments are provided.

About the Presenters

Chris Welsby has been making films and film/video installations since 1969. His art practice 
focuses on creating holistic models of nature and technology through media. Inspired by both the 
arts and sciences, Welsby’s work seeks to explore consciousness, the role of the observer and 
our relationship to all things in our environment. He collaborated with Alan Steeves on the 
exhibition Ovoids: Moving Images Between Nature and Art at the InterUrban Gallery in 
Vancouver in 2006. The British Film Institute honoured his contribution to British experimental 
cinema by issuing a retrospective dvd of his work.

Co-founder of Intermission arts collective and trained in computer science Brady Marks is an 
“audio-active emitter” in the fields of new media and new music. She is an explorer – artist and 
former scientist – using computer code the way others use words, paint or notes to communicate 
ideas, pose questions, observe and interpret her environment. Her work has been presented in 
Canada, Europe and Asia.

About Heaven's Breath

The new media installation Heaven’s Breath is inspired by an eleventh century figurine depicting 
the Hindu God, Shiva, performing the dance of creation and destruction. According to the Hindu 
faith, Shiva's dance brought the universe into existence. Some thinkers have linked this act of 
creation to the Big Bang of Western scientific cosmology. Heaven's Breath combines Oddisi 
dance with interactive technology and real time weather data. The animation of images and 
accompanying sound effects is driven by the power of the wind, which is in turn driven by the 
rotation of the earth. In this way Heaven's Breath seeks to connect Shiva's dance, via the earth's 
orbit, to the gravitational forces of the solar system and the far reaches of the universe. Drawing 
on the cosmologies of East and West, and the knowledge of the ancient and modern world, the 
installation uses digital technology to make connections between science, religion and art. 
Exhibition is ongoing until August 30.
