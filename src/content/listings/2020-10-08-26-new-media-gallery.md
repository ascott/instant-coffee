"The decisive factor is that these rights and the human dignity they bestow should remain
valid and real if only a single human being existed on earth; they are independent of
human plurality and should remain valid even if a human being is expelled from the
human community." Hannah Arendt

Join us for this exciting discussion on an award-winning work that proposes and
references the reallocation of wealth through technology and the right to have rights.
LarbitsSisters will give a short Artist Talk followed by a Panel Discussion featuring Cissie
Fu and Ami
Iaria. Discussion will reference the Bitsoil POPup Tax and Hack Campaign
(winner of Prix Ars Electronica - Golden Nica, and NOVA Interactive awards). Artists and
speakers will discuss ideas connected with the Fair Distribution of Wealth, Blockchain &
Cryptocurrency, Refugees/Immigration and issues related to Borders and Loss.

Artists and panelists will discuss how this interactive installation uses and expands on the
technology behind bitcoin. They look at how the work attempts to intervene at a
conceptual level to explore the possibility of a new form of wealth redistribution for the
benefit of all, and the consequences of this proposition. LarbitsSisters work attempts to
go beyond traditional social-security systems of nation states; suggesting a system that
guarantees fundamental rights for everyone. The artists are interested in the de-
centralised and distributed peer-to-peer architecture of blockchain that does not require
any trusted third party, be it the state or any other
institution, only nodes of interconnected
computers and computing capacities to validate and legitimise transactions and
exchanges in our largely virtualized social fabric. Their work allows us to imagine and
speculate on the reallocation of wealth mechanisms in line with the data economy: data
(or bitsoil as coined by LarbitsSisters) being the primary, artificial co-owned resource, at
the core of their bitREPUBLICsomething that is captured in walled gardens by tech
corporates.

LarbitsSisters
The work of the Brussels-based artist duo LarbitsSisters (BE) is situated at the crossroads
of art, technology, political & societal issues and grew out of a shared fascination in new
media. Their practice merges research and artistic practice with concepts such as
traceability, network analysis, algorithms, automation, and data processing, focussing on
the creative drives and patterns in digital media. Central to their praxis is the friction
between public and private, online and offline;
between an unbridled faith in technological
progress and everyday life. Their work has been presented in national and international
exhibitions and symposia. In 2011 they founded the Research lab on Digital Visualizations;
Larbitslab. This project brings artists and scientists together around societal issues of
networked societies. The specificity of Larbitslab lies in a methodological approach, which
combines observational practices, media analysis, and research on technical and societal
implications. Comprising social media researcher Bndicte Jacobs and media artist
Laure-Anne Jacobs, LarbitsSisters has won the NOVA New Media Interactive Art Prize
and the prestigious Prix Ars Electronica Golden Nica.

Dr. Cissie Fu
Cissie Fu is Dean of the Faculty of Culture and Community at Emily Carr University of Art
& Design and Co-Founder of the Political Arts Initiative which invites 21st imag-e-nings of
the political through digital technology and the creative and performing arts. Born in
Hong
Kong, she has studied, taught, organized, curated and performed across cultural and
educational institutions in Asia, Europe, the UK, and the Americas. Her research interests
sit at the nexus of political theory, participatory aesthetics and artistic activism

Ami Iaria
Ami is Associate General Counsel at the British Columbia Securities Commission.
Originally from British Columbia, she has lived and worked abroad in Japan, England,
France and Italy, and has a keen interest in international contemporary art. She is an
accomplished Bharat Natyam dancer. Professionally, her work focuses on law and public
policy in the context of technology-based innovation and financial markets. She has deep
expertise in policy development for emerging marketplaces for crypto currencies and
digital securities. Prior to her career in securities law, Ami advised many immigration and
refugee clients through the legal aid clinic at her law school in Toronto, and found that
legal work very fulfilling,
being a second generation Canadian herself.
