Anthropomorphism of Media
Oana Clitan, Sylvana dAngelo and Larissa Monteiro

August 26 - Sept 3, 2017
Opening Reception August 25 
7-10pm

Dynamo Arts Association
Suite 103 - 30 East 6th Ave, Vancouver

But if cattle and horses and lions had hands
or could paint with their hands and create works such as men do,
horses like horses and cattle like cattle
also would depict the gods' shapes and make their bodies
of such a sort as the form they themselves have.
- Xenophanes 4th Century BCE

Engaging both traditional and digital platforms the artists experiment with long 
distance collaboration as a devise to create new visual concepts by examining the 
way we communicate material ideas. By exploring different social media platforms 
they use the anthropomorphism of the internet as a tool to manipulate the behaviour 
of technology. 

As we attempt to make sense of our surroundings we often attribute human 
characteristics to mechanized electronics and it is unclear whether attributing such 
behaviours to technology is logically based on function or whether it is simply a 
projection of personification. The conscious use of anthropomorphic metaphor is not 
intrinsically unwise; ascribing mental processes to a computer, under the proper 
circumstances may help you to understand what the computer will do, how our 
actions will affect the computer and conceivably how to use computer programs to 
exchange information efficiently. Information technology presents no clear 
correspondence with any other entities in the world besides humans, therefore 
interactions with this medium will inherently be in our own image.

Humans are emotionally, intellectually and physiologically biased toward social 
activity, and so when presented with even tiny social cues, deeply-infused social 
responses are triggered automatically. From this understanding Anthropomorphism 
of Media presents work that has transformed the intended nature of an application 
by ascribing new meaning to a commonly understood behaviour, activating a 
collective understanding through a contemporary form of media.
