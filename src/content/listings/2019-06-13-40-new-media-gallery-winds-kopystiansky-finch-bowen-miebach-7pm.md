WINDS
June 22 - Sept 29
Opening Reception June 21

ARTISTS 
Igor + Svetlana Kopystiansky (Russia), Art Gallery of NSW, Sydney
Spencer Finch (UK), Cisneros Fontanals Art Foundation (CIFO), Florida
David Bowen (USA), Ischa Tallieu, Brussels
Nathalie Miebach (USA) 

We invite you to join us for the opening reception of WINDS, opening June 21 evening 
with what promises to be a remarkable talk by U.S. artist David Bowen. The exhibition 
features four multi-media installations by five artists from Russia, UK & USA. Their 
conceptual and material process follows wind through physical space and time. The 
works suggest ways in which wind is physically understood or perceived; through 
pressure, sound, pattern, motion and then further appreciated through collected data, 
poetic narrative, a symphonic score, an historic time, a distant place. In each we see the 
apparent attempt to capture or allude to a specific place or moment in time, with 
inevitable suggestions of memory, mortality and memento mori.

Incidents, filmed throughout 1996-97, by Russian artists Igor + Svetlana Kopystiansky is 
an iconic meditation on the beauty and pathos of discarded objects as they are blown by 
wind along a city street. tele-present wind, by US artist David Bowen, charts the 
uncanny, robotic movement of 84 natural grass stalks as they are buffeted by wind that 
is streamed from the shores of Lake Superior into the gallery in real-time. Spencer 
Finch from the UK has created 2 hours, 2 minutes, 2 seconds (Wind at Walden Pond, 
March 12, 2007). Through the programmed movement of air blowing from a series of 
common, industrial fans, this work attempts to re-create & compress the changing 
breeze experienced on the shores of Walden Pond during the 2 year, 2 month, 2 day 
vigil of transcendentalist philosopher Henry Thoreau. Thoreaus precise, scientific and 
poetic observations on natural phenomena at Walden Pond resulted in his seminal text 
on human development, independence, social experiment, self-discovery + self-
reliance. The work by Finch is a meditation on the impossibility of accurately capturing 
or reconstructing events, emotions or places. Finally Hurricane Noel, 2007 by Nathalie 
Miebach takes collected scientific data on the destructive storm to create a woven 
object; re-imagined and performed as musical notation and then music. The work 
attempts to capture through object and sound, the hurricane as it tore up the eastern 
U.S seaboard and into the maritimes.
