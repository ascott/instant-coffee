Andreas Bunte: Lettuce partially emerging from a shopping bag
Runs October 16th ‐ November 8th, 2014

Lettuce partially emerging from a shopping bag Lettuce partially emerging 
from a shopping bag is an exhibition featuring new video work by 
Berlin‐
based artist Andreas Bunte. A 16mm film, Suspended Duration, is the 
show’s centrepiece. In it, Bunte references the scientific work undertaken 
by motion-study laboratories through the incorporation of black and white 
raster backgrounds. Taking ordinary objects as subject-matter, Bunte 
confounds such labs’ aims of achieving scientific abstraction, instead 
capturing the contingency of the filmic image through his inclusion of 
“accidental”  footage as well. In this way, the film extends both literal and 
metaphorical frames by repositioning the fringes to the centre in spatial 
and temporal ways, drawing attention to processes that do not produce 
anything significant, do not demand optimization, and that are not usually 
subject to extended filmic attention.

The exhibition’s title is taken from the book Attempt at exhausting a place 
in Paris by French writer Georges Perec, in which he explored “the 
infraordinary: the markings and manifestations of the everyday that 
consistently escape our attention as they compose the essence of our 
lives.” *

Andreas Bunte is an internationally-exhibited artist based in Berlin. His 
16mm film and installation‐based practice explores the intersection 
of 
architectural space, ideology, and technological innovation. Bunte will be 
showing twin video works, titled Two films about pressure, at the Or Gallery 
concurrently with his exhibition at Republic. Bunte will also present a public 
discussion of his work at SFU on October 1st, 2014 at the Goldcorp Centre 
for the Arts. Bunte's Audain Residence is in partnership with the Or Gallery 
and Republic Gallery, Vancouver and SFU Galleries.

Lettuce partially emerging from a shopping bag opens to the public at 
Republic Gallery on Thursday, October 16th, and runs through Saturday, 
November 8th, 2014. Gallery hours are 10am to 5pm Tuesday through 
Saturday, and by appointment.

* Georges Perec, An attempt at exhausting a Place in Paris, Wakefield 
Press, Cambridge, 2010
