BAF Gallery Fall 2023 Exhibitions

Jonathan Alfaro: High-Fructose Sun
Karice Mitchell: Intimacy Is

258 East 1st Avenue
Sept 6 - October 28, 2023
Wednesday to Saturday 12-5PM
Reception:
Sept 6, 6-9PM

On Wednesday, September 6, from 6-9PM, we will be unveiling new exhibitions from
resident artists Jonathan Alfaro and Karice Mitchell.

"High-Fructose Sun," by interdisciplinary artist, Jonathan Alfaro, features a collection
of oil paintings on aluminum panel created during the artists summer residency at
BAF. The warm-hued works use repetition and seriality, installed in tight proximity in
stacks and rows across the gallery. They explore the visual cues of written language,
evoking penmanship yet remaining illegible. With flowing loops and curves, their
echoing forms tease at recognition, like the elusive chorus of a once-familiar song. A
text by Gillian Haigh will accompany the exhibition.

Known for works that rephotograph, scan, enlarge, crop, or otherwise manipulate
found imagery, Karice Mitchells practice considers issues surrounding the
representation of the Black femme body. Through various modes of abstraction, she
endeavours to give agency to her photographic
subjects while disrupting the white
gaze. Her upcoming exhibition at BAF, titled "Intimacy Is," features a suite of new
works created during her summer residency, and sees her venture, for the first time,
in front of the camera, scanning her own body.

Images that include glimpses of the artist are presented alongside others created
from the pages of Players Magazine, a Black-centric, softcore pornographic
periodical published from 1973 to 2005. In recent years, Mitchell has engaged this
source material with increasing reverence, poring over her growing collection of
vintage issues with the cautious touch of an archivist. The works articulate a care for
their content through a selective revealing. Mitchell filters and redirects the gaze,
limiting access and prioritizing the glitches inherent in the scanner process. She
follows the magazines stylistic cues, using her body to negotiate and sit with the
tensions and concerns around the autonomy of each model. Weaving new and
found
imagery, she conflates notions of subject and object, of passive and active,
allowing the works to command and to respond.
