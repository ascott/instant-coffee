Indigenous Brilliance
A part of Growing Room Literary and Arts Festival (March 11-15)
1pm-9pm, Sunday, March 15, 2020
Beaumont
Studios (326 West 5th Avenue)

Admission: Free or PWYC (recommended: $30).
Tickets available online through Eventbrite.

Celebrate Indigenous Brilliances two-year anniversary with events that showcase
exceptional creative storytelling, from readings to drag numbers to sound performances.
Indigenous Brilliance is a collective approach to realizing resurgence: coming together
with the shared desire to raise the voices of Indigenous women, Two-Spirit, and
Indigequeer storytellers.

Luminaries: The Way Forward, 1pm - 3pm

Our legends are real. Our songs are alive. Recognizing the work of our Ancestors, we
continue to reawaken sleeping stories and languages with new breath. These artists,
Kim Senklip Harvey, Cassandra Blanchard, Helen Knott, and Francine Cunningham, are
shining examples of the radiance of Indigenous Futurisms. Hosted by Emily Dundas
Oke and Molly Cross-Blanchard.This event will feature ASL-ENG interpretation.

The Body Remembers When the World Broke Open (dir. Elle-Mij
Tailfeathers &
Kathleen Hepburn), 4pm - 6:30pm

Two Indigenous women from vastly different backgrounds find their worlds colliding as
one of them, Rosie, is fleeing a violent domestic attack. What begins as an urgent and
terrifying escape, tentatively expands as the women weave a fragile bond in their short
time together while navigating the complexities of motherhood and the ongoing legacy
of colonialism. This film will be followed by a discussion between Jade Baxter and Justin
Ducharme, two Vancouver-based filmmakers who together with the audience will reflect
in conversation on the presence of this film within our communities and cinematic history
and practice. Note: This film is close-captioned.

Afterglow: Tongue & Cheek, 7:30pm - 9:30pm

The body is a story, each rattle of the hip is a decolonial love song. The movement of
sound and body is proof that Indigenous artistry is reaching toward the stars through
contemporary song, dance, drag, and performance. To end the day of
Indigenous
Brilliance, Jody Mariko Okabe, Edziu, Dlani Valin, Kalilah Rampanen, and Liz Howard
show us the reaches of storytelling. Hosted by Sparkle Plenty.

Find more event details and artist bios at festival.roommagazine.com/indigenous-
brilliance/.
