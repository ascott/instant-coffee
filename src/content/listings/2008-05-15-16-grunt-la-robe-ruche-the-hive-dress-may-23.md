La Robe-ruche The Hive Dress
A collaborative installation by Héloïse Audy and Julie FaubertMay 24 to June 28th 2008
Opening Friday May 23@ 8pm.Artist talk 2pm Saturday May 24th La Robe-ruche (The Hive-Dress) is a collaborative installation by artists Héloïse Audy and Julie Faubert, which incorporates into its penetrable structure 1000 pensées-plumes collected from Montréal garment-district seamstresses over a nearly one-year pe-riod. Audy and Faubert’s hive-dress creates a physical narrative composed of discarded textile industry material and the written frag-ments of the seamstresses themselves. This material accord, the hive-dress itself, speaks to the everyday lives of immigrant women’s experiences and reflects the fraught lines of communication between diverse histories, practices and representational strategies.“ We collected over a thousand sentences-thoughts from the woman working in the textile industries located in Montreal. These thoughts were sewn one after the other on an infinite ribbon, made from dyed scrap fabric from these same industries, pursuing our ritual of transforming 
thought
into vibrant material. The material is shaped into an immense conic structure that hangs from the ceil-ing.This conic dress is covered with hundreds of sentences surrounded by a comforting and unsettling micro-cosmos.This ambiguous space evokes both at the same time the effervescence of work and the feeling of quietness and security that one would feel under a gigantic mothers skirt.”Based in Montreal, Héloïse Audy is currently completing her Masters in Fine Arts at Concordia University. Her interdisciplinary prac-tice deals with poetic relationships between the body, memory, language and textile. In her installation projects in collaboration with Julie Faubert, she has further explored sound as a fundamental element in the interaction with the viewer’s body. Julie Faubert is a mixed-media installation artist interested in the connective practices between the body, space and discourse. Living and working out of Montréal, where she received her Master’s degree in visual and media arts
