Philippe Raphanel: New Paintings
January 8th - February 7th, 2009

Equinox Gallery
2321 Granville Street

Equinox Gallery is pleased to present Philippe Raphanel: New Paintings.
Opening on Thursday, January 8th, the exhibition presents a new body of
work derived from the artist's recent travels between British Columbia and
Brazil. For some, the notion of abstraction raises visions of objects
analyzed and disintegrated into various geometric components and then
reassembled into new combinations with little emotional content and few
references to the natural world. For Philippe Raphanel, allusions to
abstraction are clear, but are not meant to negate the landscape genre in
any way. Beginning with a deep interest in exploring experiences and views
of nature, the artist continues to use organic cellular shapes to further
elucidate the relationship between modernist abstraction and the natural
world. Raphanel uses the organic spherical shapes and strong
perpendicular and parallel lines to inquire into our experiences and views
of nature, deliberate tensions from potential threats to the environment,
and perhaps most importantly, uncover reminders of floating images of
invisible elements that make up our experience of nature. The spherical
motif has appeared in Raphanel's paintings before, however the translucent
orb-like shapes are now joined by striations.

In 2007, Raphanel set up a studio in a 1968 Oscar Neimeyer building in the
centre of the mythic modernist city of Brasilia, Brazil where he found
himself working in a concrete environment composed almost entirely of
vertical and horizontal lines. Raphanel ascribes the contrast of colours
and shapes to changes in his environment. The work done in Brazil is
predominantly warm in tones with a heavy emphasis on the linear, whereas
those elements completed in British Columbia consist almost wholly of
blues, grays, and purple spheroidal shapes. The strips of colour work
beautifully with the nebulous rounds, suggesting a woven fabric layered
behind the paint, atop the woven canvas itself. While his work continues
to draw from sources such as photographs or earlier bodies of work, the
artist finds himself increasingly working from memories and subliminal
reactions to specific Brazilian and Canadian West Coast landscapes.

For more information and to view the works, please visit the website at
