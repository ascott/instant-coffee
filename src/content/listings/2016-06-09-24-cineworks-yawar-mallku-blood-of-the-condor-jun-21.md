Cineworks and Iris Film Collective present:

Yawar Mallku / Blood of The Condor
Tuesday Jun 21; 9:30pm - 11:00pm
Falaise Park Fieldhouse, 3434 Falaise Avenue, Vancouver
FREE

A rare 16mm feature presentation for National Aboriginal Day in Canada,
Blood of the Condor (Quechua: Yawar Mallku, Spanish: Sangre de cndor) is a
1969 Bolivian film by director Jorge Sanjins. The film tells the story of
an indigenous Bolivian community receiving medical care from the Peace
Corps-like American agency Cuerpo del Progreso (Progress Corps) which is
secretly sterilising local indigenous women.

Sanjins' Yawar Mallku is thought to have led to the expulsion of the Peace
Corps from Bolivia in an act of anti-imperialist cultural nationalism by
the indigenous people. After showings of Yawar Mallku, Sanjins learned
that many peasants had criticism about the difficulty of his films due to
the use of flashback for narration, addressing the lack of attention to
indigenous peoples' issues through filmmaking greatly influenced by
European art cinema. Sanjins took this on board in later films, leaving
Yawar Mallku to stand as an important, if culturally miscalculated attempt
to raise the profile of indigenous issues as a matter of global social
justice under ongoing colonialism.
