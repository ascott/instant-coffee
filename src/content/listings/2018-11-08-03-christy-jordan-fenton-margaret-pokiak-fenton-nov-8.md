Bios:

Christy Jordan-Fenton is the author of four award-winning childrens books about 
Inuvialuk elder Margaret Pokiak-Fentons time at Indian Residential School. Fatty Legs 
and A Stranger At Home have been added to numerous Canadian school curriculums, 
staged as musical productions, featured in a music video, and translated into French 
and Korean. Using her writings as a platform for social change, education, and 
awareness, Jordan-Fenton gives more than 100 presentations per year on residential 
school history and decolonizing, and has spoken from Inuvik to Havana. In addition, 
Jordan-Fenton herself was raised by a residential school survivor. She is a land and 
water defender, is currently studying a diploma in Human Rights and Forced 
Displacement with UPEACE, and is a 2017 Ponds Vital Voices Lead Fellow. 

Margaret Pokiak-Fenton, or Olemaun, is the best known as the indomitable subject of 
four award winning childrens books about her time at residential school in the 1940s, 
The best known being Fatty Legs (2010). She was born on Baille Island in the Arctic 
Ocean, en route with her nomadic family to their winter hunting grounds on Banks 
Island. Being Inuvialuit, her young childhood was filled with hunting trips by dogsled, and 
dangerous treks across the Arctic Ocean for supplies, in a schooner known as the North 
Star. At the age of eight, she travelled to Aklavik, a fur trading settlement founded by her 
great-grandfather, to attend the Catholic residential school there. In her early twenties, 
while working for the Hudsons Bay Company in Tuktoyaktuk, she met her husband-to-
be, Lyle, and followed him south to Fort St John, BC to become a cowboys wife. 
Together, they raised eight children. Margaret is very active speaking across Canada, 
sharing stories of resilience, the path to reclaiming cultural identity, surviving residential 
school, resilience, and traditional Inuvialuit culture. At 81, Margaret remains lively and 
inspiring, and is a proud language keeper.

04. Fazakas Gallery | Panoply of Possibilities | NOV 8
Carlos Coln

Panoply of Possibilities

Opening Reception: Nov 8, 6 " 8pm
Nov 8 " Dec 22, 2018

Tanyap Project Space, Fazakas Gallery, 688 East Hastings Street, Vancouver, BC

Fazakas Gallery is pleased to present Panoply of Possibilities, a new body of work by 
Mexican artist, Carlos Coln.

The term panoply is used to describe a splendid and impressive collection of things that 
is, or aims to be, complete. In this series, Coln uses Baroque concepts like metonymy, 
ellipsis, and hyperbole to explore how the meaning and reception of words and artefacts 
can be altered. By performatively indigenizing objects, Coln calls into question the 
representation, meaning and value of Indigenous objects in the context of 
anthropological collections.

The works in Panoply of Possibilities are made from found tools, intended for specific 
uses, which the artist transforms into artworks and weapons simultaneously. A pool cue 
is sharpened, wrapped and decorated with feathers, transforming it into a spear. Colns 
choice of objects to transform, mainly sports equipment, is suggestive of the pageantry 
and ritual aspects of sports events and reveals the close relationship of games to 
hunting techniques. Displayed on the wall like a museological artefact, the object is 
neither useful as a spear, nor a pool cue, and instead becomes an object of intellectual 
speculation.

About the exhibition

"Text and materiality play an important role in my work; I often employ humour and 
wordplay to engage and encourage the viewer to think again about the common 
thoughts and phrases we encounter daily.

1000 Shades of Grey, a digital print of 1000 crayons ranging from white to black in a 
100x10 grid, touches on themes of nuance, subtlety and subjectivity. The seemingly 
absurd task of assigning a name to each shade of grey resulted in a range of pairings 
that call to mind the small or even vast differences in how we each perceive the 
information surrounding us.

In the IDK series, a marbled black and white surface obfuscates text, creating a murky, 
swirling pattern. Beginning with I DONT KNOW," each statement fluctuates between 
ignorance, action, anxiety, doubt and ambivalence. I think we are all at the mercy of this 
inner voice, continuously cycling through fleeting musings, which are often contradictory 
and uncertain."

About Ben Skinner

Bens practice explores the nature of language through the use of text and materiality. 
Calling on a vast knowledge of materials and methods, Skinner applies his hand to 
concepts that are by turn ironic, witty, introspective and questioning. His works often 
combine mechanical production methods common in sign making with traditional 
materials and techniques such as hand-applied gold leaf and silk marbling. His 
conceptual practice moves him between an expanding list of media including foil, 
flocking, plaster, vinyl lettering, plastics, neon and mirror. Skinner has a keen eye for 
finishes that borders on obsessive, resulting in highly polished works with a quasi-fetish 
quality.
