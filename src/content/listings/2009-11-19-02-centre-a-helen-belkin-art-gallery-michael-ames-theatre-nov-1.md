UBC Curatorial Lecture Series

Anselm Franke: The Grammar of the Court
Monday, November 23, 6:30 to 8 pm
Room 104, Frederic Lasserre Building
6333 Memorial Road, The University of British Columbia

Anselm Franke is a curator, writer and Director of Extra City-Kunsthal
Antwerpen, Belgium. He was co-curator of "Manifesta 7" (2008) in Italy and
was Curator of the KW Institute for Contemporary Art, Berlin until 2006
where he organized the exhibitions, "Territories. Islands, Camps and Other
States of Utopia" (2003); "Image Archives (2001/2002); and co-developed the
project, "No Matter How Bright the Light, the Crossing Occurs At Night"
(2006). He has edited and published extensively and is a contributor to
magazines such as "Metropolis M," "Piktogram," and "Cabinet."

This talk, "The Grammar of the Court" will examine 'performance' in the
court of law, as it operates in a social topography whose borders --such as
the regimes of difference between artifice and facticity, construction and
truth-- are drawn up differently from any other 'staged' or scripted space.
The exhibition, "Judy Radul: World Rehearsal Court" at the Morris and Helen
Belkin Art Gallery will be included in the presentation. Among other
influences, Franke will refer to Bruno Latour's ethnography of
'associations' and 'enunciation regimes'.

The Curatorial Lecture Series is organized by the Morris and Helen Belkin
Art Gallery in collaboration with the Department of Art History, Visual Art
and Theory; the Museum of Anthropology; and the Department of Anthropology;
with the support of the Audain Endowment for Curatorial Studies; and the
Faculty of Arts at The University of British Columbia.

UBC Curatorial Lecture Series Fall 2009

Nicolaus Schafhausen: Curating in Front of a Local Backdrop

Thursday, November 26, 2009, 6:30 to 8 pm
Room 104, Frederic Lasserre Building
6333 Memorial Road, The University of British Columbia

Nicolaus Schafhausen is Artistic and Managing Director of Witte de With
Centre for Contemporary Art, Rotterdam. He was curator of the German
Pavilion at the 52nd (2007) and 53rd Venice Biennial (2009). As Director of
Frankfurter Kunstverein from 1998 to 2005, he curated thematic exhibitions
on contemporary social issues such as the "local," migration, and a
triology on the phenomenon of globalization. To mark the 100th anniversary
of the birth of Theodor Adorno, he curated, "Adorno. The Possibility of the
Impossible" (2003).

This talk will focus on the traditional European art centre. Operating in
front of a local backdrop, the centres are internationally oriented and
address a public that is often made up of tourists. The centres face a
number of financial and demographic challenges. Though they are
increasingly being integrated into the marketing strategies of cities, they
are being left more and more to fend for themselves financially and the
centres continue to assume their core audience is their local citizenry,
though none of the demographic factors favour this relationship.
Schafhausen will also talk about his current project, "Morality" which he
has initiated as a leitmotif for Witte de With.

The Curatorial Lecture Series is organized by the Morris and Helen Belkin
Art Gallery in collaboration with the Department of Art History, Visual Art
and Theory; the Museum of Anthropology; and the Department of Anthropology;
with the support of the Audain Endowment for Curatorial Studies; and the
Faculty of Arts at The University of British Columbia. This lecture is
organized in collaboration with the Walter Phillips Gallery, Visual Arts at
The Banff Centre; and the Curatorial Practice MA Program at the California
College of the Arts.

SHAHEEN MERALI:
Fear in the Transitional Community:
A Response by Artists and Curators

The University of British Columbia's Critical and Curatorial Studies
Program, in conjunction with the Morris and Helen Belkin Art Gallery and
Centre A present Fear in the Transnational Community: A Response by Artists
and Curators a talk by Shaheen Merali as part of the Curatorial Lecture Series.

Shaheen Merali is a curator and writer based in Berlin and London. Between
2003 and 2008 he was the Head of Department for Exhibitions, Film and New
Media at the Haus der Kulturen der Welt in Berlin; in 2008, he was the
Artistic Director of the Bodhi Galleries in New York, Mumbai, Berlin and
Singapore; and in 2006, he was the Co-curator of the 2006 6th Gwangju
Biennale of Korea. His recent curatorial project consisted of a large-scale
historical show,The Untold (the rise of) Schisms, held at Alcala 31 in Madrid.

This talk, Fear in the Transnational Community: A Response by Artists and
Curators will examine issues and commentators who use language specific to
the arts to address a major shift in perspectives taking place in the new
millennium. This shift is marked by a desire to engage with the notion of
reality, which has become complex and entangled, as have all other global
concepts and beliefs.

The rubric art no longer refers
primarily to a series
of catalogued
works or objects but also to a set of practices. Despite this, the language
of art practice continues to express specific concepts and beliefs, which
are entangled within a system where potential spectators are not
necessarily included. In the new century, artists and curators are
charting the efficacy of adopted methods of curation in the condensed
visuality of the globe. In trying to understand these concerns, questions
are raised about diminishing resources, the role of diplomacy, and the move
away from the notion of an abundant Earth and toward the notion of a world
in constant provocation.

The talk will be concluded by an examination of Meralis recent
exhibition
at the Kunsthalle Brot, Vienna, The Promise of Loss, An Index of
Contemporary Iran.

The Curatorial Lecture Series presents lectures on contemporary curatorial
practice. It is organized by the Morris and Helen Belkin Art Gallery in
collaboration with the Department of Art History, Visual Art and Theory;
the Museum of Anthropology; and the Department of Anthropology; with the
support of the Audain Endowment for Curatorial Studies; and the Faculty of
Arts at The University of British Columbia. This lecture is organized in
collaboration with Centre A: Vancouver International Centre for
Contemporary Asian Art.
