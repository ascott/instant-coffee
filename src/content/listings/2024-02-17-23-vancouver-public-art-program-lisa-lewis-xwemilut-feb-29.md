The City of Vancouver Public Art Program has recently installed the work of Lisa Lewis
(Xwemilut) on transit shelters throughout the city for "Platforms: Nine Places for
Seeing." Her work, titled, "Always in Our Hearts" is a photograph of the artists niece
wearing regalia that Lisa has woven to honour missing and murdered Indigenous
women, and the people working to end violence and bring justice under the banner of
Missing and Murdered Indigenous Women, Girls, and Two Spirit (MMIWG2S). Lisa
has this to say about the weavings: The dress is woven out of white wool to represent
the purity of the souls of the murdered and missing women.
The Salish red robe (worn
over the dress) represents the blood that has been shed. The red colour also
represents protection, and, in this way, the robe is meant to act as a safe keeping for
the souls that have been taken from their people. The swan feathers that you see in
this work are traditionally used to adorn our sacred ceremonial Skway Skway (Mask
Dancers) regalia. As a Squamish Nation member, I have witnessed our families
grieving their family member that have been missing or found murdered. My hope with
this work is to bring attention to this issue. You can see this work from now until
March 10th. To learn more about this artwork and the other Platforms projects, please
visit:

https://vancouver.ca/platforms
