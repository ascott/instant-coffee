The Writing Table with Maryse Larivire and Shazia Hafiz Ramji " Writing the 
Atmospheres

Wednesday, November 14, 7PM
Or Gallery

This event is free but space is limited. Please email writingtable@orgallery.org to 
register.

Please join The Writing Table for a workshop with artist and writer, Maryse Larivire, and 
poet and critic, Shazia Hafiz Ramji. The two-part workshop begins with an introduction 
to Larivires multi-sensory and autofictional practice that explores the idea of the 
captive author, which encourages performative and embodied strategies towards 
writing. Held in conjunction with Larivires exhibition, Orgazing, Writing the 
Atmospheres invokes the artists recent book, Orgazing, which takes the form of an 
epistolary novel written by an unnamed female protagonist held captive on a remote 
island.
Maryse Larivire is an artist, writer and scholar whose work crosses sculpture, 
performance, collage, text, and film. She is currently a PhD candidate in the Department 
of Visual Arts at Western University, where her research focuses on womens textual 
production. Larivire has exhibited widely in Canada, including Optica, Montreal; 
Untitled Art Society, Calgary; DNA Art Space, London; The Rooms, St. Johns; and 
Walter Phillips Gallery, Banff. Her writing has been published in periodicals such as 
Canadian Art and C Magazine, and she has written three books including Orgazing 
(2017), Hummzinger (2016), and Where Wild Flowers Grow (2015).

Shazia Hafiz Ramjis first book, Port of Being (Invisible Publishing), received the Robert 
Kroetsch Award for Poetry and was published in October 2018. Her poetry is 
forthcoming in Best Canadian Poetry 2018 and her criticism has appeared in Canadian 
Literature, Quill & Quire, and Chicago Review of Books. Shazia was recently 
interviewed by Sheryl MacKay of CBCNorth by Northwest, where she talked about Vito 
Acconci and the influence of listening and perambulatory practices in her writing.
Orgazing is produced with the support of the Conseil des Arts and des Lettres du 
Qubec, Untitled Art Society (Calgary), and CCA Glasgow.

Weiyi Changs curatorial residency is supported by the British Columbia Arts Council.
