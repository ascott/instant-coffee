August 15 - September 20, 2014

"Robert Kleyn and Mitch Speed: Systems"

Reception: Friday, September 12, 7-9pm

The Apartment is pleased to present "Systems," a summer exhibition of works
by Vancouver artists Robert Kleyn and Mitch Speed. Working within defined
systems or methods with a deep painterly sensibility, these two artists
mark making incorporates modified imagery adapted from architecture,
topography and anthropology.

Presenting three bodies of work from the years 1999, 2011 and 2014, Kleyn
explores the formal abstraction of found, industrial products in his use of
cardboard, commercial paints and scanning beds. In his newest work,
"Spent," he presents a grid of images that exploit the planar geometry and
chance elements of scanner technology to create "thought images". Through
his aesthetic projects Kleyn challenges the innate frameworks of material
production.

Speed employs bold serial and taxonomic graphics on found magazine pages
and large format fibre paper. He culls patterns of black and white forms or
characters from the material’s surfaces in his recent series "New Yorker
Collages," mirroring systems of base technology and forms of automatic
writing. Speed’s collages and drawings are presented on sculptural bases
and hanging mechanisms, giving a gravitas to the works’ physical materiality.

Robert Kleyn is a visual artist and practicing architect who studied
mathematics and architecture at UBC in the early 1970s. His works have been
included in solo exhibition at the Belkin Satellite, 2006, Catriona
Jeffries Gallery, 2009 and "Traffic: Conceptual Art in Canada 1965-1980"
which toured internationally.

Mitch Speed is an MFA candidate at Rutgers University, NJ. He co-edits the
publication SETUP and has contributed writing to Frieze, Camera Austria,
The Bartleby Review, and Turps.
