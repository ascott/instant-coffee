Join us for the opening reception of Last Resort a painting exhibition featuring works 
by artists Danielle Roberts & Jack Arthur Wood Jr.

Opening reception June 17, 7-9pm

Exhibition Run June 17 - July 15 2017

* Both artists will be in attendance for the opening reception

The Bakery
1670 Franklin St.
Vancouver BC

info@thebakeryonfranklin.com

Danielle Roberts is an artist born in Stockton California and raised on Vancouver 
Island. She completed her BFA at Emily Carr University of Art and Design in 2013. 
As a child she was enamored by the fantastical experience of American amusement 
parks. This fascination drove her artistic practice into the musings of constructed 
environments. Her work often draws from public spaces based on memory, 
perception and facades. She appropriates mass produced images and objects into 
her narrative landscapes. Instigating a response to social relationships between 
materialism and idealistic pursuits of constructed values. Intuitively her work plays 
out in the way she observes the world, at once inviting, spontaneous and 
disquieting.

Jack Arthur Wood Jr. is an artist and writer working in printed and painted media. 
Born in Cincinnati, Ohio. Printing and painting are equally weighted in Wood's 
creative moves; in the shared space of a print shop his paintings are activated 
simultaneously, likewise he mixes print into paint. Processes and materials are 
dissolved into product disappearing origin and medium hierarchy. The content of 
Wood's work is often tethered to landscape, and is heavily laced with hectic patterns 
where figurative subjects also hold space. Wood feels grounded by queer theories of 
horizonal futurity and infinite becoming in his practice. He refers to his practice as 
Pretty Color Time (chanted with enthusiasm). Aiming to be fearless in creating, he 
approachs shadowy traces of dysphoria with the cleansing light of critical thinking, 
compassion, and hard work. Wood's images describe the precipice of change and 
the excitement of impermanence relative to all aspects of living. Ultimately, the work 
is a declaration that being alone is physically forever, but that ultimately there is joy 
to reap in the sharing of space and our journey through it.
