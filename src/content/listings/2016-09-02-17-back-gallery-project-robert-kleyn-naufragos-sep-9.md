Back Gallery Project, (Vancouver, BC) is pleased to invite you to 
the opening reception of "Naufragos" with works by Robert Kleyn, 
Manuel Pia and Tonel. 

The exhibition is the result of the conversations among three artists 
born "elsewhere" but who currently live in Vancouver. This city is 
not explicitly present in the works, but is present as a locus for the 
stories that brought these artists together.
Naufragio is the Spanish (and Italian) word for shipwrecked, a state 
of emergency and diversion from the crisscross of global 
commercial, exploratory and migratory routes that have been 
established over time. Shipwrecks can lead to the founding of new 
routes or represent temporary-to- permanent exclusion from the 
historical and universalized narratives that equate constant 
movement and expansion with progress. The implied 
temporariness of Naufragios counters the cultural associations 
between sea, sky and eternity, and brings the documented 
seascape in Pia's work in line with the series of visual disruptions 
and glitches that define the formal characteristics of his video (text 
by Denise Ryner). 

The title of the exhibition is based on the body of work that Manuel 
Pia is currently developing and serves as a common thread to the 
works that he, Robert Kleyn and Tonel that are presenting.

Throughout much the duration of Manuel Pia's video work, the 
viewer's focus is directed towards the liminal visual space where an 
anonymous body of water meets the sky. Even before encountering 
Pia's work, the viewer is already accustomed to looking towards 
such horizons as a point of orientation. The artist plays with this 
visual tendency through a series of digital manipulations, thereby 
forcing the viewer to repeatedly search the shifting, morphing and 
folding images for each new horizon (text by Denise Ryner).

Robert Kleyn, in a new series of pictures takes the printed page of 
text as his point of departure, in particular the page as the 
historically pre-eminent site for the organization, storage and 
display of text. We avid readers know so many books, so that when 
we read, other texts spring to mind, carrying on their own dialogue. 
Kleyn's pictures depict that special momentfamiliar to everyone--
when the primary text dissolves to becomes a visual thing, drifting 
free from story and page to intertwine with our own dreams and 
fantasies. These text pictures can be seen like the 'thought 
balloons' in a comic strip, where the characters are absent and only 
their words appear .For this exhibition he has chosen particular 
texts--adventure stories and lost histories--that embody different 
senses of the show's theme.
. 

The drawings by Tonel are intended as representations of the 
Space Race and by extension, of particular chapters from the Cold 
War era. His images can be considered as hybrids, inspired by 
existing visual documents of well-known episodes in the 
development of the Soviet and American space programs. The 
notion of Naufragio could apply, in this case, to men and machines 
left adrift in the cosmos, if not in the fog of history. Viewers may 
recognize the references to American astronauts walking on the 
surface of the moon or to the Soviet robot, the "Moonwalker" 
(Lunokhod) that landed in the same cosmic body. This selection of 
drawings springs from a larger body of works that aims at 
connecting the events taking place miles away and above the earth 
with some of the realities of the Cold War and post-Cold War 
period, including the expansion of suburban life with its 
quintessential golf courses and golf carts.
