SNACKS / BEER BY DONATION / READINGS / GIVEAWAYS

NOV 8th, 7PM

Join us in celebrating the launch of our (if we can brag a little) fantastic, terrific, 
exciting, blow-your-socks-off wonderful new fall issue! 

"FEATURING READINGS BY"

MALLORY AMIRAULT
MACKENZIE GROUND
FRED WAH
TCRs 2018 Writer-in-Residence JULIANE OKOT BITEK

& MORE 

Come eat, chat, listen, and enjoy an evening of words and good company. Doors 
open at 7PM and readings begin at 7:30PM.

"ADDRESS"
Unit 425, SUM Gallery space
268 Keefer Street, between Main Street and Gore Avenue. 

This event takes place on mky"m (Musqueam), swxw7mesh (Squamish), 
and sel"l"witulh (Tsleil-Waututh) First Nations territories; it also takes place in 
Chinatown, home to low-income and Asian immigrant communities. 

"GETTING HERE"
Public transit buses 3, 8, 19, and 22 stop within a block of the building. Buses 14, 16 
and 20 stop two blocks away on Hastings Street. 
There is no parking available at Sun Wah after 7PM. There is an EasyPark at 180 
Keefer Street. Street parking is also available.

"ABOUT TCR ISSUE 3.36 (Fall 2018)"

3.36 is jam-packed with new poetry by Fred Wah and Rita Wong, Paige Cardinal, 
David Dowker, and Scott Jackshaw, as well as an excerpt from Six Boxes, Helen 
Guris essay on sexual violence in and outside of Canadian writing communities. 

This issue also includes two interviews hosted by TCRs own Colin Browne"one 
with curator Pantea Haghighi, whose exhibit Modernism in Iran: 1958-1978 
appeared at Griffin Art Projects in North Vancouver earlier this year, and one with 
Esther Shalev-Gerz, whose installation The Shadow stretches across a plaza on the 
University of British Columbias Vancouver campus.

Our interview with Roberto Rodrguez (also known as Dr. Cintli) focuses on the maz 
culture that has spanned this continent for thousands of years, linking Indigenous 
peoples and nourishing forms of resistance and creation.

In our review section, see-to-see, 2018 Griffin Poetry Prize-winner Billy-Ray Belcourt 
imagines Mercedes Eng brandishing her book Prison Industrial Complex Explodes 
against the settler-police, preliminary to the work of eat[ing] [their] brain[s], and 
Mackenzie Ground await[s] the conversations that are taking their first breaths in 
the pages of Gregory Youngings Elements of Indigenous Style.
