BAF Gallery Winter 2020 Exhibitions

Howie Tsui: Parallax Chambers
Cindy Mochizuki: The Sakaki Tree, a Jewel, and the Mirror
Candice Okada: Emergency Situation

Opening Reception
Friday, January 10, 2020
7pm-11pm
258 East 1st Avenue

Two of the shows were produced by the latest participants in BAFs Residency Program,
Howie Tsui and Cindy Mochizuki. During the program, the two artists worked side-by-side in
the two studios at BAFs purpose-designed facility in the False Creek Flats. Also opening is
new work from Candice Okada in BAF's Garage; this street facing exhibition window
displays art to the public 24 hours a day and showcases early-career artists. The show
opens on
Friday, January 10 and closes on Saturday, March 7. The show is free to the
public during BAF Gallerys opening hours of Tuesday to Saturday, noon to five.

For these three very different exhibitions, the three artists have created works in animation,
textile, and installation according to their own unique practices.

Cindy Mochizukis 'The Sakaki Tree, a Jewel, and the Mirror' is a material exploration that
works within Shinto cosmology, exploring the spirits of these Japanese legends and folklore.
Through performance and material assemblage, the exhibition furthers Mochizukis interest
in the performance and manifestation of story through material forms, and the recovery of
Japanese folklore in contemporary society.

In 'Parallax Chambers', Howie Tsui brings together a suite of new works that reveal his
explorations in serial, sequential and interlaced imagery. The project conflates the anarchic
universe of martial arts fiction with the ungoverned community that formed the Kowloon
Walled City. Through animation and lightbox works, the show refines and tinkers further with
Tsuis production methods, which integrate ink drawing, animation and computer
programming.

Candice Okada's 'Emergency Situation' consists of hundreds of hand-made fabric
sculptures that resemble airplane oxygen masks, evoking the ominousness of the
contemporary political climate, and addressing our understanding of what constitutes a state
of emergency. The installation explores Okadas research interest in the current state of
democracy and our lack of trust in government institutions and officials.

Burrard Arts Foundation (BAF)
