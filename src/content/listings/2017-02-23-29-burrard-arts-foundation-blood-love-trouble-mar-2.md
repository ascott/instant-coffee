BAF Studio Spring 2017 Exhibition

Blood Love Trouble: Joan Bell-Irving, Julia Dahee Hong, Vivienne Kubik, Katrina 
Niebergal, David Ostrem, Noboru Sawai, Pamela Speight, Marcia Wedeking and Casey 
Wei

Opening Reception
Thursday, March 2nd, 2017
7pm - 10pm

The exhibition, Blood Love Trouble is programmed by the efforts of Malaspina 
Printmakers Society, the Burrard Arts Foundation and the City of Burnaby Permanent 
Art Collection, courtesy of the Burnaby Art Gallery. The exhibition will take place at the 
Burrard Arts Foundation in both halves of the gallery.

Drawing from the historical trajectory in Akira Kurosawa's film Ran (乱) and the 
woodblock print by Meiji period master Taiso Yoshitoshi's Promenading (Society Wife), 
from the series 32 Aspects of Living (1888), the curatorial premise for this exhibition 
invites responses from artists Julia Dahee Hong, Katrina Niebergal and Casey Wei. The 
violence of property ownership, opening trade channels, and then consequently the 
cultivation of theatre, music and visual art located womxn as exemplars of desire and 
leisure in this time of prepubescent modernity. Hong, Niebergal and Wei respond the 
the echoes of this representation by revisiting and creating new works using the 
language of ritual, mourning, place and identity through various moments in history 
using video, sculpture and sound.

In addition to their response, the artists and curator have selected prints from City of 
Burnaby Permanent Art Collection, courtesy of the Burnaby Art Gallery and Malaspina's 
Print Consignment Collection by Joan Bell-Irving, Vivienne Kubik, David Ostrem, Noboru 
Sawai, Pamela Speight and Marcia Wedeking. Spanning 30 years of Malaspina's print 
based community, this selection of lithographs, screen prints and etchings alongside the 
works of three emerging, Vancouver based artists observes patterns of historical 
sentiments that mutate the borders of individual and shared subjectivities.
