06. Vancouver Art Gallery | PATRICK FAIGENBAUM | MAR 8
Exhibition Opening: Friday, March 8, 2013, 7-9 pm

PATRICK FAIGENBAUM
March 9 to June 2, 2013
Organized by the Vancouver Art Gallery and co-curated by director Kathleen 
S. Bartels and artist Jeff Wall

Since receiving international acclaim in the mid-1980s for his portraits of 
Italian aristocratic families, photographer Patrick Faigenbaum has situated his 
work within the heart of the pictorial tradition, emphasizing the expressive 
power of photography and its position within the lineage of art history. This will 
be the first major exhibition of Faigenbaum's work in North America in almost 
two decades, featuring over 75 photographs from various bodies of work from 
the 1970s to the present.

For this Paris-born artist, the photograph-whether presented alone or within a 
series-offers a time and space set apart from life and held in tense, 
contemplative stillness by its frame. Over the past 25 years, Faigenbaum has 
travelled throughout Europe creating portraits of people and places, inscribing 
the dense past and a textured present within his compositions. Initially trained 
as a painter, his large-scale photographic tableaux are infused with the artistic 
effects of light and shadow, textures and other visual cues that reference the 
histories of painting, photography and cinema. His portraits of individuals 
reveal both the fleeting intimacy and ultimate distance between artist and 
sitter, while his photographs of urban peripheries and rural landscapes grasp 
the sensations of the uneven socioeconomic changes taking place within 
Europe. By capturing the details of the situations he encounters, Patrick 
Faigenbaum creates a compelling ambience that isolates a moment outside 
the incessant flow of time, if only to have us acknowledge the impossibility of 
fully understanding the complex narratives that extend beyond the image.

07. Interurban Gallery | ROLLING THUNDER | MAR 9

Curtis Grahauer
ROLLING THUNDER

Interurban Gallery // 1 East Hastings Street, Vancouver, BC
Opening: March 9th 2013, 7pm - 12am
Artist walkthrough: March 10th, 2 - 4pm
Exhibition: March 13th - 30th | Wed - Sat, 12pm - 5pm

Inspired by the 2011 Stanley Cup riots, ROLLING THUNDER is an exhibition
of new works by Vancouver artist Curtis Grahauer, presented by The
Storyboard Label and the Interurban Gallery, encompassing installation,
sculpture, video and sound.

In relation to other riots and protests that were happening around the world,
from Greece, Iceland, and London, to the Arab Spring, the Vancouver riot was
an absurd response of violence and destruction to the results of a simple
hockey game. A few individuals, with the specific intent to loot and vandalize,
influenced a massive group of people within the anonymity of the mob, to
participate in violent acts. Businesses were vandalized and bystanders were
assaulted, but the greatest casualty of the riot were vehicles. A 2012 report,
released by the Vancouver Police Department, indicated that 122 vehicles
were smashed, turned over and set ablaze as the riot swarmed its way
through a few city blocks, leaving a series of smoldering monuments in its
wake.

Using the spectacle and consequences of group conformity as its subject,
ROLLING THUNDER includes work that pays tribute to the twisted and
scorched carcasses of vehicles that were the primary product of this mob.
The centrepiece of the exhibition (accompanied by smoke, lighting effects,
and a soundtrack inspired by 80s action movie scores) is a vehicle, stripped,
flipped and suspended from the ceiling. A series of small maquettes and
archival footage of the riots are also included in the exhibition. These works,
as well as the films that inspired the soundtrack to the exhibition, act as
evidence of absurd violence and wanton excitement: the release of male
aggression in a physical world.

Curtis Grahauer has exhibited his work both in Canada and USA and has
participated in the LIVE Biennale, Scotiabank Nuit Blanche and the KIAC
Artist Residency in Dawson City. He is a member of the performance-
collective Weekend Leisure, with recent events in Vancouver, Portland, and
Seattle. Currently, he is working on a feature film project, Steel Viper Force,
inspired by straight-to-DVD action movies, acting as producer, co-writer and
director and has an upcoming residency at SÃƒ M in Reykjavik.

08. UNIT/PITT Projects | Kelly Roulette | MAR 9

Kelly Roulette: Traditional Road Warriors

March 9 to April 27, 2013

Opening Friday, March 8 2013, 8pm. UNIT/PITT Projects, 15 East Pender
Street, Vancouver.

The third project in the What Future series of commissions at UNIT/PITT
Projects is Traditional Road Warriors by Kelly Roulette.

Traditional Road Warriors consists of two paintings depicting the struggle of
native people living in the inner city. The two images contrast the oppressive
hopelessness imposed on native people by lifetimes of poverty and racism
with the persistence and vibrancy of indigenous culture and identity that
emerges in unexpected places.

Kelly Roulette is Ojibway from the Long Plain First Nation in Manitoba. She
has been in the creative arts for more than 20 years and worked in a number
of genres including theatre, radio and television. Kelly, a former lawyer, has
expanded her creative work to include writing and painting.

09. VAG at Vancity Theatre | Lecture: Philip Tinar | MAR 11
Lecture / Philip Tinari: ON/OFF / Asia Contemporary Speaker Series
VIFF / Vancity Theatre - 1181 Seymour Street
Monday, March 11, 7pm
The rise of Asia on the international scene is one of the most compelling 
stories in contemporary art. Today, Hong Kong, Singapore, Dubai, Tokyo and 
Beijing are major hubs for contemporary art, competing directly with London 
and New York. In order to understand this phenomenon and its connection to 
economic and political power, the Asia Contemporary Speaker Series 
presents On/Off: The Double Consciousness of China's Newest Generation of 
Artists with Philip Tinari, Director of the Ullens Centre for Contemporary Art, 
Beijing.

Presented by the Canadian Art Foundation International Speaker Series in 
collaboration with the Asia Pacific Foundation of Canada's National 
Conversation on Asia and the Vancouver Art Gallery. Free for Vancouver Art 
Gallery Members and students (with ID), $10 Non-Members. Tickets at the 
door.
