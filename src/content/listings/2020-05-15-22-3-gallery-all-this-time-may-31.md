All This Time

Online Exhibition/Blog
Deadline May 31 2020

For many, our art practices have taken the back burner-alongside various behavioural
norms that belonged to routines and mindsets that have been forced to suddenly stop.
Seemingly, some have found a sense of consolation in new routines that involve tasks
like sewing, candlemaking, doing push-ups, gardening, starting a diary, baking 20
variations of sourdoughtalking on the phone. A new relevance for tasks that might
otherwise be considered busy work or domestic can lead us to critique institutional
frameworks and question how we ascribe value to activities that might be dismissed by
some schools of conceptual thought.

With online exhibitions being one of the few ways arts practitioners can cling to their
former sense of professional achievement-via-display, and with it their disembodied
communities; Number 3 Gallery plans to initiate a blog space to double as an online
exhibition. The combination of high and low brow scrollable spaces will not only reflect
which
platforms we consider to be valid carriers for art content, but also play tongue
and cheek on a format often used to share creations some might consider as craft with a
lowercase c (see film Julie and Julia - based on the novel by Julie Powell and dare to
compare it to time based performance art).

Here is where you come in:

This call is open not only to visual artists but also to those who belong to any other
creative field.

What to submit:

Evidence of anything that you have made or achieved that is not immediately art
related (could be considered general research/exploration).

Was done either during or before isolation under similar circumstances

Please submit 1-3 images (JPG, PNG, PDF) and/or (approx) 200 words

All media / activities are considered appropriate (ie. audio clips, lists, etc.)

Your bio + contact info.

Send submissions to number3gallery@gmail.com by May 31st.

*we want to acknowledge that not everyone, (especially those with low income or
members of at-risk and
disabled communities) can relate to the experiences described
above as a direct result of COVID 19 in 2020. It is not our intention to limit the reach of
this project to the length of the current pandemic but to extend it beyond so that it may
continue to subvert standards of productivity associated with display culture.
