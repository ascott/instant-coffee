SIGNAL + NOISE MEDIA ART FESTIVAL

VIVO Media Arts Centre presents the ninth annual Signal + Noise
Festival, three full days of innovative contemporary media art.
Showcasing a spectrum of audio & video works, live action performances
and immersive installations, Signal + Noise is Vancouver's premier
alternative media event.
Signal + Noise is a highlight of VIVO's programming. The festival
presents the work of local, national and international artists.
Created nine years ago in response to Vancouver's explosive
contemporary arts underground, Signal + Noise has become an essential
forum for creative exchange.

VIVO MEDIA ARTS CENTRE

VIVO Media Arts Centre is a not for profit media production,
exhibition and distribution centre. We are an evolving organization
operated by and for artists and media/community activists. Since 1973,
with the founding of the Satellite Video Exchange Society (SVES), VIVO
has grown into a vibrant artist-run centre with state of the art
facilities and educational workshops. We have production equipment,
studio space, audio, video and new media post-production facilities,
and during regular hours of operation, light-technical support is
available to producers as they work. Other exciting facets of the
centre include Video Out's International Distribution network, a Media
Arts Archive and Resource Centre, and ongoing public events and
exhibitions.

A humorously hopeful reaction to the practice of nationalizing,
ethnicising, and multi-ethno-nationalizing of personal identity for the
purposes of market and politics, Navin Party emerged in 2006 out of a
conversation Navin Rawanchaikul had with Tyler Russell in a Chiang Mai
cafÃ&#65533;Â©. Navin had recently been invited to participate in Diaspora a
multimedia presentation featuring diasporic artists of South East Asia,
part of the cultural programme of the 2006 Board of Governors meeting of
the World Bank and the IMF in Singapore. Playfully taking issue with being
labeled a diaspora artist Navin thought it would be fun to slightly
destabilize the theme of the event and rather than offer a sentimental
exploration of what it means to be a person of Hindu-Punjabi descent
growing up in Chiang Mai. His basic idea was to go to India, a country he
had never been to, and interview people who shared his name: Navin or
Naveen. He was hoping to somehow link this project to his long held desire
to make a bollywood-style music video.

Tyler Russell was, as now, an aspiring cultural worker/curator primarily
concerned with how we identify ourselves and relate with one another. He
was well aware of North American multiculturalism's habit of urging
not-white and not-quite-white people to identify as hyphenated creatures
lurking somewhere between ethno-national categories. With this in mind, he
proposed that it might be interesting to globalize the issue and initiate a
campaign to unite the Navins of the world under the banner of Navin Party,
www.navinparty.com. Navin completely concurred and immediately contacted
his web guy to reserve the domain name. When the two later searched
Navin on the internet, they discovered that it is not just an East Indian
name derived from the Sanskrit word for novel or new, but that Steve Martin
sports the name in the 1979 film The Jerk, and among other autonomous
origins, as a last name it has Gaelic roots.

From there, Tyler and Navin began collaborating on the production of series
of works based on a story where Rawanchaikul, a lonely son of diaspora,
founds a global party for people who share his name. Traveling the globe in
search of fellow Navins, the project has gone on to incorporate various
cultural and propaganda forms into the ever-evolving narrative of the Navin
Party. Navin Party projects have been developed in India, Japan, China,
Korea and most recently for Dan Cameron's Prospect New Orleans in the USA.
Works from the series have been collected internationally including by
institutions such as the UBS Art Collection.

We Love You Comrade Navin presents two works from this series Navins of
Bollywood and Quotations from Comrade Navin. Navins of Bollywood, directed
by Mumbai-based director Naren Mojidra, is a bollywood-style short musical
film that recounts the founding tale of the Navin Party. It was filmed in
Mumbai in June of 2006. Meanwhile, Quotations from Comrade Navin, complete
with a ceramic bust of Navin Party Chairman Navin Rawanchaikul, a book of
navinist quotes and a series of woodblock propaganda prints features the
video of a mao-inspired propaganda campaign executed by the Navin Party in
Beijing in the summer of 2007.

The career of internationally renowned artist Navin Rawanchaikul now spans
almost 17 years. Over this time, working mainly in collaboration with
others (predominantly under the auspices of Navin Production Inc.), he has
executed projects that have playfully and extravagantly questioned the
structure of the art world system, offered interventions into day-to-day
cultural experience and questioned the relationship between art and market
through giving unique gifts to members of the public, and built elaborate,
interactive storytelling environments replete with sculptures, videos,
comic books, and movie-style billboards. He first gained international
recognition in the 1990s, when in collaboration with Bangkok taxi driver
Chawee Sitabut, he poignantly questioned the status of the relationship
between contemporary art and the general public by creating Navin Gallery
Bangkok, an art gallery in a taxi-cab. With this project, for over five
years he invited contemporary artists from Thailand and around the world to
create exhibitions for the taxi-using public. Always asking questions about
the place of art in contemporary human experience, Navin has continuously
sought a space for the human, day-to-day, cultural purposes of art beyond
the purely decorative, economic or political.

What's in the Box! Is a multi-stage touring project, instigated by David
Horvitz and
Lukas Geronimas, in collaboration with Renata Christen, The Black Hole
Space and
curator Terri C. Smith, The Madiman Arts interaction Center, and all those that
participate in the project. 

Project stages consist of: 
1. A website providing event information and project updates 
2. A series of games played at pre-determined as well as impromptu
locations throughout North America over the course of one month 
3. A statistical analysis of game data, leading to a conclusive theory of
what is inside the box 
4. A long-term phase of constructing the answer to what's inside the box 
5. An exhibition of the constructed answer along with documentary material
and ephemera from the Box Game tour.

The Box Game is a portable event that allows a player to guess or 'vote'
for what's inside
the box (the process of guessing and voting at the same time is explained
later). The
Game's initial run will take place during the months of March and April
2009 at over a
dozen pre-determined venues, and twice as many impromptu locations. The
setup and
rules are as follows:

Rules:
The rules are simple: using a pen and paper ballot, the player uses his or
her imagination
to decide what's in the box. The only information provided by the hosts is
that there is
art in the box, and that the player's effort will help determine what it
is. Once the player
has written down his or her decision, (s)he proceeds to put the paper
ballot into the box.
Every player's decision is a guess that counts as a vote that will elect
the work of art
produced as the answer to what's in the box. There are no bad or 'wrong'
guesses.
The collected ballots at each location will be compiled and analyzed both
within the
entire set of answers from all events, and separately as a local subset of
answers.
Construction of the final answer (the 'artwork') is contingent upon the
results of the data
once analyzed; the artwork will be produced according to frequency of
answers, as best
interpreted by the artists involved in the project and panel members
selected by the
artists. For example, if more players guess that the there is a statue
inside the box than a painting, greater weight will be placed on statue
when realizing the artwork. Each player
will have the chance to receive a printout of the data analysis, concluding
statements, and specially made art-works for their participation.

David Horvitz is an artist from the Los Angeles area, currently residing in
Brooklyn and
studying photography at the Milton Avery School at Bard College. His work finds
thoughtful ways to contemplate time and distance, and unconventional methods to
distribute and exhibit art.

Lukas Geronimas is an artist from Toronto, who has lived and worked in
Vancouver,
Tokyo, and New York. His art questions object-hood as a stable state in
art, and how
artistic narrative can move outside the context of art and into the
community-at-large.
He is currently studying sculpture at the Milton Avery School at Bard.

Renata Christen is an arts administrator from Brattleboro, VT who enjoys
recontextualizing
print media. Her current interests include homesteading, the ethics of
technology, and uniting theory with practice. She is acting as press
liaison for the Box
Game.

The Black Hole Space is a portable exhibition space resembling a pedestal,
constructed
by Joshua Clayton and run by curator Terri C. Smith. Artists are invited to
submit
proposals for the Black Hole Space and evaluated based on the quality and
creativity of
their proposal.

The Madiman Arts Interaction Center is a pilot residency program promoting
artists
who bring research out of the university and to the general public. The
program takes
place against the backdrop of Yale University in New Haven, Connecticut.

13. REPUBLIC GALLERY | Hamed Teymouri | MAR 21
Stoppage
Hamed Teymouri

March 21st through April 23rd
Opening reception Friday, March 20th, 6.00-8.00pm

Stoppage is about the necessity of urban war, the implacable urgency to
fire ammunition, set off ordinance from all directions and dominate by
force of arms the population in a city. It is about hunting people through
their streets and across their walls. It is about the transformation of
urban space into battle space.

The images in Stoppage are registers to the process of response to urban
war, an over-saturated outside view of the urgency of something that one
should not so easily pretend to understand. This urgency is driven by the
question of what comes first: the economic, cultural and political
conflicts, or the civilian armed conflicts that go with them. Where does
one find the ultimate stratum of meaning in these rounds of fear, hate and
destruction?

Hamed Teymouri holds an M.A. in studio art and art history from the
University of British Columbia. Stoppage is his second solo show with
Republic Gallery. Most recently he was featured in the exhibition The Most
Violent Thing, curated by Vanessa Sorenson.
