SONG IN THE DEVIL S THROAT is an experimental video exhibition that offers rare 
insight into the extensive material process behind classically animated artwork. 
Featured videos by Jesi Jordan and Rafaela Kino examine the symbolic hauntings of 
the human psyche, and the contortions of our inner world within a dizzying display of 
video, collage and hand-drawn frames. 

It was in the smoke and noise of the city that Rafaela Kino produced HANS 
NIGHTMARE. Her narrative remake of a Freudian case study, aims to reveal the 
paranoid psychic material that exists beyond appearances. Within the literature of 
psychoanalysis, Kino discovered imagery that perfectly encapsulated the absurdities 
woven into our cultural fabric, like a cinema of the mind., the absurdities woven into our 
cultural fabric. Through the medium of collage-animation her piece was set to motion. 

To Kino, the tectonics of collage further compliment the aesthetics of dream work as 
outlined by Freud: its disproportions, condensations, displacement and dramatization of 
original source materials, mirrored the process of dreaming itself. 

Alongside Kinos work, traditional animator Jesi Jordan will be exhibiting her latest and 
longest animated piece; BLOSSOM. Blossom is a vibrant, life-affirming work that 
contrasts the intellectual cynicism and low hanging clouds of Hans Nightmare. 
Blossom is an animated diary that Jesi Jordan hand drew daily with pencil on paper over 
the course of four years. Created by process of automatic drawing, Jesi Jordans 
autodidactic animations are created as an obsessive form of emotional exorcism. 
Without structure or timeline, she draws out her feelings and watches them shift and 
transform organically. 

In her animation, she expresses her emotions through the metamorphosis and the 
contortions of her own image. Every contortion presented in chronological order, 
represents a particular feeling and experience in time. Jordan drew in school books and 
loose sheets of printing paper, each sheet was then divided into twelve parts in order to 
save on material.

Alongside the video, Jesi will be exhibiting the 9000+ drawn animation frames that went 
into the completion of this piece. The unedited timeline gives a look into the course of an 
emotional 1460 days between the years of 2013 to 2017. 

Both Hans Nightmare and Blossom together, challenge the illusions of the moving 
image by exhibiting the works material backbone while probing the phantoms of our 
inner world.
