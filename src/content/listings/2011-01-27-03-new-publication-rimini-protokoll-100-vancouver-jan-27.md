New Publication
Rimini Protokoll: 100% Vancouver

Fillip is pleased to announce the release of 100% Vancouver, a 
publication developed in collaboration with the PuSh Festival and 
Theatre Replacement. 100% Vancouver functions as a print 
companion to 100% Vancouver: A Statistical Chain Reaction, a 
performance by the German-based group Rimini Protokoll (Helgard 
Haug, Stefan Kaegi, Daniel Wetzel). The project represents the 
latest in a series of performances, including 100% Berlin and 100% 
Vienna, engaging the collaborative, dramatic participation of one 
hundred “statistically significant” residents who play themselves on 
stage. 100% Vancouver, the publication, features colour 
photographs of each of the participants in the performance as well 
as essays by Frances Bula, Tim Carlson, and George Pendle, with 
a foreward by Rimini Protokoll. Edited by Jeff Khonsary and Kristina 
Lee Podesva.

A PDF of the essays from the book are available free on Fillip’s 
website. Stage performances of 100% Vancouver are scheduled as 
part of the annual PuSh Festival, which premieres 100% Vancouver: 
A Statistical Chain Reaction this coming weekend.

05. Shudder Gallery | DRIL | JAN 27
Shudder Gallery presents:

Move It On Over

DRIL

Opening reception Thursday January 27th, 7 - 11 p.m.

28 January - 19 February, 2011

A drift of tumbleweed are accumulating within the walls of Shudder, 
carried
in by the wind of DRIL, propelled by their collective interest in the
iconography projected onto these simple plants. Through Western 
film, the
North American conscious has come to understand the icon of the 
tumbleweed
as a metaphor for transience and inhospitable land. By transposing 
this
iconic symbol from the badlands of film into the heart of DTES, DRIL 
poses
how this device's currency contextualizes our understanding of the 
changing
social and economic landscape of the surrounding area. In 
removing our
vicarious understanding of this icon, viewers are no longer 
witnessing the
story of the wild west, they are directly implicated into this ambiguous
narrative.

DRIL is a Vancouver based art collective comprised of Dylan 
McHugh, Rachel
White, Ian Prentice and Leisha O'Donohue. The collaborative group 
crafts
their practice around the politics and poetics of space. Through 
Move it on
Over you will see a continuation of the collectives interest in 
developing
site-responsive installations that traverse the intersections between
geography, history and propaganda.

06. 221A Artist Run Centre | My Big Family, Hua Jin | JAN 28
Exhibition: Jan 29 ¨C Feb 27, 2011
Opening Reception: 8 pm, Jan 28
Artist Talk: 2 pm, Feb 19

221A Artist Run Centre is pleased to present My Big Family, a
long-term and ongoing project by Vancouver-based artist Hua Jin. In
the summer of 2010, Jin traveled to seven places in Northern and
Eastern China equipped with one hundred rolls of film. Through
photography, video and sound, Jin documented the lives of over fifty
people in her immediate and secondary family. More than just a 
family
portrait, this project attempts to illustrate the changing family
structure in China and the gradual disappearance of traditionally
large families since the inception of the One-child Policy.

Hua Jin was born in Hebei, China, and currently lives in Vancouver 
and
studies at Emily Carr University. Originally trained as an animator
and graphic designer, Jin worked as an artistic director for 10 years
before moving to Vancouver in 2007. Jin has shown work in 
exhibitions
in China, Netherlands and Canada since 2005. Her photo-based 
works are
included in many publications and private collections, as well as the
public collection of Museum of Contemporary Art in Shanghai 
(MOCA). My
Big Family is her first solo exhibition in Canada. For more work by
Hua Jin please visit http://phoebejin.com.

Debra Zhou was born in Shanghai and immigrated to Canada in 
1999. She
is a current recipient of Canada Council Assistance to Culturally
Diverse Curators for Residencies in the Visual Arts (2010 & 2011) at
Centre A, Vancouver International Centre for Contemporary Asian Art.
She curated Politics of Romance, 2009, at 221A Artist Run Centre for
LIVE Biennale. Shanghai Duolun Museum of Modern Art, Yishu 
Journal,
ISSofBC (Immigration Service Society of BC), and VIVO Media Arts
Centre. She is also the president of the Board of LIVE Biennale.

221A Artist Run Centre is a registered non-profit organization funded

and
private donors who share a vision to provide qualitative space for the
development, education and exploration of nascent interdisciplinary
contemporary art and design practises. The My Big Family exhibition
and talk was made possible through the support of Emily Carr
University of Art and Design, SurfaceCollective.com, Vancouver 
Review,

07. Langara College | TALK: Nato Thompson | JAN 28
You are invited to hear Chief Curator Nato Thompson speak next 
Friday,
January 28th, at 12:30pm at Langara College.

In this talk, "Creative Time: Challenging Conceptions of Art in Public,"
Nato will highlight several projects of the last four years embarked 
upon
by New York City's premiere public art organization Creative Time. 
These
projects include Paul Chan's acclaimed Waiting for Godot in New 
Orleans
(2007), Democracy in America: The National Campaign (2008), 
Jeremy
Deller's: It is What it Is (2009), and Paul Ramirez Jonas's: Key to the
City (2010). In commissioning large scale public art projects, 
Creative
Time has supported artists to produce historically important work in 
sites
where cultural production can challenge the status quo.

Since coming to Creative Time in 2007, Nato has organized large 
scale
social based projects including Democracy in America: The National
Campaign, Paul Chan's Waiting for Godot in New Orleans, Jeremy 
Deller's It
is What it Is: Conversations about Iraq (a project in collaboration with
the New Museum), and most recently the Creative Time Summit. 
Previous to
Creative Time, he served as curator at MASS MoCA. His writings 
appear in
numerous magazines on the subject of art and activism, and his 
book Seeing
Power: Socially Engaged Art in the Age of Cultural Production is due 
out by
Melville House Publishing in January 2012. Nato is in Vancouver to
participate in the PuSH Festival as keynote speaker on Jan 28th:
"Cartographic Exploits" - Marking Territory in the Contemporary City.
Nato's talk at Langara College starts at 12:30pm in lecture hall 
A136a, and
runs until 2:15pm. Admission is free. He is sponsored by the 
Langara
College Centre for Art in Public Spaces Speakers Series.
