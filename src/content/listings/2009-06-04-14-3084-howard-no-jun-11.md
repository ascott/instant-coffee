Courtesy of the collections department within the privately owned and operated Hotel Ceteris 
Paribus organisation, 3084 presents a short-term, open storage event and rare glimpse at works 
by semiotist sculptor, Howard No (1955–2006). Originally commissioned by the Vancouver-based, 
luxury private hotel-space network in 1989, No's Fuck Computational Theory of Mind! series is 
contractually precluded from public exhibition in formal arts institution contexts, as are all his 
works. Presented in storage in advance of its charitable donation to the city's 2009/2010 
discursive tourist-vessel project, Le Bateau L'Avenir (dedicated to refugee issues), this collection 
of four sculptures provides a valuable insight into the conceptually dense practice of one of the
world's most enigmatic and commercially successful 'outsider' artists. While the work cannot be 
displayed aboard Le Bateau L'Avenir, its value provides security against which the innovative 
facility might choose to acquire significant continuation funds.

Believed to have worked under this pseudonym to hide his origins, Howard No is purported to 
have come from privileged, 'Old Europe' lineage with prestigious gallery/museum ties. While his 
conceptual idiom suggests a refined higher education in the arts, No operated outside the formal 
establishment, producing work exclusively for corporate collections in reaction to "the rank 
aesthetics of cronyism and self-serving cultural manipulation, wrapped elegantly in the guise of 
good taste and public illumination" (HCP collections, 18.04.89) – a comment believed to be 
leveled at the largely pre-reflexive art establishment of the time. While this position is thought to 
have directly inspired the radical anti-public stance of first-wave Vienna Conceptualism, there are 
no grounds to suggest the artist harbored misanthropic sentiments, supported by the declaration 
that his exclusive-market strategy merely '[felt] less fraudulent' (ibid).

Fuck Computational Theory Of Mind! was inspired by 'the frame problem' in Artificial Intelligence
research in the late 1970s, which required a simplification of proposition logic to 'frame' simple 
event-based statements without qualifying all possible contextual factors that could be assumed 
to remain the same under the common sense law of inertia. While technological scholarship 
found a practical coding solution to the problem within a few years, the concept unintentionally 
reanimated modernity's philosophical chimaera, epistemological recursion, among selected 
analytic philosophers of the time (Dennet 1978; Fodor 1983; McDermott 1987), notably those 
associated with computational theory of mind – the concept that all mental phenomena are 
computations: cause-effect events mediated by processing. A self-professed semiotist, Howard 
No sought to short-circuit the unnecessary invocation of anachronistic idealist/materialist division 
underpinning the theory, with a method and materiality of sculptural assemblage suggesting the 
inter-subjectivity of signification and the socio-linguistic triangulation of objecthood – semiotic 
positions refuting mind-dependence/independence as mutually exclusive qualities of matter. 

At the heart of No's approach were the 'contravariant and transordinate framing modalities of 
embodied and conscious animal awareness', articulated sculpturally by the artist in the 
disturbance of conventional relationships between the structural materials of painting and 
framing. For Howard No, the common sense law of inertia highlighted a fundamental aspect of 
sign use in animal life – common-sense coping – a practical but illusory stabilisation through 
which a material world characterised by flux is constantly subject to filtration, circumscribing 
conscious being within a small horizon of dynamic attentiveness, outside of which things are 
assumed, albeit temporarily, to remain equal. Echoing the Latin meaning of the Hotel Ceteris 
Paribus name (all other things being equal), this concept formed the basis of No's commission, 
inspiring the conceptually challenging and aesthetically compelling work at the centre of this rare 
opportunity.

3084 is a project space in a high-specification Vancouver Self Storage facility. Throughout June 
and into July it will facilitate the short-term storage of materialised ideas / ideated materials by a 
selection of artists based in Vancouver, or artists responsible for work owned by collectors in the 
city. These projects will be free for public encounter through a series of open storage events and 
viewings by appointment – both accessible by email registration on a first-come, first-served basis.
