Chernoff Fine Art
12 January - 18 February 2017
OPENING RECEPTION: Thursday, January 12, 7-9pm

Rue Julian Levy, Galeriste Bonnieux, France

By convincing his father, a New York City real estate developer, to purchase four of the 
Constatin Brancusi sculptures Marcel Duchamp had recently brought to New York from 
Paris to sell, Julian Levy met Duchamp. Duchamp was charmed by the young American 
and invited Levy to go with him back to Paris the following year and possibly make a film 
with Man Ray. Through Duchamp, Levy was introduced to the Parisian scene including 
his future wife, the surrealist Minna Loy's daughter, Joella. Like Duchamp, Levy was 
charismatic, irreverent and very handsome.

When Levy returned from this journey, he was resolved to start a gallery in New York 
City and began plotting. Opening in 1931, the Julian Levy Gallery introduced Surrealism 
to the United States. He gave Salvador Dali his first show in New York. He discovered 
Joesph Cornell, who had wandered in off the street with some of his work under arm, 
soon after the galley was open and it here that Cornell first showed his collages and 
films. Deeply influenced by the modernist vision of Alfred Steiglitz, Levy was an early 
champion of 'photography as art'. With Bernice Abbot he purchased a portion of 
Eugene's Atget photographic archive that Abott had discovered and saved after Atget's 
death in 1927. Levy also showed the work of Stieglitz, Nadar and Henri Cartier-Bresson. 
Levy had an illustrious and vivid career.

At a much later point in his life he returned to France and purchased a home in the 
ancient hill town of Bonnieux, a place which appears to have changed little over time. 
It's often why people vacation in the Luberon, for the chance to step into this rich past. It 
was in this very home that I had the amazing fortune of spending a week in October, 
2015, as a guest of its current owner and our dear friend, the artist Joanna Staniskis. 
She had thoughtfully stocked the guest room with books on the life, gallery and 
collection of it's previous resident.

Rue Julian Levy, Galleriste is situated on a short, narrow and steep stone passageway 
that ends at its upper boundary at the Rue Voltaire, Everyday we passed through this 
tall, narrow and ancient street on our way to his last home. With my camera as 
companion, I grew weary after a few hours of photographing the intensely scenic, 
almost cliche narrative that is this steep walled town. I decided to deconstruct this 
performed tourist outlook. I felt Levy's influence and deeply regarded his interest in 
surrealism and the under-seen. I took Gerhard Ricther's perspective that it is the act of 
seeing that is compelling. My lens was turned to the strange, the barely seen, the 
elaborate extension of the uncanny parts that formed what was otherwise a gorgeous 
but picturesque tableaux. I would not say that my snap shots resonate with the urgency 
of Surrealism in the 30s, but Levy's dedication to photography that pictures the unreal 
from within the very real definitely informed their composition. In the end the project is a 
homage; to a great and inspiring man, to the influence of the master photographers 
whom he showed and to the opportunity to experience, in situ, the Rue Julian Levy, 
Galeriste.
