Sarah Schulman's latest non-fiction book, The Gentrification of the 
Mind (University of California Press, 2012) examines the intersection of 
the American AIDS crisis and urban development in New York City. 
Part memoir, part social analysis, The Gentrification of the Mind 
acutely examines the intersections of urban development, social 
welfare, government action, and the mainstream consumerism of 
queer identity. 

Sarah Schulman (b. 1958) is a prolific American writer, historian, and 
activist. She is the co-founder of ACTUP (AIDS Coalition To Unleash 
Power) and MIX NYC. She was the first person in America to write on 
AIDS and the homeless (The Nation and The Village Voice). Currently, 
she is Professor of English at The City University of New York, College 
of Staten Island, a Fellow at The New York Institute for the Humanities 
at NYU, and on the Advisory Collective of the Human Rights and Social 
Movements Program at Harvard's Kennedy School.
