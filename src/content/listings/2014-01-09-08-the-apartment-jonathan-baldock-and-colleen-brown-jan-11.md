To launch the 2014 season The Apartment is pleased to present Hot 
Spots, Jonathan Baldock's first solo exhibition in Canada, organized by 
Tobin Gibson.

For this exhibition, Jonathan Baldock has developed a new installation 
that references a specific lineage of art history and absurdist theatre to 
do with rituals and materials of healing, conflating the forms of 
sculpture, painting and performance against the body. Through 
juxtapositions of genre and material, Baldock encourages the 
exhibitionâ€™s ongoing metamorphosis. Hot Spots presents felt, ceramic, 
sand, and scent through methodologies of painting: the unique 
alchemy of the artist amongst the transcendental "I". 

Hot Spots will be activated by a number of public events, including a 
collaborative performance of butoh set within the exhibition featuring

January 15, 7pm and a screening of plays by Samuel Beckett and 
Baldock's past performances on Friday, February 14th, 8pm.

Born in the UK, Jonathan Baldock graduated from the Royal College of 
Art, with focus in painting, in 2005. He has shown extensively in 
institutions across Europe, as well as in the United States and Canada, 
having recently presented solo exhibitions at the Wysing Art Centre, 
Cambridge, UK, 2013; AMoYA, Prague, 2012; PeregrineProgram, 
Chicago, USA, 2011 and The Fine Art Society, London, 2008. Baldock 
participated in the group exhibition ARE YOU ALRIGHT? New Art From 
Britain at the Museum of Contemporary Canadian Art (MOCCA), 
Toronto in early 2013, along with the exhibition Please Be Quiet at the 
British School in Rome, 2013; Jonathan Baldock / Karla Black / Morag 
Keil / Jonathan Monk, MALLORCA Landings, Mallorca, Spain 2011; 
SV10, Studio Voltaire, London, 2010, curated by Rebecca Warren; 
NEWSPEAK at Saatchi Gallery, London, 2010 and the Hermitage 
Museum, St. Petersburg, Russia, 2009. His work is held in the Saatchi 
Collection and the David Roberts Art Foundation, London. Baldock 
continues to live and work in London.

The Apartment is also excited to present a site specific exhibition of 
new sculpture by Vancouver based artist Colleen Brown. Known for her 
poetic and original negotiations of the civic environment, Brown will 
move her entire recent output of sculpture en masse down E Pender 
Street, and downstairs to our lower gallery. Over the course of the 
exhibition Eli Bornowsky, Aaron Peck, Martin Ordonez and Dawn Cerny 
will rearrange this installation of objects in dialogue with Brown.

Municipal textures are created out of materials that need to be handled 
by an entire community and endure the inherent erosion. What makes 
these materials sharable often makes them unyielding to the touch, a 
hard response to a shared desire. Through their positioning (and 
subsequent rearrangement) the objects in Rezoning surrender to 
relationships, sympathetic narratives of support and collapse. The 
common frontal, albeit three-dimensional, relationship with sculpture 
and the immersive experience of installations are two common modes 
that speak to different aspects of an individualâ€™s experience. In 
Rezoning, Brown initiates an additional relationship of being beside, 
near and with; drawing attention to our social relationships in the built 
landscape. In this way Rezoning is an active investigation of 
besideness. Just as cooking or performing other simple tasks can 
make a conversation flow, the act of arranging the sculptures will 
provide Brown and her dialogue partners with the impetus to stand 
beside each other and the work, realizing their active/passive position 
within this landscape of things.
