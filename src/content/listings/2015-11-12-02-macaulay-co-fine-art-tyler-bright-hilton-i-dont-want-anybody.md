Macaulay & Co Fine Art is thrilled to present a solo show by Tyler
Bright Hilton "I Don't Want Anybody Else” , curated by W. Bruce C.
Bailey. Please join us on Thursday, November 12th for the opening
reception.

Tyler Bright Hilton expresses a range of emotions illuminating the
human condition in his narrative print series and in figurative drawings.
He makes work of great beauty, and magical power using both
classical and contemporary techniques.

Using Old Master techniques reminiscent of Goya and Durer in his 20
print narrative series "Minmei Madelynne Pryor Went Into the Dryer"
Hilton explores identity through the adventures and exploits of his alter-
ego female protagonist. The story line is ultimately an
expression of female empowerment from an inspirational perspective
of Canadian feminist philosophy. These etchings draw on anime, comic
books, video game imagery as well as Old Master drawing influences;
resulting in a perfect marriage of the classic and the contemporary.

In his drawings - done in silverpoint as well as in ink washes - Hilton
uses portraits of the late River Phoenix as well as the artist's alter ego
Minmei to comment on both the complexity and the simplicity of
contemporary relationships.

– W. Bruce C. Bailey, November, 2015

Born in 1979 in Ontario, Canada, Tyler Bright Hilton studied at the
Ontario College of Art and Design before moving to London, England
for Masters studies at the Chelsea College of Art and Design,
University of Arts London. Upon his graduation he instructed drawing
for the Tate Modern and Battersea Arts Centre from 2009-2011.

Since 2011, he is again based in Toronto and has been at work on the
second installment in a three-part narrative of drawings and etchings
focused on a fictional protagonist, Minmei Madelynne Pryor. This
sprawling project fuses themes exploring adolescent solipsism,
escapism and bodily alienation with disparate formal pictorial and
storytelling approaches- ranging from 16-bit video-game-inspired
isometric perspective to early Renaissance Sienese predellas.

His work is held in the Permanent Collection of the Montreal Museum
of Fine Art, Canada and The Archive of Modern Conflict, London UK.
