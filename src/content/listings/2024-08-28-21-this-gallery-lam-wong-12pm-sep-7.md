Vancouver-based artist Lam Wong explores our propensity for destruction in a new
solo exhibition entitled The World Wont Listen.

September 6 - 21, 2024 " Lam Wong, a Chinese-Canadian artist renowned for his
evocative and contemplative works, continues to delve into profound themes with his
upcoming solo exhibition. In his new exhibition Lam Wong: The World Wont Listen,
Wong draws inspiration from The Smiths' song Ask (1986) with the poignant lyric, "If
it is not
love, then its a bomb, a bomb, a bomb, that will bring us together" This
exhibition assembles a selection of paintings that encapsulate Wong's reflections on
the state of the world, both historically and in its present tumultuous condition. The
exhibition resonates with urgency and a somber contemplation of our collective fate:
"If we dont learn how to love, then we perhaps dont deserve to live. The stakes are
high."

This exhibition is not an isolated inquiry but rather an extension of Wong's consistent
exploration of existential themes and the human condition. Throughout his career,
Wong has been committed to using his art as a medium for meditation on life,
interdependence, and the pressing issues of our times. His previous works, such as
the installation MA No.1 - The Space Between Objects (Griffin Art Projects, 2019),
have often navigated the delicate balance between hope and despair, love and
conflict, inviting viewers to engage in introspection and dialogue.

There will be an
opening reception on Saturday, September 7 from noon to 4pm.
The artist will be in attendance. Simultaneously, there is an opening reception for
Wong's curatorial project featuring work by Marian Penner Bancroft down the hall at
Canton-Sardine. Wong will be present at both events.
