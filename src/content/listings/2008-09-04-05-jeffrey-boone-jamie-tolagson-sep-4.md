Jamie Tolagson's newest work employs the marginalized process of negative
photography as an aid to picture making and historical reflection. The
unique aesthetic of photography's 'middle step' (utilized to great effect
by the surrealists of the 1920's, and substantially lesser effect by
populist art photographers of the 1970's) is here re-enacted by the artist
on the occasion of it's own impending irrelevance to the reproductive
process. The formal elements of various outmoded art genres - minimalism,
abstract expressionism, vanitas painting - are combined with archival
traces of aborted 1960's idealism and 1970's Thoreauism to form a
disquieting reverse-portrait of the utopian impulse in art and life.

Jamie Tolagson is a self-taught artist working in photography, film and
video. His work has been shown at CSA Space, The Vancouver Film Center,
and published in Doppleganger magazine and "To The Dogs" (an upcoming
Presentation House/Arsenal Pulp Press publication.)

New Work 1973-2008 will be showing at Jeffrey Boone Gallery from September
4th though 28th; Wednesday through Sunday noon-6PM.
