Artwork by Justin Camilli, Todd Evanger, Val Loewen, Andrew Powe
Mix tapes by Andy Dixon, Instant Coffee, Donato Mancini, Randy Grskovic
Saturday March 9, 2013
Preview at 7pm, music till late
Drinks provided by Steam Whistle Brewing
Credit and debit accepted
Presented by Malaspina Printmakers and Good Luck Art Gallery
Location: East Van Studios, 870 East Cordova Street, Vancouver

This one-night exhibition will feature new work by our current scholars. Please 
join us in celebrating their work.

Justin Camilli is an Albuquerque-based artist who recently graduated from the 
Colorado State University with a focus in printmaking. By combining elements 
of personal narrative, imagination, and mystery, his prints provide an entrance 
into his own illustrious world of masked and changing characters. 
Experiences of his own growing identity and those of friends and family are 
depicted in whimsical and humorous ways, reflecting nostalgic moments and 
predicting future selves.

Todd Evanger is an interdisciplinary artist living and working in Vancouver. He 
recently graduated from the Emily Carr University of Art & Design and is 
currently a master of fine arts candidate in the department of art history, visual 
art and theory at the University of British Columbia. Evangerâ€™s current 
research explores the gendered body and the possible ways in which 
heteronormativity and hegemonic masculinity dominate discourse within visual 
culture.

Val Loewen is a Vancouver-based artist who recently graduated from the Nova 
Scotia College of Art & Design with a focus in printmaking and sculpture. The 
material aspects of image construction through accidental mark making and 
hand manipulation are evident in her work. She is influenced by the images 
and concepts of astrophysics and phenomenology, particularly how we come 
to interpret and normalize things that are far removed from common visual 
experiences. References include diagrams of star clusters, photographic 
images of space, and visual patterns of disorder.

Andrew Powe was born in Philadelphia and raised in London, Canada, and 
Riyadh, Saudi Arabia. He studied civil structural engineering and architecture 
at Acadia and Dalhousie Universities and visual art at the Nova Scotia College 
of Art & Design. Currently, he is completing his bachelor of fine arts from the 
University of British Columbia. He is interested in how the internet is affecting 
visual culture and changing the way society views, consumes, and interacts 
with images. He explores these ideas through large-scale print works that 
incorporate digital and traditional methods.
