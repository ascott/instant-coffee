Giovannone Thursday March 8 @ 5:30 p.m. in the Emily Carr University library 520 East

Christopher Pollon is a Vancouver-based independent journalist who reports on the 
politics of natural resources, focusing on energy, mines and oceans. His work has been 
published by National Geographic Books, The Walrus, Readers Digest, The Globe and 
Mail, and The Center for Investigative Journalism. He has been a feature writer and 
contributing editor at The Tyee since 2008, and currently reports for Vancouvers 
Discourse Media. The Peace in Peril, his first book, has been nominated for the George 
Ryga Award for Social Awareness in Literature, and is also shortlisted for the 2017 
Roderick Haig-Brown Regional Prize.

Aaron Giovannones poetry collections are The Loneliness Machine (Insomniac, 2014) 
and The Nonnets (Book Thug, forthcoming 2018). His prose has appeared in The 
Walrus and Vice, and with Naomi K. Lewis, he is co-editing an anthology of poetry and 
creative non-fiction titled Money. He has a Ph.D. in English Literature and Creative 
Writing and has taught at the Universita' di Siena (Italy), Brock University, and 
Okanagan College. He currently lives in Calgary.

04. Dynamo Arts Association | Mother Tongue | MAR 9

Mother Tongue

CLOSING RECEPTION - March 9, 2018
Dynamo Arts Association : 7pm - 10pm

This exhibition brings together new work by Gabriela Godoi and M.E. Sparks. Both 
artists explore painting as a material language that, although tied to dominant patriarchal 
histories, continues to offer potential for empowered feminist discourse surrounding 
concepts of visual pleasure, resistance, and bodily experience. The artists approach 
painting as a shared mother tongue of sorts - not only as a shared material language, 
but as a shared feminist narrative that speaks from and to the body. Through contorted, 
cut and confrontational forms, symbolism and inner monologues, material opacity and 
tension, the artists explore the concept of painting as present body, historical body, and 
misbehaving body; painting as carrier, as woman; painting as an indexical, political, and 
material utterance.

Gabriela Godoi is a Brazilian artist based in Vancouver, BC. She graduated from the 
MFA program at Emily Carr University of Art and Design in 2017 and holds a BFA from 
F.A.A.P. (So Paulo - Brazil). Recent exhibitions include O negativo como primrio at 
Espao Cpsula (So Paulo) and Moist, mucous, membrane at ECUAD Concourse Gallery
(Vancouver).

M.E. Sparks received a MFA (2016) from Emily Carr University of Art + Design and BFA
(2013) from NSCAD University. She was a finalist in the 2017 and 2016 RBC Painting
Competition and the recipient of the 2016 Nancy Petry Award. Recent exhibitions 
include A Beast Salient at Franc Gallery (Vancouver) and A Terrible Signal at Access 
Gallery (Vancouver).

05. gallery gachet | Neighbourhood Sketchbook Project | MAR 9

In March gallery gachet is pleased to present

Neighbourhood Sketchbook Project

Exhibition runs: March 9th - 17th, 2018

Opening reception: Friday, March 9, 6:00-9:00 PM 

Neighbourhood Sketchbook Project features hand-bound and art-filled sketchbooks 
from members of ten Downtown Eastside organizations. Each sketchbook tells a story 
of the person who created it"the pages resonating with the joy, wisdom, and resilience 
of these artists and of their community. 

The sketchbook is a medium that features the process of art-making while also 
becoming an art piece in and of itself. Experimental, freeing, and eclectic, this process 
makes way for the artist to take risks and to grow their creativity. 

Much like a sketchbook, the Neighbourhood Sketchbook Project is a collective 
experiment. In an effort to honour the strength and vision of communities that comprise 
the Downtown Eastside, it also takes risks and remains open to possibilities. The project 
builds upon the tireless work of neighbours, workers, groups, and organizations who 
strive to build relationships and trust with the participating artists. Multiple considerations 
of access, support, and translation were mounted in order to meet community needs. 
This groundwork allowed us to bring something new to those for whom the 
neighbourhood is vital.

The work on display is the culmination of a series of workshops, where artists of the 
Downtown Eastside gathered to learn and experiment with various art-making 
techniques, guided by WePress artist facilitators. Artists lovingly composed content and 
artwork using block and letterpress printing, drawing, collage, Chinese brush painting, 
hand quilting and embroidery as well as their own innovations.

At the opening reception, visitors are invited to fashion their own sketchbooks as they 
draw inspiration from the featured collection to start a storytelling journey.

06. Republic Gallery | Antonia Hirsch | MAR 9

Antonia Hirsch
DIALOG II
March 9 - April 14
Opening reception: Friday, March 9, 6-8pm

The exhibition DIALOG II presents a new body of work by Antonia Hirsch. The show 
borrows its name from a cardiac pacemaker, the Siemens DIALOG II. Together with the 
iPhone 6, museum display armatures, and the Faraday cage, its visual presence within 
this exhibition reflects on devices that connect and isolate, that frame experience, and 
that set the pace. Cotton, cast glass, copper, and expanded polystyrene are some of 
these works agents, collapsing contemporary technology and archaic craft.

All of these works are about communication in some form, about trying for connection 
or screening oneself off from it. The work using displays and screens that I had done 
previously had me look at the black mirror, an occult tool that, in terms of its 
appearance, resembles a smartphone in sleep mode. This might have triggered the 
question of magic for me"whether an object could be imbued with magic powers. And 
that jibes of course completely with the fact that we carry around an iPhone like a 
talisman. People get nervous if they dont have it on them, like its bad luck. Not 
because they need to be reachable, but because it means something quite indefinable 
to them. It has something to do with a sense of connection"perhaps to something 
larger than themselves. But whether you have your worry beads with you or you check 
your phone all the time, the difference is minimal, except that the worry beads perhaps 
do a better job in calming a restless mind. When I did research into placebos, relics, and 
lucky charms I wanted to find out: how does a charm actually become charmed?

-- Antonia Hirsch, January 2018

DIALOG II is accompanied by a conversation between Antonia Hirsch and the artist and 
writer Mitch Speed, available in print at the gallery or as a PDF online at 
republicgallery.com.

Antonia Hirschs work has been exhibited at MIT List Visual Art Center, Cambridge; The 
Power Plant, Toronto; the Taipei Fine Arts Museum; Tramway, Glasgow; Salzburger 
Kunstverein; Witte de With Center for Contemporary Art, Rotterdam; and ZKM Museum 
of Contemporary Art, Karlsruhe, among others. Her work can be found in public 
collections such as that of the Vancouver Art Gallery, the National Gallery of Canada, 
and the Sackner Archive of Concrete & Visual Poetry, Miami Beach. She is the editor of 
the anthology Intangible Economies, published by Fillip in 2012 and Negative Space: 
Orbiting Inner and Outer Experience, published by SFU Galleries in 2015.

NOT SENT LETTERS & GUESTS form public constellations of engaged 
interdisciplinary practice, unmediated by the realpolitik conditions of contemporary art as 
a competitive professional sphere. The interrelatedness of art, society and everyday life 
is critically explored amongst a diverse plurality of artists and publics. Jeremy Todd 
instigates each event as an extension of his ongoing Not Sent Letters project, an 
entanglement with the production of meaning, self and cultural memory, involving 
image/text epistolary detours online (since 2005), digital shorts, interdisciplinary 
performance works and cooperatively realized public events: 
www.notsentlettersproject.com

This is the twenty-fourth NOT SENT LETTERS & GUESTS event.

ANDREW WITT, a Vancouver-based art historian, contributes to NSL&G for the first 
time with the letter I tried the door and it was unlocked ...". Written during a research 
trip to Los Angeles, Witt's letter discusses the collaborative photography of Maya Deren 
and Alexander Hammid, the art writings of Marcel Proust, the memoirs of Christa Wolf, 
the architecture of Richard Neutra, the aesthetics of friendship, the epiphanic and 
ecstatic. 

ELIZABETH MILTON is a performance and media artist who lives as a guest on the 
unceded territory of the Musqueam, Squamish and Tsleil-Waututh First Nations in 
Vancouver, BC. Milton returns to NSL&G to debut Pink Noise, a performance work 
investigating screen space, materiality and the maximalist spectacle of video distortion. 

DANIEL CODE & SEAN ARDEN, two Vancouver-based multi-disciplinary artists & 
friends, collaborate with ANTHEM JACKSON PRODUCTIONS to debut Forever 
Spaghetti, a response to the citys shrinking cultural landscape. This interactive work 
immortalizes a moment in time at Nicks Spaghetti House Restaurant (a pasta and 
meatball institution that has bled starchy Calabrese and Neapolitan tradition for over 
sixty-three years). As sterile glass and steel buildings entirely displace histories of 
cultural practice and community, the virtual capsule of Forever Spaghetti might become 
the last vestige of a world as wholesome and comforting as your mothers old country 
cooking.

ALISHA WENG, a Vancouver-based interdisciplinary artist and photographer, returns to 
NSL&G with her sister ESTHER WENG to screen a new work inspired by a box of 
strangers' memories (gleaned from the sidewalks of a changing city). The Weng sisters 
consider commonalities between disparate peoples, wondering if and how their paths 
have crossed. Stories lost and found are magnified, obscured, and reflected through the 
glass of the camera, revealing intimate, oblique and fragmented memories taking on 
new life.

SARAH SHAMASH, a Vancouver-based artist and educator, returns to NSL&G to 
screen a new video experiment exploring a brief moment of inward reprieve amidst the 
humdrum of domesticity. 

NEAL ROCKWELL, JORDAN MINKOFF & LEE McCLURE, three Montreal-based 
interdisciplinary artists and friends, screen Want, a work of experimental comedy 
inspired by third rate reality television. Set in the near future, Want imagines a world 
defined by economic precarity, in which the only chance of making a living involves 
broadcasting one's daily struggles through the carnival lens of low budget spectacle. 

JEREMY TODD presents a new NOT SENT LETTERS SET featuring digital projection, 
scoring by BEN WILSON and letters selected and read from the project archive by 
ANDREW WITT (To Willing Conflators Of Emily Dickinson And Jeremy Todd), SARAH 
SHAMASH (To Gratefulness) & SEAN ARDEN (To The Last Person On Earth).

About the show
When I was a little child, I always saw my mother working on her loom. A wooden 
shuttle would fly back and forth between two layers of cotton thread. Plaid patterns 
slowly appeared: big ones, small ones, red ones, blue ones, rectangular ones, and 
square ones. Every piece was woven with a chirping song.

Since the globalization, everything has been changed. The loom disappeared, and the 
plaid is only left in my memory. A big migration is brewing in China. Farmers pour into 
cities to work, and city people go to bigger cities or foreign cities in order to looking for 
opportunities. My family was part of the great migration. We moved from the countryside 
to the city, and I left China to be educated in Canada. When I see high-rise buildings 
blooming among the cities, I feel familiar with their square structure even though they 
are in different sizes. People live, struggle, and chase their dreams in these spaces. 
Although we are restricted by the social system all the time, and our thoughts are 
influenced by our older generations, we will constantly seek and build a new self in the 
challenges.

Mother had worked hard to weave plaid with her hands, and the beautiful design was 
her blueprint. These pieces cloth would be made into the clothes for her family, which 
embodies her love for her family and her hope for the future. Today, the design has 
become a memory. And high-rise buildings are springing up in cities around the world. 
They have a similar grid structure and set up a home for people. Although there are 
invisible social constraints that regulate our lives and thoughts, the world is changing, 
and no one can stop it, just as the pattern has evolved into a grid of high-rise buildings.

14. The Polygon Gallery | INTERWEAVING N. VANCOUVER | MAR 11

INTERWEAVING N. VANCOUVER
March 11, 1pm
The Polygon Gallery

On March 11 at 1pm, The Polygon Gallery is honoured to present Interweaving N. 
Vancouver, a discussion with Indigenous artists Xwemilut"Lisa Lewis, Katxnamet"
Shelley Thomas, and Melvin Williams, moderated by Chief Janice George of the 
Squamish Nation. Admission is free.

In 2003, Chief Janice George helped to revitalise the art of Salish weaving, which had 
been declining for many years. In this conversation, the artists will talk about why Salish 
weaving was not being widely practiced, how it has been restored, and why they were 
motivated to help bring this art form back. They will also explore the history and customs 
behind weaving, and how woven works are used in the community. In addition, artist 
Melvin Williams will introduce the traditions and techniques of cedar weaving, which has 
been practiced in his family for generations.

Interweaving N. Vancouver is presented in conjunction with N. Vancouver, The Polygon 
Gallery's inaugural exhibition, on view until April 29.

15. Audain Gallery | Claudette Lauzon | The Unmaking of Home in Contemporary Art | MAR 14

Book Launch: Claudette Lauzon's The Unmaking of Home in Contemporary Art
Wednesday, March 14, 2018, 7pm
Audain Gallery, Vancouver

Join us for the launch of Claudette Lauzon's book The Unmaking of Home in 
Contemporary Art (University of Toronto Press, 2017). Lauzon will read excerpts from 
the book and reflect on recent artworks that employ the figure of home as a strategy for 
mediating legacies and conditions of human suffering.

In a world where the notion of home can be more unsettling than it is comforting, artists 
are using this literal and figurative space to reframe human responses to traumatic

transnational analysis of contemporary artworks that challenge the assumption of home

Celebrating the excessive abundance of the archive, "Beginning with the Seventies:

1996) in the second iteration of a project by Alexandra Bischoff.

Rereading Room (2016-18) is activated by "The Readers" (2018); a performance 
orchestrated by Bischoff wherein thirteen trans- and cis-women, and non-binary artists, 
authors, and activists have been invited to occupy the installation for the duration of the 
exhibition. They work with and against the inventory by reading, annotating and 
supplementing the collection to form a dossier of responses.

The Readers are given an opportunity to respond to their time in the Gallery by 
participating in the monthly series The Readers Respond. They may share excerpts 
from the installations available books, read a text that theyve authored, screen a 
related video, or moderate a group discussion surrounding topics that have become 
important to them during their time in Rereading Room.

Events occur at the Morris and Helen Belkin Art Gallery monthly throughout the
