The On Edge readings series presents:

a reading by Jordan Scott
7 pm, SB 406
Thurs, Oct 28
Emily Carr University
Granville Island, Vancouver
Coast Salish Territories

Jordan Scott is the author of Silt (New Star Books 2005) which was
nominated for the Dorothy Livesay Poetry Prize, and Blert (Coach House
Books 2008). In the fall of 2006, Jordan worked on the final sections of
Blert while acting as a writer in residence at the International Writers
and Translators Centre in Rhodes, Greece, and has recently returned from
readings in Oslo and Slovenia. Blert was adapted into a Bravo Fact film
which is in rotation and was the subject of an online interactive
documentary commissioned by the National Film Board of Canada. Jordan is a
member of the Kootenay School of Writing and co-host of the series
RespondencyWest. He lives and works in Mount Pleasant, Vancouver BC.

The On Edge series gratefully acknowledges the support of the Canada
Council and Emily Carr University.

Note: There is free parking in the parkade under the ECU South Building
after 7 pm.

Bios of upcoming writers:

Lydia Kwa has published a collection of poems, The Colours of Heroines, and
her first novel, This Place Called Absence, was nominated for two Canadian
and two US literary awards. Her novel The Walking Boy was nominated for the
2006 Ethel Wilson Fiction Prize. Her new novel, Pulse, is out in Spring
2010. She is working on a book of poetry entitled sinuous. Kwa also works
as a writer and psychologist.

Meredith Quartermain is a Vancouver writer, publisher and community
builder. In 2006 her collection Vancouver Walking won the BC Book Award for
Poetry. Matter, another book of poems, appeared in 2008, and Nightmarker
was a finalist for the 2009 Vancouver Book Award. Her fourth book of poems,
Recipes from the Red Planet, will be published in October 2010. Her work
has appeared in many Canadian magazines, including The Walrus, CV2, the
Literary Review of Canada, Prism International, The Capilano Review, West
Coast Line, Canadian Literature, and Matrix.
