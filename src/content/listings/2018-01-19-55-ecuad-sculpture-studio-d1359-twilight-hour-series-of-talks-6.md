Twilight Hour

A series of talks hosted by the Painting and Sculpture programs at Emily Carr 
University of Art + Design.

Spring 2018 Schedule

Derya Akay
Wednesday, February 21st @ 6:00 pm 
Sculpture Studio D1359

Derya Akay (b, 1988, Turkey) is an artist living in Vancouver. He received the 2016 
Portfolio Prize Emerging Artist Award in Vancouver. He has an upcoming two-person 
project with Dilara Akay at Grunt Gallery, Vancouver in January 2018 titled Ghost 
Spring and a solo exhibition at Unit 17, Vancouver in May 2018. Recent solo 
exhibitions include with bread, Campbell River Art Gallery, 2017; Pumice, Del Vaz 
Projects, Los Angeles, California, 2017; Painting with Light, Kunstverein Toronto, 
2015; Breakfast, Lunch, Dinner & Supper, Centre A, Vancouver, 2014. Recent group 
shows include The Lulennial II: A Low-Hanging Fruit, Lulu, Mexico City, 2018; 
Nature's Way, Cooper Cole, Toronto, 2017; Here, Aga Khan Museum, Toronto, 2017; 
Ambivalent Pleasures, Vancouver Art Gallery, 2016. He recently completed his 
project Mant-, Brek, Baklava at the Burrard Marina Field House Residency program 
hosted by the Contemporary Art Gallery, Vancouver. For this project Akay worked 
with elder women with immigrant backgrounds to host a series of cooking workshops 
and lunches open to the public. 

Mike Bourscheid
Wednesday, February 28th @ 6:00 pm
Sculpture Studio D1359

Mike Bourscheid was born in 1984 in Esch-sur-Alzette (Luxembourg). He studied at 
Luniversit dAix-Marseille (France) and Universitt der Kunste Berlin (Germany). 
Today, he lives and works in Vancouver, Canada. In 2017, Mike Bourscheid 
represented Luxembourg at the 57th Venice Biennale. His recent exhibitions include: 
Access Gallery, Vancouver (Canada); Nanaimo Art Gallery, Nanaimo (Canada); 
Casino- Forum dArt Contemporain, (Luxembourg); Insitu, Berlin (Germany).

Gabrielle LHirondelle Hill
Wednesday, March 7th @ 6:00 pm
Sculpture Studio D1359

Gabrielle L'Hirondelle Hill is a Metis artist working and living on unceded Musqueam, 
Tsleil-Waututh, and Squamish land. Gabrielle works mostly with sculpture, often 
using found materials to build forms that investigate the relationships between place, 
property, occupation, and settler and Indigenous economies.

***All talks take place in the Sculpture Studio, located on the ground floor, closest to 
the east entrance to the building. Free and open to the public.***
