5:30pm-7pm
Sttewekw / Second Beach, Stanley Park
Meet at the Fieldhouse, 8701 Stanley Park Drive
August 23rd 2023, 5:30pm introduction and walk, 6:30pm tea

Cease Wyss
Leah Weinstein

How can we foster empathy for forests, plants, rocks, earth?

How can we imagine time scales for other beings/entities " insects, flowers, trees,
mountains?

Join artists Cease Wyss and Leah Weinstein as they contemplate these questions
through a tree listening activity at Sttewekw / Second Beach in Stanley Park. We
will gather at the Fieldhouse at 5:30pm for a brief introduction, followed by individual
and/ or collective wanderings to trees. We will return to the Fieldhouse at 6:30pm for
tea.

This event is free and wheelchair accessible. Dress for the weather, and bring a
blanket
to sit on. Children and animal friends are welcome.

Tuyttanat Cease Wyss (Skwxwu7mesh, Sto:lo, Hawaiian, Swiss) is an educator,
interdisciplinary artist and Indigenous ethnobotanist engaged in community based
teaching and sharing. Throughout Wysss 30 year practice, Wysss work
encompasses storytelling and collaborative initiatives through their knowledge and
restoration of Indigenous plants and natural spaces. Wyss has been recognized for
exchanging traditional knowledge in remediating our relationship to land through
digital media, site-specific engagements and weaving. Wyss has participated and
exhibited at galleries, museums, festivals and public space such as Vancouver Art
Gallery, Morris, Helen Belkin Art Gallery, Contemporary Art Gallery and the PuSh
Festival to name a few. Their work can be found in various collections such as the
National Library of Canada, Special Collections at the Walter Phillips Gallery, and the
Vancouver Public Library.

Leah Weinstein is grateful to live
and work on unceded xmky"m,
Skwxw7mesh and sl"lwta" territories. Her artistic practice investigates
connections between material culture and the social ideals of a larger collective.
Using everyday objects and readymade materials to create sculpture, textiles and
performance, Leah examines relationships between individuals, collectives, action
and display. Her work has been supported by Canada Council for the Arts, BC Arts
Council, Vancouver Board of Parks and Recreation, Touchstones Nelson Museum of
Art and History, and City of Richmond Public Art Commissions. She completed a
Master of Fine Arts from Emily Carr University of Art + Design.
