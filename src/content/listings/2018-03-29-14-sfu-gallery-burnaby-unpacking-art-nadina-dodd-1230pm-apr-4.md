Nadina Dodd on Marc Chagalls, Man and Violin, n.d.
Clinical Counsellor and Art Therapist at FIC and SFU
SFU Gallery, AQ 3004, Burnaby
WED, APR 4 / 12:30 - 1:30PM

8888 University Drive
Burnaby, B.C.

Unpacking Art: Lunchtime Talks on Works in the SFU Art Collection is a speaker 
series that invites members of the SFU community to unpack the context and the 
questions raised by works of art in the collection.

In conversation with Marc Chagalls painting, Man and Violin (n.d.), Nadina Dodd, a 
Registered Clinical Counsellor and Art Therapist, will draw connections between 
Chagalls painting, the artists journey as a displaced person, and the identity 
formation of young individuals as they navigate life in a new place. Examples of her 
students work will also be on display.

Nadina Dodd currently works at Fraser International College (a partner of SFU) as a 
Case Manager supporting international students in their health and wellbeing. Over 
the past two years she has been developing and facilitating an art therapy group at 
the SFU Housing premises for students who struggle with mental and emotional 
health or have been impacted by stressful environmental circumstances. This group 
aims to foster self-expression, exploration, and social connections, working towards 
developing forms of communication around social issues. 

Marc Chagall (1887-1985) was an early modernist artist of Belarusian Jewish origin. 
His figurative work is a synthesis of Symbolism, Fauvism and Cubism. Born in 
Russia, Chagall moved to France in 1910, spending time traveling between Saint 
Petersburg, Paris and Berlin, which influenced his representations of Eastern 
European Jewish folk culture. Chagall later escaped occupied France in 1941 to the 
United States during WWI and the Nazi campaigns against modern art.
