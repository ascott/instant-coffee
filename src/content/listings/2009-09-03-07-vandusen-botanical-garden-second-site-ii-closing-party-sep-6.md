Second Site II at VanDusen Botanical Garden (37th and Oak)

We are having a "Closing" on Sunday September 6th, noon to 4 pm.

It's your last chance to see the collection of outdoor electronic
works at the garden.

Presenting "Audio Pools" by Diana Burgoyne, "Poison Mentor" by Peter
Courtemanche, "Diaspora" by David Floren, "Alate" by Robin Ripley,
"Pondering" by Matt Smith, and "Weidenhammer's Sonic Therapy Spa" by
Lori Weidenhammer.

From July through September 2009, Second Site collective is presenting
its second series of outdoor interventions and installations at the
VanDusen Botanical Gardens in Vancouver. The works illustrate simple
mechanisms of nature as well as form a hybrid between bio-diversity
(as opposed to bio-industry) and electronic art (as opposed to
industrial technology). The works include hybrid sculpture that merges
plants with electronics, robotics, a fungus that monitors levels of
man-made radiation, hand-crank-powered audio pools, and a sonic
therapy spa based on the biorhythms of plants. All of the pieces are
self-powered either by the sun, or through interaction with the
viewer. They are designed to be autonomous, with no need for
electrical outlets or other materials from the site. In this way the
objects become independent organisms that inhabit the gardens in the
same way as biological creatures.

Second Site collective is a group of artists who create electronic art
(sound, kinetics, robotics, primal circuitry, sculpture) in green
spaces – parks, gardens, areas of urban agriculture, beaches, and
"urban wilderness". The art created by Second Site causes the audience
to do a "double take", transforming a familiar space into a
"meta-site" – a place where art has created a heightened sensory
experience. It causes a sense of curiosity and enchantment in the
viewer that is designed to make people think about the relationship
between human intervention, technology, and bio-diversity.
