The COLD WAR CONFIDENTIAL Symposium will be held at the Morris and Helen
Belkin Art Gallery (UBC) on February 4th, 2011 from 10-3pm with
presentations by Daniel Grausum, Finis Dunaway, Martha Langford, and John
Langford on the relationships between nuclear war, art, photography,
diplomacy, and culture.

AND

John O'Brian will present Picturing Nuclear Risk as the third part of the
Bomb in the Wilderness: Nuclear Photography, the Atomic Age, and Canada
Series in Canadian Studies at the Liu Institute for Global Issues (6476 NW
Marine Drive) Tuesday Feb. 8, 2011 at 7 pm.
He will examine the place of photography in the construction of nuclear
narratives since World War II and ask to what extent the mushroom cloud,
the meta-symbol of the atomic age, is laced with Canadian content.

Western Front Media Arts is excited to announce a pop-up exhibition and
accompanying artist talk by current Media Artists in Residence Oliver Laric
and Aleksandra Domanovic. For one week only, Berlin based artists Laric and
Domanovic, are presenting an in-process video installation in the Western
Front gallery featuring recent works, including work completed during their
month-long production residency at Western Front. Both Laric and Domanovic
work primarily on the internet, but often extend their respective practices
into physical space.

The installation takes its point departure from two words Shanzhai (Ã¥Â±Â±Ã¥Â¯Â¨)
and Turbo, terms that refer to imitation, copying and the ongoing way in
which images and objects are translated and appropriated across cultures.

Shanzhai (Ã¥Â±Â±Ã¥Â¯Â¨) defines a production system outside of the legal domain,
centered in Shenzhen in the province of Guangdong, China. Shanzhai
literally means mountain stronghold, implying a place of creativity
unsupervised by state monopoly. Shanzhai products adapt to local needs,
reflect current developments and progress at a more dynamic pace than
official products. Mobile phones are equipped with flashlights, solar
panels, dual SIM card slots. Shanzhai redefines the perception of copy and
original. It is not pure mimesis, rather an eclectic translation of ideas.

The prefix "Turbo" is used in ex-Yugoslavia to describe hybrid folkloristic
and nationalistic forms that imitate contemporary technological aesthetics.
Turbo Folk, a highly popular genre of Serbian music of the 90s, freely
samples traditional and contemporary sources resulting in a mix of techno
rhythms and traditional melodies. Accordingly, terms such as Turbo
Urbanism, Turbo Politics and Turbo Architecture developed. War time Turbo
Culture was mostly associated with exaggerated nationalism, almost
pornographic kitsch and crime glorification, the post war "Turbo" boldly
contrasts nationalist xenophobia while retaining its stylistic identity.

Oliver Laric and Aleksandra Domonovic have exhibited their respective works
widely including exhibitions at the New Museum (New York, NY), Seventeen
Gallery (London, UK) and MU Eidenhove in the Netherlands. Laric and
Aleksdandra curate the contemporary art website www.vvork.com

Adam Gandy exhibits new video, sculpture and photography that stems
from an exploration of our lived world. Gandys work examines the cult
of the masculine ideal reflected through film, philosophy and history
exploring the notion of resourcefulness as heroism and an interest in
the moment before climax.

Adam Gandy is an artist and writer living in Vancouver. He received a
