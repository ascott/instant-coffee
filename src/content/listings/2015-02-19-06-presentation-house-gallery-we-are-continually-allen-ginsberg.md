“We Are Continually Exposed to the Flashbulb of Death:” The Photographs of
Allen Ginsberg (1953-1996).
February 21 - April 5, 2015
Opening reception February 20, 7PM.

Presentation House Gallery’s forthcoming exhibition celebrates the artistic
pursuits—both visual and verbal—of Allen Ginsberg (1926-1997), one of the
most prolific poets of the twentieth century.

During his career as a revolutionary writer, composer, and activist,
Ginsberg extensively documented his life in photographs, amassing an
archive that now encompasses thousands of images. Last year, the University
of Toronto received a donation of 7,600 of Ginsberg’s prints, the largest
single collection in the world. Presentation House Gallery is thrilled to
bring a selection of these images to British Columbia for the first time.

Taken over the course of four decades, the photographs juxtapose Ginsberg’s
ever-changing surroundings with a steadfast group of friends, lovers and
muses, alongside fellow Beat poets including Jack Kerouac, Lucien Carr,
Neal Cassady and William S. Burroughs. The prints extend beyond mere
depiction, however, tracing the poet’s literary influences through music,
political freedoms and even Buddhist philosophy. Ginsberg captioned each of
the gelatin silver prints in handwritten script, further integrating his
photographic and textual mediums. Indeed, the same shrewd honesty and
liberated spontaneity that guide Ginsberg’s poetry are also channelled
through his lens.

Organized by the University of Toronto Art Centre and the Justina M.
Barnicke Gallery with the Thomas Fisher Rare Book Library at the University
of Toronto, with special thanks to the Larry & Cookie Rossy Family
Foundation. This exhibition features a brochure with essays by Louis
Kaplan, Professor of History and Theory of Photography and New Media,
University of Toronto, and curator John Shoesmith of the Thomas Fisher Rare
Book Library, University of Toronto.
