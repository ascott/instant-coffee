UNDER MY FEET: AN EVENING OF DANCE CINEMA

Under My Feet is a program of stunning cinematic works by filmmakers who train their lenses on dancers. Curated by local filmmaker Steve Rosenberg, part 1 highlights works from hot-shot directors, Daniel Conrad and Moze Mossanen, two of Canada’s pre-eminent dance filmmakers, as well as a collection of stylish Bravo!FACT dance films. Part 2 features the documentary gem Ballerina, which had four, sold-out screenings at last year’s Vancouver International Film Festival and placed in the top 10 for audience favourites of the festival.

UNDER MY FEET
02 April 2008, 7pm
Scotiabank Dance Centre [677 Davie Street]
$8 Cineworks members, $10 Non-members

DANCE ON FILM: A CINEMATIC SALON WITH ACCLAIMED DANCE FILMMAKERS

This moderated panel discussion will screen clips from participating filmmakers to guide a dialogue about the rewards and challenges of capturing moving bodies on film. Swapping trade secrets and discussing effective game plans, Dance on Film will take place in an intimate yet informal venue where audience members are encouraged to join the exchange. 

The panelists will be Daniel Conrad (Afternoon of the Chimeras, Subways, 7 Universal Solvents), Tara Hungerford (Mon amour mon parapluie, Break a Leg, Rosie, Annabelle, Wearing the Unknown), and Bo Myers (Blossom). Steve Rosenberg (Blink, Divine Waters, Watching Mrs. Pomerantz) will moderate. 

DANCE ON FILM
03 April 2008, 7pm
Cineworks Studio [1131 Howe, back lane entrance]
Free

BODIES IN MOTION, BODIES AT REST: A DANCE FILM WORKSHOP WITH DANIEL CONRAD

Bodies in Motion, Bodies at Rest is a workshop for dancers, choreographers and filmmakers who want to make dance film–that is, they want to work with a truly hybrid form.

Most dance films try to film dance that was originally choreographed for stage. This usually fails: film cuts the world into shots from many angles, but stage-dance is made as a continuum from one angle. Going from stage to film must start with filmic choreography, montage, angles, camera movement, and locations. The best solutions are to re-choreograph a stage work for the camera, or to compose de-novo, in fragments with “sticky ends,” shot by shot. In both cases, the choreographer must understand film form. All this and much more will be explained in the workshop, with films and film clips to watch, and some hands-on practice with camera, lights and a dancer.
