Scrivener's Monthly 
Spring 2016 Season
Featuring Andrew Berardini, Antonia Hirsch & Layli Long Soldier

Andrew Berardini: Friday, April 22 @ 8:17pm
Antonia Hirsch: Thursday, May 12 @ 7pm
Layli Long Soldier: Saturday, June 11 @ 2pm
Free Admission

Grand Luxe Hall
Western Front
303 East 8th Avenue
Vancouver, BC Canada

Western Front is pleased to announce the Spring season of our 
ongoing program Scrivener's Monthly. On April 22, 2016 we welcome 
Los Angeles-based writer Andrew Berardini for a twilight talk during 
which he will muse on the varieties of clouds and how to look at 
sunsets. Berlin-based artist and writer Antonia Hirsch will join us on 
May 12, 2016 to consider the relationship between inner and outer 
worlds, reflecting on images and ideas that emerged during the making 
of her recent book, Negative Space: Orbiting Inner and Outer 
Experience. Finally, on June 11, 2016, Layli Long Soldier reads from 
her forthcoming book Whereas, a poetic text that critiques political 
language, specifically in response to the Congressional Resolution of 
Apology to Native Americans signed by Barack Obama in 2009. 
Following this, Long Soldier will be joined by curator cheyenne turions 
for a conversation about her work. 

Presented by the Exhibitions Program at Western Front, Scrivener's 
Monthly is an ongoing speaker's series that explores the space 
between material practices and spoken words: a periodical that talks. 
Situated in relationship with the visual art programs at Western Front, 
this experiment in "not publishing" invites writers, artists and other 
thinkers from Vancouver, across Canada, and internationally for 
readings, performances, and lectures. 

Biographies:

Andrew Berardini. A writer between essay and poetry, occasional editor 
and curator of art. Teacher at the artist-run Mountain School of Arts. Art 
Book Review co-founder. Los Angeles Editor for Mousse and 
Contributing Editor to Art-Agenda and Momus. Warhol/Creative Capital 
Art Writer and 221A Curatorial Resident Co-Grantee. Author of the 
memoirish Danh Vo: Relics (Mouse, 2015) and a perennially in-
progress book about color. With poet Quinn Latimer, faculty in Spring 
2016 for a residency on art writing as literature at the Banff Centre. 

Antonia Hirsch is a Berlin-based artist, writer, and editor whose work 
has been exhibited and published internationally. She is the editor of 
Intangible Economies (Fillip, 2012). Her exhibition Boxing Shadows 
opens at Republic Gallery in Vancouver on May 13 and runs to June 
25, 2016.

Layli Long Solider is a graduate of the MFA program at Bard College in 
New York and her writing has appeared in The American Poet, 
American Indian Journal of Culture and Research, PEN America, The 
Brooklyn Rail, and Mud City, among others. She lives in Tsaile, AZ on 
the Navajo Nation.
