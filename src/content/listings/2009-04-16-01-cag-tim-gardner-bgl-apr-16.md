The Contemporary Art Gallery opens two new exhibitions: the first solo exhibition in Canada by 
British Columbia painter Tim Gardner and the first exhibition in Vancouver by the Quebec-based 
artist collective BGL.

April 17 – June 28, 2009

OPENING RECEPTION: Thursday, April 16th, 6-9pm

Tim Gardner

Canadian artist Tim Gardner is well-known internationally for his intimate and precise watercolor
paintings and oil pastel drawings. Gardner is most recognized for his figurative works, which are
based on personal snapshots of family events, vacations with friends and day to activities. For 
his early work, he used photographs of his older brother and friends as source material, capturing 
the sometimes excessive leisure activities of these post adolescent men engaged in sporting 
activities or partying. For his solo exhibition at the Contemporary Art Gallery, his first in Canada, 
Gardner places new emphasis on the landscape and watercolour. In this new work, the 
landscape becomes a place to formally engage with the proprieties of water colour as a medium 
that offers a unique immediacy. 

Tim Gardner received his B.F.A. from the University of Manitoba (1996) and his M.F.A. from 
Columbia University, New York (1999). Since graduating he has exhibited widely with his most 
recent solo exhibition at The National Gallery, London (2007). In 2007, Gardner also participated
in group exhibitions including Salon Nouveau, Engholm Engelhorn Galerie, Vienna; All the More 
Real: Portrayals of Intimacy and Empathy, The Parrish Art Museum, Southampton, New York; 
and XXS, Sommer Gallery, Tel Aviv. Gardner is represented by 303 Gallery, New York.

BGL 

BGL is an artist collective based in Québec City, and is comprised of three artists Jasmin 
Bilodeau, Sébastien Giguère and Nicholas Laverdière, who have been working together since 
1996. They create large scale installations that combine comedy and social critique. BGL uses 
humour as a means to engage audiences, to investigate the formal properties of installation and 
sculpture, and to address global and local political issues. For their exhibition at the 
Contemporary Art Gallery, they depict Johnny Cash’s burning ring of fire and the clichés of 
camping through the aesthetic constructs of Alexander Calder’s formal hanging mobiles and floor 
stabiles.

BGL has exhibited widely, including solo exhibitions at the Musée d'art contemporain de 
Montréal; Musée d’art moderne, Lille Métropole, France; and Mercer Union, Toronto. They 
participated in the 2007 Montrèal Biennial, and were finalists for the Sobey Art Award in 2006. In 
2008 they exhibited as part of Caught in the Act at the National Gallery of Canada, Ottawa and 
were commissioned by No. 9 to create a public art work in Toronto’s Don River. Their work has 
been featured in Canadian Art, Toronto and Parachute, Montreal. They are represented by Diaz 
Contemporary Art, Toronto and Parisian Laundry, Montreal.
