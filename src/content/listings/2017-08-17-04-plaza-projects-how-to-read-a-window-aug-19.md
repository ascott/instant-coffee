Come join us for readings by Donato Mancini and Alex Bovril on August the 19th. As 
you get off Aberdeen station pause briefly for the last look at Plaza Projects before 
joining us at the Aberdeen Centre food court.

4:30 PM - 7 PM
PLAZA projects
#2370-4000 No.3 Road, Richmond

The windows are shut yet the curtains are drawn back.

How to Read a Window is a response to an overwhelming pressure of bureaucracy 
and censorship, its effects on knowledge, information, and dissemination.

Plaza Projects opens its door as an Artist-Run Centre with a dedication to 
Contemporary Art and its expressions amidst the consumerist paradise that is 
Aberdeen Square. In our attempts of providing alternative modes to the shopping 
mall experience and engaging in a dialogue, our past year is filled with constant 
negotiations with bureaucracy and restrictions. Unfortunately, we cannot outrun our 
conditions and Plaza Projects is coming to a close.

And yet still an exhibition can still run while the doors are closed. Forms, shapes, 
and words can be glimpsed on the other side of the glass. To gain access, the ability 
to do so correlates with power, authority, and mechanisms of control. Said control is 
often to limit what is given to the other. Yet some things only reveal themselves in 
their inaccessibility. Readings, texts, and books are all themes which run through the 
works assembled. From Louis Cabris poem installed on the exterior of window 
panes of the space where they can be felt/read by anyone and Donato Mancinis 
poem on a blackboard written out in his new Hashtag font. Bernadette Phans 
drawings of books left to her by her aunt which has since extended to drawings of 
books in general. And Carey Ann Schaefers painting on vellum of the flora 
described and found in books she has read. The pleasure, frustration, and 
complications of reading are addressed. The works have been installed slowly and 
over the course of the month, an artist and a work every week. A slow accumulation 
of words, texts images as accretions onto the envelope, a final residue of art and 
culture against the window panes before the space returns once again to the void.
