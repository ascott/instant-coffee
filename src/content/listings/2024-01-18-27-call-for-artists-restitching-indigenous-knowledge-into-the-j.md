CALL FOR ARTISTS: Pawsitively Reclaiming Heritage: Restitching Indigenous
Knowledge into the Social Fabric of Dog Blankets (T-pis/tuppies)

Adele ''"' Arseneau invites BC bead workers belonging to Indigenous
Communities with historic connections to the use
of dogs in travel, to participate in a 20-week immersive workshop focused on the
creation of traditional t-pis (sled dog blankets).
This unique opportunity aims to reestablish and promote the rich cultural heritage of
Indigenous communities and their connection
to dog kin.
Originating from Indigenous communities located in the Boreal ecological zone, dog
blanket regalia (t-pis in nehiyaw
(Cree)) are
blankets made from wool, draped over the backs of sled dogs. They are highly
embellished with colourful and intricate beadwork,
silk embroidery, rick rack and moose or caribou hair tufting, and edged with fringes
made of hide, ribbon or yarn and adorned with
bells. Tapis heralded the arrival of a visitor, bringing joy and celebration to the
community. Imagine the sound of ringing bells and
howling, barking sled dogs dressed smartly in brightly colored regalia entering the
village, announcing the arrival of loved ones
or packages. Sled dogs were symbols of our independence and transportation
around
the North. We hold a deep, and respectful
kinship with them. Tapis are an acknowledgement of our deep respect and care for
our
dog kin.
This opportunity has space for 6 Indigenous beaders to make Tapis regalia for their
communities. This residency will be conducted
as weekly
virtual meetings from March - August 2024 with participants completing
homework each week. Beaders will
learn
how to create a traditional Tapis for a special dog in their life.
All materials will be provided, free of charge, and mailed to participants
Beaders selected to participate will be paid an honorarium of $2,000.
Once completed, the created works will be exhibited at the Seymour Art Gallery in
North Vancouver from September "
November 2024.
Application deadline is midnight, January 31, 2024.
ELIGIBILITY CRITERIA:
Experienced Bead Workers: Participants must possess a high level of expertise in
beadwork techniques. Mature Indigenous
artists who are working to achieve widespread recognition are encouraged to apply.
Age and Cultural Background: Preference will be given to applicants with a lived
experience as a Native/Indigenous person.
Priority will be given to individuals who belong to Nations with historic connections to
the use of dogs in travel.(For example:
Northern Nehiyaw
(Cree), Woodland Cree, Northern Dene Nations, Gwichen, Kaska
Dene, Northern Tutchone, Hn, Metis
from the
Batoche/Willow Bunch or North of those areas, Inuit, or any other Nation
with
proven cultural ties to dogs with
regalia.)
Commitment to Cultural Transmission: Successful applicants must agree to share
the
knowledge gained during the
workshop with others within their respective Nations. This commitment to cultural
transmission is vital for the preservation
and continuation of traditional practices.
Proof of Indigenous Identity: Applicants must belong to an Indigenous community
and
provide First Nations, Inuit or Metis
identification or a letter of acceptance from their community if non-status.
Dog Ownership: Artists must have access to a dog model to create the work for this
project, reflecting the historical
connection between Indigenous communities and sled dogs.
Time Commitment: Participants should be available for a minimum of 2 hours per
week for 20 weeks to attend workshops,
collaborate with fellow artists, and and able to allocate additional time to progress
toward project
completion. A strong sense
of responsibility is essential to meet the project deadline.
Location: Preference will be given to artists based in British Columbia.
APPLICATION PROCESS:
Interested artists are invited to submit their applications via google form link (see
below) by January 31, 2024. Applications should
include 3-5 photos showcasing their best beadwork, a photo of their dog model, their
community/tribal affiliation, a personal
statement outlining their cultural background and connection to sled dog traditions,
current CV and committing to attending all
zoom sessions and completing the project within the stated guidelines.
Selected artists will be notified by February 16, 2024, and workshops will commence
by March 18, 2024. For inquiries, please
contact Adele at metiscaron@gmail.com
Submissions can be completed through this link:
https://forms.gle/bLdpPJDzPTcigfvh9

About the
Artist:
A disabled Nehiyaw/Mtis multi-disciplinary artist, (carver, bead contemporary Mtis
and
plains style traditional beadwork, create
traditional hide textiles, along with painting in the mediums of acrylic and digital art to
educate about my displaced heritage and
languages.); I create work utilizing stories to engage viewers through relatable
feelings
and experiences. Weaving connections
from cultural, social and environmental issues to people in the way my family taught
me, to learn and share knowledge. Displaced
from my familys traditional territory of Northern Saskatchewan, I grew up with the
Dakelh (Carrier) people in Prince George/
Fraser Lake, and was adopted into the Luk sil loo Clan. Although it was confusing to
grow up outside of my familys cultural
lands and traditions, the mentors I learned from impressed upon me the importance
of
cultural integrity. The intent behind my art
is to first create space for others like me and secondly counteract the colonial
narrative
with
a lived Indigenous perspective along
with amplifying native cultural, societal and
environmental issues.
Having spent over a decade dedicated to finding a mentor to reclaim Tapis regalia
back
for my community; I know it is my
responsibility to share knowledge that aligns with our cultural traditions. My
mentorship
with Northern Tutchone beader Velma
Olsen as part of FPCCs Individual Scholarship grant, was exactly for that purpose: to
rekindle traditions extinguished by colonial
powers and ensure it continues to grow with my kin to assist more Indigenous
knowledge keepers who have not had access to
these teachings. I continue to resist colonial erasure and make hard-earned
knowledge
with appropriate protocols accessible to the
communities who had these traditions taken from them.
We look forward to celebrating the cultural richness and artistic talent within our
Indigenous communities.
This online residency is made possible by a grant from First Peoples Cultural
Council.
