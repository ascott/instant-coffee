Building on recent interest in subject/object relations, this lecture
series brings together speakers who address the question from two main
vantage points: how the material world assembles the social, and how
objects becomes things that matter; and how, on the other side, art and
culture respond to the forces that control the bio-political, and
ultimately deal with that Thing which defies signification.

Laura Mulvey came to prominence in the 1970s as a filmmaker and theorist.
She wrote essays for such periodicals/journals as Screen, Spare Rib, and
Seven Days. Much of her early work investigated questions of spectatorial
identification and it’s relationship to the male gaze, and her writings,
particularly the 1975 essay Visual Pleasure and Narrative Cinema, helped
establish feminist film theory as a bourgeoning legitimate field of study.
Between 1794 and 1982, Mulvey co-wrote and co-directed six theoretical
films with Peter Wollen, films which integrated the discourse of feminist
theory semiotics, psychoanalysis and leftist politics. Their most
influential film, Riddles of the Sphinx (1977), presented avant-garde film
as a space in which female experience could be expressed. Mulvey
co-directed Disgraced Monuments with Mark Lewis in 1991-92, a film
examining the fate of revolutionary monuments in the Soviet Union after the
fall of communism. In 1996, she published Fetishism and Curiosity and in
2006, Death 24x a Second: Stillness and the Moving Image.
