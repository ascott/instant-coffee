Charles Stankievech

Audain Distinguished Artist-in-Residence Spring/Summer 2016

Lecture: CounterIntelligence: A Glossary of Doubled Agency
Wednesday March 30th at 8pm

ECU Lecture Theatre, South Building Room 301

Please join us in welcoming Charles Stankievech, recipient of the Audain
Distinguished Artist-in-Residence Program for Spring/Summer 2016.
Stankievech will be giving a public talk in the Emily Carr Theatre,
followed by a reception in the Charles H. Scott Gallery.

From Ancient Warfare to Cyber Warfare, artefacts have helped us to
understand culture in conflict. What does it mean to extract these
artefacts and place them in the new context of the museum or art
exhibition? Stankievech's CounterIntelligence attempts to wrestle with the
complex desires and problematics of artefacts embedded within the discourse
of conflict and thus saturated with the richest aggregate of ideological
forces, asking the question: what is the contemporary role of the
exhibition as caught in the no-man's land between the didactic museum and
the conceptual gesture?

Stankievech is an artist whose research has explored the notion of
"fieldwork" in the embedded landscape, the military industrial complex, and
geopolitics. His diverse body of work has been shown internationally at
institutions including the Louisiana Museum of Modern Art, Copenhagen;
Palais de Tokyo, Paris; Haus der Kulturen der Welt, Berlin;
Thyssen-Bornemisza Art Contemporary, Vienna; MASS MoCA; Muse d'art
contemporain de Montral; Canadian Centre for Architecture, Montreal; and
the Venice and SITE Santa Fe Biennales, among others. 

He's lectured at dOCUMENTA (13) and the 8th Berlin Biennale and his writing
has been published by Sternberg, eflux Journal, MIT Press and Princeton
Architectural Press. Stankievech has participated in such residencies as
The Banff Centre, Marfa Fieldwork, Atlantic Centre for the Arts,
Museumsquartier Vienna, and the Canadian Military. His curatorial projects
include Magnetic Norths and CounterIntelligenceboth critically acclaimed
as the top Canadian exhibitions of 2010 and 2014 respectively. In 2015 he
won the OAAG award for best solo exhibition Monument as Ruin. He is an
editor of Afterall Journal out of London, and since 2011, he has been
co-Director of the art and theory press K. in Berlin. In 2007 he was a
founding faculty member of the Yukon School of Visual Arts in Dawson City,
Canada,-a partnership with the indigenous sovereign nation of Tr'ondk
Hwch'in. Stankievech lives and works in Toronto where he is currently
Director of Visual Studies in the Faculty of Architecture, Landscape and
Design at the University of Toronto.

The Audain Distinguished Artist-in-Residence Program, established in 2013,
is generously funded by Michael Audain. The residency's mandate is to bring
renowned contemporary artists to Vancouver, to create curriculum specific
to each individual visiting artist, and to support the creation of new
works. The Program provides support for two artists per year to live and
work in Vancouver for a three month period.
