Multidimensional spoken word poet Janelle ecoaborijanelle Pewapsconias will
explore survivance narratives and nehyaw spoken word art and storytelling. The
spoken word and storytelling custom is a careful balance of speaking truth and practice
of active listening from the heart and thoughts in mind. Catch their online presentation
as ecoaborijanelle shares thoughtful decolonial approaches to spoken word, sound art,
and reconnecting to your story.

Janelle ecoaborijanelle Pewapsconias (she/her) is a nehyaw Spoken Word Poet,
Community Engaged Researcher, Social Innovator, and arts educator based in the
Treaty Lands now known as Little Pine First Nation, Treaty 6 Territory on
Saskatchewan. As a single mother she continues oral tradition through poetry and
spoken word
art. Her unique spoken word poetry style has been performed from east
to west coast, sharing stories of connection to this land. ecoaborijanelle speaks from
the experience of an rez-based Indigenous femme, but reminds everyone that her
perspective does not represent all Indigenous people to Turtle Island.

This talk is part of Janelle ecoaborijanelle Pewapsconias Remote Research
Residency with Arts Assembly and Access Gallery, 2021.
Support for RRR 2021 was provided by Canada Council for the Arts and The Audain
Prize.
