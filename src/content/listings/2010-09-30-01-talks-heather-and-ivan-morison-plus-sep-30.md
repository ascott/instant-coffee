Talks at the Vancouver Art Gallery this week

Thursday, September 30, 7pm

Offsite: Heather and Ivan Morison

Location: Vancouver Art Gallery Offsite is located on West Georgia
Street between Thurlow and Bute streets.

Join artist Ivan Morison for a talk and a walk through of their installation.

Heather and Ivan Morison explore architecture as it relates to ideas
of fantasy, escape, shelter and refuge. For Offsite, they have created
a pavilion which hovers between sculpture and architecture. The walls
lean outward towards the street, as if they are peeling away from the
site. The interior structure is raised above the water, propped up by
crossing diagonal supports, and the roof is open to the sky. The walls
are made of heavy timber beams, burnt to a dark charcoal using a
Japanese technique for preserving and protecting wood from the
elements called shou-sugi-ban. The blackened structure evokes a
distopic narrative as if it is built upon the ruins of a desolated
city charred by the scorching sun of an apocalyptic future.

Free.

03. BLIM ART SHOW | Eden Veaudry | OCT 1
BLIM ART SHOW (October 2010)
Blim presents:
The Chaos of Comfort
by Eden Veaudry
The Chaos of Comfort: when I create, I create order. There is something
beautiful about the physical need to make things; free form crochet is
random thought/dream made physical. A video is a painting made of light:
aisles of sky obliterated by pixels. I go for walks with my eyes closed and
revel in my vulnerability. What pours out of my hands later on are maps of
what I wanted to see, or what I thought I saw ...
Eden Veaudry is a self-taught multimedia artist whose practice incorporates
drawing, textiles, photography and video. She has exhibited in galleries
and artist-run spaces throughout Canada and the United States. She lives in
Victoria, BC.

04. The Dance Centre | Helen Walkley: How is sleep beautiful? | OCT 1
The Dance Centre presents Helen Walkley: How is sleep beautiful?

One of Vancouver's most distinctive and compelling dance artists brings
an evocative new work to the stage in the opening performance of The
Dance Centre's Global Dance Connections contemporary dance series. Helen
Walkley's How is sleep beautiful? is a richly-textured memoir which
explores wishes and dreams, love and aspiration, family and confusion,
and death as a transformation. In a career spanning three decades,
Walkley has become known for creating original and deeply felt works,
and for her extraordinary stage presence and eloquent, supple dancing.
Created in collaboration with composer James Maxwell, lighting designer
John Webber and sculptor John Noestheden, How is sleep beautiful? is her
first full-length work since 2006.

Fri-Sat Oct 1-2 at 8pm at Scotiabank Dance Centre. Info: 604.606.6400
