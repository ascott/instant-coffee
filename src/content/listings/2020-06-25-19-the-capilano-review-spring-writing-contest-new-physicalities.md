The Capilano Review's Spring Writing Contest will be open for submissions until
June 30, 2020. This year's contest is judged by acclaimed poet, educator and
advocate, Cecily Nicholson. The entry fee is a sliding scale ($5-$35 CAD) with 50%
of the contest proceeds going to
support the DTES Support Fund. Three winning
entries will receive a $300 cash prize and publication in our web folio, ti-TCR.

Contest Statement:

There is no chance that we will fall apart
There is no chance
There are no parts

- June Jordan, from Poem Number Two on Bells Theorem, or The New Physicality of
Long Distance Love

As civic selves we have been called upon to do our part, to distance from one
another to slow the spread of a virus. We retreat and stock up in preparation for
emergency measures even while, as a whole, we are reminded of the necessity of
connection, relations, and community. We share stories, salt dough recipes, and
homemade remedies. Anxiety, precarity, loss, and isolation are also common
amongst us. How do we thrive? Evoking generational knowledge, what lessons,
strategies of care, and innovations are your mainstays? What new qualities are
being brung to the notion of staying in touch? In some ways slower, stiller, and more
reliant on the dematerial-- what is
elegant; what holds; what is value IRL?

- Cecily Nicholson, contest judge

About our contest judge:

Cecily Nicholson coordinates public and school programs with the Surrey Art Gallery
and is part of the Joint Effort prison abolitionist group. She is the author of Triage
(Talonbooks, 2011), and From the Poplars (Talonbooks, 2014), which won the
Dorothy Livesay Poetry Prize. Her most recent work, Wayside Sang (Talonbooks,
2017), won the 2018 Governor Generals Literary Award for poetry.
