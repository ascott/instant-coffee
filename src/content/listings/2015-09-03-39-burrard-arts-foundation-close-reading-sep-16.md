Curated by Bischoff, Close Reading brings together two artists, two
poets and an artist/curator to read works from their repertoire that
explore alternative intimacies. Whether in the literal terms of textual
content, or more abstractly through the relationships between image
and text, the authors investigate the intricacies and disparities evoked
by closeness. Each performance inhabits the divergent space between
reading a book in private, and the exhibitionist quality of reading
before an audience; it is within the unique context of this divergence
that Close Reading encourages the audience to
interpret the offered texts.

Adèle Barclay’s writing has appeared in The Literary Review of
Canada, The Pinch, Poetry Is Dead, The Rusty Toque, The Puritan,
Matrix, Plenitude, and others. She writes and edits arts and culture
coverage for VANDOCUMENT and is currently writing her doctoral
dissertation on modern American poetry and film. Her first collection of
poetry was shortlisted for the Robert Kroetsch Award for
Innovative Poetry and is forthcoming from Nightwood Editions in 2016.

Alexandra Bischoff is a multimedia performance artist who works with
identity politics and sexuality. Humour is a tactic that she often employs
in order to discuss private subjects in public spaces.
Bischoff grew up in Sherwood Park, AB and began her academic
career at MacEwan University in Edmonton, AB. Completing her BFA
at Emily Carr University of Art + Design in 2015, she now lives and
works in Vancouver, BC. Bischoff is represented by the Robert Lynds
Gallery in Vancouver.

Shaun Robinson was born in 100 Mile House, BC and now lives in
Vancouver. His poems have appeared in Fugue, Versal, and The City
Series: Vancouver from Frog Hollow Press, and are forthcoming from
Echolocation and QWERTY. He is an MFA student in Creative writing at
UBC.

Alina Senchenko is an Ukrainian artist living and working in Vancouver,
Canada. She is in her last year of studies at Emily Carr University of
Art and Design, Vancouver Canada, majoring in Photography. Most
recently, Senchenko has exhibited at Access Gallery, Dynamo Art
Association, Square Project, and the Concourse Gallery. In 2014, she
was the finalist for the AIMIA/AGO photography prize.
In the past few years, Senchenko has begun to closely examine
cultural and social structures while interested in the urban spaces that
we create and occupy. The current political situation in Ukraine has
firmly dictated the emotional tone in her practice. Photography, found
images, text, collage, and performance are a few of the mediums and
elements that exist in her work.

Sung Pil Yoon is an artist and curator. He currently runs SPARE
ROOM, a site-specific project located in Chinatown, Vancouver that
investigates the relationship between object and space. He has studied
Art History at UBC.
