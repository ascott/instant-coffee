The Vancouver Art Gallery will unveil two new exhibitions drawn from
the Gallerys permanent collection, celebrating a pair of significant figures in
conceptual art: American artist Lawrence Weiner (1942-2021) and Canadian artist
Garry Neill Kennedy (1935-2021).

One of the leading figures in the development of conceptual art, Weiner was a
pioneer of language as art in the mid-1960s. Throughout his fifty-year career,
Weiners text-based works have appeared in museums, galleries and public
buildings around the globe. The Vancouver Art Gallerys relationship with Lawrence
Weiner was established when the Gallery commissioned its first public artwork for
the collection"PLACED UPON THE HORIZON (CASTING SHADOWS)"in 1990,
which remains on view today on the Gallery facade along the Robson Plaza and

Lawrence Weiner originally began making posters in the 1960s, as a way to reach a
broad audience outside of museums and galleries. The Lawrence Weiner Poster
Archive was established at the Nova Scotia College of Art and Design in 1978 and
then transferred to the Vancouver Art Gallery in 1991. The Archive has grown
substantially over time through annual gifts from the artist and is now the most
significant representation of Weiners work in this form held in a public collection.
Opening March 9, OF & ABOUT POSTERS: THE LAWRENCE WEINER POSTER
ARCHIVE (1965-2021) AT THE VANCOUVER ART GALLERY showcases the most
comprehensive collection of the artists posters to date and marks the Gallerys 30-
year relationship with Lawrence Weiner. Guest Curator Grant Arnold has
collaborated with Lawrence Weiner since the 1990s to develop
exhibitions of the
artists poster archive at venues worldwide; he began work on this landmark
exhibition in 2018 in close consultation with Weiner, prior to the artists passing in
2021.

A total of 250 posters made between 1965 and 2021 will be complemented by a
selection of Weiners editions, books, audio works and ephemera that attest to the
various ways the artists work has permeated visual culture and changed the way we
think about text and art.

Presented as a companion to OF & ABOUT POSTERS, HORIZONS is a conceptual
art project by Garry Neill Kennedy in which a selection of landscape paintings are
rehung so that the horizon lines are at the eye level of the artist, and"when viewed
together"these otherwise disparate landscapes form one continuous horizon line
around the gallery walls. Conceived in 1980, Kennedys proposal extended from the
artists parallel engagements with the medium of painting and institutional critique in
the late 1970s and early 1980s. The concept is
characteristic of Kennedy in its
playfulness, but the idea also quietly undermines curatorial control and intervenes
within the conventions of museum display. The exhibition features a selection of
historical and modern paintings from the Gallerys collection, and has been
developed collaboratively with Cathy Busby, a celebrated visual artist and Kennedys
spouse.

HORIZONS is being revisited today to offer visitors the opportunity to witness the
realization of Kennedys concept for the first time, and to help build an understanding
of the longstanding friendship and professional ties between Kennedy and Weiner,
which spanned more than forty years. The exhibition features a selection of
correspondence, drawings and notes from Weiner to Kennedy that offers a glimpse
into the nature of their relationship and conveys a sense of support, respect and
mutual admiration between the two artists.

At this extraordinary moment in the Gallerys history, it is timely to be highlighting
two luminaries
who boldly encourage visitors to consider new ways of thinking,
making and experiencing art, says Anthony Kiendl, CEO & Executive Director of the
Vancouver Art Gallery.
