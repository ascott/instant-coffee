BAF Gallery Spring 2022 Exhibitions

Sara Gulamali: [green heart emoji]
Jack Kenna: Tumbling Blocks
Karl Hipol: Tagpi-tagpi

258 East 1st Avenue
Apr 1 - June 11, 2022 (Tagpi-tagpi closes June 12)
Tuesday to Saturday 12-5PM
Reception: April 1, 6-9PM

On Friday, April 1, we will be unveiling new exhibitions from resident artists Sara
Gulamali and Jack Kenna, and a new Garage installation from Karl Hipol.

A public reception will be held on
Friday, April 1 from 6 to 9PM. Proof of vaccination is
required for the reception and masks are recommended.

Sara Gulamalis work explores a post-colonial navigation of the hyper-visibility or
invisibility of Muslim women using green screen technology. For the artist, green
screens and the colour green more broadly reference the notion of otherness and the
othered body within her practice"a nomadic space that can become anything or
transport you anywhere.

As we encounter the ubiquitousness of screens and technologies, Gulamalis interest
in digital spaces, and how screens can become proxies for the body, increases. The
green screen in "[green heart emoji]" becomes not only a tool but a material to perform
in and create with.

"Tumbling Blocks" by Jack Kenna takes its name from an age-old, tessellating pattern
of diamond shapes arranged to produce a three-dimensional effect"an illusion of light
falling on row upon row of stacked, angled cubes. Known in geometry as rhombille
tiling,
countless examples bearing the pattern range from ancient floor mosaics to
contemporary quilts.

Kenna applies the term to a new suite of sculptural works, produced during his winter
residency at BAF, that combine found plastic milk crates with abstract compositions in
stained glass.

"Tagpi-tagpi" by Karl Hipol is a constellated exploration of the artists Filipino heritage,
The title translates into English as patchy, from the root word Tagpi, meaning patch.
The installation combines handwritten Baybayin script, pasted manila paper and a
centralized painting of an abstracted bahay kubo, a type of pre-colonial Filipino
architecture.

Through the work, Hipol aims to comprehend a non-western perspective, both
practically and metaphorically. The installation patches lost and loose pages, aiming to
gradually rebuild broken Filipino identities. This exhibition closes Sunday, June 12,
2022, on Philippines Independence Day.
