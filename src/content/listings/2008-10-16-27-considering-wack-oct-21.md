Considering WACK!

A six class course at the Vancouver Art Gallery

Taught by Elizabeth MacKenzie

Tuesday evenings, 6:30 - 9:00 PM, October 21- December 2, 2008

(no class on November 11, 2008)

Closing Event: Tuesday, December 2, 6:30 - 9:00 PM

WACK!: Art and the Feminist Revolution represents a collection of work that
contributed to a radical transformation in how art is made and understood.
This course offers a unique collaborative learning opportunity for
participants to engage deeply with this extraordinary survey of
international feminist art. Participants will creatively explore the
exhibition within a number of feminist frameworks including activism, the
body, language, material practices and spirituality. Shared investigations
will respond to the interests and concerns of the participants. The course
will culminate in a collaborative community-based event.

Through her practice as an artist and teacher, Elizabeth MacKenzie
interrogates social relations from a feminist perspective. Her artwork has
been characterized by an interest in representations of maternal
ambivalence, notions of the parasite, interrogations of portraiture and the
representation of ambiguous identities. Her drawings, installations and
videos have been presented in numerous festivals and exhibitions across
Canada, United States and Europe.

2 West Hastings Street

For the 12th edition of Our Tube, we invite you to join us with Debra Zhou
for a report of her two-week research trip to Shanghai last month.
September is an exciting month for the art scene in Shanghai. The 7th
Shanghai Biennale, SHcontemporary 2008, and Shanghai Art Fair 2008 opened
in the same week, along with a dozen of other exhibitions at Bizart,
Shanghart, MOCA, Zendai MOMA, Duolun MOMA, Ke Centre for the contemporary
arts, etc, featuring local and foreign artists. Although not replacing
Beijing as the art centre of China anytime soon, Shanghai is slowly cooking
up its own artistic flavour. Small groups of young emerging artists are
focusing on more experimental and conceptual art practices. These practices
are outside the institutional boundaries and without much attention from
commercial art dealers. Ke Centre and Fei Contemporary, two non-profit
artist-run centres, opened in the last two years to join the oldest
non-profit art centre Bizart. These organizations create a dynamic platform
for contemporary art in Shanghai.

Debra Zhou is Centre Aâ&#65533;&#65533;s gallery coordinator. She has worked for Shanghai
Duolun Museum of Modern Art as a curatorial assistant and Yishu Journal as
a project coordinator. She is preparing an exhibition at Centre A featuring
artists based in Shanghai.

OurTube will be held at Centre Aâ&#65533;&#65533;s lounge space on the last Wednesday night
of every month after gallery hours. Each program will be curated by local
and visiting curators and artists, who will also invite the curator for
next month so that it continues like a chain event. The guest curator will
select series of videos for projection, and initiate the discussion
following the screening. Drinks will be served during the screening, so the
atmosphere is pretty casual just like at home.

Our Tube is a play on YouTube. The idea behind the project is not only to
broadcast your video, but also to share the experience of watching video
with others and talking about it.

30. MKG 127 | Toronto | INSTANT COFFEE: say nothing in bright colours | OCT 18
MKG127 is pleased to present SAY NOTHING IN BRIGHT COLOURS, an
exhibition of new work by INSTANT COFFEE.

Opening Saturday October 18 2-5 PM.

Since the artist collective's inception in 2000, Instant Coffee has
used language like colour, incorporating slogans like "it doesn't
have to be good to be meaningful and "get social or get lost" into
their architectural installations, which often involve a redesign or
reconfiguration of domestic social spaces (a sunken living room, a
bedroom or kitchen). It is through succinct catchphrases, which at
times drip with the sincerity of self-help jargon, ring with the
urgings of advertising slogans or resound with a dissonant irony,
that Instant Coffee gradually build a lexicon that lays the ground
for its particular way of making art -- one that emphasizes the
relationship between form and social interactions.

As part of Instant Coffee's self-proclaimed "Year of Bright Days,"
they will exhibit "Say Nothing in Bright Colours," a series of
language-based prints and objects that have been pared to the sign.

Instant Coffee is an internationally recognized artist collective
based in Toronto and Vancouver. Most recently Instant Coffee has
exhibited as part MDE07 encuentro internacional curated by Jose Roca,
Casa del Encuentro, Medellin, Colombia; Instant Coffee Nooks +
Everyone, Mercer Union Gallery, Toronto; If You Lived Here You'd Be
Home By Now, Henry Satellite, Bumbershoot Festival, Seattle; and
Instant Coffee Romance Posters, Art Metropole, Toronto. In 2008
Instant Coffee participated in What We Bring to the Table, curated by
Marnie Fleming, Oakville Galleries, On; Instant Coffee: Light Bar,
Flaggfabrikken, Bergen, Norway; and Kuenstlerhaueser Worpswede,
Germany. Upcoming projects include Main Street Translink Public Art
Commission, Vancouver; Light Bar for the group exhibition Assume
Nothing: New Social Practice, Art Gallery of Greater Victoria and
Disco Bomb Shelter for The Toronto Sculpture Garden. Texts on their
work have been published in Canadian Art, Toronto; The New York
Times; Journal for Northeast Issues, Hamburg; The Seattle Stranger
and C Magazine, Toronto. Instant Coffee's most consistent members
are Cecilia Berkovic, Jinhan Ko, Khan Lee, Kelly Lycan, Kate Monro
and Jenifer Papararo.

Instant Coffee: it doesn't have to be good to be meaningful.
