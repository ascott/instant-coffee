Permutations: Barb Choit and Sylvain Sailly 
curated by Lee Plested

September 10 – October 17, 2009
Opening Reception Thursday September 10 6-9pm
Artists in attendance. 

Blanket 
Contemporary Art Inc.
235 Alexander
Vancouver BC
V6A 1E3 

gallery 1-604-709-6100

Permutations is a set of solo exhibitions which are installed on top of each 
other in a single exhibition space to create a dialogue between two artist's 
projects which both consider how material in our built environment evolves 
over time. 

Barb Choit is a Vancouver trained photographer that lives in New York, NY. 
Sylvain Sailly is a French artist now based in Vancouver. 

These two artist's begin with opposing methods to realize their forms. Choit 
has taken the processing of photography as a procedure for eroding found 
material, here posters by graphic artist Peter Nagel exposed to the 
chemicals and ultra violet lights found in beauty salons. Sailly's carefully 
composed pictures of basic materials are precisely rendered in potentially 
infinite multiplicity. Computer generated images of torn posters, stacked 
chip-board and overlapping sheets of glass are here represented in clean 
graphic iconography. 

Through the contrasting strategies of incidental/ conceived, eroded/ built, 
textured/ reductive the pairing arrives from these polarized perspectives to 
emphasize their common interest in material permutations as they occur in 
contemporary commodity culture. 

The exhibition installation will further complicate these aesthetic 
contrasts through a traditional framed presentation of Choit's eroded 
works layed over an incidental installation of Sailly's digital pieces 
leaning against and taped to the walls. Each artist will present a full 
exhibition of their individual project. Through the forced 
co-habitation the oscillating material strategies and formal concerns of 
their individual projects may combine into a single blur, imaging 
irrepressible systems of production and erosion. 

(written by Lee Plested)
