Torrie Groening: A Maximalist for Dinner at

The Burnaby Art Gallery

September 9 - October 26, 2008

(BURNABY, BC) The Burnaby Art Gallery is pleased to present 
A Maximalist
for Dinner a large format staged photography exhibition by 
artist Torrie
Groening. A decade of exploration into the rapidly expanding 
digital arena
of printmaking has resulted in this exciting series of nine works 
that
examine sight, touch, smell, hearing, and taste. Using 
traditional
printmaking concepts within the context of new digital media 
has resulted
in these wonderfully unique, engaging, rich trompe l'oeil visual 
images.
Multifaceted and complex, the viewer is challenged to consider 
concepts of
inter-relationships between people, objects, and their 
environment.

Torrie Groening is a contemporary visual artist based in San 
Francisco,
California. Her work has been exhibited extensively throughout 
Canada's
museums including the Surrey Art Gallery, Open Studio and 
Memorial
University Art Gallery. Groening is represented in the 
collections of Art
Gallery of Hamilton, Burnaby Art Gallery, Carlton University Art 
Gallery,
Vancouver Art Gallery and the Canada Council Art Bank. 
Internationally her
work has been placed in the collections of the University of 
California Art
Gallery at San Diego, Brauer Museum of Art in Valparaiso 
Indiana,
Georgetown University Gallery in Washington D.C. and the 
University of
Maine Museum of Art in Orono, Maine.

’An interest in the use of symbolic personal objects to 
create visual
narratives has been a constant thread throughout the work by 
Torrie
Groening. The new digitally collaged works presented in A 
Maximalist for
Dinner represent a new focus for the artist that incorporates 
traditional
and non-traditional approaches to creating prints. The concepts 
of identity
and representation are present in each work signifying 
Groening’s interest
in exploring her own identity and the multiplicity of meaning 
that objects
may symbolize in relation to one another and within the 
context of each
digital collage.’ says the exhibition’s curator 
Darrin Martens.
