CALL: Call for Submissions 2024 |
Gallery Gachet

Gallery Gachet currently has two calls, a call for gallery submissions and a
publication call, both due September 22nd.

We accept proposals for solo, group, curated, or major exhibition projects, as well as
screenings, workshops, performances and publications. Themes addressed through
the artwork could include (but are not limited to): modes of healing, ritual and culture,
decolonization; Feminism, activism art, housing, displacement, and land restitution;
(dis)ability justice, Indigeneity, Blackness; substances, medicines, harm reduction,
and safe supply; poverty, criminalization, and marginalization; climate crisis and
mental health.
Gallery Gachet pays artist fees that meet 2023 CARFAC rates. We
provide support for openings, catering, marketing, A/V equipment, and installation for
a five- week exhibition run at our location at 9W Hastings St.

Gallery Gachet is also accepting applications for a publication series from both
individuals and collectives for our upcoming 2024/2025 programming year. Selected
artists will be given the unique opportunity to create a limited edition publication, with
the potential for collaboration and mentorship with traditional and experimental artist
bookmakers/publishers. The gallery unequivocally supports a wide range of
publications, including but not limited to theoretical texts, artist books, essays,
journals, political manifestos, and postscripts related to artwork. Do note that other
types of publications may also be considered.

Please visit https://gachet.org/call-for-submissions-2024 for submission details.
Submissions are due September 22, 2023.

Gallery Gachet is located
in the Downtown Eastside of Vancouver on the
unceded and occupied territories of the xmkwy"m (Musqueam),
Skwxw7mesh (Squamish) and Sl"lwta (Tsleil-Waututh) Nations.
Gallery Gachet has a mandate to support artists and offer art programs
addressing mental health and socio-political marginalization, while
promoting art as a means for survival, cultural participation, and human rights.
