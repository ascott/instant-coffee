Join us for a talk with Vancouver based artist Judy Radul. 

Judy Radul's latest works, involve an original computer controlled system for live 
and pre-recorded video. Her practice also includes sculpture, photography, 
writing and performance. Her large-scale media installation World Rehearsal 
Court (2009) has been shown in Vancouver, Vienna, Seoul, and Oslo. Related to 
this project, with Marit Paasche she co-edited a book of collected essays and 
images A Thousand Eyes: Media Technology, Law and Aesthetics (Sternberg 
Press, Berlin, 2011). Recent exhibitions include Bienal de Nicaragua, 2016; 
Agnes Etherington Art Centre, 2015; Berlin Biennale 8, 2014; DAAD Galerie, 
2013. She has a B.A. in Fine and Performing Arts from Simon Fraser University 
and a MFA in Visual and Media Arts from Bard College, New York. She is 
represented by Catriona Jeffries Gallery, Vancouver and teaches at Simon Fraser 
University, Vancouver.

17. Capture Photography Festival 2017 | | NOV 15
SUBMISSIONS DUE November 15, 2016 at 11:59pm

To submit please visit http://capturephotofest.com/submissions/

The 4th annual Capture Photography Festival will take place April 1-28, 2017 
around Metro Vancouver and parts of the Lower Mainland. 

Capture Photography Festival is now accepting submissions for exhibitions and 
events for the 2017 iteration of the Festival. Proposals must be submitted by 
November 15, 2016 at 11:59pm.

This call is open to all exhibitions of photography and lens-based works 
addressing any subject matter. Additionally, we welcome proposals for innovative 
events.

EXHIBITION PROPOSALS:

Applicants can choose either the juried "Selected Program" or the non-juried 
"Open Program".

The "Selected Program" is open to exhibitions presented in professional spaces. 
Capture defines a professional space as any space that operates full-time as a 
gallery or exhibition space. This includes public galleries and museums, 
commercial galleries, artist-run centres, artist collectives, etcetera. Proposals 
submitted to the "Selected Program" will be evaluated by a jury of artists and arts 
professionals.

The "Open Program" is open to exhibitions presented in any space, from 
conventional gallery spaces, to community venues, to the side of a bus. 
Exhibitions can be any length, including one-day pop-ups. Submissions to this 
category will not be juried, and will automatically be included in Capture 2017 if 
they meet the Festival's mandate.

Exhibition Participation Fees:

The submission fee is $300 (plus GST) for the "Selected Program". All proposals 
are subject to a $100 fee upfront, and upon acceptance into the Selected 
Program, applicants will be invoiced for the remaining $200 fee (not-for-profits are 
exempt from the latter).

The submission fee for the "Open Exhibition" category is $100 (plus GST).

EVENT PROPOSALS:

Event submissions must be related to photography and can include exhibition 
openings, talks, panels, tours, workshops, family events, films, and more. Events 
will not be juried, and as long as they fall within the scope of the Festival, they 
will automatically be included in Capture 2017.

Submission Fees for Events:

There are NO submission fees for events.

HOW TO APPLY: 

For more information, please visit: http://capturephotofest.com/submissions/faq/

To apply, please visit: http://capturephotofest.com/submissions/ 

If you have further questions, please contact Katherine Neil at 
programming@capturephotofest.com

19. Vancouver Art Gallery | Scott Bertram | NOV 15
Tuesday, November 15th, 7pm
Art Rental & Sales at the Vancouver Art Gallery 
1st Floor Annex, 750 Hornby Street, Vancouver

Join Art Rental & Sales at the Vancouver Art Gallery for an artist talk with 
Featured Artist Scott Bertram on Tuesday, November 15, 2016 at 7pm.

Scott Bertram's practice combines improvisational methods with systems of self-
imposed parameters, with an aim to sustain formal ambiguity, subvert 
interpretation, and emphasize material process. Focusing on the point between 
the viewer's sensorial encounter of an image and the naming of it, Bertram is 
particularly interested in detecting areas of unintended agency in these 
improvised interactions.

Bertram's work will be featured in the showroom through December 9, 2016
---
Scott Bertram's studio practice is an exploration into the possibilities of form, 
perception, and uncertainty. He has a BFA from the University of British 
Columbia Okanagan and an MFA from NSCAD University. He was a semi-finalist 
in the 12th RBC Canadian Painting Competition and has received many other 
awards such as Honourable Mention for the HRM Contemporary Visual Art 
Purchase Program, a SSHRC Canada Graduate Scholarship, and two BC Arts 
Council Senior Scholarship Awards. His work has been shown in solo exhibitions 
at Two Rivers Art Gallery in Prince George, BC; Forest City Gallery in London, 
ON; and Galerie BAC in Montreal, QC; as well as in group exhibitions at The 
Power Plant in Toronto, ON; Dalhousie Art Gallery in Halifax, NS; and the Art 
Gallery of Calgary, among other exhibitions in Australia, Germany, Mexico, and 
the UK. Scott Bertram currently lives and works on Vancouver Island, BC, 
Canada.

20. West Vancouver Museum | A Decade of Collecting Art | NOV 15
A Decade of Collecting Art at the West Vancouver Museum
November 16, 2016 - January 14, 2017
Opening Reception Tuesday, November 15, from 7 to 9 p.m.
Curator's Talk: Saturday, December, 3, from 1 p.m.

The West Vancouver Museum's permanent art collection was established in 2006 
with a donation of work by local artist Jane Billaux, who immigrated to Canada 
from London in 1934. The donation coincided with the Museum's successful 
exhibitions on the important history of modernism in the community and the 
confluence of creative individuals, who chose West Vancouver as a place to live 
and cultivate their careers. In the intervening decade the collection has grown in 
both size and scope and now includes over 300 historical and contemporary 
works by important Canadian artists, including Emily Carr, Gordon Smith, Douglas 
Coupland, Ian Wallace, Kim Kennedy Austin, and Babak Golkar, just to name a 
few. Gordon Smith's major donation of his personal collection in 2015 added 
substantially to the Museum's holdings of Canadian art. This exhibition provides 
an overview of the breadth of the Museum's art collection and highlights recent 
donations. 

West Vancouver Museum | 680 17th Street, West Vancouver, BC V7V 3T2 | 
Open Tuesday to Saturday 11 am to 5 pm

Scott Rogers, ALPTRUTH, 2015
40 min
ALPTRUTH examines the intricacies and intimacies of backcountry skiing. 
Dealing with the relationship between risk management, heuristics, marketing 
and outdoor adventure, the video documents an ill-fated solo ski trip which 
Rogers undertook in January 2013. The footage from this journey forms the 
basis of an alternative ski movie, focusing on the viscerally slow pace, layers of 
personal history, and existential decision-making connected to the sport rather 
than its 'extreme' popularization.

24. FIELD Contemporary | Is Crimson The Colour Blue? Simone Jarvis | NOV 17
Is Crimson The Colour Blue?

November 17th - December 17th, 2016
Opening Thursday, November 17th, 7:00 10:00PM

Is Crimson The Colour Blue?, a solo exhibition by Simone Jarvis, presents new 
paintings and sculptures that regard the presence of form and colour. The 
exhibition gets it's disposition and titles from significant moments in the artist's 
life. Representative emotive subjectivities similar to that of a conversation with a 
friend, a witnessed happening, or a sighting on a walk or bike ride attest in 
making her work autobiographical. Forms and gestures found in the paintings 
reference spaces from flora to urban structures and domestic objects. Rich colour 
and monochromatic hues are exalted, guiding the viewer through the numerous 
spaces within the paintings.

Simone Jarvis is an artist currently working out of Vancouver BC. Originally from 
Calgary, Alberta, she received her Bachelor of Arts from Emily Carr University. Her 
most recent exhibitions include Towards a Fictional History of Colour at Burrard 
Arts Foundation and How Long Can You Contain an Echo? at Field 
Contemporary in 2015, Vancouver BC. Jarvis's painting practice explores formal 
qualities and processes of the material. Her paintings, drawings, and sculptures 
continue to be organized primarily by colour and form.

26. Two Rivers Gallery | AND... | NOV 20
2017 represents the 150th anniversary of the confederation of Canada, a 
solidifying moment in the history of this country. Along the timeline that connects 
us to that moment on 1 July, 1867, this anniversary is the fulcrum between past 
and future. It is a time for celebration and a time for sober reflection. It is the 
conjunction of experiences and a tale to which there is always another side. 
And... is the acknowledgment of a familiar story, but more importantly a device 
with which to add to it and consider what comes next. "And..." invites the 
celebration of this milestone And... a robust examination of a past more 
prosperous for some than for indigenous peoples and immigrant populations. It 
aspires to look at the accomplishments and the failures of the last 150 years and 
take stock of the present so that we might honour the foundations on which 
confederation was built by forging a stronger tomorrow. In this context Two Rivers 
Gallery is seeking artists from across BC working in any media to respond to this 
call with three to six artworks produced within the last three years in order to add 
perspectives to this anniversary and reflect upon the future.

New Media Gallery, 3rd Floor Anvil Centre

New Westminster SkyTrain (or) Anvil Centre Pay Parking

All Welcome : Complimentary Morsels and Cash Bar 

Six artists explore the spectrum of human perception, imagination, identification 
and expectation through the vehicle of children. Humorous, critical and authentic 
the works challenge assumptions by disrupting easy or sentimental narratives. 
We are presented with intelligent, imaginative beings who long for ideas, who 
question the meaning of life, human interaction and beginnings, who imagine 
and become 'other' and who stretch the limits of familial bonds. Six digital works 
range from large, projected installations to more intimate multi-screen works. 
Together they offer a rich trajectory of the human condition.
