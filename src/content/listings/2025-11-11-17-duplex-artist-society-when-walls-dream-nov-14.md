When Walls Dream
Opening: Nov 14, 6:30 - 9:30 PM
Exhibition: Nov 14 - 23
Duplex Artist Society *BACK ALLEY ENTRANCE
1889 Franklin Street, Vancouver, BC

When Walls Dream presents works by Alison Fast, Natalie Schmidt, Diana Hanitzsch
and Stephanie Wu, whose practices vary in approach to investigate the mutable
boundaries of perceptivity, semiotics, and materiality.

This collection of works oscillates between the real and cerebral as they conjure
insightful narratives through primarily painting, sculpture and animation. These works
invite viewers to heed a calling to the brown study between passive and active
engagement, meanwhile the varying mediums at use allow the viewer to remain
cognizant of the differing experiences of the participatory act of seeing each
individual work.


Alison Fast (she/her) is a visual artist based in Vancouver, BC. Her paintings draw
on the fluidity of thought and memory, where playful color fields and layered
transparencies reveal both the immediacy of gesture and the slow unfolding of
awareness. She holds a BFA in Visual Arts from Emily Carr University of Art +
Design (2017) and an MA in Art Education from the University of British Columbia.
She is a recipient of the Jack and Doris Shadbolt Scholarship, Dornacilla Drysdale
Peck Memorial Scholarship and Herb Kohl Scholarship. Fasts most recent
exhibitions include Say Hi (2023. TAS Gallery, Vancouver), TAS & MOV Pop-Up
(2021. Museum of Vancouver, Vancouver) and Still Hills (2020. Slice of Life Gallery,
Vancouver).

Diana Hanitzsch (she/her) is a German-born multidisciplinary artist concerned with
tracing psychological processes and relationships focusing on power dynamics and
perception. In addition to self-exploration in different environments, she witnesses
shapes and forms in everyday spaces and the gallery. She holds a BFA (2019) in
painting and sculpture from Emily Carr University of Art + Design and has exhibited
in Canada, Germany and the Czech Republic. Hanitzsch recently had her solo
exhibition Faces (2022. BINGLE Studio, Vancouver).

Natalie Schmidt (she/her) is an interdisciplinary artist is an interdisciplinary artist
exploring the intersections between painting and textiles. Her work makes reference
to techniques of embroidery and garment-making while also existing as dynamic
sculptural paintings that engage with architecture and space. She is based in
Vancouver, B.C., on the unceded territories of the Musqueam, Squamish and Tsleil-
Waututh First Nations. She holds a BFA (2017) in painting from Emily Carr University
of Art + Design. Schmidt most recently exhibited work in the group exhibition, The
Curators Sauna (2023. Canton Sardine, Vancouver).

Stephanie Wus interests lie in the relationship between the notion of reality, the
language of contemporary art, and translingual semiotics. Her multimedia works
function as lexicons that are simultaneously informed by research and our
experiences of space in situ. Wu also integrates sensibilities from her writing and
curatorial practice into space-making. She completed her BFA at Emily Carr
University of Art + Design in 2019, most recent exhibitions include: Under
Construction (2025. Ranger Station Art Gallery, Harrison), Holland Bank Cheque
(2023. Sunset Terrace, Vancouver), and Uncertain Borders (2021. Chateau Koganei,
Tokyo). She is currently based in Vancouver, BC.
