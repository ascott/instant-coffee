Peripheral Review is a non-profit platform for documenting and expanding the
emerging and underrepresented Canadian art scene, as well as enabling access for
emerging
writers through encouraging expanded, productive, and accessible critical
dialogue. Our mandate states that we prioritize the voices of artists and writers who are
considered emerging, underrepresented, Indigenous, LGBTQ2S+, from diverse cultural
backgrounds, and those working in alternative practices or abilities.

For this call, we would especially like to highlight writing and creative practices from
Indigenous communities and groups both inside and beyond so-called Canada and the
United States.

Additionally, the Board and Editor would like to acknowledge the recent actions of
extreme censorship and unjust hostility by Canadian art institutions, funders, and
patrons to those demanding a ceasefire and defending the human rights of Palestinian
people.

As an independent platform, we unequivocally stand for freedom of expression,
especially for Indigenous and those historically and intentionally excluded. We
therefore condemn the actions of various institutions, such as the Art
Gallery of Ontario
and the Royal Ontario Museum, and publications such as ArtForum and Art Canada
Institute, among others, that have punished or censored their staff and contributors for
speaking out against the ongoing genocide of Palestinian people. We believe art plays
a pivotal role in advancing social discourse, promoting understanding and compassion
for cultural differences, and building spaces for community healing. Artists and writers
who champion Indigenous perspectives while challenging systematic and racial
oppression, settler colonialism, and social norms in Canada and globally, should not be
censored. Our platform is open to and supports diverse perspectives that seek to
encourage such generative dialogue.

For our Fall 2023 open call, we would like to prioritize submissions that advocate for
practices, actions, events, exhibitions, and artists who work towards building solidarity
across cultural differences and decolonial ways of thinking, issues relating to land
ownership, censorship of criticism and speech (both online and in the public realm), as
well as affirmative actions that art and artists provide to a world in crisis.

The Fall 2023 submissions deadline will therefore be extended to Friday, December
15, 2023 to accommodate for further engagement with our call. If you have further
questions please reach out to contact@peripheralreview.com in advance of the new
deadline. Additionally, we understand the considerable unpaid labour involved in
developing a submission, if we can make this process more accessible through a
recorded presentation or a scheduled appointment over Zoom let us know, we aim to
find methods of accommodation.
