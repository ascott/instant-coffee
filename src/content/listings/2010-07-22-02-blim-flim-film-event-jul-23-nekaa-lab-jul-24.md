Feature: Dr. Caligari [1989], Directed by Stephen Sayadian (a.k.a. Rinse Dream)

Stephen Sayadian's 1989 surreal depiction of sex-crazed lunacy was based on
the German Expressionist film The Cabinet of Dr. Caligari (Robert Wiene,
1920). Equally bizarre, but nothing like the original, Sayadian takes us
through a non-stop parade of dysfunction in his highly stylized version of
storytelling through performance art.

Originally, and briefly, billed as Dr. Caligari 3000 when it debuted at
selected theaters in 1989, the film promptly faded into obscurity. The film
is considered a cult classic and was filmed entirely inside of large
studios in a bizarre landscape combination of both artificial outdoor and
indoor scenes merged together. The overdramatic, stylistic acting is
amusing and pokes fun at cliches and stereotypes and thus it has the film
play out as an artistc performance piece rather than a standard

Nekaa Lab

Saturday July 24 | 8 - 11 PM | $10 (no pre-sales)

Featuring: Sachiyo Takahashi

Nekaa Lab / Sachiyo Takahashi will present a new production, The Nekaa
Room: Galaxy Green, on July 24, 2010, at Blim. It is the fourth piece in
the The Nekaa Room / Microscopic Live Cinema-Theatre series where miniature
worlds are magnified by projection onto the big screen. In a world
populated by figurines and plush toys, a loose, fairytale narrative unfolds
among abstract sound and visuals, creating a dream-like experience.

The Sheep, a major character in previous productions, returns in The Nekaa
Room: Galaxy Green searching for a place to call Home.

Come follow the Sheep's journey in The Nekaa Room live!

Sachiyo Takahashi is a Japanese contemporary artist who blurs the
boundaries of theatrical performance, film and art exhibition. With
background in contemporary theatre and electroacoustic music, she fine
tunes images and sound to create odd yet intimate abstract narratives.
Physical play is an important aspect in her work: her pieces often
include the performer's presence while also incorporating audience
participation.

Since 2006 Sachiyo has been exploring storytelling in Microscopic Live
Cinema-Theatre where she uses a miniature stage and props that are
magnified through live video projection.

Sachiyo has presented her work in Europe, Japan, the US and Canada. She
currently lives in Vancouver.
