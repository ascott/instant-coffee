1. July 1 at the Museum
Tuesday, July 1, 10 A.M. - 5 P.M.

July 1 is a day of deep significance in Chinese Canadian history.

Historically, it is the day the Chinese Exclusion Act of 1923 went into effect "
essentially barring all Chinese from entering Canada for a period of 24 years.
Dubbed "Humiliation Day, July 1 was a reminder of second class status for the
Chinese Canadian community until the repeal of the Exclusion Act and the granting
of equal rights.

Today, July 1 takes on a different meaning for Chinese Canadians. With the opening
of the Chinese Canadian Museum " the first museum in the country dedicated to
Chinese Canadian history and heritage " on this day in 2023, it serves as a marker
for the progress made in the inclusion of Chinese Canadian voices within the
Canadian national narrative.

Join us in reflecting on the legacy of July 1 and celebrating Chinese Canadian
legacies, both past and present. From Dr. Yao Sweden Xiaos lecture
commemorating marginal histories from the archives of The Chinese Times
newspaper during the Exclusion Act era, journalist Christopher Cheungs talk on the
persisting model minority myth in the contemporary Canadian media landscape, to
the unabashed celebration of Cantopop as a rallying point for the diaspora " theres
something for everyone to connect to in celebrating Chinese Canadian legacies this
July 1.
