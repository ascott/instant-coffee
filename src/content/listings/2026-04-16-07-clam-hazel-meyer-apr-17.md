CLAM presents Hazel Meyer: The Weight of Inheritance"cruising Joyce's house,

a screening of video works by Hazel Meyer, including a collaboration with Alysha Seriani,
a film by Joyce Wieland and a reading by Yasmine Whaley-Kalaora at DUPLEX Gallery on
April 17, 2026.

The evening will bring together elements of Meyers multi-faceted projects The Weight of
Inheritance (2019/2024) and Marble in the Basement (2020/2024), framed by a
conversation-turned-email-exchange between Meyer and Whaley-Kalaora. Their
correspondence became the foundation of a text by Whaley-Kalaora that was published
by Le Sigh last spring, centered on the artists shared interests, including inheritance,
archives, the w/holes in archives and mattresses (stained & abandoned, and beloved),
chronic illness, and the Canadian experimental filmmaker and artist, Joyce Wieland.

The screening will feature Meyers video works, The Weight of Inheritance"cruising
Joyces house (2020) and Marble Presents: Its My House (2021), made in collaboration
with Alysha Seriani, alongside Joyce Wielands Water Sark (1965) (thanks to the CFMDC).

There will be raisin-ably light refreshments and some of Meyers research materials will
also be on view. The event is free to attend.

About the artists:

Hazel Meyer works with installation, performance, and text to investigate the relationships
between sexuality, feminism, and material culture. This work recovers the queer
aesthetics, politics, and bodies often effaced within histories of infrastructure, athletics,
and chronic illness. Drawing on archival research, she designs immersive installations that
bring various troublemakers"lesbians-feminists, incontinent-queers, gender-outlaws"
into a performative space that centres desire, world-making, and sweat.

Recent presentations of the work have taken place at Copenhagen Contemporary (DK),
Libby Leshgold Art Gallery (CA), Tale of a Tub (NL), Lowe Art Gallery (US), and at the
BFIs London International Film Festival (UK). In 2023 Hazel was the recipient of the Jack
and Doris Shadbolt Foundation VIVA Award, and in 2025 she was on the Sobey Art Award
longlist.

Hazel lives in Vancouver, on the unceded traditional territories of the xmkwy"m,
Skwxw7mesh, and Sl"lwta Nations with her frequent collaborator and partner Cait
McKinney.

Yasmine Whaley-Kalaora is an interdisciplinary artist/host of CLAM who works as a writer
and critic, a studio assistant for Tom Burrows, and in archives for artists and artist-run
organizations. Yasmine holds a BA in Art History from the University of British Columbia
and currently lives and works between the unceded territories of the xmky"m,
Skwxw7mesh and Sl"lwta" and Kmoks First Nation.

The 2026 season of CLAM is gratefully supported by the BC Arts Council and the Province
of British Columbia.

Fillip welcomes radical feminist activist and theorist Leopoldina Fortunati for a discussion of
social reproduction and histories of feminist revolt moderated by Jaleh Mansoor and Arlen
Austin. The conversation will draw on recent publications, including Gendered Labour and
Clitoridean Revolt, a collection recently released by Fillip that pairs Fortunatis work with texts
of Carla Lonzi, as well as secondary literature considering the two thinkers as important poles
of postwar feminist thought.

The conversation will focus on the traditions of autocoscienza, and experimental aesthetic
practice associated with Lonzis work in relation to the analysis of reproductive labour,
developed in Italian workerist feminism, specifically Leopoldina Fortunatis classic work, The
Arcana of Reproduction: Housewives, Prostitutes, Workers and Capital, recently released in
unabridged translation by Verso. For more information, visit: https://fillip.ca/o2cu

Afternoon Projects is pleased to announce Von Coffins solo exhibition Layout Fluid.

Von Coffin (b 1983) lives in Redmond, Washington with their wife and two young children.
Coffin received their MFA in Painting and Printmaking from Yale University (2016) and BFA
from the School of the Art Institute of Chicago (2008). They run Coffin Farm, an experimental
sculpture park on their familys farm. Coffins experience raising animals, working at grocery
stores, and building museum display cases evolved into the objects, paintings, and food
works that constitute their practice. These modes act to render experiences of psychosis and
gender dysmorphia into, or onto, something else. Coffin engages various levels of abstraction
to examine such spectrums as sane-insane, F-M. Color is used as both symbol and signifier,
triggering nostalgia through specific imitations of candy and other material.
