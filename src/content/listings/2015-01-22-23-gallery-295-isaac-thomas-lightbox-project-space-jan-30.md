Sara’s Bat Mitzvah is large format photographic work that brings to light
the spectacle occasionally immersed within the suburban landscape. Working
through the traditions of straight photography, the conceptual elements of
the composition are explored in the meticulous depiction of a scene – that
of a stilt walker waving to a vehicle, directing passers by towards the
#partyoftheyear – Sara’s Bat Mitzvah. Whether it is a coming of age
ceremony or a fully-fledged over the top celebration, there is a cultural
tradition of thinking up the most impressive and creative ways to eclipse
the last (party). The arrow pointing down the road, off camera into the
distance, mimics Sara’s future. She has taken her mitzvah vows and declared
a woman in the eyes of her family, peers, and community. In this work Isaac
Thomas extracts the nuances from his own personal encounters of the
everyday and depicts them in an aesthetic value in which religious and
cultural traditions are practiced today in the 21st century. Thomas’
interest in debuting this work in the Lightbox Project Space is to frame
this tableau in homage to the rich tradition of straight conceptual
photography that has come into prominence through the Vancouver school.

ISAAC THOMAS was born in 1989 in San Francisco California. He was awarded
the 2014 BMO 1st Art Invitational Student Art Award in Photography, and his
work has been exhibited at the Museum of Contemporary Canadian Art in
Toronto. He is in his final term at Emily Carr University of Art and
Design, where he majors in Photography. His divergent photographic practice
is influenced by a social practice that allows Isaac to isolate subjects he
identifies with through the structure of a constituted conceptual
photographic image or series.
