"Taking Care of Business" is a performance/installation that 
lasts the run of the exhibition. The performance involves the 
artist creating a multi-wall, floor-to-ceiling mural of an office 
space out of post-it-notes. The artist, Immony Men, will spend 
each day of the exhibition working 9-5 printing out a 360Â° view 
of an office one post it note at a time until the main walls of 
grunt gallery are filled. The artist writes:

"I am mesmerized by the drudgery of daily office routine. The 
pattern I have established in the creation of these kinds of 
murals attempts to replicated this drudgery. The performance 
aspect of my work is the actual construction of the mural. I want 
the raw elements behind the creation of the project to be in the 
forefront: the physical space taken up, the artist's repetitive 
movements, the materials used in full evidence. By subjecting 
myself to a mind-numbing process, I will also create and 
exhibit the mental state fallen into in performing iterative tasks" 
(Immony Men)

Immony Men is a Canadian visual artist currently based in 
Montreal and Toronto. He is a recent graduate of the MFA

13. The Remington Gallery | Atlas Imaginariis | JUL 8
Atlas Imaginariis: An Exhibition on Mapping the Possible

8 - 15 July 2011
The Remington Gallery
108 West Hastings Street Vancouver
open 12:00pm - 5:00pm daily

Exhibition Opening Friday 8 July, 7:00pm - 11:00pm

The impetus for this exhibition was an undergraduate seminar 
held at Emily Carr University in the spring of 2011. The aim of 
the class was to explore histories of cartography and theories of 
mapping in order to consider the diverse ways contemporary 
artists draw from these visual and spatial languages to imagine, 
inform, or incite. During the semester, it became strikingly clear 
that many of the issues discussed in class resonated with the 
studentsâ€™ established artistic practices. To culminate the 
seminar, and in response to the ideas debated and shared, 
each participant undertook his or her own mapping project, 
which are exhibited together here. We create maps when we 
want to visualize our understanding of a particular space, to 
demonstrate the relationships that exist between objects, 
places and ideas. These eleven artists approach mapping in 
markedly different ways. Some appropriate the language of 
cartography to chart the unexpected: mundane sites and 
experiences that exist below or beyond the threshold of 
visibility in everyday life, or places deliberately rendered 
inaccessible by structures of power. Others engage the body 
directly, tracing habitual movements or activities. Still others 
take intensely personal tacks, to map a fragile sense of self, the 
limits of language, memory or loss. Together these works form 
an atlas of sorts, a collection of maps illustrating not a single 
geography but many possible ones.

Participating artists: Tiffen Breen, Jules Francisco, Jeremy 
Jaud, Adam Johannesson, Anna-Karin Johansson, Kaoverii 
Marapon, Kathleen McGiveron, Adrienne Rempel, Adam 
Shaw, Alex Slaughter, Andrew Smith

Co-curated by Kimberly Phillips and the students of Emily Carr 
University's "Art Now: Topics in Contemporary Art" Seminar, 
Spring 2011.

16. Access Gallery | Elizabeth Milton: Auditions | until | JUL 23
Elizabeth Milton: Auditions

Exhibition runs June 25th â€“ July 23rd, 2011

This summer Access Gallery supports Vancouver-based artist 
Elizabeth Milton in the production and presentation of a new 
project entitled Auditions. Utilizing the performative structure of 
acting auditions, job interviews, psychotherapy, celebrity 
confessionals, talent contests and pornographic audition 
scenarios, this new work will critically investigate the voyeuristic 
consumption of trial and critique. Auditions is a project in two 
phases. First as a residency, Milton will transform the Western 
Front Lux space into an audition studio throughout May and 
June, inviting professional and amateur actors for auditions. 
The project will base itself around a series of one to one 
interactions that take place between the artist and a varied 
group of off camera participants. Playing with staging, props, 
camera positioning, and scripting Milton investigates questions 
of authentic subjectivity, identity construction and the 
boundaries between amateur and professional practice. 
Beginning June 25th Access Gallery will present an installation 
of Auditions.
