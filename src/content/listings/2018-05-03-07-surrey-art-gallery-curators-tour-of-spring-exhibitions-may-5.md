Curators Tour of Spring Exhibitions
Surrey Art Gallery
May 5, 2:30'4pm

Surrey Art Gallery curators Jordan Strom and Rhys Edwards host an
exhibition tour of Flow: From the Movement of People to the Circulation of
Information and Elizabeth Hollick: Body Politic at the Gallery on Saturday,
May 5 from 2:30 to 4pm. The event is free.

Recently launched as part of the Gallerys spring season, these two
exhibits showcase a world thats always changing on an individual and
societal level. This casual exhibition tour provides the opportunity for
visitors to reflect on how contemporary art engages with the world and the
different methods the artists choose to do so. Both regular tour
participants and first-time Gallery visitors are encouraged to ask
questions and share their responses to the artworks.

As its title suggests, Flow: From the Movement of People to the Circulation
of Information addresses movement in various forms such as spills,
torrents, migrations, bodily gestures, and the exchange of information
through twenty-seven artworks all drawn from the Gallerys robust permanent
collection. Artist in the Exhibition are Sean Alward, Mary Frances Batut,
Edward Burtynsky, Soheila Esfahani, Monique Fouquet, Sara Graham, Antonia
Hirsch, Brian Howell, Ian Johnston, Myron Jones, Laura Wee Ly Lq, Simon
Levin, Vicky Marshall, Val Nelson, Philippe Raphanel, Helma Sawatzky, Hari
Sharma, Haris Sheikh, Meera Margaret Singh, Reva Stone, Brendan Tang, Jer
Thorpe, Paul Wong

Some of the images and objects chosen from the collection make visible
ordinary and everyday actions, while others highlight some of the most
central conflicts and issues of our time, says Curator Jordan Strom. A
video work by Meera Margaret Singh titled Community Centre (2012)
celebrates the joy and spontaneity of human movement, capturing an informal
gathering of people dancing at a community centre. On the other end of the
spectrum, the photograph Oil Fields #22 (2001) by Edward Burtynsky depicts
a natural gas pipeline zigzagging through a northern British Columbia
landscape, calling to mind the current debate over the Trans Mountain
pipeline in Canada.

Elizabeth Hollicks large, vibrant paintings merge her personal stories"a
daughter giving birth, a husband suffering from a debilitating
illness"within imagery of nature, buildings, and construction sites from
her hometown of White Rock. Assistant Curator Rhys Edwards says,
Hollicks art manifests a joyful desire to transfer the medicalized body,
in all of its fleshy reality, into the public at large. In a world where
the interior of the body is a forbidden subject"both in art and
elsewhere"Hollick imagines an alternative, where it is literally monumental.

Visitors from the White Rock and South Surrey area may recognize themselves
or familiar faces in many of her figure paintings like Polar Bear Swim
White Rock (2009) and Pumpkin Carving (2007), drawn directly from her
experiences.
