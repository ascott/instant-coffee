MAIL ART GALLERY
UNTIMELY MISSIVES by Penelope Hetherington
Show runs until 28 April

Filling out a coupon clipped from a 1964 edition of a magazine and 
then mailing it in an envelope with a dime taped to piece of cardboard 
requires a certain optimism--an innocence not so much feigned as 
permitted. The process of undertaking this slightly goofy task produces 
affect that wants examining. Anticipation, excitement and a pleasant 
feeling of destabilization suggest an underlying reluctance to accept 
that the past has completely disappeared. The slowness of the post 
spares us the anticlimactic flatness of assuming that something is over 
and gone. Instead, we get suspense--the space between sending and 
receiving.

Untimely Missives uses the materiality of the postal system to look for 
lapsed time in geographical space, and the Mail Art Gallery as a place 
to document the search. This project investigates the possibility that 
playing puckish games with time is one way of remaining limber in 
relation to temporality. It has little to do with nostalgia.

Visitors are encouraged to send their own untimely mail. Envelopes, 
scissors, coin cardboard, copies of advertisements from old 
publications, and a working post office are provided. 

Penelope Hetherington is a Vancouver-based performer and 
installation artist with an ongoing interest in animating collapsed space.
