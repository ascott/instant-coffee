We are very excited to announce an extra-curricular event, featuring the return of VISR 
alum Dan Adleman, to take place at Dynamo Arts Association on December 20th at 
7pm, on the topic of:

Rhetoric, Ecology, Media

Words are like planets, each with its own gravitational pull.
"Kenneth Burke

" Rhetoric, ecological thought, and media theory are three overlapping approaches 
to material-semiotic environments. My talk will explore the stakes of thinking about 
rhetoric, ecology, and media (R.E.M.) as three interpenetrating environmental registers. 
Contemporary media theory experienced its Big Bang moment with the entry of 
Canadian scholar Marshall McLuhan into the dream theatre of popular culture. Still, not 
many of McLuhans readers are aware that he was a Cambridge-trained rhetorician 
whose PhD dissertation tracked and analyzed the historical exclusion of rhetoric by its 
rival disciplines. Indeed, as McLuhan, Innis, Havelock, and Derrida have observed, the 
western philosophical tradition more or less began with Socrates large-scale 
banishment of reading, writing, music, poetry, narrative, and democracy from his 
imagined philosophers republic on account of their power to contaminate the influence 
of philosophical Truth. Socrates also intermittently banished and subordinated rhetoric, 
the sophistic art of persuasion, on similar grounds. 
Rhetoric, which Socrates disparaged as a mere knack for pandering to a range of 
audiences, did not even constitute a legitimate craft or discipline (techne) and hardly 
merited theoretical consideration. The subsequent history of philosophical theory has 
involved a wide variety of reconfigurations and renegotiations of the field in relation to 
the disciplines indivisible remainders (including not only the aforementioned but also 
race, class, gender, animality, etc.). More recently, new rhetoricians working in the 
tradition of Kenneth Burke have endeavoured to bring rhetoric into a dehierarchized 
realignment with contemporary theory. And yet, rhetorical theory has been 
disappointingly slow to congeal with its two most kindred fields, media theory and 
ecology (or simply media ecology, the commingling of media and so-called nature).
In my paper, I will discuss this particular constellation: rhetoric (the study of 
symbolic environments), ecology (the study of natural environments), and media 
theory (the study of media-infrastructural environments) with a view to shepherding 
them into more comfortable cohabitation. It is my view that these bedfellows are not 
nearly as incompatible as weve been led to believe. They may even, in time, give birth 
to monstrous new theoretical sensibilities that we are currently barely able to envision. "
