Other Sights for Artists' Projects
INSTANT COFFEE: NOTHING HAPPENS IN GOOD WEATHER
 
Workshop on site, Saturday, May 26, 2012, 1:00 – 4:00 p.m.
Fraser Street and 46th Avenue, Vancouver, Canada

Please join us for the official launch of artist collective Instant Coffee’s Nothing Happens in Good Weather, a short-term public art project that activates a narrow empty lot on a busy section of South Fraser Street. With wide stripes, strident colour, and casual sculptural (stump) seating, Instant Coffee has transformed what was once a gap in the streetscape into an aesthetic and social space.

To coincide with the 10th Annual South Hill Festival, Instant Coffee will open a portable studio from 1:00 to 4:00 p.m. at the artwork site for stump preparation and button making. Paint it pink and take it away while supplies last.

Instant Coffee is a service oriented artist collective based in Toronto and Vancouver. They have an extensive art practice, spanning over 12 years. At present, their most active members are Jinhan Ko, Kelly Lycan, Jenifer Papararo, Khan Lee, Cecilia Berkovic and Kate Monro. As a collective, they have been invested in combining the social with the aesthetic, and as such have worked in public spaces to engage large audiences. They have shown internationally, have exhibited in many prominent art institutions, and have produced numerous projects in public space.

This project represents a unique partnership between Other Sights for Artists’ Projects and the South Hill BIA Public Art Committee (active members representing South Hill community groups, organizations, and businesses), in the commissioning of Instant Coffee to produce and present an artwork that addresses the goals and objectives of two different art initiatives: The South Hill Mural Project curated by Barbara Cole, and South Hill Surfaces coordinated by the South Hill BIA Public Art Committee

Other Sights gratefully acknowledges the support of the City of Vancouver 125th Mural Program.
The South Hill BIA Public Art Committee is grateful for project assistance through a City of Vancouver Community and Neighbourhood Art Development Grant.
