3101 Granville St, Vancouver BC

Sophia Boutsakis is an emerging visual artist of Greek and British descent. Born and
raised in North Vancouver, she is privileged to live, work, and create as a guest on
the traditional, ancestral, and unceded territory of the Squamish, Musqueam and
Tsleil-Waututh Nations. Boutsakis earned her Bachelor of Fine Arts from Emily Carr
University of Art + Design (2022). Her recent exhibitions include Mirrored Hearts, a
solo show at The Art Shop in 2024, and group exhibitions such as Interplay (2024)
and Familiar Faces (2023), also at The Art Shop. Her work was included in
Intermediate States (2022) on Granville Island as part of Capture Photography
Festival, and Take The Egg (2022) at ECUADs Neighborhood Gallery.

Boutsakis work employs repeated motifs as semiotic tools, transforming symbols into
glyphs, arrangements into sentences, and patterns into a distinct visual lexicon.
Alluding to architectural elements like railings, fences, and windows, these pieces
function as a screen between the viewer and physical space, layers of material, or
meaning. Hearts, diamonds, spirals, bows, butterflies, and flowers evoke themes of
love, nurturement, growth, and strength, while gates, fences, and windows suggest
transparency, liminality, fragility, neglect, and repair.

Paige Elton is an emerging visual artist based in
Vancouver, BC; she resides on the
traditional,
ancestral, and unceded territories of the Musqueam, Squamish and Tsliel-Watuth
Nations. Elton has recently graduated from the Emily Carr University of Art + Design
with her Bachelor
of Fine Art. She has recently been featured within two group exhibitions: Interplay, at
The Art
Shop (2024), and Take the Egg (2022) at Emily Carr Universitys Neighbourhood
Gallery.

Eltons work deploys both figurative and abstract modes of representation, through
mediums such as: drawing, painting, digital embroidery and whittling frames.
Often creating surreal scenes inspired by story book, folklore images. Notable
themes that take
place throughout her work are gender identity, theatrics, fertility, comedy, anxiety,
fantasy,
mortality, absurdity and existentialism.
