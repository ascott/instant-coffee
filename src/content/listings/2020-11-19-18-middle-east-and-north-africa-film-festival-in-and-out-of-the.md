Time and time again, Beirut faces loss, tragedy and upheavals. People contend with
the intensity of tragic events
while artists find ways to resist, remember and reclaim.
Renowned Lebanese artists and filmmakers Lamia Joreige, Joana Hadjithomas, Walid
Sadek, Ghassan Salhab and Canadian artist of Lebanese descent, Jayce Salloum
speak to the Lebanese War, loss and tragedy. In this panel discussion curated as a
casual conversation, these renowned artists will unpack how they represented war, its
aftermaths, and the on-going Lebanese condition. They share with us how they relate
to Beirut and its ever-changing landscape while reflecting on their roles as artists in
representing this problematic local history to the world.

Curated and moderated by Ghinwa Yassine (@arts.embodiment) and Mandana
Mansouri

About the panelists:

Lamia Joreige:

Born in Beirut in 1972, Lamia Joreige is a visual artist and film-maker who lives and
works in
Beirut. She uses archival documents and elements of fiction to reflect on
history and its possible narration, and on the relationship between individual stories
and collective memory. In her practice, rooted in her countrys experience, she
explores the possibilities of representing the Lebanese wars and their aftermath. Her
artworks and films have been presented in various international exhibitions and
festivals including: MAXXI and Fondazione Sandretto Re Rebaudengo (Italy); Centre
Pompidou, the San Francisco Museum of Modern Art (USA); Liverpool Biennial, Tate
Modern, Serpentine Gallery, Medfilm Festival, Rome (Awarded Best film), FID
Marseille and others.

Joana Hadjithomas:

Joana Hadjithomas is an artist and filmmaker. She collaborates with Khalil Joreige
producing cinematic and visual artworks that intertwine. For the last 15 years, they
have focused on the images, representations and history of their home country,
Lebanon, and questioned the fabrication of imaginaries in the
region and beyond. Their
films have been multi awarded in international festivals and enjoyed releases in many
countries. Their artworks have been shown in museums, biennials and art centers
around the world, such as Musee dart Moderne de la Ville de Paris, FNAC France, the
Guggenheim, New York, the Centre Georges Pompidou, France, V & A London, and
the Sharjah Art Foundation, UAE. They are the recipients of the 2017 Marcel Duchamp
Award.

Walid Sadek:

Walid Sadek is an artist and writer. He is professor in the Department of Fine Arts and
Art History at the American University of Beirut. Walid Sadek uses poetic and
metaphoric language to evoke on post war Lebanon. In 1999, he produced Bigger than
Picasso a tiny and unexpected book using word and image to criticize a political
situation in the country. Sadek, who has regularly collaborated with Beirut-based artist
centre Ashkal Alwan, has participated in numerous exhibitions and events worldwide.
In 2010, he presented his first solo
exhibition at the Beirut Art Center. He was guest
editor of the academic journal Third Text on issue 117, July 2012, titled "Not, Not Arab".

Ghassan Salhab:

In addition to making his own films, Ghassan Salhab collaborates and teaches film in
Lebanon. He has directed eight full-length films: Beyrouth Fantme, Terra Incognita,
The Last Man, 1958, The Mountain, The Valley, An open Rose/Warda and The river,
recently finished, in addition to numerous essays, and different video works,
including (Posthumous), Chinese ink, Son Image, and Le voyage immobile, with
Mohamed Soueid. La Rochelle International Film Festival, JC Carthage and La
Cinmathque du Qubec made a tribute to his work. He has also published different
texts and articles in various magazines, and a book, fragments du Livre du naufrage.

Jayce Salloum:

As if an itinerant partaker of conflicted territories (most everywhere), Salloum observes
the world and creates/collects images/texts to make meaning from. His projects are
rooted in an intimate engagement with place. A grandson of Syrian or Lebanese
immigrants he was born and raised on others land, the Sylix (Okanagan) territory.
Jayce works on the unceded stolen lands of the Xmkey"m, Swxw7mesh +
Sl"wta". Recognizing and acting on this is an everyday practice, but lets face it, he
could do a lot more. In/out of this context not that it really matters, Salloum has
lectured and published pervasively and exhibited peripatetically at the widest range of
local and international venues possible and most improbable, from the smallest
unnamed storefronts in his downtown eastside Vancouver neighbourhood to
institutions such as the Muse du Louvre, Museum of Modern Art, Guggenheim
Museum, Centre Georges Pompidou, National Gallery of Canada, and more.
