The Waldorf Launches a Month Long Screening of Fassbinder's 
Berlin Alexanderplatz with Introductory Talk by Stan Douglas.

The Waldorf and the Goethe-Institut are proud to present Rainer 
Werner Fassbinder’s legendary television program as part of our 
Sunday afternoon screening series, Day for Night. The thirteen 
episodes and epilogue will be screened through the month of April, 
four episodes every Sunday afternoon. The entire series will be 
launched Sunday, April 3rd with an introductory talk by renowned 
Vancouver artist Stan Douglas.

About Fassbinder and Alexanderplatz
Fassbinder was a German actor, screenwriter and influential and 
prolific filmaker. Berlin Alexanderplatz is considered one of 
Fassbinder's greatest works and is the culmination of his lifelong 
relationship with Alfred Doblin's novel of Berlin in the 1920s. The 
controversial, fifteen-hour film follows Franz Biberkopf as he 
attempts to seek redemption in Weimar-era Germany.

These screenings are a part of Art in April at The Waldorf, a month of 
art programming including gallery openings, film screenings, 
slideshows and artist talks. Every week. Alexanderplatz will screen 
alongside Michael Turner's installation, The Rolling Stones Trilogy.

About Art in April
Art in April at The Waldorf features works by Atilla Richard Lukacs, 
Paul Wong, Michael Turner, Simon Redekop, Sean Montgomery and 
Maya Beaudry with support from organizations and galleries 
including Presentation House Gallery, Unit/Pitt, The Cheaper Show, 
Off Main and The Goethe-Institut. It also marks the official opening of 
the new project space Black and Yellow.
