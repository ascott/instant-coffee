Macaulay & Co. Fine Art is pleased to again welcome a Ceremonial/Art project to 
the gallery with a presentation of paintings, drawings, plaster casts, and argillite 
carvings by the late Pat McGuire. 

Born in 1943 and raised in Skidegate, Queen Charlotte Islands, Pat was a 
member of the Staastas Eagle clan. His father, Sam Mcguire, was of Ojibwaay 
Irish decent and his mother, Nora Tulip, was Haida. Several mentors within Pat's 
family circle guided him to explore traditional Haida carving customs. These 
figures included his uncle Edmund Calder and another uncle, Joe Tulip, who 
provided the young Pat McGuire with books to study Haida carving conventions. 
Pat was also a descendant of Charles Edenshaw.

These early introductions to Haida carving books speak to the importance of 
transmitting knowledge after the interruptions of colonialism and the cultural 
genocide of First Nations peoples. When Pat moved to Vancouver in the 1960s 
he was faced with the challenges of becoming commercially viable while staying 
true to the customs of his ancestry. Much of Pat's practice reveals his adroit 
ability to incorporate both traditional design elements with the modernist formal 
energies that were occurring in the creative landscape around him.

Ceremonial/Art programs highlight the complicated relationship between the 
modern and the traditional within Northwest Coast art history with special 
attention paid to cultural production made towards the end of the Potlatch Ban 
(1885-1951) and the several decades that followed.
