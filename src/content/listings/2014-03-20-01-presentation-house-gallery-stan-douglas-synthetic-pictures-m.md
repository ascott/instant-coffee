March 21 to May 25, 2014

Stan Douglas: Synthetic Pictures

Opening Reception: Thursday, March 20, 7PM

Presentation House Gallery is proud to launch "Stan Douglas: 
Synthetic 
Pictures", an exhibition of new photographs by Vancouver artist Stan 
Douglas. 

This exhibition marks the artist's first foray into abstract imagery. 
"Corrupt Files" is a suite of photographic scans extracted from 
Douglas' film works, reduced to patterns of data. These synthetic 
images capture a sense a 'ghost in the machine' interfering with 
electronic information. The exhibition features a large-scale black 
and white photographic panorama depicting postwar Hogan's Alley 
in Strathcona. Distilled from the artist's new online web application 
"Circa 1948"--which constructs an immersive experience of Hogan's 
Alley and the Hotel Vancouver during the postwar period--the 
digitally reconstructed scene blurs distinctions between artifice and 
realism. The exhibition also includes archival images and 
photographs in the style of postwar news photography from Douglas' 
recent "Midcentury Studio" series, and reference his cinematic stage 
production "Helen Lawrence", a multimedia film noir thriller about the 
social tensions and corruption in postwar Vancouver.

Stan Douglas was born in 1960 in Vancouver, where he continues to 
live and 
work. He graduated from Emily Carr College of Art in the early 1980s. 
His solo 
exhibitions include: the Studio Museum in Harlem, New York (2005) 
and the 
Staatsgalerie and Württembergischer Kunstverein in Stuttgart (2007). 
He has 
participated in group exhibitions worldwide, including Documenta IX, 
1992; X, 
1997; and XI, 2002 as well as the Venice Biennale in 1990, 2001 and 
2005. 
He is represented by David Zwirner Gallery, New York/London and 
Victoria 
Miro, London. 

Two new books on Stan Douglas, published by Prestel and Steidl, 
will be 
launched in the coming months and the world premiere of "Helen 
Lawrence" 
is currently at the Stanley Theatre, Vancouver. 

Research for this exhibition, as well as "Helen Lawrence" and "Circa 
1948", 
was supported through financial assistance from a grant from Arts 
Partners 
in Creative Development.
