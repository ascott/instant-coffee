SUSAN MACWILLIAM

Susan MacWilliam is a visual artist from Belfast, Ireland who will give a
lecture on her body of work concerned with histories that pertain to the
paranormal, the supernatural and perceptual phenomena. Over the past
decade, she has made artworks about mediums, table tilters, optograms,
trance and clairvoyance. Through archival research and direct contact, she
investigates the bizarre and extraordinary powers of legendary figures such
as the x-ray vision of mystic Kuda Bux, the ectoplasmic materializations of
Helen Duncan, the fingertip vision of Rosa Kuleshova, and the dermo optical
perception of Madame Duplessis. MacWilliam's interest in the idea that

8:30, April 30, An Invocation

The exhibition opening of Not Necessarily in that Order will be launched by
a short music performance by Vancouver visual artist, performer and
musician, Carol Sawyer. An Invocation will be a form of improvised
explanatory notes in response to the exhibition. Sawyer's work as a
vocalist is notable for its expressive inventiveness and broad palette of
vocal colours and techniques blending extemporized texts, polyphonic
chords, common sounds, and spontaneous melodies. She will be accompanied by
bass player Dave Chakroun. Carol Sawyer performs improvised music with her
quartet ion Zoo and recently released her second CD Venus Looks Good with
ion Zoo. Her artworks have been exhibited widely and she is represented by
Republic Gallery, Vancouver.

This exhibition considers the disjunctives of narrative through
contemporary media art. The title refers to filmmaker Jean-Luc Godard's
dictum that a story should have a beginning, middle and an end, but not
necessarily in that order. Similar to Godard's experiments, the artists in
this exhibition embrace contradiction to involve the viewer in an active
process of making sense. Unfolding through elliptical structures of
repetition and dissonance, the stories here tend to run amok and at times
veer towards the irrational. Referencing the conventions and clich's of
film noir, melodrama, documentary reportage and music videos, these artists
acknowledge that the act of telling becomes part of the story, even if the
narrator is understood to be unreliable. Key to their works is the act of
enunciation whereby spoken language and sound itself takes on visual qualities.

The performative tone of the exhibition is signaled by the 1976 video
performance, The Ballad of Dan Peoples by Canadian artist Lisa Steele's
emphatic storytelling. The frenetic rhythms and rapid jump-cuts of American
artist Aida Ruilova's low-tech videos generate a sense of discomfort as in
her version of the German expressionist play Lulu. The short films of
Keren Cytter, an Israeli artist, novelist and dance company director living
in Berlin, are disturbing psychodramas as in Untitled (2009), a remake of
John Cassavetes film Opening Night, that elides stage and backstage,
artifice and authenticity. Rossella Biscotti (Italian living in Holland)
and Susan MacWilliam from Belfast, Ireland both use documentary interview
formats as a form of portraiture to explore the limitations of testimony.
Stylistically reminiscent of film noir, Biscotti's film The Undercover Man
(2008) is based on firsthand testimony of former special FBI agent Joseph
Pistone who successfully infiltrated the New York City mafia in the guise
of a jewel thief named Donnie Brasco. Susan MacWilliam also assumes the
